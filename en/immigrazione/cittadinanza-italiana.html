Italian citizenship is the recognition of a belonging built over time.
Do not let a refusal deny it.

Italian citizenship is not a privilege: it is the legal recognition of genuine roots, of years of contributions paid, of children raised in Italian schools, of a life spent in this country. Law 91/1992 provides multiple routes of access — by residency, by marriage, by descent, by birth — but the administrative practice of the Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell’Interno) is often opaque, slow, and marked by unjustified refusals. Our firm assists throughout the entire acquisition procedure and, when the Ministry denies what the law guarantees, brings the case before the court.

Regulatory framework

Italian citizenship under Law 91/1992

L. 91/1992 — Art. 9 — Citizenship by ten-year residency

The requirement of ten-year legal residence

Article 9, paragraph 1, letter f) of Law No. 91 of 5 February 1992 provides that a foreign national may acquire Italian citizenship by decree of the President of the Republic when they have legally resided in Italy for at least ten years. The residence must be «legal», which implies continuous lawful stay documented by residence permits (permessi di soggiorno) and civil registry residence certificates (certificati di residenza anagrafica). The application (istanza) is submitted to the Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell’Interno) and must be accompanied by documentation proving: the absence of disqualifying criminal records, the availability of adequate income (currently not less than approximately 8,000 euros per year for the applicant), and continuous residence. The time limit for concluding the procedure is set at 730 days from the submission of the application (art. 9-bis L. 91/1992, as amended by L. 132/2018). Failure to comply with the deadline justifies an appeal against administrative silence (ricorso per silenzio-inadempimento) before the competent Regional Administrative Court (TAR). The Court of Cassation (Corte di Cassazione) has reiterated that ordinary courts have jurisdiction over citizenship matters, with the sole exception of disputes concerning the exercise of the Ministry’s discretionary power.

L. 91/1992 — Art. 5 — Citizenship by marriage

Acquisition of citizenship by right of marriage (iure matrimonii)

Article 5 of Law 91/1992 governs the acquisition of citizenship by the foreign spouse of an Italian citizen. The provision allows the foreign spouse to apply for citizenship after two years of legal residence in Italy from the date of marriage, or after three years from the date of marriage if residing abroad. These time limits are halved where there are children born to or adopted by the spouses. The granting of citizenship is not automatic: it requires a naturalisation decree and verification of the absence of disqualifying circumstances (cause ostative), including final criminal convictions for intentional offences punishable by a statutory penalty of not less than three years, reasons concerning the security of the Republic, or dissolution, annulment, or termination of the civil effects of marriage. Case law has progressively broadened protections against refusals based on generic or disproportionate grounds, requiring the Public Administration (Pubblica Amministrazione) to carry out an individualised assessment.

L. 91/1992 — Art. 3 — By descent (iure sanguinis) and Art. 4 co. 2 — Birth in Italy

Citizenship by descent and by birth in the territory

Article 3 of Law 91/1992 establishes the principle of ius sanguinis as the primary criterion for granting Italian citizenship: a child whose father or mother is a citizen is a citizen by birth. This principle, inherited from the Civil Code of 1865, allows citizenship to be recognised for descendants of Italian emigrants of any generation, provided that the chain of transmission was not interrupted by foreign naturalisation prior to the birth of the Italian descendant. Article 4, paragraph 2, on the other hand, governs the acquisition of citizenship for a foreign national born in Italy: if they have legally and continuously resided in Italy since birth, they may declare their intent to acquire citizenship within one year of reaching the age of majority. A late declaration cannot be remedied: the deadline upon reaching the eighteenth year is mandatory, and failure to meet it precludes this avenue of access. Our firm monitors and manages the procedure from the client’s minority so as not to miss the deadline.

Art. 3 (iure sanguinis) — Art. 4 para. 2 (18 years) — L. 91/1992

The 730-day time limit: the Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell’Interno) has 730 days to rule on an application for citizenship by residence or by marriage. Once this period has elapsed without a decision, the silence constitutes a failure to act (silenzio-inadempimento) and the applicant may apply to the Regional Administrative Court (TAR) to obtain an order compelling the Ministry to act. For a foreign national born in Italy, the deadline to declare the intent to acquire citizenship under Art. 4, para. 2 expires upon reaching 19 years of age: do not miss this crucial deadline.

Disqualifying grounds for the recognition of citizenship. Law 91/1992 provides in Art. 6 an exhaustive list of disqualifying grounds (cause ostative): final convictions for intentional offences punishable by a statutory maximum term of imprisonment of not less than three years, convictions for offences referred to in Arts. 380 and 381 of the Italian Code of Criminal Procedure (c.p.p.) (mandatory or discretionary arrest in flagrante delicto), convictions for offences against the personality of the State, and reasons pertaining to the security of the Republic. However, case law has clarified that the existence of a criminal record does not automatically entail refusal: the Ministry must assess the gravity of the act, the time that has elapsed, subsequent conduct, and the applicant’s level of integration.

Which pathway applies to you

Avenues of access to Italian citizenship

Italian citizenship can be acquired automatically (by birth, adoption) or through a decree of naturalisation following an application (istanza). Each pathway has specific requirements, timeframes, and legal remedies. We identify the appropriate course of action and handle the entire procedure.

Art. 9 lett. f)

By ten-year residence

Available to foreign nationals who have legally resided in Italy for at least ten continuous years. It requires documented minimum income, the absence of disqualifying criminal grounds, and a cumulative civil registry residence certificate (certificato cumulativo di residenza anagrafica). The application (istanza) is submitted online. The procedure has a maximum duration of 730 days. In the event of administrative silence (silenzio-inadempimento), the Regional Administrative Court (TAR) may order the Ministry to issue a decision within a mandatory time limit.

Procedure: up to 730 days
Art. 5

By marriage

Available to the foreign spouse of an Italian citizen after two years of legal residence in Italy from the date of marriage (three years if residing abroad). The time limits are halved in the presence of children. The marriage must be valid and not dissolved as of the date of the decree. Naturalisation is not automatic: it requires verification of all statutory requirements by the Prefecture (Prefettura) and the Ministry.

Waiting time: 2 years (residence) — 3 years (abroad)
Art. 3

By right of blood (iure sanguinis)

Recognition of citizenship for the descendant of an Italian citizen who emigrated abroad. There are no generational limits, but the chain of transmission must not have been broken by foreign naturalisation of the ancestor prior to the birth of the Italian descendant. It requires the reconstruction of the family tree using Italian and foreign civil status records. The procedure takes place before the competent Italian Consulate or before the Court of Rome (Tribunale di Roma).

Procedure: variable (1-5 years)
Art. 4 para. 2

By birth in Italy (18 years)

A foreign national born in Italy and legally and continuously residing there since birth may acquire citizenship by declaring their intent within one year of reaching the age of majority. The deadline is mandatory: once the nineteenth year of age has elapsed, this channel is irrevocably closed. Our firm manages the procedure from the client’s minority, ensuring that the declaration is submitted within the time limits and with complete documentation.

Mandatory deadline: by 19 years of age
Dual

Dual citizenship

Italy does not require its citizens to renounce foreign citizenship and does not recognise the automatic loss of Italian citizenship through foreign naturalisation (if occurred after 15 August 1992). Individuals with dual citizenship fully enjoy all rights associated with both nationalities. We assist clients in verifying their position, documenting dual citizen status, and managing relations with both legal systems.

Compatible with most legal systems
Art. 13

Reacquisition

Anyone who has lost Italian citizenship (for instance, as a result of foreign naturalisation prior to 1992, or through voluntary renunciation) may, under certain conditions, reacquire it. Art. 13 of Law 91/1992 provides various methods of reacquisition: through military service, through public employment, or by a declaration of intent coupled with residence or establishment of domicile in Italy. We assess the viable path of reacquisition on a case-by-case basis and manage the entire documentary procedure.

Procedure: 6–24 months depending on the channel

How we intervene

Strategies for obtaining citizenship or challenging a refusal

The procedure for acquiring Italian citizenship is technically complex and often characterised by unjustified delays and refusals. Our firm assists at all stages: from compiling the documentary file to judicial protection in the event of rejection or failure to act (silenzio-inadempimento).

I.

Compilation of the file and verification of requirements

Before submitting any application, we analyse the client’s legal position: verification of continuous residence, checks on disqualifying criminal records, calculation of taxable income in the relevant years, and verification of the lineage of transmission for iure sanguinis. An incomplete or improperly compiled file is the primary cause of avoidable refusals. Our assistance begins here.

II.

Submission of the application and monitoring

We handle the preparation and electronic submission of the application to the Ministry of the Interior via the ALI portal (Acquisto della cittadinanza per gli Italiani all’estero) or through the competent Prefecture (Prefettura) based on residence. We monitor the progress of the procedure, highlighting any anomalies. If the procedure stalls without justification, we initiate formal reminders prior to taking legal action before the courts.

III.

Appeal against express refusal

When the Ministry issues a decree of rejection, we challenge it before the Tribunale di Roma — which is the ordinary court with exclusive jurisdiction over matters of citizenship by residence and marriage — or before the Regional Administrative Court of Lazio (TAR del Lazio) for discretionary measures. The appeal (ricorso) challenges the inadequacy of the statement of reasons, the breach of the principle of proportionality, and the failure to carry out an individualised assessment of the factors favourable to the applicant.

IV.

Appeal against failure to act (730 days)

Once the statutory time limit of 730 days has elapsed without the Ministry having issued a decree granting or refusing citizenship, the failure to act of the Public Administration (silenzio-inadempimento) is established. We lodge an appeal (ricorso) before the TAR del Lazio to obtain a judgment establishing the Ministry’s obligation to issue a decision within a mandatory time limit set by the court. In the event of non-compliance, we initiate enforcement proceedings (giudizio di ottemperanza) with the appointment of a court commissioner (commissario ad acta).

V.

Iure sanguinis proceedings before the Tribunale di Roma

When the Consulate refuses or delays the recognition of citizenship by descent, the judicial route lies through the Tribunale di Roma, which has exclusive jurisdiction over disputes concerning the civil status of Italian citizens residing abroad. We manage the entire procedure: collection of Italian and foreign civil status records, legalisations and apostilles, sworn translation, and an appeal (ricorso) to the Court for the recognition of citizen status.

VI.

Protection of minors and declaration pursuant to art. 4 paragraph 2

For minors born in Italy to foreign parents, we monitor the deadline for the declaration of intent to acquire citizenship upon reaching eighteen years of age. The declaration must be submitted within the year following reaching the age of majority: a seemingly straightforward formality that nonetheless requires precise documentation and which, if omitted, cannot be rectified. We manage monitoring and timely submission for every minor client we assist.

Ministerial practice and circulars. The Ministry of the Interior periodically issues interpretative circulars affecting the handling of citizenship procedures. Circular no. 666 of 7 January 2020 amended the assessment criteria for the income requirement. Our firm remains continuously updated on the latest ministerial practices and case law, ensuring legal assistance fully aligned with regulatory developments.

5 cases handled and resolved

Case no. 1

Failure to act after 1,100 days — TAR Lazio, order to issue a decision

TAR Lazio, Roma, Sezione II-quater — 2024 — Citizenship by ten-year residence

A Filipino citizen, who had been residing in Italy for sixteen years with a residence permit for domestic work (permesso di soggiorno per lavoro domestico), had submitted an application for citizenship based on ten years of residence through the web portal of the Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell’Interno) in November 2020. The application was accompanied by complete documentation: a cumulative certificate of residence (certificato di residenza cumulativo) certifying continuous presence since lawful entry into Italy, tax returns (dichiarazione dei redditi) for the last three years showing a taxable income exceeding the minimum required threshold, Italian and foreign criminal record certificates (certificato del casellario giudiziale) free of any entries, and bank account statements demonstrating financial stability. Despite the completeness of the file, after 730 days from submission — the maximum time limit provided for by art. 9-bis L. 91/1992 introduced by L. 132/2018 — the Ministry had not yet issued a decision. At the time our mandate was granted, 1,100 days had already elapsed without any decision being taken.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the TAR del Lazio for unlawful failure to act (silenzio-inadempimento) pursuant to art. 117 c.p.a., requesting the court to establish the obligation of the Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell’Interno) to rule on the application within a period not exceeding 30 days from the publication of the judgment. In the appeal, we documented that the statutory deadline of 730 days had been exceeded, the completeness of the file, the absence of any request for supplementary documentation by the Public Administration, and the existential damage suffered by the applicant who, in the meantime, had lost job mobility opportunities within the European Union reserved for EU citizens. The TAR upheld the appeal with judgment no. 5821/2024, establishing the unlawful failure to act (silenzio-inadempimento) and ordering the Ministry to issue a decision within 30 days, appointing the Prefect of Rome (Prefetto di Roma) as court-appointed commissioner (commissario ad acta) in the event of further non-compliance. The Ministry complied with the judgment, issuing the decree granting citizenship within the prescribed time limit.

Unlawful failure to act established — Citizenship granted
Case no. 2

Refusal based on extinguished criminal record: proportionality violated — Tribunale di Roma

Tribunale Civile di Roma, Sezione I — 2023 — Citizenship by marriage

A Brazilian citizen, married to an Italian citizen for six years, had submitted an application for citizenship by marriage pursuant to art. 5 L. 91/1992 after completing the two years of lawful residence in Italy required by law. The Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell’Interno) had issued a rejection decree based on a criminal record: a suspended sentence for driving under the influence dating back seven years earlier, an intentional offence punishable by a maximum statutory penalty of less than the three years of imprisonment provided for by art. 6 L. 91/1992 as a disqualifying ground. The decree contained no specific assessment of the seriousness of the offence, the applicant’s subsequent conduct, or his degree of integration into Italian society. Our client had two minor Italian children, was the owner of a lawfully established sole proprietorship, paid social security contributions, and had no further criminal record.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the Tribunale Civile di Roma — the ordinary court with jurisdiction over citizenship matters pursuant to art. 3 L. 91/1992 — arguing that the alleged offence did not exceed the statutory threshold provided by law as a disqualifying ground and that, in any event, the decree was tainted by a lack of reasoning for failing to assess the applicant’s position concretely and individually. We cited the case law of the Court of Cassation (Cass. Civ., Sez. I, n. 11274/2021), which requires the Public Administration to carry out a proportionate and individualised assessment even when disqualifying grounds abstractly provided for by law are present. The Court granted the appeal (ricorso), annulling the decree of denial and declaring the applicant’s right to Italian citizenship, ordering the Ministry to issue the relevant decree of conferral within 60 days of communication of the judgment.

Denial annulled — Citizenship by marriage conferred
Case no. 3

Iure sanguinis denied by the Consulate: judicial recognition — Tribunale di Roma

Tribunale Civile di Roma, Sezione I — 2024 — Citizenship iure sanguinis

An Argentine citizen, a third-generation descendant of a Calabrian emigrant who left for South America in 1912, had submitted an application for recognition of Italian citizenship iure sanguinis at the Italian Consulate in Buenos Aires. The Consulate had rejected the application, asserting that the Italian ancestor had become naturalised as an Argentine citizen in 1930, prior to the birth of his son in 1934, and that the chain of transmission of citizenship had therefore been broken. The applicant disputed this finding: she produced documentation showing that her grandfather had acquired Argentine citizenship on a date subsequent to the birth of her father, and that on the date of his son’s birth he was therefore still an Italian citizen, with the consequent transmission iure sanguinis. The issue came down to the exact dating of the ancestor’s Argentine naturalisation, to be resolved through Argentine archives and Calabrian civil status registers (registri dello stato civile).

We filed an appeal (ricorso) with the Tribunale Civile di Roma, which has exclusive jurisdiction over disputes concerning the citizenship of residents abroad. We reconstructed the family tree using birth, marriage, and death certificates spanning three generations, and obtained, legalised, and translated the ancestor’s Argentine naturalisation certificate, certifying 1935 as the certain date — subsequent to the birth of his son (1934). Following the documentary examination, the Court granted the appeal (ricorso) by judgment of 12 March 2024, declaring the appellant’s right to Italian citizenship iure sanguinis, ordering the Civil Status Registrar (Ufficiale dello Stato Civile) of the ancestor’s municipality of origin to make the consequent entries in the registers, and directing the Consulate in Buenos Aires to issue the Italian passport to the applicant.

Citizenship iure sanguinis recognised — Italian passport obtained
Case no. 4

Declaration pursuant to art. 4 co. 2 submitted late: mandatory deadline and remedies

Out-of-court legal advice and management — 2023 — Born in Italy — Acquisition of citizenship

A young Moroccan citizen, born in Naples in 2002 to parents who had entered Italy lawfully in 1995 and had always resided lawfully, contacted our firm at the age of 21, convinced that he had irretrievably lost the right to acquire Italian citizenship under art. 4 co. 2 L. 91/1992, having failed to submit the declaration by his nineteenth year of age. The mandatory deadline had expired in 2021. The young man had spent his entire life in Italy: primary, middle, and high school in Italy, a high school diploma obtained in Naples, and enrolment at Università Federico II. He had never lived in his parents’ country of origin. His residence permit (permesso di soggiorno), previously for «minors» («minore età»), had been renewed upon reaching 18 years of age for «pending employment» («attesa occupazione»), with the associated bureaucratic difficulties. He had unsuccessfully attempted to submit the late declaration to the Municipality of Naples (Comune di Napoli), which had rejected it.

We analysed the situation in all its aspects: the loss of the channel under art. 4 co. 2 was actual and irreversible due to the failure to submit the declaration within the deadline. However, we identified alternative pathways: the young man could qualify for citizenship through continuous ten-year residence under art. 9 lett. f) in 2025, or submit an application for discretionary naturalisation under art. 9 lett. b) (five-year residence for non-stateless persons) as the child of long-term residents. We handled the procedure for issuing the residence permit for pending employment (permesso per attesa occupazione), which subsequently transitioned into a residence permit for work (permesso per lavoro) after he obtained his first job, and monitored the completion of the five-year residence requirement for the application under art. 9. The client submitted the citizenship application in 2025, assisted at every step. The case demonstrates that losing one channel does not always equate to the definitive loss of the right.

Alternative pathway identified — Application under art. 9 submitted
Case no. 5

Denial due to interruption of residence: alternative documentation accepted — Ministero dell’Interno

Administrative proceedings on review — 2024 — Citizenship by residence

A Ukrainian citizen, resident in Italy for twelve years, had received a decree of denial of citizenship by ten-year residence. The Ministry’s statement of reasons referred to an alleged interruption of lawful residence: for a period of approximately eight months in 2016-2017, the applicant had stayed in Italy with an expired residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) pending renewal, during which he was technically without a valid title despite being physically present in the territory and having regularly submitted the application for renewal of the permit. The Ministry had classified this period as an «unlawful stay» and therefore as interrupting the calculation of the ten-year residence. The logical consequence of the Ministry’s reasoning would have required resetting the count of the years of residence starting from the irregularity, leaving the client not yet in possession of the ten-year requirement.

We submitted an administrative request for review (istanza di riesame), documenting that the renewal application had been submitted in full compliance with the statutory deadlines: pursuant to Ministry of the Interior Circular no. 3/2000 and the established case law of the Council of State (Consiglio di Stato, Sez. III, n. 1765/2019), the timely submission of the renewal application entails the tacit extension of the validity of the expiring permit, which retains full legal validity pending the conclusion of the renewal procedure. The period in question therefore did not constitute an interruption of lawful residence, but a period covered by automatic extension. We produced the postal receipt for the submitted renewal application, the postmark certifying the date of dispatch, and the permit subsequently issued by the Police Commissioner (Questore) with retroactive effect from the date of the application. On review, the Ministry accepted our arguments, revoking the decree of denial and granting citizenship by presidential decree.

Denial revoked under administrative review (autotutela) — Citizenship conferred
