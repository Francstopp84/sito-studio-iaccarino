Applicable Legislation

The legal framework of the Flows Decree

Entry into Italy for work purposes is governed by the Consolidated Act on Immigration (Testo Unico sull’Immigrazione - D.Lgs. 286/1998) and by the Flows Decree (DPCM Flussi), which sets the maximum quotas for permitted entries each year (or on a multi-year basis). The system provides for a centralised electronic procedure — the so-called click day — which exhausts the available quotas within a matter of minutes or hours, leaving out thousands of workers and employers who would have been entitled to entry. Applications rejected or lapsed on technical or formal grounds may be appealed. Highly qualified workers have a privileged channel through the EU Blue Card (Carta Blu UE).

Art. 3 TUI — Migration policy Art. 22 TUI — Subordinate employment Art. 27 TUI — Particular cases Annual Flows Decree (DPCM Flussi) Legislative Decree 108/2012 — EU Blue Card

Quota or no quota? Art. 27 TUI establishes categories of workers who may enter outside the quota system: professional athletes, performing arts workers, managers and highly qualified personnel of companies with registered offices in Italy, nurses, researchers, and domestic workers of lawful foreign residents. Ascertaining whether your situation falls within these categories is the first fundamental step.

Our approach

Defense strategies

Every immigration quota decree (decreto flussi) case requires a distinct strategy, tailored to the specific situation of the worker and the employer. Here are the six pillars of our approach.

01

Advance preparation for click day

Even before the Immigration Quota Decree (DPCM Flussi) is published in the Official Gazette (Gazzetta Ufficiale), we prepare all required documentation: compliant employment contract, INPS/INAIL records, pre-filled forms, employer's SPID/CIE credentials. A one-second delay can result in losing the quota.

02

Appeal (ricorso) against rejection

The denial of the work clearance (nulla osta) is not final. We analyze the grounds for rejection — missing documentation, contract incompatibility, income requirements — and prepare the appeal (ricorso) to the Regional Administrative Court (TAR) with an application for interim relief to prevent the worker from being forced to leave Italy pending judgment.

03

Verification of available quota

Before initiating the procedure, we verify in real time the status of available quotas by professional category and the worker's country of origin. If ordinary quotas are exhausted, we evaluate alternative channels: extra-quota pursuant to art. 27 of the Consolidated Immigration Act (TUI), supplementary quota decrees (decreto flussi bis), bilateral agreements.

04

Conversion from study to work

A student who has completed a degree course in Italy has the right to convert their study permit into a work permit, including under the immigration quota decree (decreto flussi). We verify the requirements (registration with a professional register, job offer, etc.) and handle the conversion with the competent Police Headquarters (Questura).

05

Highly qualified workers — EU Blue Card (Carta Blu UE)

For workers holding a university degree and a qualified job offer, the EU Blue Card (Carta Blu UE, Legislative Decree 108/2012) allows entry outside ordinary quotas. We verify income requirements (minimum salary 1.5 times the average gross salary), manage the procedure, and provide assistance in case of refusal.

06

Employer documentation

The employer must be up to date with social security contributions and have no convictions for labor exploitation offenses. We assist the company in preparing the documentation required by the Single Desk (Sportello Unico): compliant certificate of social security compliance (DURC), tax returns, contract compliant with National Collective Bargaining Agreements (CCNL), availability of suitable accommodation for the worker.

Results achieved

Five specific cases

Real stories of workers and employers whom we have assisted through the complexities of the immigration quotas decree (decreto flussi). Names have been modified for confidentiality reasons.

Case 01

Work clearance (nulla osta) rejected for non-compliant contract — Regional Administrative Court (Tribunale Amministrativo Regionale)

Sector: construction — Origin: Morocco — Procedure: DPCM Flussi 2022

A Neapolitan construction company had submitted an application for a work clearance (nulla osta al lavoro) for a Moroccan bricklayer under the 2022 flows decree (decreto flussi). The Unified Immigration Desk (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione) of the Prefecture (Prefettura) had rejected the application, noting an alleged discrepancy between the salary indicated in the employment contract (1.450 euro per month) and the pay scale minimums under the applied National Collective Bargaining Agreement for the Construction Industry (CCNL Edilizia Industria), claiming that the salary was lower than the contractual minimums.

The analysis of the applied collective agreement (CCNL) revealed that the Prefecture (Prefettura) had used an incorrect job classification level: the worker had been classified as a “third-level qualified worker”, whereas the contract correctly classified him as a “second-level skilled worker”, with lower pay scale minimums. The appeal (ricorso) to the Regional Administrative Court of Campania (TAR Campania) documented the error through a technical appraisal by an employment consultant (consulente del lavoro), a detailed analysis of the applicable CCNL pay scales, and proof that the contractual salary was even higher than the minimum prescribed for the correct classification. The Regional Administrative Court (TAR), granting the petition for interim relief, suspended the rejection measure and subsequently, on the merits, annulled the denial, ordering the administration to issue the work clearance (nulla osta) within sixty days. The worker was able to enter Italy and regularize his status.

Work clearance (nulla osta) issued — TAR Campania
Case 02

Click day: application lapsed due to portal malfunction — Extraordinary appeal (Ricorso straordinario)

Sector: agriculture — Origin: Tunisia — Procedure: Click Day 2023

An agricultural company in Campania had initiated the submission procedure for the work clearance (nulla osta) application for three Tunisian seasonal workers at 9:00 sharp on the click day. The Ministry of the Interior portal, overloaded with simultaneous connections, repeatedly returned an error message, preventing transmission. At 9:47, when the connection was finally stable, the quota allocated for the “seasonal agricultural work” category was exhausted.

The case involved elements of administrative liability for the malfunction of the IT system operated by the Ministry. We gathered documentary evidence of the malfunction: screenshots of the error message with timestamps, screen recordings during the submission attempt, and connection logs from the company's internet provider certified by a digital notary. An extraordinary appeal to the President of the Republic (ricorso straordinario al Presidente della Repubblica) pursuant to art. 8 D.P.R. 1199/1971 was filed, seeking the retroactive admission of the application. In parallel, an application for administrative self-redress (autotutela) was submitted to the Ministry of the Interior, documenting the objective impossibility of transmission. In the self-redress (autotutela) phase, the Ministry acknowledged the portal malfunction and admitted the applications, allowing the subsequent issuance of the work clearance (nulla osta) for all three workers.

Application admitted under administrative self-redress (autotutela) — Work clearance (nulla osta) issued
Case 03

Conversion of study permit into work permit — Outside quotas under art. 6 TUI

Sector: information technology — Origin: India — Procedure: Conversion outside quotas

An Indian citizen, who graduated with honors in Computer Engineering from Università Federico II di Napoli, had received a job offer as a software developer from a tech company in the Naples area, with an open-ended contract and a gross annual salary of 38.000 euro. The Police Headquarters (Questura) of Naples had rejected the application to convert the study permit into a subordinate employment permit, citing the exhaustion of quotas under the flows decree (decreto flussi) in force.

The appellant, however, fell within the category of “highly qualified workers” pursuant to D.Lgs. 108/2012 (EU Blue Card / Carta Blu UE): the master's degree in a STEM discipline, the qualified job offer, and the salary exceeding the minimum requirement (equal to 1.5 times the average gross salary calculated by ISTAT) were all satisfied. The appeal (ricorso) to the Justice of the Peace (Giudice di Pace), competent in the matter pursuant to art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011, demonstrated that the Police Headquarters (Questura) had erroneously applied the ordinary quota regime, ignoring the special EU Blue Card (Carta Blu UE) route, which is structurally outside the quotas. The Justice of the Peace (Giudice di Pace) upheld the appeal and ordered the Police Headquarters (Questura) to re-examine the application by applying the rules governing the EU Blue Card (Carta Blu UE). The Police Headquarters (Questura) subsequently issued the permit for highly qualified work.

EU Blue Card (Carta Blu UE) issued — Giudice di Pace Napoli
Case 04

Employer with irregular social security contribution status (DURC) at the time of click day — Settlement and reopening

Sector: restaurant and catering — Origin: Bangladesh — Procedure: Flows Decree (DPCM Flussi) 2023

A Neapolitan restaurant owner had submitted an application for a work clearance (nulla osta) for a Bangladeshi cook specializing in the cuisine of his country of origin. The Prefecture (Prefettura) had rejected the application, observing that at the time of the click day the company was non-compliant according to the Single Document of Contribution Regularity (DURC - Documento Unico di Regolarità Contributiva), with an outstanding liability toward INPS of approximately 3.200 euro concerning unpaid contributions from the previous quarter.

The analysis of the social security contribution status showed that the debt was already subject to an installment plan duly authorized by INPS prior to the click day, with the installment arrangement approved and the initial installments paid. The case law of the Council of State (Consiglio di Stato) has clarified that an authorized and regularly honored installment plan is equivalent to contribution regularity for the purposes of the DURC (Cons. Stato, Sez. V, n. 2279/2021). We filed an appeal (ricorso) with the Regional Administrative Court (TAR), producing the INPS installment decision, the receipts of payments made, and the subsequent compliant DURC issued. The Regional Administrative Court (TAR) stayed the refusal on an interim basis and, on the merits, annulled it, ruling that at the time of the application the employer was to be considered in a position of contribution regularity. The work clearance (nulla osta) was subsequently issued and the Bangladeshi cook was able to take up his employment.

Work clearance (nulla osta) issued — TAR Campania
Case 05

Entry outside quotas for registered nurse — Art. 27-ter TUI

Sector: healthcare — Origin: Romania (non-EU pre-2007) / Ukraine — Procedure: Extra-quota work authorization under art. 27-ter

A private clinic accredited with the National Health Service (SSN) had hired a Ukrainian registered nurse who was a temporary refugee in Italy pursuant to Directive 2001/55/EC following the outbreak of the conflict, holding a regular degree in nursing from a Ukrainian university and professional qualification recognized by the Order of Nursing Professions (OPI - Ordine delle Professioni Infermieristiche) of Naples. The clinic intended to stabilize the employment relationship, also in view of the expiry of temporary protection, by converting the status into a stable residence permit for work purposes (permesso di soggiorno per lavoro). The Police Headquarters (Questura) had initially directed them toward the ordinary immigration quotas procedure (decreto flussi), with waiting times potentially extending to years.

Art. 27-ter TUI explicitly provides that healthcare professionals included in shortage lists may enter Italy outside the ordinary immigration quotas (decreto flussi), upon a simple request by the employer and subject to the clearance (nulla osta) of the competent Regional Authority certifying the staff shortage. Nursing is consistently included in the shortage lists of the National Health Service (SSN). We assisted the clinic in submitting the application for an extra-quota clearance (nulla osta fuori quota) to the Prefecture (Prefettura), attaching the regional shortage certification, the professional’s registration with the OPI, and an employment contract compliant with the National Collective Bargaining Agreement for Private Healthcare (CCNL Sanità Privata). The Immigration Single Desk (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione) issued the clearance (nulla osta) in eighteen days. The professional obtained a residence permit for healthcare professions, without having to wait for the next annual quota application day (click day).

Extra-quota work clearance (nulla osta) issued — Art. 27-ter TUI
