Art. 10 co. 3 Constitution  •  1951 Geneva Convention

Has the Commission rejected your asylum application?
The denial can be appealed. Protection is a right.

The right of asylum is enshrined in Art. 10 co. 3 of the Italian Constitution and the 1951 Geneva Convention: a foreign national who cannot return to their country because they are persecuted for their political opinions, religion, race, or membership of a social group has the right to be protected. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) can make mistakes: a superficial assessment of the conditions in the country of origin, an inadequate preliminary investigation, or an interview conducted without appropriate linguistic or procedural guarantees are defects that render the denial voidable. Our firm appeals denials before the Specialized Section of the Court (Tribunale), with thorough knowledge of European and national case law on international protection.

Regulatory framework

Political asylum and international protection: the legal sources

Art. 10 co. 3 Constitution — 1951 Geneva Convention

The constitutional right of asylum and the Geneva Convention

Art. 10 co. 3 of the Italian Constitution provides that «a foreigner who is denied the effective exercise of the democratic liberties guaranteed by the Italian Constitution in their own country has the right of asylum in the territory of the Republic». This is an absolute personal right (diritto soggettivo perfetto), directly actionable before the ordinary court. The Geneva Convention of 28 July 1951, ratified by Italy by L. 722/1954 and supplemented by the New York Protocol of 1967, defines a refugee as anyone who «owing to well-founded fear of being persecuted for reasons of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion, is outside the country of his nationality and is unable or, owing to such fear, is unwilling to avail himself of the protection of that country». The two sources — constitutional and international — complement each other and ensure multi-level protection that Italian case law has progressively expanded.

Art. 10 co. 3 Const. — 1951 Geneva Conv.
D.Lgs. 251/2007 — Qualification Decree (Decreto Qualifiche)

The three forms of international protection: refugee, subsidiary, and special

D.Lgs. 19 November 2007, n. 251, transposes Directive 2004/83/EC (the so-called Qualification Directive) and governs the conditions for the recognition of refugee status and subsidiary protection. Refugee status (artt. 7–16) requires the existence of acts of persecution sufficiently serious by their nature or repetition, originating from actors of persecution (State, parties, organisations), attributable to Convention grounds (race, religion, nationality, political opinion, membership of a social group). Subsidiary protection (artt. 17–20) protects those who, although not refugees in the strict sense, would face a real risk of suffering serious harm in their country of origin: death penalty, torture or inhuman treatment, indiscriminate violence in situations of armed conflict. Special protection (formerly humanitarian protection, governed by art. 19 co. 1.1 TUI as amended by D.Lgs. 130/2020) covers cases where serious humanitarian grounds exist that do not reach the thresholds of the major forms, including the applicant's personal vulnerability. The assessment of each form is progressive and autonomous: denial of refugee status does not preclude examination of subsidiary protection, and so forth.

D.Lgs. 251/2007 — Qualification Decree (Decreto Qualifiche)
D.Lgs. 25/2008 — Procedures Decree (Decreto Procedure)

The procedure before the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale)

D.Lgs. 28 January 2008, n. 25, transposes Directive 2005/85/EC on asylum procedures and governs the administrative proceedings before the Territorial Commission for the recognition of international protection (Commissione Territoriale per il riconoscimento della protezione internazionale). The provision guarantees the applicant a number of fundamental procedural rights: the right to a personal interview (art. 12), the right to an interpreter (art. 14), the right of access to files (art. 20), and the right to receive information on the COI (Country of Origin Information) used. Art. 35-bis, introduced by D.L. 13/2017, governs judicial proceedings challenging a denial: the appeal (ricorso) must be filed within 30 days of notification of the decision (reduced to 15 days in cases of manifest unfoundedness, accelerated procedure, or an applicant from a safe country of origin). The Court (Tribunale) rules in chambers (rito camerale) with a single judge, through dedicated specialised sections.

D.Lgs. 25/2008 — Procedures Decree (Decreto Procedure)
1967 New York Protocol

The universality of protection: overcoming geographical and temporal limitations

The New York Protocol of 31 January 1967, ratified by Italy by L. 95/1970, eliminated the geographical and temporal limitations originally laid down by the 1951 Geneva Convention, which applied only to events occurring before 1 January 1951 and, for States that had exercised the option of geographical reservation, only to European refugees. Through the Protocol, the definition of refugee became universal and without temporal limits, allowing for the recognition of protection for any foreign national persecuted on Convention grounds, regardless of time and geographical origin. The Protocol requires acceding States to apply the substantive provisions of the Convention (artt. 2–34) to all refugees without limitation. Italy, as a State Party to both instruments, is under an international obligation to guarantee a fair asylum procedure and an individualized examination of every application.

1967 New York Protocol — Universality of protection

Attention to deadlines: the appeal (ricorso) against the denial by the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) must be filed within 30 days of the notification of the decision (reduced to 15 days for accelerated procedures and safe countries). Missing the deadline precludes any judicial remedy. Do not wait: contact us immediately for an urgent assessment.

Automatic suspensive effect. The appeal (ricorso) against the denial of international protection has an automatic suspensive effect on any expulsion or repatriation order, except in the cases strictly provided for by art. 35-bis co. 4 D.Lgs. 25/2008 (manifest infoundation, accelerated procedure, safe country of origin). In these latter cases, the suspension may be requested from the Court with an urgent motion.

Which situation applies to you

The grounds for persecution we protect

The Geneva Convention identifies five grounds for persecution that establish the right to refugee status. Each ground has specific legal characteristics and requires a different argumentative strategy before the Commission and the Court.

Politics

Persecution for political opinions

Persecution based on political opinions protects those who are persecuted by the State or paramilitary organizations for opposition to the regime, political activism, critical journalism, or the defense of human rights. Jurisprudence has clarified that political opinion attributed to the applicant by the agents of persecution is also relevant, even if not actually held. The fear must be objectively founded: subjective fear is not enough; a credible individualized threat or a systematic context of persecution documented by COI is required.

Sources: art. 1 Geneva Conv. — art. 10 D.Lgs. 251/2007
Religion

Persecution for religious reasons

Persecution for religious reasons is among the most common in asylum applications. It protects not only those who belong to a religious minority persecuted by the regime, but also those who have abandoned the majority religion (apostasy), those who refuse to follow religious precepts mandatory by law, and those who practice their faith in a manner not compliant with State prescriptions. In many countries, apostasy from Islam constitutes a crime punishable by death: in these cases, refugee status must be recognized almost automatically, given the gravity of the threatened harm.

Sources: art. 1 Geneva Conv. — art. 10 D.Lgs. 251/2007
Ethnicity

Persecution for race or ethnicity

Racial or ethnic persecution protects ethnic minorities subject to systematic discrimination, violence, or extermination by the State or organizations that the State cannot or will not control. This category includes persecution against Roma minorities, persecution against Hazara minorities in Afghanistan, violence against non-Arab ethnicities in Sudan, and discrimination against the Rohingya in Myanmar. The assessment requires an in-depth analysis of the COI related to the specific situation of the ethnicity in the country of origin.

Sources: art. 1 Geneva Conv. — art. 9 D.Lgs. 251/2007
Social Group

Persecution for membership in a social group

The ground of membership in a particular social group is the most open and controversial among the conventional ones. European and Italian jurisprudence has applied it to: LGBTQ+ persons in countries where homosexuality is criminalized or persecuted, women subject to female genital mutilation or forced marriages, victims of human trafficking, and family members of political opponents. The social group must be defined by shared innate or immutable characteristics, or characteristics so fundamental to identity that one cannot be expected to renounce them.

Sources: art. 1 Geneva Convention — art. 10 co. 1 lett. d) D.Lgs. 251/2007
Gender

Persecution for gender-related reasons

Persecution for gender-related reasons is expressly mentioned by the Qualification Directive (transposed by D.Lgs. 251/2007) as an autonomous ground for international protection. It includes: domestic violence perpetrated with State connivance or impunity, female genital mutilation, forced marriages, honor crimes, and sexual violence as a weapon of war.

Sources: art. 10 co. 1 lett. d) D.Lgs. 251/2007 — UNHCR Guidelines
Conscience

Conscientious objection and desertion

Conscientious objection to military service can constitute a ground for international protection when the legal system of the country of origin does not recognize this right and severely punishes those who refuse. Protection is strengthened when military service would force the applicant to participate in conflicts or operations where war crimes or crimes against humanity are systematically committed.

Sources: art. 10 Geneva Conv.

How we intervene

Defensive strategies for political asylum

The appeal (ricorso) against the denial by the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) is not a simple bureaucratic challenge: it is a full trial in which the Court fully re-evaluates the facts and evidence. Our defensive strategy is built on in-depth work of document collection, COI analysis, and up-to-date knowledge of European and national jurisprudence.

I.

Urgent appeal (ricorso) to the Specialized Court (30 days)

The appeal (ricorso) against the denial by the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) is filed before the Specialized Section on immigration matters of the territorially competent Court, within 30 days of notification (15 days in accelerated procedures). The proceedings are held in chambers (rito camerale) and the judge evaluates the case with full cognizance, being able to independently acquire documents and information on the country of origin. We personally handle the drafting of the appeal (ricorso), the translation and authentication of documents, and assistance during any hearing of the applicant before the Court.

II.

Analysis of Country of Origin Information (COI)

COI — information on the human rights situation in the country of origin — are the heart of the asylum procedure. A good defensive strategy requires identifying the most authoritative and up-to-date sources: UNHCR, EASO/EUAA, Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch, U.S. State Department, ACCORD. We build a personalized COI dossier for each case, challenging the Commission's assessments with official and updated documents that demonstrate systematic persecution in the country of origin and the absence of State protection.

III.

Precautionary motion for suspension of repatriation

When the denial is accompanied by an executive expulsion order, or when the accelerated procedure does not grant an automatic suspensive effect to the appeal (ricorso), we request the Court for an urgent precautionary suspension of the repatriation pending the judgment. The motion must document the fumus boni iuris (apparent validity of the appeal) and the periculum in mora (irreversibility of the damage in case of repatriation).

IV.

Request for the most favorable form of protection

The defensive strategy is not limited to the form of protection originally requested: the Court must independently examine all three forms (refugee status, subsidiary protection, special protection) in descending order, and recognize the one most suitable for the specific case. We structure the appeal (ricorso) to request refugee status as the primary claim, subsidiary protection as a secondary claim, and special protection as a further secondary claim. This structure maximizes the chances of obtaining at least one form of protection for our client.

V.

Protection of specific vulnerabilities

Asylum seekers in conditions of particular vulnerability — unaccompanied minors, victims of torture, trafficking, gender-based violence, individuals with severe medical conditions — are entitled to enhanced procedural safeguards. D.Lgs. 25/2008 requires the Commission (*Commissione Territoriale*) and the Court (*Tribunale*) to adopt appropriate measures, including the appointment of a guardian for minors and the provision of suitable settings for hearings. Where these safeguards have not been respected, the procedural flaw constitutes an independent ground for annulment of the refusal (*diniego*) and referral back for re-examination.

VI.

Appeal to the Court of Cassation (*ricorso in Cassazione*) for breach of law

When the first-instance Court (*Tribunale*) upholds the refusal, we evaluate an appeal to the Court of Cassation (*ricorso in Cassazione*) for breach of law or flaws in the statement of reasons. We keep our knowledge of the case law of the First Civil Section of the Court of Cassation, which is the section competent in immigration matters, continually updated.

The Commission's refusal is not final. The Territorial Commission (*Commissione Territoriale*) performs an administrative, not a judicial function: its refusal (*diniego*) can be fully re-evaluated by the Court (*Tribunale*), which is not bound by the Commission's assessments and may grant protection even in cases where the Commission denied it. The judicial overturn rate of refusals has historically been significant in many territorial sections. Do not resign yourself to the Commission's refusal: contact us to assess the merits of an appeal (*ricorso*).

Case no. 1

Refugee status recognised for Afghan political activist — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Division — 2023 — Refugee Status pursuant to Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 251/2007

An Afghan citizen of Hazara ethnicity, residing in Italy for three years under an expiring permit for humanitarian protection, had submitted an application for international protection alleging that he had been a member of a youth civil rights organisation in Kabul and had suffered serious threats from the Taliban following their seizure of power in August 2021. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) of Naples rejected the application, considering the account not credible because it was «generic» and lacked adequate documentation. The decision failed to consider the objective context of the country of origin, where the systematic persecution of Hazara political activists by the Taliban was extensively documented by leading international sources.

We challenged the refusal before the Court of Naples (Tribunale di Napoli), structuring an appeal (ricorso) around three distinct arguments. First, we challenged the Commission's negative credibility assessment, showing that the applicant had provided detailed and consistent facts concerning his political activities, the places he frequented, and the manner in which he had been threatened, and that the «generic nature» noted by the Commission was in fact the result of an inadequate transcript of the interview. Second, we submitted a COI dossier of over 40 pages documenting, through UNHCR, EUAA, and Amnesty International reports, the systematic persecution of Hazaras and political activists by the Taliban in post-August 2021 Afghanistan.

The Court upheld the appeal by decree of 14 March 2023, recognising refugee status pursuant to arts. 7 and 10 Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 251/2007. The decision affirmed that, in a country where the persecution of Hazara political activists by the Taliban is systematic and documented, the subjective assessment of the narrative's credibility cannot overshadow the objective fact of generalised persecution in the country of origin. Our client was granted a five-year renewable residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for refugees.

Refugee status recognised — 5-year permit issued
Case no. 2

Subsidiary protection for Nigerian woman victim of trafficking and gender-based violence — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Division — 2024 — Subsidiary Protection pursuant to art. 14 Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 251/2007

A young Nigerian woman, an established victim of human trafficking, had submitted an application for international protection alleging that, in the event of repatriation to Nigeria, she would be re-integrated into the exploitation network from which she had fled thanks to the intervention of an organisation assisting victims of trafficking. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) had granted special protection but denied subsidiary protection, deeming that the risk of re-entry into the trafficking network did not reach the threshold of «indiscriminate violence in situations of armed conflict» required by art. 14 lett. c) Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 251/2007, and that gender-based persecution did not satisfy the grounds set forth in letters a) and b) of the same provision (death penalty, torture, or inhuman treatment).

We lodged an appeal (ricorso) challenging this restrictive interpretation. Our primary argument was that the violence to which the applicant would be exposed in the event of repatriation — including physical and sexual violence by her exploiters, extensively documented by the report of the anti-trafficking centre assisting her — fully met the definition of «inhuman and degrading treatment» under art. 14 lett. b) Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 251/2007, as well as a violation of art. 3 ECHR. We produced a specific COI dossier on the situation in Nigeria regarding human trafficking and the practices of Owerri criminal networks, as well as reports from the anti-trafficking centre documenting the threats received by the applicant.

The Court upheld the appeal by decree of 22 January 2024, recognising subsidiary protection pursuant to art. 14 lett. b) Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 251/2007. The decision affirmed that systematic sexual violence organised by a criminal network that the Nigerian State cannot effectively counter constitutes inhuman and degrading treatment relevant for the purposes of subsidiary protection. The applicant obtained a five-year residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for subsidiary protection.

Subsidiary protection recognised — 5-year permit issued
Case no. 3

Refugee status for persecuted journalist in Pakistan — Tribunale di Roma

Tribunale di Roma, Specialized Immigration Division — 2023 — Refugee Status for political opinion

A Pakistani journalist, a contributor to an online news outlet opposing the military government, had submitted an asylum application alleging that he had been arrested, detained, and tortured by the Pakistani security services (ISI) due to his work exposing corruption within the armed forces. Following his release, he had fled Pakistan and reached Italy, where he had lodged the asylum application. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) of Rome had rejected the application, holding that the applicant's journalistic activity did not have a political nature sufficient to satisfy the grounds for Convention persecution, and that the violence suffered was attributable to individual actions by corrupt officers rather than to a systematic State policy. The refusal had failed to consider the medical records certifying the signs of torture, as well as the threats received after his release.

We framed the appeal (ricorso) on three main pillars. First: persecution on account of journalistic investigative work of political reporting fully constitutes the Convention ground of “political opinion”, without requiring formal membership in a political party. Second: the Pakistani intelligence services (ISI) are not “corrupt individual” actors but a recognised State institution, and their actions were therefore attributable to the State. Third: the medical documentation evidencing the signs of torture, consistent with the applicant's account, had not been properly assessed by the Commission, in breach of arts. 4 and 5 of D.Lgs. 251/2007 concerning the burden of proof. We submitted reports from RSF (Reporters Without Borders) and Human Rights Watch on the systematic persecution of journalists in Pakistan, as well as a forensic medical report drawn up by an expert in the documentation of torture.

The Court upheld the appeal (ricorso) by decree of 9 June 2023, granting refugee status on grounds of persecution for political opinions. The decision held that the persecution of investigative journalists by State apparatuses constitutes political persecution under the Convention, and that medical documentation of injuries consistent with torture imposes upon the court a heightened duty of inquiry that the Commission had omitted.

Refugee status granted — Political persecution established
Case no. 4

Protection for a Gambian homosexual: sexual orientation as a ground for protection — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2024 — Social Group pursuant to art. 10 D.Lgs. 251/2007

A young Gambian national had lodged an asylum application alleging that he was persecuted in his country of origin due to his sexual orientation. The Gambia criminalises homosexuality with penalties up to life imprisonment. The applicant had reported having been denounced to the police by a family member, detained, and beaten, and having fled after managing to bribe the prison guard. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) had rejected the application, stating that the narrative was not credible because the applicant had not been able to describe precisely the layout of the prison where he had been held — an assessment that ignored the fact that the detention took place in darkness — and because he had not produced documents regarding the police report filed against him — an unreasonable request with respect to a system such as that of The Gambia.

We challenged the refusal, criticizing first and foremost the method by which the Commission had assessed credibility: the criteria used were unreasonable, disproportionate, and took no account of the traumatic circumstances of the detention nor of the structural impossibility of producing documentation from a country where being gay is a crime. Thirdly, we documented, with updated Country of Origin Information (COI), the situation of systematic persecution of homosexuals in The Gambia, including statements by the President who had described homosexuals as “a cancer to be eradicated”. The Court upheld the appeal (ricorso) by decree of 5 March 2024, granting refugee status on account of membership of a particular social group.

Refugee status granted — Sexual orientation protected
Case no. 5

Special protection for radicalisation in the country of origin: absolute non-refoulement — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2024 — Special Protection pursuant to art. 19 para. 1.1 TUI

A Tunisian national, residing in Italy for four years and the father of two minor Italian citizen children, had filed an application for international protection following an expulsion order issued due to unlawful stay. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) had rejected all forms of protection, stating that Tunisia was a safe country of origin and that the applicant's personal situation did not present relevant protection elements. However, the applicant had documented a specific situation of vulnerability: in Tunisia, he was known as an individual who had lived in Italy for years and associated with culturally “Westernised” circles; credible local sources had reported that his return would be met with hostility by local Salafist groups who considered him a “de facto apostate” due to the lifestyle he had adopted.

We structured the appeal (ricorso) not merely around the typical framework of international protection, but by clearly articulating three distinct grounds. First: the principle of non-refoulement under art. 33 of the Geneva Convention applies even in the absence of formal refugee status where the risk of serious harm in the country of origin is credible. Second: the strong family ties in Italy — two minor cohabiting Italian citizen children — per se satisfy the requirements for special protection (protezione speciale) pursuant to art. 19 para. 1.1 TUI, given that expulsion would violate the right to family life protected by art. 8 ECHR. Third: the classification of Tunisia as a safe country is subject to concrete verification in the individual case and cannot be assumed automatically when the applicant demonstrates exposure to specific individual risks. The Court granted special protection (protezione speciale) and ordered the issuance of the relevant residence permit (permesso di soggiorno).

Special protection granted — Expulsion averted — Family unity preserved

Have you received a refusal from the Commission?

The Commission's refusal is not the last word. The Court re-evaluates everything.

The Territorial Commission (*Commissione Territoriale*) can make mistakes, and often does. The Court's (*Tribunale*) assessment is independent and comprehensive: the judge may grant protection even where the Commission has denied it. However, the time limit to file an appeal (*ricorso*) is short: 30 days from notification of the refusal (15 days under the accelerated procedure).
