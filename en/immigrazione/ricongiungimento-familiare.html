The right to family unity is not a discretionary favor of the Public Administration.
If the clearance (nulla osta) has been denied, it can be appealed.

Family reunification is the right guaranteed by Art. 29 of the Consolidated Immigration Act (Testo Unico sull’Immigrazione) and by Art. 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights to live together with one's family members. Yet Police Headquarters (Questure) and Immigration Single Desks (Sportelli Unici per l’Immigrazione) routinely deny clearance (nulla osta) on formal grounds, alleged documentary defects, or restrictive interpretations of income or housing requirements. Every refusal represents an interference with family life that the law and European case law require to be strictly justified. Our firm challenges these refusals and obtains, often through interim relief, the reunification of separated families.

Regulatory framework

Family reunification in the Consolidated Immigration Act

Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 286/1998 (TUI) — Art. 29 — Family reunification

Family members eligible for reunification and sponsor requirements

Art. 29 of Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) of 25 July 1998, no. 286 (Consolidated Immigration Act) constitutes the fundamental provision governing family reunification. Paragraph 1 identifies eligible family members: a spouse who is not legally separated and is not under eighteen years of age; minor children, including children of the spouse or born out of wedlock, unmarried, or adult dependent children unable to provide for their own needs due to health reasons; dependent parents if they have no other children in their country of origin or provenance, or parents over sixty-five years of age whose other children are unable to support them due to documented serious health reasons. Paragraph 3 requires the foreign national applying for reunification — the so-called “sponsor” — to demonstrate: availability of accommodation meeting the minimum standards required by regional law; a minimum annual income equal to the annual amount of the social allowance increased by half for each family member to be reunited (approximately 6,085 euros for the first family member in 2024, with proportional increases for each additional family member). The application for clearance (nulla osta) is submitted to the Single Desk for Immigration (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione) having jurisdiction over the sponsor's place of residence.

Art. 29 TUI — Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 286/1998
Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 286/1998 — Art. 29-bis — Reunification for refugees

The special scheme for beneficiaries of international protection

Art. 29-bis TUI, introduced by Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) of 21 February 2014, no. 18 transposing Directive 2003/86/EC, provides for a special and simplified regime for family reunification of refugees and beneficiaries of subsidiary protection. The fundamental rule establishes that the refugee is not required to demonstrate possession of the income and housing requirements provided for by Art. 29, paragraph 3. Exemption from the requirement to prove income and suitable accommodation is the main benefit of the special scheme: the refugee is exempt from the conditions that are most difficult to meet during the initial phase of resettlement in Italy. The provision broadens the family unit eligible for reunification: in addition to the standard family members under Art. 29, the direct parents of a refugee are also eligible if the refugee is an unaccompanied minor.

Art. 29-bis TUI — Refugees and subsidiary protection
Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 286/1998 — Art. 30 — Residence permit for family reasons and Art. 8 ECHR

Family cohesion and supranational protection of private life

Art. 30 TUI governs the residence permit for family reasons (permesso di soggiorno per motivi familiari), issued to the reunited family member upon completion of the family reunification procedure. The permit has a duration equal to that of the sponsoring family member's permit and must be renewed under the same conditions. The provision establishes that, in cases where residence in Italy for family reasons is no longer possible due to the termination of the family relationship (separation, divorce, death of the sponsor), the holder may convert the permit into another title if the relevant requirements are met. The protection of family life finds its supranational foundation in Art. 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights, which guarantees the right to respect for private and family life. Directive 2003/86/EC requires Member States to interpret grounds preventing reunification restrictively and to assess always the best interests of the minor.

Art. 30 TUI — Art. 8 CEDU — Dir. 2003/86/CE

Pay attention to deadlines: the appeal (ricorso) against the refusal of the clearance certificate (nulla osta) for family reunification must be lodged before the ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario - Specialized Immigration Section) pursuant to Art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011 within 30 days of notification of the refusal. The Court may issue urgent interim measures permitting the entry into Italy of the family member pending the proceedings. Do not delay: deadlines are short and their expiry precludes all ordinary judicial protection.

The income requirement and its calculability. The calculation of the minimum income required for reunification is one of the main causes of refusal. It is essential to know that: (1) the income of all family members already cohabiting in Italy may be aggregated; (2) income from employment, self-employment, pensions and social security benefits, and capital income are included; (3) the threshold is calculated on the annual amount of the social allowance (assegno sociale) and varies each year; (4) for refugees, the income requirement does not apply. A refusal based on an erroneous calculation of income by the Single Desk for Immigration (Sportello Unico) may be appealed and, in our experience, is frequently set aside.

Which situation applies to you

Types of family reunification

Family reunification is not a single uniform procedure: the status of the sponsor, the category of family member to be reunited, and the residence title determine different pathways and requirements. We identify the correct route and handle the entire procedure from the clearance certificate (nulla osta) to the issuance of the residence permit (permesso di soggiorno).

Art. 29 co. 1 lett. a)

Reunification for spouse

The foreign spouse of the sponsor may be reunited provided they are not legally separated and are at least eighteen years of age. The marriage must be valid under the legal system of the country in which it was contracted and not contrary to Italian public policy. De facto partnerships are not equated to marriage for the purposes of reunification, unless specific bilateral provisions apply. Case law has progressively extended protection to registered partnerships recognized in other EU countries.

Clearance certificate (nulla osta): 90-180 days (Questura)
Art. 29 co. 1 lett. b)

Reunification for minor children

Minor children of the sponsor or spouse, including those born out of wedlock and unmarried, hold the strongest right to reunification. For minor children over 14 years of age, an entry visa issued by the Italian diplomatic mission in the minor's country of residence is also required. In the presence of minor children, the courts are particularly rigorous in reviewing refusals: the best interests of the minor are a paramount criterion that must always be assessed.

Procedural priority: minors are always handled with urgency
Art. 29 co. 1 lett. c) e d)

Reunification for dependent parents

Dependent parents of the sponsor are eligible for reunification provided that they have no other children in their country of origin (or that the other children are unable to support them due to documented serious health reasons), or that they are over 65 years of age and the other children are unable to support them. Proving the “absence of other children” in the country of origin is often the critical point: our firm manages the collection of the necessary documentation, including foreign civil status records with legalisation and certified translation.

Documentation: civil status records + consular declarations
Art. 29-bis

Reunification for refugees

The special regime under Art. 29-bis TUI exempts refugees and beneficiaries of subsidiary protection from income and accommodation requirements. The advantage is significant: during the initial resettlement phase in Italy, a refugee rarely has stable income or certified independent accommodation. The procedure may be initiated through the Police Headquarters (Questura) or through UNHCR. We assist the refugee in gathering family documentation and in managing the entire procedure.

Exemption: income and accommodation requirements not required
Art. 30 TUI

Family cohesion (coesione familiare)

Family cohesion applies when the foreign family member is already present in Italy under another residence title and requests the conversion of the permit into a permit for family reasons (permesso di soggiorno per motivi familiari). It differs from ordinary reunification (which presupposes entry from abroad) and typically applies when the spouse or child is already in Italy on a study, work, or protection permit. The procedure is less complex and does not require an entry visa.

Procedure in Italy: without an entry visa
Art. 29 co. 7

Clearance certificate (nulla osta) and appeal (ricorso)

The clearance certificate (nulla osta) for family reunification is the administrative measure issued by the Single Desk for Immigration (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione) authorizing the entry into Italy of the family member. In case of refusal, an appeal (ricorso) may be lodged before the ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario - Immigration Section) pursuant to Art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011 within 30 days of notification. The ordinary court has the power to annul the refusal and to order the Single Desk to issue the clearance certificate (nulla osta), including by way of interim relief with immediate effect.

Appeal (ricorso): within 30 days of refusal

How we intervene

Strategies for obtaining reunification or challenging a refusal

The family reunification procedure is fraught with bureaucratic pitfalls that often result in avoidable refusals. Our firm handles both the preventive stage — building an unassailable evidentiary file — and the litigation stage, challenging refusals with the highest degree of effectiveness.

I.

Prior verification of requirements and preparation of the file

Before submitting any application, we verify the sponsor's position: precise calculation of aggregable income, verification of accommodation suitability according to the parameters of the regional law of Campania (or the region of residence), and review of the documents of both the sponsor and the family member to be reunited. A deficient file is the primary cause of avoidable refusals: investing in preparation is the most effective strategy.

II.

Urgent appeal under Art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011

When the refusal of the clearance certificate (nulla osta) is unjustified or based on erroneous interpretations of the law, we lodge an appeal (ricorso) before the ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario - Specialized Immigration Section) within the mandatory deadline of 30 days. The summary proceeding (rito sommario di cognizione) ensures a relatively prompt decision. In interim proceedings, we seek the suspension of the refusal and temporary authorization for the family member's entry pending the decision on the merits.

III.

Protection of minors and interim urgency

When the family member to be reunited is a minor, urgency is at its highest: separating the minor from parents constitutes a violation of the principle of the best interests of the child enshrined in Art. 3 of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child. We request the immediate entry of the minor on an interim basis.

IV.

Challenging the income calculation

Many denials are based on an incorrect calculation of the minimum required income. Our firm analyses the denial order in detail, verifies whether the Administration correctly calculated all combinable income (including that of cohabitants), applied the correct threshold for the reference year, and took into account permitted deductions. In numerous cases, the appeal (ricorso) is upheld due to an error in the income calculation.

V.

Procurement and legalisation of foreign documentation

The bureaucratic complexity of family reunification often lies in the need to produce foreign civil status records (birth, marriage, and family status certificates), have them legalised (or apostilled in countries party to the Hague Convention of 1961), and have them translated by a sworn translator. We manage the entire document chain, working with local correspondents in the main countries of origin.

Case no. 1

Denial for insufficient income: miscalculation successfully challenged — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2024 — Spousal family reunification

A Pakistani citizen, holding a residence permit for permanent employment (permesso di soggiorno per lavoro a tempo indeterminato) and residing in Naples for eight years, had filed an application for entry clearance for family reunification (nulla osta al ricongiungimento familiare) for his wife residing in Pakistan. The Single Desk for Immigration (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione) of Naples had rejected the application, noting that the income documented by the applicant — amounting to approximately 11,500 euros per year as an employee of a logistics company — fell below the minimum threshold required for the reunification of the spouse, set at the annual value of the social allowance increased by half (approximately 8,400 euros for the spouse alone in 2023, according to the updated threshold). However, the rejection decision had failed to take into account the income of the applicant's mother, cohabiting with him in the same apartment, who received an invalidity pension of approximately 4,200 euros per year. The cumulative income of the cohabiting family members therefore amounted to approximately 15,700 euros, well above the required threshold.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) with the Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Section on immigration matters, pursuant to art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011. The appeal highlighted that art. 29 para. 3 TUI expressly allows the accumulation of the applicant's income with that of other members of the household already residing in Italy. The Single Desk (Sportello Unico), therefore, had erroneously excluded from the calculation the pension income of the cohabiting mother, reaching an erroneous arithmetic result. In interim proceedings, the Court immediately stayed the rejection. On the merits, by decree of 28 February 2024, it upheld the appeal and ordered the Single Desk (Sportello Unico) to issue the entry clearance (nulla osta), establishing that the accumulated income well exceeded the minimum threshold required by law.

Rejection annulled — Clearance issued — Wife reunited
Case no. 2

Reunification of minor denied due to curable documentary defect — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2023 — Minor child reunification

A Nigerian citizen, holding a residence permit for self-employment (permesso di soggiorno per lavoro autonomo), had requested the reunification of her thirteen-year-old minor son who remained in Nigeria with his maternal grandmother. The Single Desk for Immigration (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione) had rejected the application because the minor's birth certificate — issued by the Nigerian authorities — did not state the father's name, making it impossible, according to the Administration, to verify parentage. The applicant had produced additional documentation: a certificate from the local parish church confirming the mother-son relationship, photographs of the family, and a sworn statement from the grandmother. The Single Desk (Sportello Unico) had deemed these documents insufficient to cure the defect in the birth certificate and had confirmed the rejection.

We filed an urgent appeal (ricorso) with the Tribunale di Napoli, pleading first of all that the absence of the father's name on the Nigerian birth certificate could not constitute a ground for rejection, as reunification was requested by the mother, not by the father. The maternal parentage bond was in any event documented by the mention of the mother on the birth certificate. We also highlighted that the defect noted by the Single Desk (Sportello Unico) was, if anything, at most a formal curable defect that should have led to a request for supplemental documentation, not to a final rejection: the consolidated practice of the Single Desk and the governing legislation (art. 10-bis L. 241/1990) required the prior notice of rejection (preavviso di rigetto) with the granting of a time limit for submitting observations. We also produced in court a consular statement from Nigeria confirming the registration of the minor as the applicant's child. The Court upheld the appeal, annulled the rejection, and ordered the issuance of the entry clearance (nulla osta) for the reunification of the minor.

Rejection annulled — Minor child reunited
Case no. 3

Refugee: expedited reunification denied — regime pursuant to art. 29-bis recognized

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2024 — Art. 29-bis TUI

A Somali citizen, holding refugee status recognized by the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) of Naples, had submitted an application for family reunification for his wife and two minor children who remained in Somalia. The Single Desk for Immigration (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione) had processed the application as an ordinary application pursuant to art. 29 TUI — rather than as an application subject to the special regime of art. 29-bis TUI reserved for refugees — and had rejected the application for insufficient income: the applicant, at that time enrolled in a SPRAR project with a minimum reception income, did not meet the ordinary threshold. The applicant had not even been informed of the existence of the facilitated regime and had submitted the application using the ordinary form provided by the office.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) with the Tribunale di Napoli, pleading the erroneous application of the ordinary regime instead of the special regime of art. 29-bis TUI, which expressly exempts the refugee from the income requirement and from the obligation to demonstrate housing suitability (idoneità alloggiativa). We cited Directive 2003/86/EC (art. 12, para. 1), which requires Member States not to demand that refugees satisfy the ordinary requirements for reunification. The Court upheld the appeal, declaring the application subject to the regime of art. 29-bis TUI and ordering the Single Desk (Sportello Unico) to issue the entry clearance (nulla osta) without verifying income and housing requirements. The refugee's wife and children were able to enter Italy within four months of the ruling.

Special regime recognized — Clearance issued — Family reunited
Case no. 4

Rejection for unsuitable housing: inspection challenged and documentation restructured

Administrative review proceedings + Tribunale di Napoli — 2023 — Spouse and children reunification

A Moroccan citizen, residing in Italy for ten years with a residence permit for subordinate employment (permesso per lavoro subordinato), had submitted an application for clearance for family reunification (nulla osta al ricongiungimento familiare) for his wife and three minor children. The Single Desk (Sportello Unico) had rejected the application following a housing inspection carried out by the Police Headquarters (Questura) which had classified the 65 sq m apartment as unsuitable to accommodate six people (the applicant, his wife, and the three children), deeming the surface area insufficient under the minimum parameters established by Campania regional law. However, the certificate of suitability issued by the Municipality attested to the conformity of the accommodation. There was therefore a contradiction between the municipal housing suitability certificate (certificato di idoneità alloggiativa, issued by the Regional Agency of Campania) and the assessment of the Questura.

We first submitted an application for internal administrative review (riesame), highlighting the contradiction between the two administrative acts and requesting that the suitability certificate issued by the competent authority prevail. In parallel, we produced a technical report prepared by an engineer registered with the professional association, certifying the complete compliance of the apartment with the minimum surface area requirements per person laid down by Campania regional law (7 sq m for the first inhabitant, 4 sq m for each of the others), reaching a total of 65 sq m for 5 members (5 x 7 = 35 sq m minimum, abundantly met). The Single Desk (Sportello Unico) did not accept the request for review, confirming the rejection. We then filed an appeal (ricorso) with the Tribunale di Napoli, which upheld our arguments and annulled the rejection, ordering the issuance of the entry clearance (nulla osta). The family was reunited in September 2023.

Rejection annulled — Housing suitability established — Family reunited
Case no. 5

Parents over sixty-five years of age: reunification obtained despite other children abroad

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2024 — Art. 29 para. 1 lett. d)

A Bangladeshi citizen, residing in Italy for twelve years as a permanent employee, had submitted an application for family reunification («ricongiungimento familiare») for both parents, both over sixty-five years of age and residing in Bangladesh. The Single Desk for Immigration («Sportello Unico») had rejected the application, noting that, according to the available information, the applicant had two other siblings residing in Bangladesh, and that the parents therefore were not without other children in their country of origin, as required by art. 29 co. 1 lett. d) TUI for parents over sixty-five years of age. The applicant had provided sworn statements from the siblings certifying their financial impossibility of providing for their parents’ support, but the Single Desk («Sportello Unico») had deemed them insufficiently documented.

We filed an appeal («ricorso») before the Tribunale di Napoli, pleading that the provision — in the case of parents over sixty-five years of age — does not require the complete absence of other children in the country of origin, but rather that the other children be «unable to provide for their support due to documented serious health reasons». We submitted updated medical records certifying that both siblings suffered from medical conditions that rendered them partially dependent and that their financial conditions were critical, with incomes below the absolute poverty line in Bangladesh. We also produced an economic report documenting the average standard of living in Bangladesh and the siblings’ practical impossibility of providing for the dignified support of their elderly parents. The Court upheld the appeal, deeming the inability of the other children to support the parents to be proven, and ordered the issuance of clearance («nulla osta») for both of the applicant’s parents.

Parents over sixty-five eligible for reunification — Clearance («nulla osta») issued

Have you received a denial of family reunification?

The family has the right to be together. The law provides for it. Let us ensure it is respected.

A denial of clearance (nulla osta) for family reunification is an administrative act challengeable before the court. The deadlines to appeal (ricorrere) are short — only 30 days — but the judge can immediately suspend the denial by way of precautionary relief. Do not wait: every day of separation from your family is an additional day that the law should not impose on you.
