D.L. 34/2020 art. 103 | Regularisation of undeclared work (emersione lavoro irregolare)

Amnesty application rejected?
Regular status is still possible.

Procedures for regularising undeclared work (emersione) are complex, the requirements are interpreted restrictively by the police headquarters (Questura) and the prefecture (Prefettura), and applications are often rejected for reasons that an appeal (ricorso) can overturn. If you have submitted an amnesty application and received a rejection, or if you are seeking a way to regularise your status, we can assist you.

Applicable Legislation

The legal framework of regularisation

Procedures for regularising undocumented foreign nationals are rooted in extraordinary provisions introduced over time: from the Bossi-Fini amnesty (L. 189/2002, art. 33) to the 2012 regularisation (emersione, D.L. 109/2012), up to the latest 2020 amnesty introduced by art. 103 of D.L. 34/2020 (the so-called “Decreto Rilancio”). The latter enabled the regularisation of approximately 220,000 undeclared employment relationships in domestic work, personal care, and agriculture. Added to these is the special protection system (art. 19 TUI), which in certain cases allows regularisation on humanitarian grounds and social integration.

D.L. 34/2020 art. 103 — 2020 Amnesty L. 189/2002 art. 33 — Bossi-Fini D.L. 109/2012 — 2012 Regularisation (Emersione) Art. 18 TUI — Social protection Art. 19 TUI — Non-refoulement

2020 Amnesty: how many applications are still pending? Years after submission, many applications for regularisation under art. 103 D.L. 34/2020 are still awaiting determination by the prefectures (Prefetture). Unlawful administrative silence (silenzio-inadempimento) can be challenged before the Regional Administrative Court (TAR): the worker and the employer have the right to receive an answer within a reasonable time. We have already obtained judgments ordering the Prefecture (Prefettura) to issue a decision within 30-60 days.

Our approach

Defense strategies

Every regularisation procedure is different. The key to success lies in the quality of the documentation and in-depth knowledge of favourable legal precedents.

01

Appeal (ricorso) against regularisation rejection

The denial of an application for regularisation (emersione) is an appealable administrative decision. We analyse the reasoning, identify flaws of illegality (lack of preliminary investigation, violation of due process, misapplication of requirements), and lodge an appeal (ricorso) with the Regional Administrative Court (TAR) with an application for precautionary relief to halt any expulsions pending trial.

02

Proof of the employment relationship

When written contracts or payslips are absent, the law permits proof of undeclared work by any means: witness statements, bank statements showing periodic credits, WhatsApp messages, receipts for purchases of materials, telecommunications service logs, utility bills paid by the employer, statements from neighbours. We build a solid evidentiary file.

03

Contribution documentation

Applications for regularisation (emersione) require the payment of a flat-rate contribution to INPS (500 euros for domestic work, variable for other sectors). We handle relations with INPS, the completion of F24 payment forms, verification of the employer's INPS access credentials, and the correct allocation of payments.

04

Appeal (ricorso) to the Regional Administrative Court (TAR) for administrative silence

Thousands of 2020 legalisation applications are still unanswered. The silence-inaction (silenzio-inadempimento) of the Prefettura can be challenged before the TAR through the special proceedings against silence pursuant to art. 117 c.p.a. We obtain judgments ordering the public administration to decide within a mandatory deadline, unblocking situations that have been stalled for years.

05

Regularisation based on family ties

When the ordinary regularisation procedure is not viable, we assess whether there are family ties that allow for regularisation: marriage to an Italian or EU citizen, minor children born in Italy, registered cohabitation, care of a non-self-sufficient Italian family member. The right to family unity is protected by art. 8 ECHR and may prevail over the State's interest in expulsion.

06

Conversion into a work permit

Once the provisional residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) pending regularisation is obtained, we plan the conversion into a definitive work permit. We verify the requirements, assist in the administrative phase before the Police Headquarters (Questura) and, if necessary, lodge an appeal (ricorso) against any denial of conversion.

Results achieved

Five specific cases

Five stories of workers whom we assisted through the regularisation process, from the initial documentation to the definitive permit. Names have been changed for confidentiality reasons.

Case 01

Rejection of 2020 regularisation («sanatoria») due to alleged absence from Italy — TAR Campania

Sector: domestic care — Origin: Sri Lanka — Legislation: art. 103 D.L. 34/2020

A Sri Lankan worker, employed as a home caregiver («badante») for a dependent elderly person in Naples, had submitted an application for regularisation («emersione») in August 2020. The Prefecture («Prefettura»), in March 2023, had rejected the application, noting that according to the records of the Ministry of the Interior Information System (SIMI) the applicant appeared to have been absent from Italian territory between 1° January and 7 March 2020, the date required by the provision for continuous presence.

An analysis of the case file revealed that the SIMI data were incorrect: the worker was lawfully present in Italy, but in December 2019 had stayed for ten days in London for family reasons, without ever losing Italian residency. The passport with entry and exit stamps from the Schengen area, the receipts for expenses incurred using the Italian debit card during the stay in London (demonstrating that it was a temporary absence and not a permanent return to her country of origin), the testimony of the employer certifying the immediate resumption of duties upon return from abroad, and the utility bills for an Italian telephone line active throughout the entire period, constituted an evidentiary basis that disproved the Prefecture’s («Prefettura») reconstruction. The TAR Campania upheld the appeal («ricorso»), annulling the refusal for failure to carry out an adequate factual investigation and violation of the principle of proportionality, acknowledging that the brief temporary absence did not constitute the “non-presence” contemplated by the provision. The residence permit («permesso di soggiorno») was subsequently issued.

Application granted — Permit issued — TAR Campania
Case 02

Prefecture silence-failure to act («silenzio-inadempimento») — Appeal under art. 117 c.p.a.

Sector: agriculture — Origin: Senegal — Legislation: art. 103 D.L. 34/2020

A Senegalese worker employed on a farm in the province of Caserta had submitted an application for regularisation («emersione») in the agricultural sector in August 2020. Nearly four years later, the Prefettura di Caserta had still not concluded the proceedings, leaving the worker in a legal limbo: neither expelled nor regularised, unable to obtain lawful employment, enter into contracts, or open a bank account. His state of uncertainty had severe repercussions on his daily life and on the possibility of sending remittances to his family in Senegal.

The appeal against the silence-failure to act («silenzio-inadempimento») was brought before the TAR Campania (Naples registry, tenth division, territorially competent). The summary procedure provided for by art. 117 c.p.a. allows for the holding of the hearing in chambers within thirty days of the filing of the appeal, under an expedited procedure compared to the ordinary track. The TAR delivered a judgment in the applicant’s favour within six weeks, ordering the Prefettura di Caserta to decide on the regularisation («emersione») application within sixty days of the notification of the judgment, with the appointment of a court-appointed commissioner («commissario ad acta») in the event of further inaction. The Prefecture concluded the proceedings within the deadline, issuing the clearance («nulla osta») for regularisation. The worker subsequently obtained a residence permit for subordinate employment («permesso di soggiorno per lavoro subordinato») from the Police Headquarters («Questura»).

Prefecture ordered to act — Permit obtained
Case 03

Rejection due to criminal conviction of the employer — Proportionality and workers’ rights

Sector: catering and restaurant services — Origin: Peru — Legislation: art. 103 D.L. 34/2020

A Peruvian worker, a cook in a Neapolitan restaurant, had submitted an application for regularisation («emersione») together with his employer. The Prefecture («Prefettura») had rejected the application, noting that the restaurant owner had received, some years earlier, a conviction for a tax offence (failure to file a tax return pursuant to art. 5 D.Lgs. 74/2000), with a suspended sentence and the offence extinguished through compliance with the court-ordered requirements. The Prefecture had considered that this criminal record constituted the disqualifying ground provided for by art. 103 co. 10 D.L. 34/2020 (conviction for the offences referred to in artt. 600, 601, 602 c.p., or for unlawful labour exploitation). The equation of the tax offence with the disqualifying offences was manifestly unfounded.

The appeal to the TAR highlighted that the provision sets out an exhaustive list of disqualifying offences — all serious offences against the person relating to enslavement and exploitation — and that a tax offence that has been purged with the sentence extinguished does not fall within that catalogue. The TAR, invoking the principle of strict interpretation of disqualifying grounds concerning fundamental rights and the legislature’s policy favouring the regularisation of workers in vulnerable positions, annulled the refusal. The Prefecture («Prefettura») subsequently issued the clearance («nulla osta»). The worker obtained his residence permit («permesso di soggiorno») and was able to regularise his legal status after waiting more than three years.

Refusal annulled — TAR Campania — Regularisation («emersione») granted
Case 04

Regularisation on grounds of family ties — Minor child with a disability

Sector: care — Origin: Ecuador — Legislation: Art. 19 TUI, art. 8 CEDU

An Ecuadorian woman, without a valid residence permit for over three years, was living in Italy with her minor child, born in Italy, who suffered from a severe intellectual disability requiring continuous assistance and attendance at a dedicated National Health Service (SSN) centre. The mother did not have formal employment, but provided full-time care to her child. The Questura di Napoli had issued a deportation order («decreto di espulsione») against her for irregular stay. The physician from the child neuropsychiatry service had certified that separation from the mother, the child’s sole stable attachment figure, would cause grave harm to the minor’s development and potentially worsen his condition.

The appeal (ricorso) against the deportation order (decreto di espulsione) was based on two distinct legal pillars: the right to respect for private and family life under Art. 8 ECHR, which the Strasbourg Court has systematically applied to prohibit the deportation of the parent of a minor with a severe disability residing in the country (ECtHR, Berisha v. Switzerland; Üner v. the Netherlands); and the best interests of the child under Art. 3 of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, incorporated into the Italian legal system. The Tribunale di Napoli, during the validation hearing of the deportation, granted the petition for suspension and subsequently annulled the order. The Police Headquarters (Questura) issued a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for special protection reasons, renewable.

Deportation order annulled — Special protection residence permit issued
Case 05

Regularisation (emersione) in the agricultural sector — Proof of employment through alternative documentation

Sector: fruit and vegetables — Origin: Pakistan — Legislation: art. 103 D.L. 34/2020

A Pakistani worker employed on a small farm in the Sele plain (province of Salerno) had submitted an application for regularisation (emersione) in the agricultural sector. The employer, who had died in the meantime, had never entered into a written contract with the worker, nor paid social security contributions. The heirs to the agricultural business, although willing to regularise his position, possessed no documentation of the prior employment relationship. The Prefecture (Prefettura) of Salerno had rejected the application due to a lack of evidence of the employment relationship.

The collection of alternative evidence required intensive investigative work: witness statements from other workers and regular customers attesting to the applicant's presence in the fields; receipts for purchases of agricultural equipment from local retailers bearing the worker's name as the purchaser (reconstructible via copies of receipts kept by the retailers); bank statements showing periodic cash deposits compatible with monthly wages; photos and videos on the worker's social networks showing his activity in the fields with geolocated timestamps; WhatsApp messages with the deceased employer retrieved from the mobile phone of the inheriting son; the worker's health booklet with the occupational medical examinations carried out at the farm. The evidentiary file thus constructed persuaded the Prefecture (Prefettura), upon re-examination, to grant the application. The worker obtained a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for subordinate work.

Re-examination granted — Work permit issued — Prefettura di Salerno
