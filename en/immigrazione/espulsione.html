Have you received an expulsion decree (decreto di espulsione) or are you being held in a CPR?
Every hour counts. Immediate legal intervention can halt repatriation.

The expulsion decree (decreto di espulsione) is one of the most serious measures that the legal system can adopt against a foreign national: it can disrupt, in just a few days, years of life built in Italy, separate families, and interrupt employment and integration paths. Yet expulsion (espulsione) is not always lawful: art. 19 TUI lists the grounds for absolute non-expellability (inespellibilità assoluta), case law has increased the formal and substantive defects that can lead to its annulment, and judges have precautionary measures (strumenti cautelari) at their disposal to immediately block its execution. Regulatory framework

Expulsion and detention in the Consolidated Immigration Act (Testo Unico Immigrazione)

Legislative Decree 286/1998 (TUI) — Art. 13 — Administrative expulsion

Types of expulsion and legal requirements

Art. 13 of Legislative Decree 25 July 1998, no. 286 (Consolidated Immigration Act / Testo Unico Immigrazione – TUI) governs the two main forms of administrative expulsion. Ministerial expulsion (art. 13, paragraph 1) is ordered by the Minister of the Interior for reasons of public order or state security: it is the most serious form of expulsion and provides for immediate forced escort to the border, with no possibility of a period for voluntary departure. Prefectural expulsion (art. 13, paragraph 2) is ordered by the Prefect (Prefetto) in the following exhaustive cases: (a) illegal entry into the territory of the State; (b) overstaying in Italy beyond the validity of the visa or residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) without having applied for renewal; (c) the foreign national being subject to preventive measures. The expulsion decree (decreto di espulsione) is served on the foreign national and must state the reasons, in a language understandable to them, indicating the legal remedies and the time limits for filing them. Paragraph 13 provides that an appeal (ricorso) against the expulsion decree lies before the ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario) – specialised immigration section, with automatic suspensive effect unless ordered otherwise by the judge.

Art. 13 TUI — Prefectural and ministerial expulsion
Legislative Decree 286/1998 (TUI) — Art. 14 — Detention in a CPR

The Pre-Removal Detention Centre (Centro di Permanenza per il Rimpatrio – CPR): prerequisites and time limits

Art. 14 TUI governs the detention of the expelled foreign national in Pre-Removal Detention Centres (Centri di Permanenza per il Rimpatrio – CPR). When it is not possible to carry out the immediate repatriation of the expelled foreign national — due to a lack of travel documents, the need to verify their identity or nationality, or the unavailability of carriers or other means of transport — the Police Commissioner (Questore) may order their detention in a CPR. Detention is ordered by the Questore through a reasoned decree and must be immediately validated by the justice of the peace (giudice di pace) or by the specialised justice of the peace within 48 hours. The maximum duration of detention has progressively increased over the years through legislative reforms: it can currently reach up to 18 months (a provision introduced by Law Decree 20/2023, the so-called Cutro Decree). However, detention exceeding 90 days requires periodic renewals by the court. The validation of the detention and each of its renewals may be immediately challenged before the Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello).

Art. 14 TUI — CPR detention — Max 18 months
Art. 19 TUI — Absolute prohibition of expulsion — Art. 3 ECHR

Limits on expulsion: absolute non-expellability and non-refoulement

Art. 19 of the TUI lists the categories of foreign nationals who are absolutely non-expellable, against whom an expulsion decree cannot be issued or, if issued, must be annulled. The following are non-expellable: minors under eighteen years of age; foreign nationals holding a long-term resident status (carta di soggiorno), unless the disqualifying grounds provided for by art. 9 apply; foreign nationals cohabiting with relatives within the second degree or with a spouse who are Italian citizens; pregnant women or women within six months following the birth of their child; foreign nationals suffering from serious health conditions requiring non-deferrable treatment not available in their country of origin (art. 19 para. 2 letter d-bis TUI). Alongside the grounds of absolute non-expellability, art. 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights — which prohibits torture and inhuman or degrading treatment — imposes an absolute ban on expelling anyone to a country where they risk facing such treatment, irrespective of the conditions of irregular stay. Italy, Grand Chamber, 28 February 2008).

The re-entry ban and its impact. The expulsion decree is normally accompanied by a ban on re-entering Italy (and, in certain cases, the entire Schengen area) of variable duration: from 3 to 5 years in ordinary prefectural expulsions, up to 10 years or permanent in ministerial expulsions for reasons of public order or security. Violation of the re-entry ban constitutes a criminal offence under art. 13 para. 13 TUI. The re-entry ban may be revoked or reduced upon application by the expelled foreign national, provided that at least half of the ban period has elapsed and the foreign national proves that they left the territory voluntarily. The ban can be challenged independently before the specialised Court (Tribunale ordinario).

Which order applies to you

The types of expulsion and detention

The Italian removal system for foreign nationals encompasses various orders with different legal grounds, challenge procedures, and remedies. Correctly identifying the type of order received is the first essential step in building an effective defence.

Art. 13 para. 2 TUI

Prefectural expulsion

Ordered by the Prefect (Prefetto) in the mandatory cases of irregular entry, failure to renew the residence permit (permesso di soggiorno), or the imposition of preventive measures. It is the most common form of expulsion. It must be reasoned and served on the foreign national in a language they understand. It can be challenged before the specialised Court (Tribunale ordinario) pursuant to art. 18 Legislative Decree 150/2011 within 30 days. The appeal (ricorso) has an automatic suspensive effect on the forced escort measure, unless otherwise ordered by the judge.

Appeal: 30 days — Automatic stay
Art. 13 para. 1 TUI

Ministerial expulsion

Ordered by the Minister of the Interior for reasons of public order or state security: it is the most serious form and entails immediate forced escort to the border. It does not provide for a voluntary departure period and entails a re-entry ban for the Schengen area. The appeal (ricorso) is brought before the Regional Administrative Court of Lazio (TAR del Lazio) in exclusive jurisdiction over public order matters, or before the ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario) according to more recent case law. It requires immediate, urgent action.

Urgent intervention: 24/7
Art. 13 para. 4 TUI

Forced escort to the border

When the expulsion must be enforced immediately, the Police Commissioner (Questore) orders the forced escort of the foreign national to the border by law enforcement. Forced escort is only permitted in the specific cases provided for by law: risk of absconding, lack of cooperation by the returnee, need for identity or nationality checks. In other cases, the foreign national must be granted a period of 7 to 30 days for voluntary departure. The unlawfulness of forced escort instead of voluntary departure constitutes grounds for appeal.

Challenge: immediate — Extremely tight deadline
Art. 14 TUI

Detention in a CPR

Detention in a Pre-Removal Detention Centre (Centro di Permanenza per il Rimpatrio – CPR) is a measure restricting personal liberty ordered by the Police Commissioner (Questore) and validated by the judge within 48 hours. The validation and any renewal may be immediately challenged before the Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello) within 15 days. The grounds for appeal are numerous: absence of the prerequisites for detention, breach of procedural guarantees during validation, inhuman or degrading detention conditions in violation of art. 3 ECHR, or exceeding the maximum statutory time limits.

Appeal: 15 days — Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello)
Art. 13 co. 13 TUI

Re-entry ban (divieto di reingresso)

The re-entry ban (divieto di reingresso) is linked to deportation and constitutes an automatic consequence thereof. Its duration ranges from 3 to 10 years (or permanent for deportations on security grounds). The ban registers the foreign national in the Schengen Information System (SIS II) and prevents entry throughout the Schengen area. It may be challenged independently before the specialized Court (Tribunale). In specific cases, early revocation may be requested upon proving that the grounds justifying deportation have ceased.

Appeal: independent — Early revocation possible
Dir. 2008/115/EC — Art. 7

Voluntary departure order (ordine di allontanamento volontario)

In implementation of Directive 2008/115/EC (the Return Directive), Italian law provides that, except in cases involving a risk of absconding or danger to public order, a deported foreign national must be granted a period of 7 to 30 days to carry out voluntary departure. Voluntary departure within this period avoids forced repatriation and may affect the duration of the re-entry ban. When the Police Headquarters (Questura) unlawfully fails to grant the period for voluntary departure, the order may be challenged for breach of the Return Directive.

Voluntary departure period: 7-30 days

How we intervene

Defense strategies against deportation and detention

Defense against deportation requires speed and precision: the time limits are very short and the execution of repatriation can take place within a few days. Our law firm is organized to intervene with extreme urgency, providing an immediate legal response even in emergency situations.

I.

Immediate verification of non-deportability under Art. 19 TUI

The first step, in any case of deportation, is to verify whether the foreign national falls within one of the non-deportable categories under Art. 19 TUI: minors under 18 years of age, persons cohabiting with an Italian spouse or relatives, pregnant women or women within six months following childbirth, and individuals suffering from severe medical conditions requiring treatment not available in the country of origin. The presence of an absolute ground for non-deportability renders the deportation order unlawful, regardless of any other consideration.

II.

Urgent appeal under Art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011

The appeal (ricorso) against a prefectural deportation order must be lodged with the specialized Court (Tribunale) within 30 days of notification. The appeal has an automatic suspensive effect on forced removal (accompagnamento coattivo), but not on the deportation order itself. In cases of extreme urgency, we seek interim suspension of the enforcement of the order, including ex parte (inaudita altera parte), submitting documentation that demonstrates the prima facie merits (fumus boni iuris) of the appeal and the risk of irreparable prejudice from delay (periculum in mora) arising from imminent repatriation.

III.

Challenging detention in a CPR

The validation of detention in a CPR (Centro di Permanenza per i Rimpatri) may be appealed before the Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello) within 15 days. The most effective grounds of appeal include: lack of prerequisites for detention (absence of risk of absconding or of obstacles to repatriation), breach of procedural safeguards during validation (lack of defense counsel, interpreter, failure to conduct a hearing), factual impossibility of repatriation (lack of identification documents, absence of readmission agreements with the country of origin), and exceeding maximum detention limits. We intervene immediately upon notification of detention.

IV.

Plea of non-deportability based on family life (Art. 8 ECHR)

A foreign national with significant family ties in Italy — spouse, children, parents — enjoys heightened protection against deportation under Art. 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR/CEDU). The deportation of a foreign national with family ties requires an assessment of the following factors: severity of any criminal offense committed, duration of stay, age and best interests of minor children, and degree of social integration. A deportation that fails to respect these criteria violates Art. 8 ECHR.

V.

Non-refoulement: Art. 3 ECHR and risk of torture

When a deported foreign national faces a risk in the destination country of being subjected to torture or inhuman or degrading treatment, repatriation is prohibited by Art. 3 ECHR, regardless of the legality of their stay and even in the presence of serious criminal records. Documenting the risk of torture or inhuman treatment in the country of destination with up-to-date and reliable Country of Origin Information (COI) sources forms the core of this defense strategy. The prohibition under Art. 3 ECHR is absolute and admits no derogation.

VI.

Challenging the re-entry ban and request for revocation

A re-entry ban may be independently challenged before the specialized Court (Tribunale) when its duration appears disproportionate to the circumstances of the case. A foreign national who carried out voluntary departure and respected the ban for at least half of its duration may apply for early revocation, demonstrating that the grounds justifying deportation have ceased to exist. We handle both appeals against the ban and applications for early revocation, seeking the full restoration of the client’s international mobility.

A disproportionate deportation violates Art. 8 ECHR and must be annulled.

Case no. 1

Expulsion annulled for violation of art. 8 ECHR: father of Italian minor children — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2024 — Art. 13 TUI — Art. 8 ECHR

A Tunisian citizen, father of three minor children (aged five, seven, and ten) all born in Italy and Italian citizens, had received a prefectural deportation order (decreto di espulsione prefettizia) pursuant to art. 13 para. 2 let. b) TUI for failing to apply for the renewal of his residence permit for subordinate work (permesso di soggiorno per lavoro subordinato) within the statutory deadlines. The expiration of the permit had occurred during a period of hospital admission for urgent surgery, a circumstance that had prevented the timely submission of the renewal application. Our client’s wife was an Italian citizen. Our client’s entire family life took place in Italy: three minor children who were Italian citizens attending local schools, a well-established family network, and an open-ended employment contract lasting seven years with the same company, which had also offered to rehire him. The deportation order contained no assessment of the applicant’s family situation or the best interests of the Italian minor children.

We lodged an appeal (ricorso) before the Tribunale di Napoli within the statutory deadlines, pleading primarily the violation of art. 19 para. 2 let. c) TUI: a foreign national cohabiting with an Italian spouse or an Italian minor child is absolutely non-expellable. In the alternative, we argued the violation of art. 8 ECHR: the deportation of a father of three Italian minor children, who had lawfully resided in Italy for twelve years, had no criminal record, and had lost his lawful stay due to documented health reasons, constituted a disproportionate and unnecessary interference with his family life. Maslov v. On an interim basis, the Court immediately stayed the execution of the deportation order. On the merits, the order was annulled with a declaration of absolute non-expellability pursuant to art. 19 TUI, ordering the Police Headquarters (Questura) to proceed with the renewal of the residence permit (permesso di soggiorno).

Deportation order annulled — Absolute non-expellability declared — Residence permit renewed
Case no. 2

Unlawful detention in a Repatriation Center (CPR): lack of risk of absconding — Corte d’Appello di Napoli

Corte d’Appello di Napoli, Immigration Section — 2023 — Art. 14 TUI — Challenge against detention validation

A Moroccan citizen had been detained in the Pre-removal Detention Center (Centro di Permanenza per il Rimpatrio - CPR) of Palazzo San Gervasio following the issuance of a prefectural deportation order for irregular stay. The Justice of the Peace (Giudice di Pace) had validated the detention in a hearing lasting only a few minutes, without hearing the detainee (who had not been provided with legal counsel), without allowing him to produce any documentation, and without the validation order containing any specific reasoning regarding the requirement of a «risk of absconding» or the existence of obstacles to immediate repatriation. Our client, in fact, had a stable and documented residential address, an employer who had offered to assist him, and a pending application file for a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) that the Police Headquarters (Questura) had not yet finalized. He had never breached any obligation connected to previous administrative proceedings.

We challenged the validation of the detention before the Corte d’Appello di Napoli within the 15 days provided by law, pleading: (1) the violation of the right of defense due to lack of legal counsel at the validation hearing; (2) the absence of specific reasoning regarding the prerequisite of a «risk of absconding», required as an essential condition for detention by art. 14 TUI and Directive 2008/115/EC (art. 15); (3) the pendency of an administrative procedure for the issuance of a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) that should have been concluded before proceeding with detention. We produced documentation proving stable residence and an employment contract. The Court of Appeal declared the validation of the detention unlawful and ordered the immediate release of our client. Following his release, the Police Headquarters (Questura) concluded the pending administrative procedure favorably, issuing the residence permit.

Detention declared unlawful — Released from custody — Residence permit issued
Case no. 3

Non-refoulement: deportation halted due to documented risk of torture — TAR Lazio

TAR Lazio, Rome, First Section — 2024 — Ministerial deportation — Art. 3 ECHR

A Turkish citizen of Kurdish ethnicity had been issued a ministerial deportation order (decreto di espulsione ministeriale) pursuant to art. 13 para. 1 TUI, adopted by the Minister of the Interior upon request of the Turkish authorities, who had issued an arrest warrant against him for alleged participation in political activities of the HDP party, classified as a terrorist organization by Turkish authorities. Our client had been in Italy for five years, held a regularly renewed residence permit for subordinate work (permesso di soggiorno per lavoro subordinato), and had already submitted an application for international protection. Repatriation to Turkey, where an arrest warrant for political activities was pending and where detention conditions for Kurdish political prisoners were systematically documented by international human rights organizations, would have exposed our client to a concrete risk of torture and inhuman treatment.

We filed an urgent appeal (ricorso d’urgenza) before the TAR del Lazio, which is the competent court for ministerial expulsions as an expression of the discretionary power of the Minister of the Interior regarding state security. By way of interim relief, we requested the immediate stay of the expulsion order (decreto di espulsione), producing: extensive COI documentation on the situation of Kurdish political prisoners in Turkey (reports by Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International, European Commission 2023 on Turkey); documentation of the appellant’s political activity, which was completely lawful in Italy; the pendency of the application for international protection. The TAR granted the application for interim relief by urgent single-judge decree (decreto monocratico d’urgenza) issued on the same day, staying the ministerial decree. On the merits, the TAR annulled the expulsion order, ruling that the Minister of the Interior had not adequately weighed the risk of torture and inhuman treatment in the country of destination, in violation of art. 3 CEDU and the principle of non-refoulement. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) granted refugee status to the applicant.

Ministerial expulsion annulled — Refugee status granted
Case no. 4

Expulsion due to criminal record: proportionality violated — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Sezione Specializzata Immigrazione — 2023 — Art. 13 co. 2 lett. c) TUI — Preventive measures

An Albanian citizen, who had been residing in Italy for eighteen years, had been the recipient of a prefectural expulsion order (decreto di espulsione prefettizia) issued following the application against him of a preventive measure (special public security surveillance — sorveglianza speciale di Pubblica Sicurezza) for alleged ties to organized crime circles. The special surveillance order was itself the subject of a separate appeal before the Tribunale di Napoli, Sezione Misure di Prevenzione. Our client had lived in Italy throughout the entire period since early childhood, had an Italian wife and two adult Italian children, worked as an employee of a construction company, and had never received any final criminal convictions: the preventive measure had been applied on circumstantial grounds, without conviction. The expulsion order did not contain any assessment of the individual’s family situation and integration in Italy.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the Tribunale di Napoli, mounting a defense on two parallel fronts: in the prevention proceedings, challenging the legal grounds for special surveillance; in the expulsion proceedings, pleading the unlawfulness of the expulsion order for breach of the principle of proportionality under art. 8 CEDU. We documented the applicant’s exceptional roots in Italy: eighteen years of continuous residence, entry as a minor, his entire life built in Italy, Italian children, stable employment, and the absence of final criminal convictions. The Court annulled the expulsion order, declaring that — in the absence of final criminal convictions and in the presence of eighteen years of family and social ties — expulsion constituted a disproportionate measure and one not necessary in a democratic society pursuant to art. 8 CEDU. The preventive measure was subsequently revoked by the Tribunale di Prevenzione.

Expulsion annulled — Proportionality under art. 8 CEDU — Deep-rooted ties recognized
Case no. 5

Entry ban: early revocation obtained following voluntary repatriation

Naples Police Headquarters (Questura di Napoli) — Administrative proceeding — 2024 — Art. 13 co. 13 TUI

A Senegalese citizen had voluntarily left Italy in 2021 in execution of a prefectural expulsion order (decreto di espulsione prefettizia), following an unsuccessful judicial appeal against the measure. The order was accompanied by a five-year re-entry ban (divieto di reingresso), expiring in 2026. In the meantime, his personal situation had changed profoundly: his daughter, born in Italy to his Italian partner, had grown up and acquired Italian citizenship by birth from an Italian mother; from Germany (where he had relocated lawfully after repatriating to Senegal), our client had maintained continuous contact with his daughter and partner, including regular visits within German territory; he had found stable, lawful employment in Germany; he had committed no offenses in the period following the expulsion. He wished to return to Italy to join his partner and daughter, but the re-entry ban to the Schengen area prevented him from doing so.

We submitted an application for early revocation of the re-entry ban pursuant to art. 13 co. 13-bis TUI to the Police Headquarters (Questura di Napoli), demonstrating: full compliance with the re-entry ban from 2021 to the date of the application (three out of five years, more than half of the period); the cessation of the reasons that had prompted the original expulsion (unlawful stay had been resolved through lawful residence in Germany); the existence of a family unit in Italy (a minor Italian daughter and a cohabiting Italian partner); the availability of adequate means of subsistence and an employment contract. We attached complete documentation: the daughter’s birth certificate, the partner’s declaration, the German employment contract, bank statements, and Italian and German criminal record certificates (certificato del casellario giudiziale). Following a three-month inquiry, the Questura granted the application and revoked the re-entry ban, allowing our client to return to Italy and initiate family reunification procedures with his partner.

Re-entry ban revoked early — Return to Italy authorized
