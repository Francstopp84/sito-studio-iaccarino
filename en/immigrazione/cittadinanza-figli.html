Art. 4 para. 2 L. 91/1992

Was your child born in Italy but is not Italian?
The law grants them the right to citizenship upon reaching eighteen years of age. Do not let the time limit expire.

Art. 4 para. 2 of Law 5 February 1992, no. 91 grants the child of foreign nationals born in Italy who has lawfully and uninterruptedly resided there from birth until reaching the age of majority the right to declare the intention to acquire Italian citizenship. This is a potestative right (diritto potestativo): if the young person submits the declaration within one year of their eighteenth birthday and the requirements are met, citizenship is acquired automatically. After that time limit — having passed fruitlessly — the possibility is lost forever. Our firm assists young people and their families at every stage: from verifying the requirements to resolving issues of interrupted residence, from appeals against municipal refusals to judicial action.

Regulatory framework

Citizenship for those born in Italy: statutory sources and case law

Art. 4 para. 2 L. 91/1992 — Fundamental provision

The right to citizenship for those born in Italy: requirements and procedure

Art. 4 para. 2 of Law 5 February 1992, no. 91 (New rules on citizenship) provides that «a foreign national born in Italy, who has resided there lawfully without interruption until reaching the age of majority, becomes a citizen if they declare, within one year of reaching the age of majority, their intention to acquire Italian citizenship». There are therefore four requirements: a) birth in Italy; b) uninterrupted lawful residence from birth until reaching eighteen years of age; c) declaration of intention to acquire citizenship; d) submission of the declaration within the year following the eighteenth birthday. Birth in Italy and uninterrupted residence are the most delicate requirements: case law has progressively clarified what is meant by «lawful residence» (necessarily accompanied by a valid residence title of the parent) and by «uninterrupted» (which admits temporary, non-prolonged absences, according to the criteria of the Civil Code). The provision constitutes a tempered ius soli: it requires not only birth in Italy but also continuous residence throughout minority.

Art. 33 L. 98/2013 — Simplified procedure

Procedural simplification of 2013: declaration to the civil registrar (Ufficiale di stato civile)

Art. 33 of Law Decree 21 June 2013, no. 69, converted with amendments into Law 9 August 2013, no. 98, simplified the procedure for the acquisition of citizenship pursuant to Art. 4 para. 2 L. 91/1992. Prior to the 2013 reform, the declaration had to be submitted to the police headquarters (Questura); since 2013, the declaration is submitted to the civil registrar (Ufficiale di stato civile) of the municipality of residence, who is required to verify the prerequisites and register the acquisition of citizenship. The simplification did not alter the substantive requirements, but made the procedure more accessible and faster. A municipality that denies registration adopts a challengeable administrative act: the young person may lodge an appeal (ricorso) with the ordinary court (Tribunale ordinario - civil division), which assesses whether the statutory requirements are met and orders the municipality to register the acquisition of citizenship if the prerequisites are satisfied.

Art. 33 L. 98/2013

The criterion of actual residence

Art. 33 L. 98/2013 allows the young person born in Italy to obtain citizenship even where the residence requirement is not met on paper because of failures attributable to the parents or to the offices. What matters is that the young person has genuinely and continuously lived in Italy, attended Italian schools, and is integrated into the Italian social fabric. A mere administrative gap, caused by bureaucratic failures rather than by a lack of actual residence, should not preclude the acquisition of citizenship for someone who has lived their entire life in Italy.

Uninterrupted legal residence — Crucial prerequisite

The calculation of residence: what is meant by «uninterrupted» and «legal»

The requirement of uninterrupted legal residence generates the greatest number of disputes. «Legal» means that the parent must have held a lawful stay in Italy for the entire duration of the child's minority: if the parent had periods of irregular stay, these negatively impact the child's legal position. «Uninterrupted» means, according to the prevailing case law, that the young person must have maintained continuous registered residence (residenza anagrafica) in Italy, without deregistrations and re-registrations evidencing transfers of residence abroad. Temporary absences for holidays, medical treatment, or visits to relatives abroad do not interrupt residence if actual residence remained in Italy. Deregistration due to untraceability (cancellazione per irreperibilità) ordered ex officio by the Municipality (Comune) may be challenged when it does not correspond to an actual absence.

Uninterrupted legal residence — art. 4 co. 2 L. 91/1992

Please note: the deadline is mandatory and strictly enforced (termine perentorio). The declaration for the acquisition of citizenship must be submitted to the civil registrar (Ufficiale di stato civile) of the Municipality (Comune) of residence within one year of turning eighteen years of age. This deadline is strictly preclusive: after it expires, a young person born in Italy permanently loses the opportunity to acquire citizenship under art. 4 co. 2 and must follow the ordinary naturalisation procedure (which requires ten years of legal residence after reaching the age of majority). Difference between pure «ius soli» and «conditioned ius soli». Art. 4 co. 2 L. 91/1992 is not pure ius soli (which confers citizenship automatically by the sole fact of birth within the territory, as in the USA and Brazil): it is conditioned ius soli (ius soli temperato), which requires birth within the territory and uninterrupted legal residence throughout minority. The political debate concerning ius soli and ius culturae is separate from this legal mechanism, which has been current law since 1992. Many families are unaware that their children born in Italy already hold this right: it is essential to promptly verify the young person's position prior to their eighteenth birthday.

Cases we handle regarding citizenship for children born in Italy

Each situation possesses specific characteristics that require a tailored legal analysis. From the simplest case (declaration when all requirements are met) to the most complex (remedying interrupted residence or acquisition for stateless children), our firm is thoroughly familiar with all scenarios.

Standard case

Declaration upon reaching 18 years of age

The typical case: the young person was born in Italy, lived here continuously with lawful registered residence (residenza anagrafica) throughout their minority, and the parents always held valid residence permits. The young person appears before the civil registrar (Ufficiale di stato civile) of the Municipality (Comune) of residence within one year of their eighteenth birthday, submits the required documentation, and acquires Italian citizenship. The Municipality sometimes raises bureaucratic obstacles or requests excessive documentation: in such cases, a formal legal notice (diffida) is often sufficient to unblock the procedure.

Deadline: within 1 year of the 18th birthday
Continuous residence

Verification and documentation of uninterrupted residence

When civil registry residence has suffered formal interruptions — registrations in AIRE (Registry of Italians Residing Abroad / Anagrafe degli Italiani Residenti all’Estero), deregistrations due to untraceability, delays in re-registration — it is necessary to assess whether the formal interruption corresponds to an actual absence from Italy or is the result of bureaucratic errors. Legal protection is available in cases where actual residence was continuous despite formal administrative gaps. We reconstruct the history of actual residence through school certificates, medical records, tax returns, and parents' payslips.

Evidence: school attendance, INPS, National Health Service (SSN)
Regularisation

Interruption of residence: regularisation and judicial protection

When the interruption of residence is real — for example, due to a period of study or living abroad during minority — the situation is more complex, but not necessarily without remedy. Case law has acknowledged that short periods of absence do not interrupt the requirement if residence was maintained as the centre of the young person's interests. Each case must be analysed individually: duration of the absence, age at the time of the absence, reinstatement of residence upon return.

Case-by-case analysis
Protection

Children of holders of international protection

Children of refugees or beneficiaries of subsidiary protection born in Italy have the same rights as others born in Italy pursuant to art. 4 co. 2 L. 91/1992, with one specific feature: the periods of the parents' stay covered by an asylum application permit or international protection are counted towards the child's legal residence. This aspect is frequently overlooked by Municipalities (Comuni), which sometimes exclude these periods from the calculation of the child's legal residence, with the result of unjustly denying the acquisition of citizenship to young people who have lived their entire lives in Italy with parents holding protection.

Legal basis: art. 4 co. 2 L. 91/1992 — equal protection
Statelessness

Stateless children born in Italy: acquisition of citizenship

The child of stateless parents born in Italy acquires Italian citizenship pursuant to art. 1 co. 1 lett. b) L. 91/1992, which provides for the acquisition of citizenship by birth for «anyone born in the territory of the Republic if both parents are unknown or stateless, or if the child does not follow the citizenship of the parents according to the law of the State to which they belong». The child of stateless parents is entitled to Italian citizenship by birth, not only upon reaching eighteen years of age. However, Municipalities (Comuni) often do not proceed ex officio with the registration: it is necessary to initiate the procedure, where appropriate by judicial means.

Source: art. 1 para. 1 let. b) L. 91/1992
Automatic

Automatic acquisition: foreign minor adopted by an Italian citizen

A foreign minor adopted by an Italian citizen or by two spouses of whom at least one is an Italian citizen acquires Italian citizenship automatically as a result of the adoption, pursuant to art. 3 L. 91/1992. The acquisition is immediate from the moment the decree of the Juvenile Court (Tribunale per i Minorenni) pronouncing the adoption becomes final. Similarly, a foreign minor recognized as a child by an Italian citizen acquires citizenship pursuant to art. 2 L. 91/1992. In both cases, the Municipality (Comune) is required to register the acquisition, and any refusal or delay may be challenged.

Source: arts. 2 and 3 L. 91/1992 — immediate acquisition

How we intervene

Defense strategies for the citizenship of children born in Italy

Citizenship is the most valuable asset that a young person born and raised in Italy can acquire. Our strategy is geared toward maximizing the chances of success, acting promptly and with documentary precision, and resorting to judicial avenues when the public administration places unlawful obstacles.

I.

Preliminary verification of requirements (before the 18th birthday)

The most effective action is preventive: analyzing the young person’s position one or two years prior to their eighteenth birthday to verify the existence of all requirements and identify any critical issues to be resolved in advance. We examine the young person’s civil registry history, verify the regularity of the parents’ residence permits (titoli di soggiorno) over the years, identify any formal interruptions in residency, and evaluate the possibilities of rectification. This preventive work avoids being faced with a refusal from the Municipality (Comune) without sufficient time to challenge it before the expiry of the one-year deadline.

II.

Assistance in submitting the declaration to the Municipality

The declaration to the Civil Status Registrar (Ufficiale di stato civile) of the Municipality (Comune) must be accompanied by the proper documentation: birth certificate, historical certificate of residency, documentation of the parents’ residence permits, and any documentation of actual residency. We assist the young person in gathering and filing all necessary documentation, ensuring that the application is complete and that the Municipality cannot raise bureaucratic pretexts. In the event of difficulties in obtaining foreign documents, we initiate the acquisition procedure through diplomatic channels or proof by equivalents.

III.

Appeal before the Ordinary Court against the refusal of the Municipality

A Municipality (Comune) that denies the registration of the acquisition of citizenship adopts an administrative act that can be challenged before the Ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario - civil law section), which rules according to the rules of proceedings in chambers (camera di consiglio). This is not an appeal to the Regional Administrative Court (TAR), but an action to establish the right to citizenship, which can be exercised even after the expiry of the one-year term if the young person had submitted the declaration in time but the Municipality had delayed the decision. The Court can establish the acquisition of citizenship and order the Municipality to carry out the registration.

IV.

Reconstruction of actual residency

When registered residency (residenza anagrafica) presents formal gaps, we build the evidentiary file of actual residency through alternative documents: school attendance certificates for each year from early childhood to high school, medical records from the general practitioner, enrollments in sports and recreational activities, parents’ tax returns indicating the tax domicile, parents’ pay slips, lease agreements, utility bills. This documentary material, organized chronologically, demonstrates that the young person has always lived in Italy even during periods in which registered residency was formally absent or interrupted.

V.

Urgent relief when the one-year deadline approaches

If the young person is nearing the expiry of the one-year deadline and the Municipality (Comune) delays in responding, it is possible to petition the Ordinary Court for urgent relief pursuant to art. 700 c.p.c. enjoining the Municipality from allowing the deadline to elapse and ordering provisional registration pending proceedings. This precautionary remedy is admissible when the refusal or delay of the Municipality risks causing the irremediable loss of a fundamental right. Our experience in summary proceedings allows us to act with the necessary speed in these situations.

VI.

Alternative path: naturalization and ius culturae

When the one-year deadline has already expired or the requirements of art. 4 para. 2 are not met, we analyze alternative paths: naturalization through residency (which, for foreign nationals born in Italy, provides for halving the required period of residency from ten to five years after turning eighteen), and any pending legislative proposals regarding ius culturae, which could expand the possibilities of acquiring citizenship for young people raised in Italy. We keep our clients updated on legislative developments to seize every new opportunity.

The one-year deadline admits of no exceptions. Case law has consistently affirmed that the one-year deadline from the eighteenth birthday for submitting the declaration pursuant to art. 4 para. 2 L. 91/1992 is mandatory (perentorio) and is not subject to relief from forfeiture (rimessione in termini), even for just cause. The only recognized exception is where the Municipality (Comune) has unlawfully rejected a declaration submitted on time: in this case, the deadline remains open until the refusal of the Municipality has become res judicata. It is therefore essential to submit the declaration prior to the deadline, even in cases of uncertainty regarding the requirements: the application is filed and the issue of the prerequisites is discussed subsequently.

Case no. 1

Citizenship obtained despite deregistration from civil registry due to untraceability — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, First Civil Section — 2023 — Art. 4 paragraph 2 L. 91/1992 — Actual residence

A young man of Moroccan origin, born in Naples in 2004, reached the age of eighteen in 2022 and appeared before the Civil Registrar (Ufficiale di stato civile) of the Municipality (Comune) of Naples to declare his intention to acquire Italian citizenship. The Municipality rejected the application, noting that his civil registry records showed an ex officio deregistration due to untraceability (cancellazione per irreperibilità) issued in 2014, when the youth was ten years old, lasting approximately fourteen months prior to re-registration upon the parents’ request. The deregistration had been ordered by the Municipality following a civil registry inspection that identified the parents’ failure to reply to a communication sent to their street address, which had in the meantime changed without timely notification to the civil registry (anagrafe). However, the youth had continued to attend schools in Naples throughout the entire deregistration period, as attested by school attendance registers.

We challenged the Municipality’s refusal before the Tribunale di Napoli, compiling a documentary file demonstrating the youth’s uninterrupted actual residence in Italy throughout his entire minority: school report cards and attendance registers from first grade of primary school through third grade of lower secondary school, the National Health Service (SSN) general practitioner’s file certifying medical visits from 2005 to 2022, gas and electricity (ENEL) utility bills registered in the parents’ names at the Naples address throughout the deregistration period, and the parents’ tax returns showing the Naples apartment as their tax domicile. The Court upheld the appeal by decree of 14 February 2023, ordering the Municipality (Comune) of Naples to register the young man’s acquisition of Italian citizenship.

Italian citizenship acquired — Actual residence recognized despite formal gap
Case no. 2

Citizenship for daughter of refugee parents: period with asylum permit counted — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, First Civil Section — 2024 — Art. 4 paragraph 2 L. 91/1992 — Protection holders

A young woman of Eritrean origin, born in Naples in 2005 to parents who had lodged an asylum application at the time of her conception, reached the age of eighteen in 2023 and submitted her declaration of acquisition of citizenship to the Municipality (Comune) of Naples. The father had held refugee status since 2009, after four years of proceedings, while the mother had obtained subsidiary protection in the same year. The Municipality rejected the application, asserting that the first four years of the young woman’s life — from 2005 to 2009, corresponding to the period during which the parents held only the asylum seeker permit (permesso per richiesta asilo) — could not be calculated toward the “lawful residence” required by art. 4 paragraph 2, on the grounds that an asylum seeker permit allegedly does not guarantee the “lawfulness” of residence in the full sense. The Municipality relied on the assertion that only after the grant of protection in 2009 had the parents become “lawful” in the proper sense.

We challenged the refusal before the Tribunale di Napoli, arguing that an asylum seeker permit is a lawful residence title for all legal intents and purposes: an asylum seeker holding such a permit is authorized to reside in Italy, is registered with the civil registry (anagrafe), is entitled to healthcare, and is permitted to work. Their residence is fully “lawful” pursuant to art. 4 paragraph 2 of Law 91/1992, and consequently, the residence of the cohabiting minor child is equally lawful. We submitted the parents’ permits for the years 2005–2009 and the young woman’s civil registry entries for the entire period. The Court upheld the appeal by decree of 12 April 2024, ruling that the period of residence under an asylum seeker permit must be calculated in full toward the child’s lawful residence.

Citizenship acquired — Asylum permit period counted
Case no. 3

Curing of interruption due to short-term study stay abroad — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, First Civil Section — 2023 — Art. 4 paragraph 2 L. 91/1992 — Temporary absence

A young woman of Chinese origin, born in Naples in 2004 to parents who were restaurateurs, reached the age of eighteen in 2022. Her civil registry history was substantially uninterrupted, but showed a deregistration from the civil registry of Naples and temporary registration in the Registry of Italians Residing Abroad (AIRE - Anagrafe Italiani Residenti all’Estero) for an eleven-month period between 2016 and 2017, when she was twelve years old, during which her parents had enrolled her in a Chinese language enhancement school in Shanghai. At the end of the school period, the family returned to Naples and the young woman resumed her schooling in Italy. The Municipality (Comune) of Naples rejected the declaration of acquisition of citizenship, asserting that registration in AIRE and the resulting eleven-month deregistration from the civil registry of Naples constituted an interruption of residence such as to preclude the application of art. 4 paragraph 2 of Law 91/1992.

We challenged the refusal (*diniego*) before the Court of Naples (*Tribunale di Napoli*), putting forward arguments on two levels. On a formal level, we pointed out that registration with the register of Italians residing abroad (*AIRE*) is optional and does not necessarily entail the transfer of actual residence (*residenza effettiva*): the young woman had maintained her centre of interests in Naples (her parents were Neapolitan restaurateurs, the family home was in Naples) and her stay in Shanghai had been temporary and driven by educational reasons that did not imply a definitive transfer of residence. The Court granted the appeal (*ricorso*) by decree (*decreto*) of 4 July 2023, stating that an absence of eleven months for educational reasons does not constitute an interruption of residence when the family unit and the centre of interests have remained in Italy.

Citizenship acquired — Educational stay abroad does not constitute an interruption
Case no. 4

Citizenship for child of parents with periods of irregular stay: protection of good faith — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Prima Sezione Civile — 2024 — Art. 4 co. 2 L. 91/1992 — Parental irregularity

A young man of Moroccan origin, born in Naples in 2004, had submitted a declaration of acquisition of citizenship upon turning eighteen in 2022. The Municipality of Naples (*Comune di Napoli*) had rejected the application, noting that the father, who had acknowledged his son and with whom the young man had cohabited, had undergone a period of irregular stay of approximately eight months in 2011, when the young man was seven years old, due to the failure to timely renew his residence permit for subordinate employment (*permesso per lavoro dipendente*) following the loss of his job. The mother, with whom the young man had subsequently cohabited, had been lawfully residing throughout the entire duration of her son’s minority without any interruption. The Municipality (*Comune*) had stated that the irregularity of the father’s stay, even if limited in time, interrupted the requirement of «legal residence» (*residenza legale*) of the child for the corresponding period, rendering art. 4 co. 2 inapplicable.

We challenged the refusal (diniego) before the Tribunale di Napoli, arguing that the requirement of «legal residence» of the child must be assessed with reference to the status of the parent with whom the child predominantly cohabited. The young man had primarily lived with his mother, who was lawfully residing for the entire duration of his minor age. The father's period of irregular status did not reflect the legal status of the cohabiting mother. We submitted the civil registry declarations (dichiarazioni anagrafiche) attesting to the predominant cohabitation with the mother, the mother’s residence permits (permessi di soggiorno) for the entire period, and school communications addressed to the mother as the young person’s contact person. The Court granted the petition (ricorso) by decree (decreto) of 17 September 2024, recognising citizenship.

Citizenship acquired — Residence assessed with respect to the primary cohabiting parent
Case no. 5

Urgent measure to prevent the expiry of the one-year deadline — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Prima Sezione Civile — 2024 — Art. 700 c.p.c. — Urgency — Art. 4 co. 2 L. 91/1992

A young man of Senegalese origin had submitted the declaration of acquisition of citizenship (dichiarazione di acquisto della cittadinanza) to the Municipality of Naples (Comune di Napoli) eleven months after his eighteenth birthday, with a one-month margin before the expiry of the one-year deadline. The Municipality had received the application (istanza) and issued a receipt, but in the course of the preliminary inquiry (istruttoria) it had raised a series of objections concerning the documentary file, subsequently requesting supplementary documentation: first the residence permits (permessi di soggiorno) of his parents for all the years of his minority, then school enrolment certificates year by year, and then the sworn translation of his foreign birth certificate (atto di nascita). The one-month margin had progressively narrowed and, just a few days before the expiry of the one-year deadline, the Municipality had still not issued a decision on the application. The concrete risk was that the Municipality would issue an adverse decision after the deadline had expired, or simply fail to rule in time, leaving the deadline to lapse without the young man having received any response.

With very few days to spare, we filed an urgent petition (*ricorso*) pursuant to art. 700 c.p.c. before the Court of Naples (*Tribunale di Napoli*), asking the judge to order the Municipality (*Comune*) to suspend the running of the one-year deadline pending the decision on the application (*istanza*) submitted on time, and to conclude the application within a judicially set deadline. We documented the timeliness of the submission of the application (receipt from the Municipality dated eleven months after the eighteenth birthday), the completeness of the documentary file as submitted, and the unreasonableness of the subsequent objections raised by the Municipality that had not been raised upon receipt of the application. The Court issued an ex parte decree (*decreto inaudita altera parte*) on the very same day, enjoining the Municipality from allowing the one-year deadline to expire and ordering that the case be finalized within thirty days. The Municipality registered the young man’s acquisition of citizenship within the time limit ordered by the judge.

Deadline preserved — Citizenship registered within judicially ordered time limits
