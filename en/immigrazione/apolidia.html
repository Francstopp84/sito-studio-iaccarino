Does no country recognize you as a citizen?
Statelessness is a denied right that can be claimed.

A stateless person is an individual whom no State considers as its citizen under its own legislation. A condition that deprives those who experience it of identity documents, the right to lawful employment, the ability to travel, access to social benefits, and full legal capacity. Italy ratified the 1954 New York Convention relating to the Status of Stateless Persons and provides for administrative and judicial procedures for the recognition of this status. The process is complex and requires specific expertise: our firm assists stateless persons at every stage, from the recognition of status to obtaining a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) and — where possible — Italian citizenship after five years of legal residence.

Regulatory framework

Statelessness in international law and the Italian legal system

New York Convention of 28 September 1954

The Convention relating to the Status of Stateless Persons: the fundamental definition and recognized rights

The Convention relating to the Status of Stateless Persons, adopted in New York on 28 September 1954 under the auspices of the United Nations and ratified by Italy by Law No. 306 of 1 February 1962, is the principal international instrument for the protection of stateless persons. Article 1 defines a stateless person as «a person who is not considered as a national by any State under the operation of its law». This is a negative definition: statelessness does not depend on an individual's choice, but on the gaps within national legal systems that do not recognize the person as their citizen. The Convention grants stateless persons a series of fundamental rights: the right not to be expelled save on grounds of national security or public order (art. 31), the right to obtain travel documents (art. 28), the right to wage-earning employment (art. 17), the right of access to education (art. 22), and the right to be protected by the same civil rights guaranteed to resident foreign nationals. Article 32 requires Contracting States to facilitate the assimilation and naturalization of stateless persons as far as possible, recognizing naturalization as the definitive remedy to end the condition of statelessness.

1954 New York Convention — Law No. 306 of 1 February 1962
Convention of 30 August 1961 on the Reduction of Statelessness

The prevention of statelessness: the obligation of States not to create new stateless persons

The Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness, adopted in New York on 30 August 1961 and ratified by Italy by Law No. 217 of 3 November 2016, imposes a series of obligations on States to prevent the emergence of new cases of statelessness. Article 1 obliges Contracting States to grant their nationality to a person born in their territory who would otherwise be stateless. Article 8 prohibits States from depriving an individual of nationality if such deprivation would render them stateless, subject to strictly defined exceptions. Article 9 prohibits the mass deprivation of nationality on racial, ethnic, religious, or political grounds. The 1961 Convention is systematically invoked in cases of statelessness arising from State succession — particularly concerning individuals originating from former Soviet countries or the former Yugoslavia, where citizenship laws enacted by successor States after the dissolution left numerous persons without nationality — as well as in cases of discriminatory revocation of citizenship.

1961 Convention — Law No. 217 of 3 November 2016
Art. 17 Presidential Decree No. 572 of 12 October 1993

The Italian procedure for the recognition of stateless status

Art. 17 of D.P.R. 12 ottobre 1993, n. 572 (Implementing Regulation of L. 91/1992 on citizenship) governs the procedure for the recognition of stateless status in Italy. Recognition is requested by filing an application (istanza) with the Ministry of the Interior (Direzione Centrale per i Diritti Civili, la Cittadinanza e le Minoranze), accompanied by documentation certifying the absence of any nationality. The procedure is administrative in nature, but case law has clarified that the rejection of the application may be challenged before the ordinary court (giudice ordinario). Recognition of the status has a declaratory and not a constitutive effect: it ascertains a pre-existing condition, with retroactive effect from the date on which the statelessness occurred. Recognition is an indispensable prerequisite for the issuance of a residence permit for statelessness (permesso di soggiorno per apolidia) and for acquiring entitlement to Italian citizenship pursuant to art. 9 co. 1 lett. e) of L. 91/1992 after five years of legal residence.

Art. 17 D.P.R. 572/1993 — Recognition procedure
Art. 9 para. 1 let. e) Law No. 91 of 5 February 1992

Italian citizenship for stateless persons after five years of lawful residence

Article 9, paragraph 1, letter e) of Law no. 91 of 5 February 1992 provides that a stateless foreign national who has been legally resident in Italy for at least five years may acquire Italian citizenship by naturalisation. The five-year period is significantly shorter than the ten years required for ordinary foreign nationals (art. 9, para. 1, let. f)), and represents the Italian legislature’s response to Article 32 of the 1954 Convention, which requires States to facilitate the naturalisation of stateless persons. The essential prerequisite is the formal recognition of stateless status pursuant to art. 17 of Presidential Decree (D.P.R.) 572/1993: without formal recognition, the five years of residence do not begin to run for the purposes of citizenship. The application is submitted to the competent Prefecture (Prefettura) and requires proof of uninterrupted legal residence, knowledge of Italian (level B1), the absence of disqualifying criminal convictions, and the absence of security concerns. Our firm assists clients from the application for status recognition through to the oath-taking ceremony.

Art. 9 para. 1 let. e) Law 91/1992 — Citizenship for stateless persons after 5 years

Pay attention to the calculation of time limits for citizenship: the five-year period for naturalization runs from the date of formal recognition of stateless status, not from the date of arrival in Italy. Every year spent without initiating the recognition procedure is a year lost for citizenship purposes. If you have been living in Italy for years without having initiated this procedure, contact us immediately for an assessment of your case.

The forms of statelessness we handle

Statelessness is not a uniform category: it manifests in different forms, each requiring distinct pathways for recognition and legal protection. We identify the applicable legal classification for your specific case and design the most effective legal strategy.

Formal recognition

Recognition of stateless status

Formal recognition of stateless person status by the Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell’Interno) is the prerequisite for accessing all protections provided for under the 1954 Convention: residence permit for statelessness (permesso di soggiorno per apolidia), travel document pursuant to art. 28 of the Convention, commencement of the five-year period required for citizenship. The application is submitted to the Central Directorate for Civil Rights, Citizenship and Minorities (Direzione Centrale per i Diritti Civili, la Cittadinanza e le Minoranze). Proof of statelessness requires documentation from all countries with which the applicant has or may have ties: gathering this documentation is often the most complex phase of the entire procedure.

Application to the Ministry of the Interior — Duration ranging from 6 to 24 months
De facto statelessness

De facto statelessness

De facto statelessness is distinguished from de jure statelessness: in this case, the individual is formally a citizen of a State, but in practice cannot exercise the rights connected to that citizenship because the country of origin does not recognize them, does not issue them documents, or does not offer them effective protection. It is frequent among ethnic-religious minorities discriminated against by their country of origin. Italian case law has recognized the relevance of de facto statelessness for protection purposes, although the formal recognition path is more complex than for de jure statelessness.

Individual assessment — COI and consular documentation required
De jure statelessness

De jure statelessness

De jure statelessness occurs when no State considers the individual as its citizen under the operation of its law. It can derive from gaps in citizenship legislation (negative conflicts of national laws), state succession (dissolution of the USSR, Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia), discriminatory revocations of citizenship, or situations where the individual has never acquired any citizenship from birth. It is the clearest form of statelessness and the one for which the recognition procedure under the 1954 Convention is best structured in the case law of the Italian giurisprudenza consolidata.

Recognition pursuant to art. 17 D.P.R. 572/1993 — Retroactive declaratory effect
Residence

Residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for statelessness

Following the recognition of stateless status, the Police Commissioner (Questore) issues a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for statelessness with a two-year validity, which is renewable. The permit allows the holder to work, to access healthcare and education services, and to accrue the period of lawful residence required for naturalization. Pending formal recognition, it is possible in certain cases to obtain a permit on humanitarian grounds or for special protection (protezione speciale) ensuring temporary regularisation. Our firm assists clients in obtaining the permit in parallel with the status recognition procedure, minimizing periods of administrative irregularity.

Two-year duration — Renewable until naturalization
Naturalization

Italian citizenship for stateless persons

A recognized stateless person who has lawfully resided in Italy for five years may apply for Italian citizenship by naturalization pursuant to art. 9 co. 1 lett. e) L. 91/1992. The five-year period is half of that required for standard foreign nationals. The application is submitted to the territorially competent Prefecture (Prefettura) and requires: uninterrupted lawful residence, knowledge of the Italian language (level B1), absence of disqualifying criminal convictions, and absence of national security concerns. The proceedings conclude with the oath of allegiance to the Republic and transcription into the civil status registers. Our firm assists clients from the preparation of the dossier through to the oath-taking ceremony.

After 5 years of lawful residence — Preferential term compared to standard foreign nationals
Minors

Statelessness of minors

Children of stateless parents risk being born stateless themselves if no State recognizes them as its citizens. The 1961 Convention requires States to grant their nationality to children born in their territory who would otherwise be stateless. A minor child of a stateless person recognized in Italy can obtain recognition of their stateless status and the relevant residence permit (permesso di soggiorno). Upon reaching the age of majority at eighteen, a minor who has lawfully resided in Italy may in certain cases benefit from accelerated routes to citizenship. Our firm handles cases involving minors with absolute priority and utmost protection of the best interests of the child.

How we intervene

Strategies for the recognition of stateless status and naturalization

The recognition of stateless status is a lengthy and technical process requiring in-depth knowledge of international nationality law, the gathering of documentary evidence abroad, and handling administrative and judicial proceedings. Here is how we operate.

I.

Analysis of the situation and identification of the legal basis

The first step is a thorough analysis of the client's situation: personal history, countries with which they have or have had ties (birth, parents, spouse, prolonged residence), citizenship laws of each relevant country, and any facts that may have resulted in the acquisition or loss of citizenship. At this stage, our firm verifies whether the client is de jure or de facto stateless, or whether there are countries that could claim their citizenship: an incorrect assessment at this stage could prejudice the entire procedure. We collaborate with comparative citizenship law practitioners for the most complex jurisdictions, including the legal systems of the former Soviet and former Yugoslav States.

II.

Collection of international documentation

Proof of statelessness requires documentation from multiple countries: birth certificates, civil status certificates, and certifications from the competent consular or ministerial authorities confirming the absence of citizenship. Collecting this evidence is often the most challenging phase of the entire procedure: many countries do not readily issue negative attestations, and foreign documents must be apostilled and translated by sworn translators. Our firm has experience in coordinating with correspondents in the main countries of origin of the stateless persons we assist, with particular familiarity with the legal systems of former Soviet countries and countries in the Middle East and North Africa.

III.

Application to the Ministry of the Interior and procedural monitoring

The application (istanza) to the Ministry of the Interior for the recognition of stateless status requires thorough preparation: each submitted document must be relevant, apostilled where required, translated, and annotated so as to make the overall factual situation immediately clear to the reviewing official. Our firm organizes the file in a structured manner and accompanies each document with an explanatory report guiding the Ministry in its assessment. We constantly monitor procedural progress, reporting unreasonable delays and requesting completion within the statutory time limits provided for by the administrative procedure act (L. 241/1990).

IV.

Judicial appeal against the rejection of the application

The rejection of an application for recognition of stateless status can be appealed before the ordinary courts (giudice ordinario). Case law has established that disputes regarding statelessness fall within the jurisdiction of the ordinary courts (rather than administrative courts), as they concern subjective rights rather than legitimate interests. The Court (Tribunale) assesses the merits of the recognition application, examining all evidence produced and applying the criteria set forth in the 1954 Convention and D.P.R. 572/1993. Our firm has experience in first-instance litigation and proceedings before the giurisprudenza consolidata in statelessness matters.

V.

Obtaining the residence permit and travel document

Following the recognition of status, we assist the client with procedures at the Police Headquarters (Questura) to obtain a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for statelessness, and at the Ministry of the Interior for the issuance of the travel document provided for by art. 28 of the 1954 Convention. The travel document for stateless persons is comparable to a passport and allows travel to countries that recognize it. We also handle any administrative hurdles in obtaining these documents, which sometimes require further legal intervention to unblock stalled proceedings.

VI.

Naturalization procedure and Italian citizenship

Upon completing the five-year lawful residence period from obtaining recognition of status, we assist the client in initiating the naturalization procedure pursuant to art. 9 co. 1 lett. e) L. 91/1992. The citizenship file is comprehensive and requires meticulous preparation: historical certificate of residence, tax returns for the last five years, criminal record certificate (certificato del casellario giudiziale), proof of Italian language proficiency at B1 level, and documentation of recognized stateless status. We monitor the ministerial proceedings and, in the event of an adverse decision, file an appeal (ricorso) before the Regional Administrative Court of Lazio (TAR Lazio), which has jurisdiction over denials of citizenship by naturalization.

The «non-citizens» of the Baltic States: a paradigmatic case of statelessness. Among the most frequent cases of de jure statelessness that we assist are individuals originating from Estonia and Latvia who, following the dissolution of the USSR, did not obtain citizenship of the new Baltic States as they belonged to Russian-speaking ethnic minorities not included in the civil registers prior to Soviet occupation. These individuals held a specific travel document («grey passport» in Estonia, «violet passport» in Latvia) certifying their status as «non-citizens», assimilable to statelessness under the 1954 Convention.

Case no. 1

Recognition of stateless person status for Latvian «non-citizen» — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Prima Sezione Civile — 2023 — Convenzione New York 1954, Art. 17 D.P.R. 572/1993

A 52-year-old woman, born in Latvia to ethnically Russian parents during the Soviet era, had emigrated to Italy in 2004 after being excluded from Latvian citizenship under the legislation adopted by the Republic of Latvia following independence. Latvian law had automatically granted citizenship only to citizens of pre-1940 Latvia and their descendants, excluding individuals who immigrated to Latvia during the Soviet occupation. The applicant held the so-called Latvian «purple passport», the document issued to «non-citizens» (nepilsonis) certifying the intermediate status provided by the Latvian legal system for those not entitled to citizenship but tied to the territory. The Italian Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell’Interno) had rejected her application for recognition as a stateless person, holding that possession of the purple passport constituted a form of Latvian citizenship sufficient to preclude statelessness.

Our firm challenged the ministerial rejection before the Tribunale di Napoli, First Civil Section, demonstrating that Latvian «non-citizen» status is not equivalent to citizenship under the 1954 New York Convention. We submitted Latvian citizenship legislation and the position of UNHCR on the status of stateless persons in Latvia and Estonia. The Court granted the petition (ricorso) by judgment of 22 November 2023, declaring that the applicant was stateless under the 1954 Convention and ordering the Ministry of the Interior to register her as such. Following the judgment, the Naples Police Headquarters (Questura di Napoli) issued the residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for statelessness, and the Ministry of the Interior issued the travel document for stateless persons. The applicant began the five-year qualifying period for Italian citizenship, which she obtained the following year, having already been in Italy for more than five years at the time of recognition.

Stateless status recognized — Residence permit issued — Italian citizenship obtained
Case no. 2

Statelessness arising from the dissolution of the former Yugoslavia: a citizen of nowhere — Corte d’Appello di Napoli

Corte d’Appello di Napoli, First Civil Section — 2024 — 1961 Convention, 1954 Convention, Art. 17 D.P.R. 572/1993

A 48-year-old man, born in Bosnia and Herzegovina during the Yugoslav period to ethnic Serb parents, had lived in an area of the former Yugoslavia that had changed state sovereignty several times during the Balkan wars of the 1990s. Following the dissolution of Yugoslavia, Bosnia and Herzegovina had adopted criteria for granting citizenship based on ethnicity and residence at the time of independence that excluded the applicant, who was in a contested area. Serbia, too, did not recognize him as its citizen. In Italy since 1995 as a refugee, he had lost his recognized refugee status (*status di rifugiato*) following the stabilization of the Balkans, but had been unable to obtain any Balkan citizenship. The Ministry of the Interior (*Ministero dell’Interno*) had rejected his application for recognition as a stateless person (*apolide*), taking the view that it was theoretically possible for him to apply for Bosnian or Serbian citizenship. The Court of First Instance (*Tribunale di primo grado*) had upheld the rejection.

Our firm lodged an appeal before the Corte d’Appello di Napoli, documenting in detail why the legislation of both Balkan countries did not grant the applicant the right to citizenship: we submitted Bosnian and Serbian citizenship legislation, with sworn translations and a report from an expert in Balkan comparative law, as well as certification from the respective consulates in Italy confirming that the applicant was not entitled to citizenship of either country. We invoked the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness and its application to cases of state succession. The Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello) upheld the appeal by judgment of 17 April 2024, recognizing his stateless status and ordering the issuance of a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for statelessness. The applicant, who has been in Italy for nearly thirty years, immediately initiated the procedure for Italian citizenship.

Stateless status recognized on appeal — Citizenship procedure initiated
Case no. 3

Statelessness of a minor born in Italy to stateless parents: urgent recognition — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, First Civil Section — 2024 — 1961 Convention art. 1, Art. 17 D.P.R. 572/1993

A couple of recognized stateless persons, originally from Syria and residing in Naples with residence permits (permesso di soggiorno) for statelessness, had a daughter in 2022 at the Cardarelli Hospital in Naples. The child was born in Italy, but neither Syria nor any other state recognized her as its citizen: Syria, engulfed by conflict, was no longer issuing birth certificates through its consulates and did not recognize parentage through consular channels; the parents were recognized stateless persons, devoid of any nationality. The child was therefore stateless from birth. The Ministry of the Interior, to which the parents had submitted an application for recognition of stateless status for their daughter, rejected the application, deeming the impossibility of obtaining Syrian citizenship for the child insufficiently proven in light of the abstract possibility of consular registration at an unspecified future date.

Our firm challenged the rejection before the Tribunale di Napoli with a petition (ricorso) based on Art. 1 of the 1961 Convention, which obliges contracting states to grant their nationality to children born in their territory who would otherwise be stateless, and on Art. 2 of the same Convention, which requires the granting of citizenship to children of stateless persons born in the territory of the state. We documented the practical — and not merely theoretical — impossibility of obtaining consular registration at the Syrian embassy in Rome, submitting documentation on the suspension of Syrian consular services in Italy and certifications from numerous humanitarian organizations regarding conditions in the country. The Court granted the petition (ricorso) by judgment of 3 July 2024, declaring the child stateless under the 1954 Convention and ordering the Municipality of Naples (Comune di Napoli) to register her in the civil registry (anagrafe) with a stateless identity document. The Ministry of the Interior issued the residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for statelessness for the minor and initiated the procedures for her travel document.

Stateless child recognized — Civil registry enrollment obtained — Identity document issued
Case no. 4

De facto statelessness of a Palestinian stateless minority: recognition and citizenship — giurisprudenza di legittimita'

giurisprudenza consolidata, First Civil Section — 2023 — 1954 Convention art. 1, Art. 17 D.P.R. 572/1993, exclusion art. 1 par. 2 letter i 1954 Convention

A 61-year-old man of Palestinian origin had entered Italy in the 1980s with a travel document issued by an Arab country, but without any citizenship. Palestinians occupy a peculiar position in the international law of statelessness: the 1954 Convention, under art. 1, par. 2, lett. i), excludes from its scope of application persons who receive protection or assistance from United Nations organs or agencies other than UNHCR, with primary reference to UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestinian refugees. The case therefore required a complex legal assessment: had the applicant ever received effective protection from UNRWA? And was that protection still available and effective at the time of the application? The Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell’Interno) had rejected the application (istanza) citing the exclusion under art. 1 par. 2. The Court of First Instance (Tribunale di primo grado) and the Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello) had upheld the rejection.

Our firm filed an appeal to the Court of Cassation (impugnazione in grado di appello), developing a comprehensive legal argument on the correct interpretation of the exclusion clause in Art. 1, par. 2, letter i) of the 1954 Convention, in line with the case law of the Court of Justice of the EU. Under this interpretation, the exclusion clause ceases to apply when UNRWA protection or assistance has ceased for any reason without the position of the persons concerned being definitively settled in accordance with the relevant UN resolutions. We proved that the applicant had never received effective protection from UNRWA, as he resided in an area not covered by the operational activities of the agency. The Court of Cassation upheld the appeal by order (ordinanza) no. 19847/2023, quashing the appellate judgment and remanding the case for reassessment in accordance with the indicated interpretive principle. The remand judge recognized his stateless status. The applicant, who has been in Italy for over thirty years, obtained Italian citizenship the following year.

Appeal to giurisprudenza di legittimita' upheld — Stateless status recognized — Italian citizenship obtained
Case no. 5

Naturalisation of a stateless person after 5 years: appeal against refusal before TAR Lazio — Citizenship obtained

TAR Lazio, Rome, Sezione I-ter — 2024 — Art. 9 para. 1 lett. e) Law 91/1992, Presidential Decree (D.P.R.) 572/1993

A 44-year-old man, originally from Eritrea, had obtained in 2018 formal recognition of stateless status pursuant to art. 17 Presidential Decree (D.P.R.) 572/1993, following a lengthy administrative procedure and a first-instance judicial appeal (ricorso). Since obtaining recognition, he had accrued more than five years of continuous legal residence in Naples, had worked as a warehouse worker under an open-ended employment contract, had obtained a CILS B2-level Italian language certification, had no criminal record, and was not flagged for security reasons. In 2023, he submitted to the Prefettura of Naples an application for naturalisation pursuant to art. 9 para. 1 lett. e) Law 91/1992. The Prefettura forwarded the file to the Ministry of the Interior, which in 2024 rejected the application on the grounds that the applicant had not demonstrated adequate economic and social integration within Italian territory, deeming the evidence submitted regarding his employment and community integration to be insufficient.

Our firm challenged the refusal before the Regional Administrative Court of Lazio (TAR Lazio), the competent court for measures issued by the Ministry of the Interior in citizenship matters. The appeal (ricorso) challenged the measure on the grounds of lack of reasoning, illogic, and disproportionality: the assessment of economic and social integration had been conducted without any reference to the specific elements contained in the applicant's file, which unambiguously demonstrated stable and continuous employment, a documented network of social ties, advanced proficiency in the Italian language, and the complete absence of any security risk. In court, we submitted: the last five years of tax returns and payslips, the open-ended employment contract, attestations from the Eritrean community in Naples, receipts of payment of taxes and social security contributions, and the CILS B2 certification. The TAR upheld the appeal with judgment no. 2341/2024, annulling the refusal and ordering the Ministry to issue a new determination within ninety days taking into account all documentary evidence submitted and providing adequate reasons for any further refusal. Following the judgment, the Ministry granted citizenship. The applicant swore the oath of allegiance to the Republic in February 2025.

Citizenship refusal annulled — Naturalisation obtained — Oath of allegiance February 2025
