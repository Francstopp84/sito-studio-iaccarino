Has the Police Headquarters (*Questura*) denied your EU long-term residence permit?
Five years of life in Italy deserve a stable residence status. We challenge the refusal.

The EU long-term residence permit (*permesso UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo*) — the former permanent «residence card» (*carta di soggiorno*) — is the most solid residence status a non-EU citizen can obtain in Italy: it is permanent, not tied to a specific reason for stay, grants rights virtually equivalent to those of an Italian citizen, and can be revoked only in strictly defined cases. It requires five years of continuous legal residence, sufficient income, suitable accommodation, and passing an A2-level Italian language test. The Police Headquarters (*Questura*) often rejects these applications alleging deficiencies in requirements that are in fact met: the refusal should be appealed. Our firm analyses each case and builds the most effective appeal (*ricorso*).

Regulatory framework

The EU long-term residence permit in Italian and European law

Art. 9 D.Lgs. 286/1998 (TUI) — Foundational provision

Requirements for the EU long-term residence permit

Article 9 of the Consolidated Immigration Act (*Testo Unico Immigrazione* – TUI) governs the EU long-term residence permit, which replaced the previous «residence card» (*carta di soggiorno*) following the transposition of Directive 2003/109/EC. Paragraph 1 establishes the cumulative requirements that a foreign national must meet to obtain this status: a) continuous legal residence in Italy for at least five years; b) availability of an income not lower than the annual amount of the social allowance (*assegno sociale*) (from 2024: approximately 6.947,33 euro per year, doubled if the application includes family members); c) availability of suitable accommodation meeting the health, hygiene, and surface-area standards required by regional law; d) passing an A2-level Italian language proficiency test. The EU long-term residence permit has an indefinite duration and is not tied to any specific reason for stay: a foreign national who obtains it may work, study, or start a business without having to update the permit at every change of circumstances. Paragraph 4 sets out an exhaustive list of grounds for inadmissibility and revocation, which must be strictly interpreted pursuant to established case law.

Art. 9 TUI — D.Lgs. 286/1998
Directive 2003/109/EC — European law

The European directive on long-term residents: direct effect and guaranteed rights

Council Directive 2003/109/EC of 25 November 2003 concerning the status of third-country nationals who are long-term residents is the European legal source governing the issuance conditions and safeguards of this permit. The directive has direct effect within the legal orders of the Member States: its provisions may be invoked directly before national courts against administrative authorities applying conflicting national rules. Article 8 of the directive provides that the permit is of permanent duration or is automatically renewable. Article 9 exhaustively lists the grounds for revocation or refusal of renewal, including absence from EU territory for more than 12 consecutive months (or 6 cumulative years). Article 11 guarantees the holder equal treatment with national citizens across numerous areas: access to employment, vocational training, education, social security, tax benefits, goods, and services.

Requirements: 5 years — Income — A2 Test — Accommodation

Calculation of the five-year period and specific requirements

The requirement of five years of continuous lawful stay is the most delicate. Case law has clarified several crucial aspects: a) periods spent in Italy holding a permit for international protection are calculated in full; b) any temporary absences from the national territory do not interrupt continuity if they do not exceed six consecutive months or ten months over five years; c) periods spent in Italy during minority are calculated if accompanied by the lawful stay of a parent or guardian; d) the pendency of an application for permit renewal does not interrupt the calculation of the period, provided the application was submitted in due time. Regarding the income requirement, the Police Headquarters (Questura) often miscalculates income by excluding items that must instead be considered: unemployment benefits, social security allowances, income of cohabiting family members. The A2 language test can be passed through CPIA centres or accredited Universities for Foreigners: the Police Headquarters (Questura) cannot oppose a refusal based on this requirement if the certificate is issued by a body accredited by the Ministry of Education.

Art. 9 para. 1 TUI — Cumulative requirements

Pay attention to the deadlines: an appeal against the refusal of an EU long-term residence permit must be lodged before the Ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario), Specialised Section on Immigration Matters, within 30 days of notification of the measure (appeal [ricorso] pursuant to art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011). An appeal (ricorso) to the Regional Administrative Court (TAR) against a refusal issued by the Prefecture (Prefettura) must be lodged within 60 days. Do not wait for the deadline to expire.

The EU long-term residence permit as a safeguard against expulsion. The holder of an EU long-term residence permit enjoys enhanced safeguards against expulsion: pursuant to art. 9 para. 5-bis TUI, the expulsion of a long-term resident may be ordered only when the foreign national represents an actual, present and sufficiently serious threat to public order or national security, and the decision must take into account the duration of the stay, age, consequences for the family unit, and ties with the country of origin.

Which situation applies to you

Cases we handle regarding residence cards

The EU long-term residence permit is subject to refusals, revocations, and disputes concerning each of the statutory requirements. Each situation requires a specific analysis of the legal prerequisites and the most recent case law.

Main title

EU long-term residence permit

The title directly governed by art. 9 TUI, issued to those who cumulatively satisfy the four requirements: five years of continuous lawful stay, sufficient income, suitable accommodation, A2 language test. A refusal is frequently based on challenges to one or more requirements: the income requirement is often calculated erroneously, or the five-year calculation excludes periods that must instead be counted. Every refusal warrants a specific analysis.

Duration: permanent — Legal basis: art. 9 TUI
Family member

Residence card for family members of long-term residents

Family members of a long-term resident who accompany or join them may apply for a residence permit for family reasons, benefiting from enhanced safeguards analogous to those of the sponsoring long-term resident. The refusal of a family permit can be challenged alleging a breach of art. 9-bis TUI and the provisions of Directive 2003/86/EC on family reunification. Protection also extends to the spouse and minor children of a long-term resident who moves to another EU Member State.

Legal basis: art. 9-bis TUI — Dir. 2003/86/EC
Criminal record refusal

Refusal based on criminal records: individual assessment

The presence of a criminal record does not constitute an automatic ground for refusal of an EU long-term residence permit. Art. 9 para. 4 TUI provides that a refusal on public order grounds must be based on an actual and individualized assessment of the applicant's present dangerousness, taking into account the severity of the offences, the time elapsed, subsequent conduct, and family integration.

Legal basis: art. 9 para. 4 TUI — principle of proportionality
Income refusal

Refusal for insufficient income: calculation errors

The income requirement is a frequent source of dispute. The Police Headquarters (Questura) often makes calculation errors: it excludes income from regularised undeclared work, fails to consider invalidity or disability benefits, fails to aggregate the incomes of cohabiting family members authorized to reside, or calculates income based on the wrong year. A refusal based on an erroneous calculation may be appealed.

Legal basis: art. 9 para. 1 let. b) TUI — social allowance amount
Revocation

Revocation of the EU long-term residence permit

The revocation of an EU long-term residence permit is governed by art. 9 para. 7 TUI and may occur only in mandatory, exhaustive cases: absence from Italian territory for more than 12 consecutive months (or 6 cumulative years), acquisition of an equivalent title in another EU Member State, danger to public order or state security, or finding that the title was obtained fraudulently. Any revocation adopted outside these exhaustive cases is unlawful. The Police Headquarters (Questura) must notify the initiation of revocation proceedings and allow the holder to submit observations prior to the final decision.

Legal basis: art. 9 para. 7 TUI — art. 9 Dir. 2003/109/EC
EU family member

Residence card for family members of an EU citizen

A foreign family member of an EU citizen is entitled to a distinct residence card, governed by D.Lgs. 30/2007 (transposing Dir. 2004/38/EC), which is distinct from the EU long-term residence permit. The family member of an EU citizen benefits from even broader safeguards, including the right of permanent residence after five years of continuous lawful stay in Italy with the EU citizen. The refusal of a residence card for an EU family member is frequently unlawful: D.Lgs. 30/2007 interprets the definition of «family member» broadly and strictly limits the grounds for refusal.

Legal basis: D.Lgs. 30/2007 — Dir. 2004/38/EC

How we intervene

Defence strategies for the EU long-term residence permit

Obtaining and defending an EU long-term residence permit requires an in-depth knowledge of both domestic and European law.

I.

Complete verification of requirements and five-year documentation

The first step is an analytical verification of the fulfilment of all four requirements: five years of stay documented permit by permit, calculation of total income including all computable items, verification of accommodation suitability, certification of the A2 test. This preliminary work is essential both to build a solid application that minimizes the risk of refusal and to respond effectively to an already issued refusal. Many refusals stem from errors in calculation or evidentiary assessment that can be rectified with the appropriate documentation.

II.

Appeal before the Specialised Court pursuant to art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011

A refusal of an EU long-term residence permit issued by the Police Headquarters (Questura) may be challenged before the Specialised Section on Immigration Matters of the Ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario) under the summary procedure set out in art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011, within 30 days. The judge evaluates the lawfulness of the refusal and may order the Police Headquarters (Questura) to issue the permit. Our experience in these proceedings allows us to construct effective appeals, referencing both domestic legislation and directly effective provisions of Directive 2003/109/EC. Where necessary, we concurrently file an urgent application for interim relief.

III.

Challenging income calculation and documentary reconstruction

The income requirement is the most frequent ground for refusal, and often the refusal is based on an erroneous calculation. We analytically reconstruct the applicant’s computable income in the reference year, including all items that the law indicates as relevant: income from employment and self-employment, social security benefits (prestazioni INPS), sickness or disability allowances, relocation allowance (assegno di ricollocazione), rental income. In the event of an income shortfall of just a few euros, we assess the possibility of submitting a new application documenting the achievement of the minimum threshold.

IV.

Challenging the refusal based on criminal records: proportionality and current relevance

When the refusal is based on criminal records, we build the defence on the principle of proportionality and on the necessity of an individualized and up-to-date assessment of danger to public order. The refusal must be preceded by a specific prognostic assessment: dated convictions, offences of limited severity, blameless conduct in subsequent years, and documented family and employment ties are all factors that militate against the refusal.

V.

Appeal (ricorso) to the Regional Administrative Court (TAR) against revocation: strict exhaustiveness of legal grounds

The revocation of the EU long-term residence permit (permesso UE per lungo soggiornanti) is an exceptionally severe measure and is admissible only in the specific cases strictly provided for by law. When the police headquarters (Questura) adopts a revocation outside of these cases, the measure is fundamentally unlawful and must be challenged urgently before the competent Regional Administrative Court (TAR) (within 60 days of notification) or before the ordinary court (Tribunale ordinario) (within 30 days). The application for interim relief (istanza cautelare) to suspend the effects of the revocation must be lodged concurrently with the main appeal (ricorso) to prevent the initiation of deportation procedures pending judgment.

VI.

European protection: direct invocation of Directive 2003/109/EC

Directive 2003/109/EC has direct effect in the legal systems of Member States. When Italian legislation or its application by police headquarters (Questure) is more restrictive than permitted by the European directive, we directly invoke the provisions of the directive before the national court, requesting the disapplication of the incompatible domestic provision. This instrument is particularly effective regarding the calculation of the five-year period (the directive permits flexibility criteria that the Consolidated Immigration Act (Testo Unico Immigrazione - TUI) does not fully transpose) and regarding revocation (the grounds for revocation in the directive are more exhaustive than those in the TUI).

The EU long-term residence permit is valid throughout Europe. One of the lesser-known advantages of the EU long-term residence permit (permesso UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo) is its European validity: the holder may move to another Member State of the European Union for employment, study, or family reunification purposes, benefiting from a facilitated regime compared to first entry. The right to move is governed by Arts. 14–23 of Directive 2003/109/EC. Those who have already obtained the EU long-term residence permit in Italy may therefore settle in Germany, France, or Spain through simpler procedures compared to those who do not hold it.

5 cases handled and resolved

Case no. 1

Denial of EU residence permit due to incorrect income calculation — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2024 — Art. 9 para. 1 TUI — Income requirement

A Tunisian national, residing in Italy for sixteen years with a series of regularly renewed residence permits for subordinate employment, had submitted an application for an EU long-term residence permit (permesso di soggiorno UE per soggiornanti di lungo periodo), documenting income from employment of 13,200 euros in the preceding year, a duly registered lease agreement for a 65 sq m flat, and the A2 level language test certificate obtained at the CPIA in Naples. The Police Headquarters (Questura) of Naples had rejected the application, asserting that the income did not reach the minimum threshold required for a two-person household (applicant plus cohabiting spouse), calculated as double the social allowance (assegno sociale), amounting to approximately 13,895 euros annually. However, the Questura had failed to consider that the applicant's spouse, residing in Italy under a residence permit for family reasons, was receiving an INPS attendance allowance (indennità di accompagnamento) of approximately 1,200 euros per month for disability, which, combined with the applicant's income, brought the overall household income to over 27,600 euros per year.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the Tribunale di Napoli, providing detailed documentation of the total household income: the applicant's employment income certified by the employer's Single Certification (CU - Certificazione Unica), the spouse's attendance allowance (indennità di accompagnamento) certified by INPS. 9 TUI. The Tribunale granted the appeal by decree of 10 January 2024, ordering the Police Headquarters (Questura) to issue the EU residence permit within 30 days of notification. The applicant obtained the permanent EU long-term residence permit (permesso UE per lungo soggiornanti).

Refusal annulled — EU residence permit issued — Income correctly recalculated
Case no. 2

Refusal due to dated criminal record: proportionality and family ties — TAR Campania

TAR Campania, Napoli, Sezione VI — 2023 — Art. 9 co. 4 TUI — Public order

A Moroccan citizen, who had been in Italy for eighteen years, had submitted an application for an EU long-term residence permit (permesso UE per lungo soggiornanti) after meeting all statutory requirements: fifteen years of work residence permits regularly renewed, a stable employment income of 18,500 euros per year, owned accommodation, and an A2 test passed. The Police Headquarters (Questura) of Naples had rejected the application citing a conviction for aggravated driving under the influence dating back eleven years, which had resulted in a six-month license suspension and a fine. The refusal decision generically stated that the conviction was an indication of «danger to public order» without any further reasoning and without evaluating the applicant's conduct in subsequent years. The applicant was the father of three minor children born in Italy and holding Italian citizenship, held a permanent employment contract for eight years, and had no further criminal record or pending charges (carichi pendenti).

We challenged the refusal before the TAR Campania, alleging a breach of the principle of proportionality and a complete lack of statement of reasons. The decision contained no individualized assessment of the applicant's current dangerousness, merely citing the conviction mechanically without considering: the minor nature of the offense (a violation of the Highway Code, not an offense against persons or property), the time elapsed (eleven years without any further criminal record), the subsequent blameless conduct, and the deep family ties (three children who are Italian citizens) and employment roots (a permanent employment contract for eight years). The TAR upheld the appeal (ricorso) with judgment n. 3841/2023, annulling the refusal and ordering the Police Headquarters (Questura) to carry out a new assessment taking into account the criteria of proportionality and individualization.

Refusal annulled — New assessment ordered — EU residence permit subsequently issued
Case no. 3

Unlawful revocation due to absence exceeding 12 months: documentation of force majeure — TAR Campania

TAR Campania, Napoli, Sezione VI — 2024 — Art. 9 co. 7 lett. d) TUI — Revocation

A Chinese citizen, holder of an EU long-term residence permit (permesso UE per lungo soggiornanti) for six years, had remained stranded in China due to international travel restrictions imposed during the COVID-19 pandemic emergency, with international flights between China and Italy suspended for over fourteen months. Upon his return to Italy, the Police Headquarters (Questura) of Naples initiated revocation proceedings regarding the EU long-term residence permit pursuant to art. 9 co. 7 lett. d) TUI, which provides for revocation when the holder is absent from Italian territory for more than twelve consecutive months. The mechanical application of the rule failed to take into account that the absence had been caused by force majeure — the suspension of international flights and movement restrictions imposed by the pandemic — beyond the holder's control and fully documented.

We challenged the revocation decision before the TAR Campania, putting forward arguments on two grounds. First: force majeure precludes the voluntary nature of the absence and therefore the legal prerequisite for revocation; the decisions of the Chinese and Italian authorities that suspended flights were public regulatory acts that the applicant could not evade. Second: we submitted documentation evidencing the forced absence: canceled airline tickets, communications from airlines, Italian Prime Ministerial Decrees (DPCM) restricting arrivals from China, and Chinese decrees limiting international flights. The TAR granted interim relief by single-judge decree (decreto monocratico) and, on the merits, by judgment n. 4102/2024, annulling the revocation decision and ruling that force majeure caused by an international health emergency precludes the prerequisite of voluntary absence.

Revocation annulled — EU residence permit reinstated — Force majeure recognized
Case no. 4

Residence card for family member of an EU citizen: refusal for failure to recognize the couple's relationship — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Sezione Specializzata Immigrazione — 2023 — D.Lgs. 30/2007 — Dir. 2004/38/EC

A Brazilian citizen, de facto cohabiting (convivente more uxorio) with a German citizen who had resided in Italy for four years, had applied for a residence card (carta di soggiorno) as a family member of a European Union citizen pursuant to D.Lgs. 30/2007. The couple had lived together in the same apartment for three years, maintained a joint bank account, had utility bills in both names, and had registered their cohabitation with the civil registry (anagrafe) of the municipality of residence. The Police Headquarters (Questura) of Naples had rejected the application, asserting that a de facto partner does not fall within the definition of «family member» under art. 2 of D.Lgs. 30/2007, which, in the narrow reading of the Police Headquarters, includes only the spouse, minor children, and dependent direct ascendants. The Police Headquarters had not taken into account art. 3 co. 2 of D.Lgs. 30/2007, which requires the State to facilitate the entry and residence of the «partner with whom the Union citizen has a durable relationship, duly attested», nor the corresponding provision of art. 3 par. 2 of Directive 2004/38/EC.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the Tribunale di Napoli, substantiating the stability of the relationship through civil registry records, the co-signed residential lease agreement, utility bills, and photographs documenting the relationship over time. On legal grounds, we argued that art. 3 co. 2 of D.Lgs. 30/2007 mandates an individualized assessment of the durable relationship, and that such an assessment had not been carried out by the Police Headquarters (Questura). The Tribunale granted the appeal by decree of 22 November 2023, ordering the Police Headquarters to issue the residence card (carta di soggiorno) to the applicant, subject to a concrete assessment of the stability of the relationship, which the submitted documentation amply demonstrated.

Residence card issued — Durable cohabitation recognized
Case no. 5

Calculation of the five-year period: periods with protection permits permitted — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Sezione Specializzata Immigrazione — 2024 — Art. 9 co. 1 TUI — Calculation of five-year period

A Somali citizen, who had held a residence permit for subsidiary protection (permesso per protezione sussidiaria) for seven years, had submitted an application for an EU long-term resident permit (permesso UE per lungo soggiornanti), documenting lawful residence in Italy for over seven years: the first two years under an asylum seeker permit (permesso per richiesta asilo), and the following five under a subsidiary protection permit. The Police Headquarters (Questura) of Naples had rejected the application, asserting that the period spent in Italy under the asylum seeker permit could not be counted toward the five-year period required by art. 9 TUI, since the asylum seeker permit is a temporary title that does not fully certify the lawfulness of residence. This reasoning was manifestly erroneous: the asylum seeker permit confers upon the applicant the right to reside lawfully in Italy while the application is under examination, and periods covered by this title must be calculated toward the five-year requirement.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the Tribunale di Napoli, documenting the applicant’s complete history of residence titles and arguing that the period covered by the asylum seeker permit constitutes, for all legal intents and purposes, a period of “lawful” residence pursuant to art. 9 paragraph 1 TUI. We cited Directive 2003/109/EC (recital 6 and art. 3 paragraph 2 letter c), which excludes only asylum seekers whose application is pending, not those who have already obtained a title (even if provisional) while the application is examined. The Court upheld the appeal by decree of 14 May 2024, holding that the period of residence under an asylum seeker permit is calculated in full toward the five-year period required by art. 9 TUI, and ordering the Police Headquarters (Questura) to issue the EU long-term resident permit.

Five-year period recognized in full — EU permit issued
