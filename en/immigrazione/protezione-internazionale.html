Those fleeing persecution have the right to be heard.
And when the Commission does not listen, the courts exist.

International protection is the legal system that guarantees refuge to those forced to leave their country to escape persecution, armed conflict, or severe violations of fundamental rights. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) evaluates asylum applications, but its rejections — too often summary in their reasoning or based on flawed credibility assessments — can be challenged before the Court (Tribunale). Our firm assists asylum seekers from the filing of the application through to appeals before the Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello), ensuring formal legal representation and an in-depth analysis of the risk of persecution in the country of origin.

Regulatory framework

The international protection system in Italy

D.Lgs. 251/2007 — Refugee status and subsidiary protection

Forms of protection: definitions and legal requirements

Legislative Decree no. 251 of 19 November 2007 (implementing Directive 2004/83/EC, subsequently replaced by Directive 2011/95/EU) defines the legal requirements for the recognition of refugee status and subsidiary protection. Refugee status (art. 7 et seq.) is granted to a foreign national who, owing to a well-founded fear of being persecuted for reasons of race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership of a particular social group, is outside the country of their nationality and is unable or, owing to such fear, unwilling to avail themselves of the protection of that country. Subsidiary protection (art. 2, letter g), on the other hand, is granted to a foreign national who does not qualify as a refugee but in respect of whom substantial grounds have been shown for believing that, if returned to their country of origin, they would face a real risk of suffering serious harm (death penalty, torture, serious and individual threat to life or person by reason of indiscriminate violence in situations of armed conflict). The provision underwent significant amendments by Decree-Law 130/2020 (converted into Law 173/2020), which restored special protection (protezione speciale) in its original formulation following the changes introduced by the 2018-2019 security decrees.

D.Lgs. 25/2008 — Examination procedure for international protection applications

The interview before the Territorial Commission and procedural safeguards

Legislative Decree no. 25 of 28 January 2008 (implementing Directive 2005/85/EC, subsequently Directive 2013/32/EU) governs the procedure for examining international protection applications. The application is submitted to the Police Headquarters (Questura) and then forwarded to the competent Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) for the applicant's place of residence. The Commission summons the applicant to an individual interview during which they present the grounds for their application (art. 12 D.Lgs. 25/2008). The interview is conducted through an interpreter, and the applicant has the right to be assisted by a lawyer. The Commission takes its decision within six months of the submission of the application, subject to extensions. In the event of a refusal, the decision is communicated in writing, indicating the reasons and the available avenues of appeal. The applicant may lodge an appeal (ricorso) before the specialised Court (Tribunale) handling immigration matters within 30 days of notification of the decision (art. 35-bis D.Lgs. 25/2008, as introduced by Decree-Law 13/2017 converted into Law 46/2017). The appeal automatically suspends the enforcement of the deportation order, except in cases of manifest groundlessness.

Supranational and constitutional sources of the right of asylum

The right of asylum has its constitutional foundation in art. 10, paragraph 3, of the Italian Constitution, which grants the right of asylum to foreign nationals who are prevented in their home country from effectively exercising the democratic freedoms guaranteed by the Italian Constitution. The Geneva Convention of 28 July 1951 relating to the Status of Refugees (ratified by Law 722/1954) constitutes the primary source of international law on this matter. Art. 33 of the Convention enshrines the fundamental principle of non-refoulement: no Contracting State shall expel or return a refugee across the borders of territories where their life or freedom would be threatened. Directive 2011/95/EU (the so-called Qualification Directive) harmonised across the European Union the definitions and requirements for the recognition of international protection, establishing uniform minimum standards of protection across Member States.

Pay close attention to deadlines: an appeal against a refusal by the Territorial Commission must be lodged with the specialised Court (Tribunale) within 30 days of the notification of the decision. In certain cases (decisions taken at border or transit zones, or applications deemed manifestly unfounded or inadmissible), the deadline is reduced to 15 days. An appeal lodged within the prescribed deadline automatically suspends any ongoing deportation procedures. The expiration of this deadline precludes any ordinary judicial remedy.

The credibility assessment and COI sources. The assessment of the credibility of the asylum seeker’s statements is the core of the proceedings before the Commission (Commissione Territoriale). A rejection based solely on the unreliability of the statements, without COI analysis, is flawed by a lack of reasoning and can be successfully challenged.

The available forms of protection

Status and protections: which one applies to your situation

The Italian international protection system provides for various forms of protection, with differentiated levels of protection and residence permits (permesso di soggiorno). We identify the form of protection most appropriate to the client’s situation and build the most effective documentation and argumentation to obtain it.

Art. 7 D.Lgs. 251/2007

Refugee status

The strongest form of protection. It is granted to anyone who has a well-founded fear of being persecuted for reasons of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion. Residence permit (permesso di soggiorno): five years, renewable. A refugee enjoys the same rights as an Italian citizen regarding social welfare, healthcare, and education, and has the right to simplified family reunification pursuant to art. 29-bis TUI without the standard income requirements.

Permit: 5 years renewable — Equated to an Italian citizen
Art. 2 lett. g) D.Lgs. 251/2007

Subsidiary protection

It is granted to those who are not individually persecuted but face a risk of serious harm in their country of origin: death penalty, torture, inhuman or degrading treatment, serious threat to life due to indiscriminate violence in situations of internal or international armed conflict (art. 14 D.Lgs. 251/2007). Residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) of five years, renewable. Access to the same services as a refugee. Family reunification exempt from income and accommodation requirements.

Permit: 5 years renewable
Art. 19 co. 1.1 TUI

Special protection

Introduced in its current form by D.L. 130/2020, special protection (protezione speciale) is granted when return would entail a violation of the applicant’s right to private and family life (art. 8 ECHR), or where risks of torture or inhuman treatment not covered by other legal instruments exist. The Court has progressively broadened the application of special protection, granting it on the basis of the applicant’s degree of integration in Italy. Residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) of two years, convertible into a work permit.

Permit: 2 years — Convertible into a work permit
Art. 35-bis D.Lgs. 25/2008

Appeal to the Court against rejection

A rejection by the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) may be appealed before the Specialized Section of the ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario) (art. 35-bis D.Lgs. 25/2008) within 30 days of notification. The judge assesses the application on the merits, including by obtaining country of origin information (COI) ex officio. The appeal (ricorso) has an automatic suspensive effect on the deportation order (decreto di espulsione), except in cases of manifest groundlessness or inadmissibility. At the hearing, the applicant may be heard in person by the judge.

Deadline: 30 days from notification of the rejection
Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello)

Appeal against the judgment of the Court

The judgment of the Court (Tribunale) rejecting the appeal against the decision of the Commission (Commissione) may be appealed before the Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello) within 30 days of notification. The Court of Appeal rules in chambers and may order a new hearing of the applicant or acquire further COI information. From the appeal, it is possible to lodge an appeal on points of law before the Court of Cassation (impugnazione in grado di appello) on grounds of law only. We handle the entire sequence of judicial protection.

Appeal: 30 days from the judgment of the Court
Art. 33 D.Lgs. 25/2008

Revocation of protection

The Commission (Commissione) may reopen the proceedings ex officio to revoke the protection already granted when the conditions justifying it have ceased to exist (cessation of persecution in the country of origin, significant change of circumstances) or when the beneficiary has committed serious crimes. The revocation order may be challenged before the specialized Court (Tribunale) under the same procedures as a rejection. Our firm also provides assistance in revocation proceedings, ensuring technical legal defense.

Challenge to revocation: 30 days from notification

How we intervene

Strategies for international protection and appeals

The outcome of an application for international protection depends to a large extent on the quality of the narrative, the documentation provided, and the ability to contextualize the applicant’s case within the framework of information on the country of origin. Our firm invests significantly in COI research and hearing preparation.

I.

Preparation for the hearing before the Commission

The hearing before the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) is the decisive moment of the procedure. Preparation is fundamental: we help the applicant structure the narrative coherently, chronologically, and with specific details. We analyze COI sources on the country of origin to contextualize the personal story within the broader picture. We assist the applicant during the hearing to ensure procedural safeguards are respected and the translation is accurate.

II.

Research and analysis of COI sources

Country of Origin Information is at the core of the adjudication of international protection. We systematically utilize the databases of EASO (EUAA), UNHCR, Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International, the annual report of the US Department of State, and country-specific sources.

III.

Appeal to the Court: challenged credibility assessment

The most frequent ground for rejection is the unreliability of the applicant’s statements. We challenge this assessment using technical-legal arguments aimed at showing that the credibility assessment was not carried out according to the criteria the Commission must respect. If the rejection violates these criteria — for instance by adopting culturally inappropriate standards or ignoring the applicant’s trauma — the appeal (ricorso) has strong prospects of success.

IV.

Urgent stay of execution of the deportation order

When the applicant is subject to a deportation order (decreto di espulsione) issued while an appeal is pending — a scenario that occurs in cases of manifest groundlessness or in certain accelerated procedures — we seek an urgent precautionary suspension of the removal measure before the competent Court (Tribunale). The interim measure can also be granted ex parte (inaudita altera parte) in situations of extreme urgency, preventing deportation while proceedings on the merits are pending.

V.

Special protection and integration in Italy

When the requirements for refugee status or subsidiary protection are not met, we evaluate the applicability of special protection (protezione speciale) based on the applicant’s level of integration in Italy (art. 19 co. 1.1 TUI) or on humanitarian grounds. Special protection has become an important instrument following the amendments introduced by D.L. 130/2020: trial jurisprudence recognizes special protection based on length of stay, employment conditions, knowledge of the Italian language, and family ties.

VI.

Assistance during detention in CPR and accelerated procedures

Asylum seekers detained in Repatriation Detention Centers (Centri di Permanenza per il Rimpatrio — CPR) or subjected to accelerated procedures at border areas have reduced, but not extinguished, procedural rights. We intervene promptly to guarantee access to legal assistance, verify the lawfulness of detention, and ensure compliance with procedural guarantees during hearings. The unlawful detention of an asylum seeker may be challenged before the civil court through urgent proceedings.

The principle of non-refoulement and its scope of application. The principle of non-refoulement, enshrined in art. 33 of the Geneva Convention and art. 3 ECHR, prohibits expulsion to countries where the person facing return risks persecution, torture, or inhuman treatment. United Kingdom, 28 June 2011) and cases of exceptionally severe health risk (Paposhvili v. Belgium, 13 December 2016, Grand Chamber). Non-refoulement applies regardless of the outcome of the international protection application and binds the State even with respect to persons who have committed serious crimes.

5 cases handled and resolved

Case no. 1

Refugee status recognised after refusal for lack of credibility — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2024 — Nigeria — Art. 7 D.Lgs. 251/2007

A young Nigerian citizen had applied for international protection after fleeing Nigeria following serious threats from a local criminal organisation that had recruited him as a drug courier and which, after he refused to continue cooperating, had killed his younger brother as a warning. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) of Naples had rejected the application with a brief statement of reasons deeming the applicant’s statements “not credible” as they contained “temporal inconsistencies” and a “lack of specific details”. The Commission had failed to consider that the applicant had suffered severe trauma (the murder of his brother) and that the temporal inaccuracies were consistent with the psychological response to trauma, nor had it examined COI sources on the situation of organised crime in rural areas of southern Nigeria.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the Tribunale di Napoli, producing: (1) a psychological report drawn up by an expert attesting to the presence of PTSD symptoms (Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder) consistent with the narrated events and explaining the temporal inaccuracies; (2) updated COI sources (2023 EASO report on Nigeria, Human Rights Watch report, UNODC report on drug trafficking in Nigeria) confirming the existence and pervasiveness of criminal organisations in the applicant’s region of origin and the inability of local authorities to protect victims of criminal intimidation; (3) the death certificate of the brother, issued by the local hospital and attesting to the circumstances of the death. The Court recognised refugee status, stating that the threat of persecution by criminal organisations tolerated or protected by local authorities falls within the framework of art. 7 D.Lgs. 251/2007. The judge specifically censured the Commission for not having acquired COI sources and for having applied credibility criteria that were not appropriate.

Refugee status recognised — 5-year permit issued
Case no. 2

Subsidiary protection due to internal armed conflict — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2023 — Mali — Art. 14 letter c) D.Lgs. 251/2007

A Malian citizen from the Mopti region, an area of central Mali affected by a severe armed conflict between government forces, Dogon militias, jihadist groups affiliated with JNIM, and Tuareg rebels, had applied for international protection, describing the situation of generalised violence in his region of origin, where he had witnessed an attack on his village by armed men who killed several fellow villagers. He had not suffered personal persecution nor was he a member of political or ethnic groups particularly at risk. The Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) had rejected the application, ruling out individual persecution and holding that the general situation in Mali, while problematic, did not constitute the indiscriminate violence required for subsidiary protection pursuant to art. 14 letter c) D.Lgs. 251/2007.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the Tribunale di Napoli, producing a detailed COI analysis of the security situation in the Mopti region in 2023: an ACLED (Armed Conflict Location and Event Data Project) report documenting hundreds of violent incidents in the region in 2022-2023, a MINUSMA (the UN mission in Mali) report, an UNHCR report on the protection situation in Mali, and the United Nations Security Council report describing the situation in central Mali as a high-intensity armed conflict with systematic attacks on the civilian population. The Court granted the appeal, recognising subsidiary protection.

Subsidiary protection recognised — 5-year permit issued
Case no. 3

Special protection for integration: permit converted into work permit — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Specialized Immigration Section — 2024 — Bangladesh — Art. 19 para. 1.1 TUI

A Bangladeshi citizen, residing in Italy for five years on an asylum application permit, had received a refusal from the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) regarding refugee status and subsidiary protection. The applicant had not been a victim of individual persecution nor did he come from an area of active armed conflict, but had left Bangladesh to escape extreme poverty. However, during his five years of stay in Italy, he had established a deep bond with the territory: he spoke Italian fluently (attested by a CILS level B2 certification), had attended and completed a vocational course as a metalworker, had found stable employment as a factory worker under a fixed-term contract, and maintained regular relationships with his employer, colleagues, and the local community. He had no criminal record or pending proceedings. None of his family members resided in Italy.

We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the Tribunale di Napoli, setting aside arguments on persecution and subsidiary protection — which were not supported by the facts — and focusing on special protection based on the applicant’s degree of integration in Italy pursuant to art. 19 para. 1.1 TUI, as reinterpreted by case law following D.L. 130/2020. We documented all elements of integration in detail: employment contract, payslips, language certification, vocational course certificate, employer statements, and involvement in local associations. We cited the case law of the Tribunale di Napoli (decree of 18 January 2024) and of the Tribunale di Roma, which had recognised special protection in similar cases of long-term residence with deep integration. The Court recognised special protection and, subsequently, the applicant converted the permit into a residence permit for work (permesso per lavoro) pursuant to art. 6 TUI, definitively regularising his status.

Special protection recognised — Permit converted into work permit
Case no. 4

Urgent stay of execution: repatriation halted in extremis during transfer to the CPR

Tribunale di Napoli, Immigration Section — 2023 — Urgent precautionary procedure — Ghana

A Ghanaian national, whose application for international protection had been rejected by the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) as manifestly unfounded, had received an expulsion order (decreto di espulsione) with forced removal. He was about to be transferred to the Pre-Removal Detention Centre (Centro di Permanenza per il Rimpatrio - CPR) of Palazzo San Gervasio when his relatives contacted us urgently. The finding of manifest unfoundedeness was particularly critical because, as such, it did not have an automatic suspensive effect on the expulsion order during the appeal: the applicant was at real risk of repatriation to Ghana even before the Court could rule on the merits of his appeal (ricorso). The young man was about twenty-two years old, had been in Italy for three years and, despite his asylum-seeker status, had pursued a commendable path of integration: he attended evening classes for adults, took part in a job-placement project, and had no criminal record.

We urgently filed an application for interim relief (istanza cautelare) with the Tribunale di Napoli, seeking the immediate suspension of the expulsion order and the resulting transfer to the CPR, on the grounds that: (1) the appeal against the refusal was based on grounds that were not manifestly unfounded; (2) repatriation to Ghana, pending the judgment on the merits, would irretrievably prejudice the applicant's right of defence and right to private life. In the appeal, we pointed out that categorising the application as manifestly unfounded appeared erroneous: the Commission had omitted any COI analysis regarding the situation of the applicant's ethnic group, which is subject to documented discrimination in his region of origin. The Court granted the application for interim relief ex parte (inaudita altera parte) by an urgent decree issued on the same day, ordering the stay of the transfer to the CPR and of the enforcement of the expulsion order until the final decision on the merits. The appeal on the merits was subsequently upheld, granting special protection (protezione speciale).

Repatriation halted at the last moment — Special protection granted
Case no. 5

Revocation of protection successfully challenged: change of circumstances not proven

Tribunale di Napoli, Sezione Specializzata Immigrazione — 2024 — Pakistan — Revocation of subsidiary protection

A Pakistani national, holder of subsidiary protection granted in 2019 due to the risk arising from the violence in the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa region, had received a decision from the Territorial Commission (Commissione Territoriale) in 2023 revoking his protection. The reasoning of the decision referred to an improvement in the security situation in the holder's region of origin, inferred from press reports concerning military operations by Pakistani forces against Taliban groups in the area. The Commission maintained that the «significant and non-temporary change of circumstances» required by art. 15 D.Lgs. 251/2007 for the cessation of protection had occurred. In the meantime, the holder had built a stable life in Italy: he worked regularly as a skilled worker, had a stable relationship with an Italian citizen, and had attended an advanced vocational training course.

We appealed the revocation decision before the Tribunale di Napoli, submitting COI documentation updated to 2023-2024 on the situation in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa that radically disproved the claim of a «stable improvement»: the report by the Pakistan Institute for Peace Studies documented an increase in terrorist attacks in the region in 2023 compared to previous years; the UNHCR report continued to recommend against expulsion to the north-western areas of Pakistan; the press reports cited by the Commission were fragmented and not representative of the overall situation. We argued that the Commission had not strictly demonstrated the «significant and non-temporary change of circumstances» required by art. 15 D.Lgs. 251/2007 and that, in any case, the revocation should have taken into account the holder's social integration in Italy and the residual risk he would face in the event of repatriation. The Court upheld the appeal (ricorso), annulling the revocation decision and confirming subsidiary protection.

Revocation annulled — Subsidiary protection confirmed

Has the Commission rejected your application?
