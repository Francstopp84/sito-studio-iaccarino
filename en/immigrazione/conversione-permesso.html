Has the Police Headquarters (Questura) refused the conversion of your permit?
The refusal can be challenged. Conversion is a right, not a favour.

The conversion of a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) from one category to another — from study to work, from protection to work, from employed to self-employed, from seasonal to non-seasonal — is a fundamental instrument of Italian immigration law. It allows individuals who have already established a life in Italy, worked, studied, and demonstrated integration, to change the legal basis of their stay without having to leave the country and re-enter with a new visa. The Police Headquarters (Questura) often rejects these applications citing quota limits under the flows decree (decreto flussi) or generic reasons: in many cases, the refusal is unlawful and open to challenge. Our firm analyses each refusal of conversion and builds the most effective defence strategy.

Regulatory framework

Permit conversion in the Consolidated Immigration Act (Testo Unico Immigrazione)

Art. 14 D.P.R. 394/1999 — Key provision

Implementing regulation: permitted grounds for conversion

Art. 14 of D.P.R. 31 August 1999, no. 394 (Implementing Regulation of the Consolidated Immigration Act), is the central provision governing the conversion of residence permits (permesso di soggiorno). Paragraph 1 establishes the circumstances in which conversion is permissible without leaving the country and re-entering with a new visa: study permit into an employed or self-employed work permit; seasonal work permit into a non-seasonal employed work permit; international protection permit into an employed or self-employed work permit; family reasons permit into a work permit. The provision distinguishes between conversions occurring within the quotas of the flows decree (decreto flussi) and those that may take place outside the quotas. Case law has progressively broadened the circumstances for out-of-quota conversions, recognising that local ties and prior lawful status justify more favourable treatment compared to a new entry. The Court of Cassation (Cassazione, Joint Divisions no. 18353/2016) clarified that the provisions on conversion must be construed consistently with the principle of favor integrationis.

Art. 14 D.P.R. 394/1999
Art. 6 paragraph 1 TUI — General conditions for the permit

Subjective and objective requirements for conversion

Article 6, paragraph 1 of Legislative Decree 286/1998 (Consolidated Immigration Act – TUI) establishes the general requirements for the issuance, renewal, and conversion of a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno): regular entry into the national territory, the absence of disqualifying factors (Art. 4, paragraph 3 TUI, which refers to the absence of convictions for specific offenses, danger to public order, an alert in the SIS), and the fulfillment of the specific requirements of the category into which the permit is to be converted. In addition, Art. 6, paragraph 1-bis, introduced by Legislative Decree 3/2018, expressly recognizes the possibility of converting the permit in cases not exhaustively provided for where the foreign national has completed educational or vocational training courses in Italy, even outside the quotas of the flow decree (decreto flussi). This provision has opened significant opportunities for young foreign nationals who studied in Italy and subsequently found employment, allowing conversion regardless of annual quotas.

Art. 6, paras. 1 and 1-bis TUI — D.Lgs. 286/1998
Art. 22 TUI — Fixed-term and open-ended subordinate employment

Conversion to subordinate employment: requirements and procedures

Article 22 of the TUI governs the procedures for work clearance (nulla osta) for subordinate employment and, indirectly, conversions to this category. The provision requires the existence of a lawful employment contract and verification — by the Single Desk for Immigration (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione – SUI) — of compliance with minimum contractual requirements. For conversions from a study permit, the mechanism is simplified compared to standard entry for work purposes: prior clearance (nulla osta) is not required in quota-exempt cases pursuant to Art. 6, paragraph 1-bis TUI; rather, submitting the conversion application to the Police Headquarters (Questura), accompanied by the employment contract and documentation certifying completion of the educational or training program in Italy, is sufficient. Case law (Trib. Milano, 14 March 2023; Trib. Napoli, 15 February 2024) has consolidated the favorable stance toward quota-exempt conversion for young people who have completed schooling or vocational training programs in Italy.

Art. 22 TUI — Subordinate employment
Art. 26 TUI — Self-employment

Conversion to self-employment: business activity and freelance professions

Article 26 of the TUI governs entry and residence for self-employment and constitutes the statutory benchmark for conversions to this category. A foreign national intending to convert their permit into a permit for self-employment must prove: registration with the professional roll or the business register, the availability of sufficient financial resources (not lower than the minimum income provided by law for exemption from mandatory health contribution payments to the National Health Service), and suitable accommodation. For freelance professionals, enrollment in the relevant professional association or bar/roll is required. Conversion from subordinate employment to self-employment is frequently rejected by the Police Headquarters (Questura) on generic grounds that fail to reflect a concrete evaluation of the submitted requirements: in such cases, the denial is systematically challengeable for lack of reasoning.

Art. 26 TUI — Self-employment

Pay attention to deadlines: an appeal (ricorso) against the denial of conversion of a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) must be lodged before the Ordinary Court, Specialized Section on Immigration Matters, within 30 days from the notification or service of the decision. Do not delay: the deadline is mandatory, and its expiration precludes any legal remedy.

Conversion and quotas under the flow decree (decreto flussi). Not all conversions are subject to the annual quota limits of the flow decree. Law and case law recognize numerous cases in which conversion may take place outside the quotas: a foreign national who has completed educational or training programs in Italy (Art. 6, paragraph 1-bis TUI), conversion from an international protection permit, and conversion from a seasonal to a non-seasonal permit after two consecutive seasons. When the Police Headquarters (Questura) denies conversion by invoking the exhaustion of quotas in cases where quotas do not apply, the denial is unlawful and must be challenged.

Which situation applies to you

The types of conversion we handle

Each type of conversion has specific statutory prerequisites, different frameworks regarding flow decree (decreto flussi) quotas, and its own body of case law. Correctly identifying the type is the first step toward building an effective defense strategy.

Study → Work

From study permit to work permit

The most common and contentious conversion. A foreign national who has completed a course of study in Italy (secondary school diploma, university degree, vocational training course) and has found employment may apply for conversion of the permit without leaving Italy. In many cases, conversion is possible outside quotas pursuant to Art. 6, paragraph 1-bis TUI. The Police Headquarters (Questura) frequently raises quota limits or the lack of SUI clearance (nulla osta): in both instances, if inapplicable, the denial is challengeable.

Source: art. 6 co. 1-bis TUI — art. 14 D.P.R. 394/1999
Subordinate → Self-employed

From subordinate employment to self-employment

A foreign employee intending to start their own business — by opening a VAT number (partita IVA), incorporating a company, or practicing an independent profession — may apply to convert their subordinate employment permit into a self-employment permit. The conversion is not subject to quotas. The Police Headquarters (Questura) examines the concrete financial capacity of the business and the fulfillment of registration requirements. Denials based on alleged insufficiency of financial means are often challengeable when founded on unreasonable criteria.

Source: art. 26 TUI — art. 14 D.P.R. 394/1999
Self-employed → Subordinate

From self-employment to subordinate employment

The reverse conversion — from self-employment to subordinate employment — concerns foreign entrepreneurs or freelance professionals who terminate their business activity and find dependent employment. Conversion requires submitting the subordinate employment contract and proving the termination or suspension of the previous self-employed activity. The main difficulties arise when the cessation of the self-employed activity is recent or when there are pending tax or social security liabilities: it is necessary to thoroughly document the regularity of one's position.

Source: art. 22 TUI — art. 14 D.P.R. 394/1999
Protection → Work

From international protection to work

Holders of a residence permit for international protection (refugee status, subsidiary protection, special protection) who obtain employment may apply for conversion of the permit into a residence permit for subordinate or self-employment. The conversion is not subject to the quotas of the flow decree (decreto flussi). This transition is often preferable when the protection permit is subject to periodic review or could be revoked if conditions in the country of origin change. A work permit provides greater stability in the long term.

Source: Art. 14, para. 3 D.P.R. 394/1999 — quota-exempt
Family → Work

From family reasons to work

Holders of a residence permit for family reasons who have found employment and wish to acquire legal independence from their sponsoring family member may apply for conversion into a work permit. This step is particularly important in the event of separation, divorce, or departure from the sponsoring family member, situations that could jeopardize the renewal of the permit for family reasons. Conversion makes the holder independent of the continuation of the family relationship and significantly strengthens their legal standing.

Source: art. 30 TUI — art. 14 D.P.R. 394/1999
Seasonal → Non-seasonal

From seasonal work to non-seasonal work

A foreign worker holding a residence permit for seasonal work who has worked lawfully in Italy for at least two seasons in the preceding two years has a preferential right of entry and the possibility of converting the permit into a non-seasonal work permit. Conversion takes place outside ordinary quotas. Frequent problems arise when the Police Headquarters (Questura) requests documentation that the employer has not preserved or issues belatedly: thoroughly and accurately documenting previous working seasons is essential to the success of the application.

Source: Art. 24, para. 11 TUI — preferential quota exemption

How we intervene

Defence strategies for the conversion of the residence permit

The conversion of the residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) is one of the areas of immigration law in which the distance between the statutory provisions and the practice of the Police Headquarters (Questure) is widest. Our strategy is structured on multiple levels: first through administrative channels, then, if necessary, through judicial proceedings.

I.

Preliminary analysis of the refusal and identification of the most expeditious route

The first step is to carefully review the denial order (provvedimento di diniego) in order to identify the main defect: misapplication of the quota limit, failure to consider art. 6 co. 1-bis TUI, lack of statement of reasons regarding the financial requirements, or inadequate preliminary investigation (carenza istruttoria). This analysis determines whether it is preferable to immediately file a judicial appeal (ricorso giurisdizionale) or whether there is still room for administrative dialogue with the Unified Immigration Desk (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione) or the Police Headquarters (Questura). In many cases, a well-argued formal administrative notice (diffida amministrativa) achieves faster results than an appeal (ricorso).

II.

Appeal before the Specialised Court pursuant to art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011

A refusal to convert a residence permit (*permesso di soggiorno*) may be challenged before the Immigration Section (*Sezione Specializzata in materia di immigrazione*) of the competent ordinary court (*Tribunale ordinario*), under the summary proceedings (*rito sommario di cognizione*) pursuant to art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011. The appeal (*ricorso*) must be filed within 30 days of the notification of the refusal. The judge verifies the lawfulness of the decision and, if the appeal is upheld, orders the Police Headquarters (*Questura*) to issue the permit. The procedure is streamlined, the proceedings are relatively fast, and our experience in this forum allows us to identify the most effective arguments.

III.

Building a comprehensive documentary file

Many conversion refusals stem from an incomplete or poorly presented file. Before proceeding with an appeal (ricorso), we always verify whether the refusal can be overcome administratively through the submission of supplementary documentation that the Police Headquarters (Questura) had not received: updated employment contract, payslips, documentation of business activity, school attendance certificates, tax returns. A well-constructed file significantly increases the chances of success, both administratively and before the courts.

IV.

Verification of the applicability of extra-quota exceptions

The crucial point in many disputes regarding conversion is establishing whether the quotas of the immigration flows decree (decreto flussi) apply or not. Art. 6 co. 1-bis TUI and numerous special provisions provide for cases in which conversion is possible outside the quota system. Our analysis includes a systematic check of all possible exemptions applicable to the specific case: completion of training or educational courses, repeated seasonal employment, holding international protection, or being a minor at the time of entry. When an applicable extra-quota scenario is identified, a refusal (diniego) based on the quota limit is unlawful.

V.

Urgent application for interim relief to preserve lawful status

When the residence permit (*permesso di soggiorno*) to be converted is about to expire or has already expired pending the decision on the conversion, we request from the Court (*Tribunale*), concurrently with the appeal (*ricorso*), the adoption of an urgent precautionary measure (*provvedimento cautelare*) that suspends the effects of the refusal and prevents the Police Headquarters (*Questura*) from initiating expulsion procedures pending the proceedings. Our experience in urgent proceedings allows us to obtain swift responses even in emergency situations.

VI.

Appeal to the Regional Administrative Court against refusals by the Single Desk for Immigration

Some conversions require prior clearance (nulla osta) from the Single Desk for Immigration (Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione - SUI), which is an organ of the Prefecture (Prefettura). When the SUI rejects the clearance (nulla osta), the decision is an administrative act that can be challenged before the territorially competent Regional Administrative Court (TAR) within 60 days. The appeal (ricorso) to the TAR is sometimes combined with an appeal (ricorso) to the ordinary Court (Tribunale ordinario) against the subsequent refusal (diniego) of the Police Headquarters (Questura): in these cases, we manage both proceedings in coordination, avoiding argumentative contradictions and maximizing the effectiveness of the overall defense.

The submission of the application for conversion preserves lawful status. The Police Headquarters (Questura) cannot initiate expulsion proceedings against a foreign national who has submitted a valid application for the conversion of the permit (permesso di soggiorno) pending the conclusion of the procedure. The principle of preservation of the residence title during the pendency of the procedure is recognized by case law and by the circular of the Ministry of the Interior (Ministero dell'Interno) of 28 July 2014. Submitting the application at the right time is essential to preserve lawful stay.

Case no. 1

Conversion from study to work outside quota limits for Nigerian graduate — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Sezione Specializzata Immigrazione — 2024 — Art. 6 co. 1-bis TUI outside quota limits

A Nigerian citizen, who entered Italy at the age of seventeen for family reasons and remained lawfully residing for eight years during which he attended scientific high school and obtained a bachelor’s degree in computer engineering at the Università Federico II di Napoli, had found permanent employment as a software developer at an IT company in Naples. The Police Headquarters (*Questura*) of Naples had rejected the application to convert his residence permit (*permesso di soggiorno*) into a residence permit for subordinate work (*permesso per lavoro subordinato*), claiming that the conversion was subject to the quotas of the flows decree (*decreto flussi*), which for the IT sector were already exhausted for the current year. The refusal made no mention of art. 6 co. 1-bis of the Consolidated Immigration Act (*TUI*), which expressly allows conversion outside the quota system for foreign nationals who have completed educational courses in Italy.

We filed an appeal (*ricorso*) before the Tribunale di Napoli pursuant to art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011, focusing the argument on art. 6 co. 1-bis TUI. The provision states that a foreign national who has completed an educational or vocational training course in Italy may obtain the conversion of the permit outside the quotas established by arts. 3 and 4 TUI. Our client had completed his entire secondary and university education in Italy, totaling more than ten years of schooling within the Italian educational system. The Police Headquarters (*Questura*) had never examined this exception to the quota limit, merely asserting the exhaustion of the quota in a mechanical manner. We produced the diplomas, degree certificates, and employment contract, arguing that the applicant’s integration into the Italian educational system equated him to those entering for work for the first time within the quota-exempt category. The Court granted the appeal by decree of 3 April 2024, ordering the conversion of the permit.

Quota-exempt conversion obtained — Residence permit for subordinate work issued
Case no. 2

Conversion from subordinate to self-employment for construction business owner — TAR Campania

TAR Campania, Napoli, Sezione VI — 2023 — Art. 26 TUI — Self-employment

A Moroccan citizen, residing in Italy for twelve years on a residence permit for subordinate work in the construction sector, had started his own building renovation business by establishing a limited liability company in which he was the majority shareholder and sole director. The company had a documented turnover of over 180,000 euros in the previous year and three employees regularly registered with INPS. The Police Headquarters (*Questura*) of Naples had rejected his application to convert the residence permit into a permit for self-employment (*permesso per lavoro autonomo*), asserting that the business income did not reach the level of «sufficient personal economic resources» required by law, a reasoning that clarified neither the calculation criterion used nor the benchmark parameter. The decision had failed to consider that the applicant was already registered with the Chamber of Commerce of Naples and with the craftsmen’s pension fund (*cassa previdenziale degli artigiani*).

We challenged the refusal before the Regional Administrative Court (*TAR Campania*), primarily pleading an absolute lack of statement of reasons: the decision did not indicate the income parameter deemed insufficient, preventing any verification or adversarial debate. Secondly, we raised the unreasonableness of the assessment: an entrepreneur with a turnover of 180,000 euros and three employees could not be deemed lacking «sufficient resources» pursuant to art. 26 TUI, regardless of which parameter was applied. Thirdly, we produced the company registration certificate (*visura camerale*), the financial statements, the employees’ pay slips, and the applicant’s personal tax returns, which showed a personal income above the minimum IRPEF taxable threshold. The TAR granted the appeal by judgment no. 2147/2023, annulling the refusal and ordering the Police Headquarters (*Questura*) to proceed with the conversion of the permit.

Refusal annulled — Self-employment conversion obtained
Case no. 3

Conversion from special protection to subordinate work — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Sezione Specializzata Immigrazione — 2024 — Art. 14 co. 3 D.P.R. 394/1999

A Gambian citizen, holding a two-year residence permit for special protection (*protezione speciale*), had obtained lawful, permanent employment as an engineering metalworker. He wished to convert his permit into a residence permit for subordinate work to acquire a more stable legal status independent of the periodic reviews of the conditions in his country of origin, which could have led to the revocation of special protection. The Police Headquarters (*Questura*) of Naples had rejected the conversion application, stating that the permit for special protection was not among the convertible categories listed in art. 14 D.P.R. 394/1999, as the applicant should have waited for the natural expiry of the permit and then applied for renewal for a change of category. This interpretation was manifestly erroneous: art. 14 co. 3 of the regulation expressly provides for the possibility of converting a permit for international protection (including special protection post-D.Lgs. 130/2020) into a work permit.

We lodged an appeal before the Tribunale di Napoli, documenting both the applicant’s special protection status and the permanent subordinate employment contract. We precisely cited art. 14 co. 3 D.P.R. 394/1999 and the ministerial clarification provided in the circular of the Ministry of the Interior of 3 August 2020, which had expressly extended convertibility to permits for special protection introduced by the 2020 reform. We also submitted relevant case law confirming this interpretation (Trib. Milano, order of 19 November 2022; Trib. Roma, decree of 7 March 2023). The Court granted the appeal by decree of 18 June 2024, ordering the Police Headquarters (*Questura*) to convert the permit into a residence permit for subordinate work within 20 days.

Conversion from protection to work obtained — Legal status stabilized
Case no. 4

Conversion from seasonal to non-seasonal work for agricultural worker — TAR Campania

TAR Campania, Napoli, Sezione VI — 2023 — Art. 24 co. 11 TUI

A Senegalese citizen had worked lawfully in Italy for three consecutive agricultural seasons, consistently with the same employer at an agricultural cooperative in the province of Caserta. He had documented the three seasons through wage slips, pay slips, and the corresponding INPS filings. At the end of the third season, he submitted an application to convert the seasonal permit into a residence permit for non-seasonal work pursuant to art. 24 co. 11 TUI, which grants a preferential right of access to quotas for seasonal workers who have already worked in Italy for at least two seasons in the preceding two years. The Police Headquarters (*Questura*) of Caserta had rejected the application, generically stating that the quotas for non-seasonal workers were exhausted, without distinguishing between ordinary quotas (to which the cap indeed applied) and preferential quotas for former seasonal workers (which the TUI establishes as a separate channel).

We challenged the refusal before the Regional Administrative Court (*TAR Campania*), documenting the three working seasons and arguing that the preferential channel for former seasonal workers provided by art. 24 co. 11 TUI is distinct from the ordinary quotas and is not subject to the same quota restrictions. We submitted the circulars of the Ministry of Labour clarifying the distinction between ordinary quotas and the preferential channel for former seasonal workers, as well as the case law of TAR Lazio (judgment no. 8245/2022) affirming the same principle. The TAR granted the appeal by judgment no. 2891/2023, annulling the refusal and clarifying that the exhaustion of ordinary quotas does not prejudice access to the preferential channel for former seasonal workers, which must be managed separately. The applicant obtained the permit for non-seasonal work.

Conversion from seasonal to non-seasonal obtained — Preferential quotas recognized
Case no. 5

Conversion from family reasons to self-employment for separated woman — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, Sezione Specializzata Immigrazione — 2024 — Art. 30 TUI — Post-separation independence

A Moldovan citizen, who had been in Italy for seven years with a residence permit for family reasons (permesso di soggiorno per motivi familiari) linked to her Italian husband, had started her own business as a beautician with a regular VAT number (partita IVA) and had consensually separated from her husband. Following the separation, the husband had withdrawn the statement of hospitality (dichiarazione di ospitalità) and the permit for family reasons could not be renewed upon its expiry, as the family tie with the Italian citizen justifying it no longer existed. The applicant had submitted an application to convert the permit into a self-employment residence permit (permesso per lavoro autonomo), submitting the Chamber of Commerce registration certificate (visura camerale), tax returns, and documentation of the business carried on for three years. The Police Headquarters (Questura) of Naples had rejected the application, asserting that the applicant should have returned to her home country and applied for a new self-employment visa, an interpretation that completely ignored the permit conversion mechanism.

We challenged the refusal before the Court of Naples (Tribunale di Napoli), documenting the ongoing commercial activity, the income generated, the tax and social security contribution compliance of the VAT number (partita IVA), and the existence of an independent lease agreement demonstrating housing self-sufficiency. We argued that art. 30 co. 5 TUI expressly provides that, in the event of separation or divorce, the foreign spouse is entitled to the renewal of the residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) for a period of not less than three years and in any case for a period sufficient to ensure the stabilization of their status, which in this case was achieved through conversion to self-employment. We cited case law (Trib. Milano, ordinanza 25 gennaio 2023) that had recognized the right to convert a family permit into a self-employment permit for separated former spouses with stable work activity. The Court upheld the appeal (ricorso) by decree of 27 February 2024.

Conversion obtained — Legal independence acquired post-separation
