We understand what you are going through

When your right to stay is at risk

Living in Italy without the certainty of your legal status is an exhausting experience. A denied or non-renewed residence permit (permesso di soggiorno), a refusal of international protection, an expulsion order (decreto di espulsione): these measures can upend your life, that of your family, and the future you are building in this Country.

Italian immigration law is a maze of rules, regulations, circulars, and constantly evolving judicial interpretations. Police Headquarters (Questure) often apply restrictive criteria that go beyond what the law provides. Territorial Commissions reject applications that merited approval. However, in very many cases, an appeal to the Court allows the decision to be overturned and the right to be recognized.

Our firm has developed specific experience in immigration litigation, with an in-depth knowledge of the Consolidated Immigration Act (D.Lgs. 286/1998 - TUI), of D.Lgs. 25/2008 on international protection, and of the most recent case law of the Court of Cassation and the Court of Justice of the European Union.

Legal assistance before every forum

Appeals to the Regional Administrative Court (TAR)

Challenge of administrative measures of the Police Headquarters (Questura) and the Prefecture (Prefettura) before the Regional Administrative Court (TAR). Urgent precautionary applications pursuant to art. 56 of the Administrative Process Code (c.p.a.).

Appeals to the Ordinary Court

Proceedings pursuant to art. 35 D.Lgs. 25/2008 for international protection. Appeals pursuant to art. 18 D.Lgs. 150/2011 for residence permits (permessi di soggiorno) and expulsions.

European Law and ECHR

Application of EU directives on asylum and immigration. Appeals to the European Court of Human Rights for violation of arts. 3, 5, and 8 ECHR.

Results achieved

Real cases, concrete outcomes

Denial of international protection — Court

Protection granted

Asylum seeker whose appeal had been rejected by the Territorial Commission. The appeal to the Court thoroughly documented the situation in the country of origin through qualified sources (UNHCR, EASO, Amnesty International) and demonstrated the actual, individualized risk in the event of repatriation. The Court granted subsidiary protection.

Refusal of permit renewal — Ordinary Court

Permit renewed

Foreign national residing in Italy for 12 years, with minor children born in Italy, who was refused the renewal of their residence permit by the Police Headquarters (Questura) due to a temporary lack of income requirements. The appeal asserted the right to family unity and the disproportion of the measure relative to the real situation, obtaining the annulment of the denial and the renewal of the permit.

Citizenship — Administrative silence (silenzio-inadempimento)

Citizenship obtained

Applicant whose application for Italian citizenship by residence had remained unprocessed for over 4 years, despite the statutory maximum deadline of 730 days. The appeal against the administrative silence of the Ministry of the Interior required the Administration to issue a decision within 30 days, granting citizenship.

Do not lose time

The time limits for appealing are short. Act now.

In immigration matters, the time limits for appealing are often only 30 days from notification of the measure. In the event of an expulsion, the time limit can be even shorter. Do not wait: every lost day can be decisive.
