Regulatory framework

The rules governing successions

Inheritance law is governed by Book II of the Italian Civil Code (artt. 456–809 c.c.) and constitutes one of the most layered and technical regulatory frameworks in the entire Italian civil legal system. Its complexity is heightened by the coexistence of intestate and testamentary succession, the existence of mandatory rights for forced heirs (legittimari), and the intersection with the rules on gifts (donazioni), matrimonial property regimes, and mandatory mediation.

Italian Civil Code — Book II

On successions in general (Artt. 456–535)

Book II of the Italian Civil Code governs the entire subject of inheritance. The opening of the succession takes place at the moment of the decedent's death (de cuius) at the place of their last domicile (art. 456 c.c.). Persons called to succeed may accept unconditionally, accept with benefit of inventory (art. 484 c.c.), or waive the inheritance (art. 519 c.c.) within the ordinary limitation period of ten years from the opening of succession.

Artt. 456–535 c.c.
Statutory Reserved Share

Reserved share in favor of forced heirs (Artt. 536–564)

The statutory reserved share (quota di legittima) is the portion of the estate that the law mandatory reserves for specific categories of statutory heirs (legittimari): spouse, descendants, and ascendants. The spouse is always entitled to at least one quarter of the net estate. Children, in the absence of a spouse, are entitled to one half in the case of an only child, and two thirds in the case of two or more children. Any testamentary disposition or lifetime gift that infringes upon the reserved share is subject to an action for reduction (azione di riduzione).

Artt. 536–564 c.c.
Action for Reduction

Reinstatement of the infringed share (Artt. 553–564)

The action for reduction (azione di riduzione) is the legal remedy by which a prejudiced or pretermitted forced heir seeks the reinstatement of their reserved share. Testamentary dispositions are reduced first (art. 554 c.c.), followed if necessary by lifetime gifts in reverse chronological order, from the most recent to the oldest (art. 559 c.c.). The action is subject to the ordinary ten-year statute of limitations running from the opening of succession. The essential calculation of the disposable portion requires the full reconstruction of the hereditary estate.

Artt. 553–564 c.c.
Intestate Succession

Devolution in the absence of a will (Artt. 565–586)

In the absence of a valid will, the succession opens according to the rules of intestate succession (successione legittima). The order of heirs called to inherit is mandatory: first children (and descendants by representation, art. 467 c.c.), then parents, then brothers and sisters, then other ascendants, then collateral relatives up to the sixth degree, and finally the State. The spouse is always entitled to the statutory reserve share reserved by law. De facto cohabitation, although protected by Law 76/2016, does not grant inheritance rights.

Artt. 565–586 c.c.
Testamentary Succession

Holographic, public and secret will (Artt. 587–712)

A will is the unilateral, revocable and personal act by which the deceased (de cuius) disposes of his or her assets for the time after death (art. 587 c.c.). The most common forms are the holographic will (testamento olografo) — entirely written, dated and signed by the hand of the testator (art. 602 c.c.) — and the public will (testamento pubblico) received by a notary (art. 603 c.c.). The voidability of a will may arise from the testator's natural incapacity, defects of consent (mistake, duress, fraud), defects of form, or material and ideological forgery.

Artt. 587–712 c.c.
Division of the Estate

Dissolution of the hereditary co-ownership (Artt. 713–768)

Until division, the co-heirs remain in co-ownership over the entire hereditary estate. Each co-heir may request division at any time (art. 713 c.c.), unless there is an agreement to remain in undivided co-ownership for a term not exceeding five years. Division may occur by mutual consent, through court proceedings (with the appointment of an expert appraisal witness, CTU), or through mandatory mediation. The collation (collazione) of lifetime gifts received from the deceased (artt. 737–751 c.c.) is frequently a source of dispute among co-heirs.

Artt. 713–768 c.c.
Mandatory Procedure

Mandatory mediation in inheritance disputes

Pursuant to art. 5, paragraph 1, D.Lgs. 28/2010 (as amended by the Cartabia Reform, D.Lgs. 149/2022), mediation is a mandatory condition precedent for legal action (condizione di procedibilità) also for disputes concerning hereditary successions and for all disputes concerning rights in rem (diritti reali). Failure to initiate the mediation procedure renders the claim inadmissible. Our firm manages the entire succession mediation procedure, safeguarding the client's interests both in the out-of-court phase and, if necessary, in judicial proceedings.

Art. 5 D.Lgs. 28/2010 — Cartabia Reform (D.Lgs. 149/2022)

Pay attention to statutory deadlines: The action for reduction (azione di riduzione) is time-barred ten years from the opening of the succession. Challenging a will for formal defects is time-barred five years from the publication of the will (art. 591 c.c.). The time limit to accept or renounce an inheritance is ten years from the opening of the succession. Do not delay: every passing year reduces your legal remedies.

What is your situation?

The cases we handle

Inheritance law encompasses very diverse scenarios. Each situation requires a bespoke strategy, built upon a precise analysis of the estate, of the relationships among co-heirs, and of the legal transactions executed during lifetime by the deceased.

⚖

Intestate succession Artt. 565–586 c.c.

When the deceased left no will, or the will is invalid, succession is governed by the law. We assist in the correct identification of the entitled heirs, in the quantification of the shares, in managing relations with the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate), and in defending against third-party claims on the estate.

📜

Testamentary succession Artt. 587–712 c.c.

A will takes effect upon the death of the testator but must comply with strict formal and substantive requirements. We provide assistance in interpreting testamentary dispositions, verifying formal and substantive validity, publishing holographic wills (art. 620 c.c.), and executing the deceased's wishes.

⛔

Challenging a will Artt. 591, 606, 624, 625 c.c.

A will may be challenged for nullity (formal defects, provisions contrary to law) or voidability (natural incapacity of the testator, defects of consent such as fraud or duress, material forgery). We manage the entire proceedings, from the handwriting expert appraisal on the holographic will through to the judgment of annulment.

⚖

Action for reduction for infringement of the forced share Artt. 553–564 c.c.

If testamentary dispositions or lifetime gifts made by the deceased have reduced your statutory forced share (quota di legittima) below the statutory minimum guaranteed by law, you are entitled to reinstatement. We accurately calculate the net estate (relictum) and lifetime gifts (donatum), identify the dispositions subject to reduction, and initiate legal proceedings to obtain reinstatement of the infringed share.

🚫

Renunciation of inheritance Artt. 519–527 c.c.

When an estate is burdened with liabilities, renunciation of inheritance (rinuncia all’eredità) is the instrument that prevents heirs from being liable for the deceased's debts beyond the value of the inherited assets. We assist in assessing the estate liabilities, drafting the declaration of renunciation before a notary or the court clerk (cancelliere) of the Tribunal (Tribunale), and managing the tax and patrimonial consequences.

📋

Division of the estate Artt. 713–768 c.c.

Hereditary co-ownership among several co-heirs is often a source of disputes, particularly when the estate includes real property that cannot easily be divided. We assist in consensual division, in mandatory mediation, and in judicial division proceedings, coordinating the appointment of the court-appointed expert (CTU), the collation of gifts, and the formation of shares.

How we protect your rights

The six operational strategies

Every inheritance dispute is unique in its complex web of family relations, notarial deeds, lifetime gifts, and documents. Our firm adopts a methodical approach: first, reconstructing the entire estate with accounting precision, then defining the most effective strategy to safeguard your rights.

01

Verification of the validity of the will

Rigorous analysis of the will from a formal perspective (entirely handwritten, dated, and signed for a holographic will; requirements under art. 603 c.c. for a public will) and a substantive perspective (capacity of the testator at the time of execution, absence of defects of consent, compliance with mandatory statutory provisions). If grounds for nullity or voidability emerge, we prepare comprehensive judicial proceedings, including handwriting expert appraisals entrusted to technical consultants with proven forensic experience.

02

Action for reduction

Detailed reconstruction of the estate according to the legal method (net estate remaining after liabilities plus lifetime gifts under art. 556 c.c.), calculation of the statutory reserve shares due, and identification of the testamentary dispositions and lifetime gifts to be reduced in the order set out by art. 559 c.c. We initiate the action before the competent Court (Tribunale), also requesting a freezing order (sequestro conservativo) over the estate assets where necessary to prevent dissipation during proceedings.

03

Division of the estate

Comprehensive assistance in the dissolution of the hereditary co-ownership: attempting consensual division among co-heirs, managing mandatory mediation pursuant to art. 5 D.Lgs. 28/2010, and — should an agreement not be reached — initiating judicial division proceedings pursuant to art. 784 c.p.c. Coordination with the court-appointed expert (CTU) for real estate appraisal and the formation of shares. Protection of co-heirs facing attempts at in-kind division disproportionate to their title.

04

Acceptance with benefit of inventory

When the extent of the estate liabilities is uncertain or potentially higher than the assets, acceptance with benefit of inventory (accettazione con beneficio di inventario, art. 484 c.c.) protects the heir's personal assets, limiting liability for the deceased's debts to the value of the inherited assets. We assist in making the declaration before a notary or the court clerk (cancelliere) of the Tribunal (Tribunale), in drawing up the inventory, and in the subsequent administration and liquidation of the estate pursuant to law.

05

Succession mediation

Mandatory mediation in inheritance and succession matters (art. 5 of Legislative Decree no. 28/2010, as amended by the Cartabia Reform) is not a mere bureaucratic formality: handled strategically, it is a powerful tool for reaching fair and binding agreements much more quickly than through ordinary court proceedings. Our firm assists clients in the mediation procedure (mediazione), negotiating with co-heirs a division that respects statutory shares and the client's expectations.

06

Preventive succession planning

The best protection is built before conflict arises. We assist in drafting formally unassailable wills, utilizing alternative instruments to testamentary succession (trusts, family pacts pursuant to art. 768-bis c.c. [patti di famiglia], life insurance policies, donations with reservation of usufruct), in the tax planning of inheritance and gift tax (Legislative Decree no. 346/1990), and in structuring family assets to minimize the risks of inheritance disputes among successors.

Results achieved

Five resolved cases

Inheritance disputes affect the deepest family relationships and often drag on for years. The cases that follow illustrate the variety of situations we handle on a daily basis and the tangible nature of the results we are able to achieve.

Case no. 1

Holographic will challenged for lack of mental capacity — Tribunale di Napoli

Matter: Challenge of will pursuant to art. 591 of the Italian Civil Code (c.c.) — Estate: approximately € 850,000 between real estate and liquidity

A deceased elderly merchant had left a holographic will executed six months prior to his death, in which he disinherited two children and bequeathed his entire estate to a granddaughter. The two pretermitted children consulted our firm, suspecting that their father had suffered from severe cognitive decline during the final years of his life, as documented in his medical records.

The defence obtained the complete medical documentation covering the last three years of the deceased’s (de cuius) life, including diagnoses of progressive vascular dementia issued by his attending physicians, neuropsychological evaluation reports, and hospital admission records. A court-appointed forensic psychiatric expert was designated, who reconstructed the clinical picture at the time the will was executed, concluding that the testator lacked mental capacity (incapacità naturale) pursuant to art. 591, paragraph 2, no. 3, of the Italian Civil Code (c.c.).

Concurrently, the defence gathered witness statements from neighbours, family physicians, and shop employees, who confirmed the deceased’s state of severe disorientation and susceptibility to influence during the period the will was drafted. The Court, adopting the conclusions of the court-appointed expert (CTU) and the defence arguments, declared the will void due to the testator’s lack of mental capacity (incapacità naturale). The two children were reinstated in the statutory succession (successione legittima) according to the statutory shares set forth in arts. 566 and 537 of the Italian Civil Code (c.c.), each obtaining approximately € 566,000 in total.

Will declared void — Statutory succession reinstated
Case no. 2

Infringement of reserved share through disguised lifetime gifts — Corte d’Appello di Napoli

Matter: Action for reduction (azione di riduzione) pursuant to arts. 553–564 of the Italian Civil Code (c.c.) — Assets involved: three properties + bank balances

A deceased entrepreneur had, over the course of the last fifteen years of his life, made numerous direct and indirect gifts (donazioni) in favour of only one of his three children — the one who managed the family business — substantially depleting the available estate. At the opening of the succession, the remaining estate (relictum) was reduced to a few assets of modest value, while the lifetime gifts, including three real estate properties and significant sums of money, had completely depleted the reserved statutory share (quota di legittima) of the two excluded children.

The defence meticulously reconstructed the entirety of the lifetime gifts made by the deceased (donatum) through the examination of notarial deeds, bank transactions, and real estate sale deeds. Indirect gifts carried out through sham registrations (intestazioni fittizie) were also identified, documented, and made subject to an action for reduction (azione di riduzione) pursuant to art. 559 of the Italian Civil Code (c.c.), starting from the most recent gifts. The calculation of the disposable share (quota disponibile) established that the reserved share (quota di legittima) of the two excluded siblings (representing a total of two-thirds of the reconstructed estate) had been entirely infringed.

At first instance, the Court had partially upheld the claim. On appeal, the Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello) amended the judgment regarding the quantum, recognising the nature of indirect gifts of further transactions and significantly increasing the value of the accountable lifetime gifts (donatum). The two children obtained the transfer of the properties required to restore the infringed reserved share, with the defendant brother ordered to pay the costs of both instances.

Action for reduction granted — Reserved share fully restored
Case no. 3

Complex hereditary partition involving indivisible real properties — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Case: Judicial division pursuant to art. 784 c.p.c. — Hereditary estate: six commercial and residential properties, corporate holdings

Four co-heir siblings had been in a state of hereditary co-ownership for over seven years regarding an estate comprising six real estate units (including two commercial properties with ongoing leases), participating quotas in two limited liability companies (società a responsabilità limitata), and bank liquidity. Relations among the co-heirs were severely compromised: two siblings were using one of the residential properties without paying an occupation indemnity, another held the de facto management of the companies without rendering an accounting to the co-heirs, and negotiations for a consensual division had repeatedly stalled.

Our firm assisted two of the four co-heirs, bringing the action for judicial division and concurrently applying for a conservatory arrest/seizure (sequestro conservativo) on the corporate quotas held by the co-heir and director. The Court appointed a court-appointed expert witness (CTU) appraiser who valued the properties and the corporate holdings, audited the rental accountings, and proposed a division plan with the formation of four lots of broadly equivalent value, providing for cash equalization payments (conguagli) for the inevitable differences in value between the lots.

The indemnities for the exclusive occupation of the jointly owned properties were quantified by the CTU and recognized in the division proceedings. The Court approved the division plan, dissolving the co-ownership and awarding each co-heir their respective lot. Our clients obtained properties of higher commercial value and the liquidation of their respective corporate quotas at the appraised value, along with the recovery of seven years of uncollected rental income.

Co-ownership dissolved — Equitable division obtained in court
Case no. 4

Renunciation of inheritance due to estate debts — Tribunale di Napoli

Case: Renunciation pursuant to arts. 519–527 c.c. — Estimated hereditary liabilities: over € 1.200.000

Two children contacted our firm a few days after the death of their father, an entrepreneur who had managed a commercial company in severe financial distress. The children had been informally designated as “heirs” by the company's creditors and by certain suppliers who had already initiated initial contacts for debt collection. The preliminary analysis revealed that the deceased (de cuius) had left assets with an estimated value of approximately € 180.000, but the liabilities — comprising bank debts, suppliers, loans, and personal guarantees provided — exceeded € 1.200.000.

Our firm reconstructed the overview of the estate liabilities in a very short time, verifying the existence and extent of each obligation, the presence of any clawback actions (azioni revocatorie) available to creditors, and the possibility of employing specific protection instruments. The advice unequivocally concluded that a renunciation of the inheritance (rinuncia all'eredità) pursuant to art. 519 c.c. was advisable, as the only way to prevent the father's debts from transferring to the personal assets of the children.

The declaration of renunciation was executed before the court clerk (cancelliere) of the Tribunale di Napoli in compliance with statutory formalities. The creditors of the deceased (de cuius) were informed of the renunciation, which was recorded in accordance with legal procedures. The two children fully preserved their personal assets, entirely separated from their father's obligations. The estate liabilities remained solely with the vacant estate (eredità giacente), subsequently devolved to the State pursuant to art. 586 c.c.

Personal assets preserved — Estate debts neutralized
Case no. 5

Inheritance disputed among siblings — Mediation and out-of-court settlement

Case: Action for reduction (azione di riduzione) + challenge to the will — Resolved in mandatory mediation

Three siblings were disputing the estate of their deceased mother, who had left a holographic will (testamento olografo) allocating the most valuable property (an apartment in the historic center of Napoli valued at approximately € 480.000) to the youngest son, who had lived with her during her final years. The other two siblings believed that the will was the product of undue influence (captazione) and that the lifetime gifts made by the mother to the cohabiting son — including a luxury car and sums of money — had eroded their reserved share (quota di legittima).

Our firm assisted the two excluded siblings, concurrently initiating a twofold action: verifying the formal validity of the holographic will and calculating the infringement of the forced share (lesione di legittima) resulting from the testamentary dispositions and lifetime gifts. The calligraphic analysis of the will highlighted certain irregularities in the dating, which could have supported an action for annulment. The calculation of the assets gifted during life (donatum) added to the estate left at death (relictum) demonstrated a significant infringement of the forced share of the two siblings, amounting overall to 50% of the estate.

On the basis of this evidence, we initiated the mandatory mediation procedure provided for by art. 5 D.Lgs. 28/2010 from a strong and documentarily substantiated negotiating position. During mediation, the cohabiting son — conscious of the weakness of his litigation position — agreed to pay the two siblings a sum equal to their forced share, retaining ownership of the property but waiving any further claim to the hereditary estate. Our clients obtained their statutory entitlement within a few months, avoiding years of judicial litigation.

Agreement in mediation — Reserved share fully recognized
