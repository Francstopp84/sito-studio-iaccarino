Arts. 1158–1167 c.c. — Acquisition of Ownership by Possession

Have you possessed a property for decades without title?
Prolonged possession is a right recognized by law.

Adverse possession (usucapione) is one of the oldest principles of civil law: whoever exercises continuous, peaceful, and uninterrupted possession of a property for the period established by law acquires ownership of that property, regardless of the existence of a formal title of purchase. Land cultivated for generations without notarial deeds, properties occupied for decades, rights of way exercised without challenge: these are widespread situations throughout Italy, particularly in the South, where cadastral fragmentation and the absence of regular chain-of-title deeds are historically rooted phenomena. The judicial ascertainment of adverse possession transforms de facto possession into full ownership enforceable against third parties, recordable in the land registers, and marketable. Knowing how to initiate proceedings, what evidence to collect, and how to handle mandatory mediation (mediazione obbligatoria) is the starting point for protecting a right you have already been exercising for years.

Regulatory framework

The rules governing adverse possession

The Civil Code devotes an organic regulatory framework to adverse possession in arts. 1158–1167, distinguishing between different categories of property and adjusting the timeframes according to the nature of the possession. Precise knowledge of these articles is essential to build a sound legal claim and to counter the defenses raised by the legal owner.

Civil Code

Art. 1158 c.c. — Twenty-Year (Ordinary) Adverse Possession

Ordinary adverse possession of immovable property requires continuous and uninterrupted possession for twenty years. The possession must be animus domini — that is, exercised with the intent to act as owner — peaceful (not acquired by force or clandestinely), and public. The provision requires neither good faith on the part of the possessor nor the presence of a title of acquisition: the expiration of the twenty-year period with the indicated characteristics is sufficient.

Timeframe: 20 years — Requirement: possession animus domini , continuous, peaceful, public
Civil Code

Art. 1159 c.c. — Ten-Year Shortened Adverse Possession

Shortened adverse possession (usucapione abbreviata) reduces the timeframe to ten years upon the satisfaction of two cumulative requirements: the good faith of the possessor at the time possession was acquired (i.e., non-culpable ignorance of the defect in title) and the existence of a title abstractly suitable to transfer ownership but invalid due to an intrinsic defect (e.g., deed of sale void due to lack of authority of the seller, erroneously interpreted will, sale of property belonging to another). The title must be transcribed in the land registers (registri immobiliari), and the term runs from the date of transcription. This scenario frequently arises in cases of purchases from non-owners or sales of estate assets prior to the submission of the succession declaration (dichiarazione di successione).

Timeframe: 10 years — Requirements: good faith + registered title abstractly suitable to transfer ownership
Civil Code

Art. 1159-bis c.c. — Adverse Possession of Rural Land in Mountain Areas

A special provision introduced to foster the consolidation of land ownership in abandoned mountain and rural areas: rural parcels of land located in municipalities classified as mountainous or partially mountainous are acquired by adverse possession in fifteen years (ordinary adverse possession) or in five years if supported by good faith and registered title (shortened adverse possession). The provision addresses the social need to regularize situations of possession exceeding twenty years over land abandoned by emigrated legal owners. The status as rural land and the classification of the municipality as mountainous must be proven documentarily by means of certifications from ISTAT or the Land Registry (catasto).

Mountain lands: 15 years ordinary — 5 years with good faith and registered title
Civil Code

Art. 1161 c.c. — Adverse Possession of Movable Property

For movable property not registered in public registers, ordinary adverse possession is completed in twenty years. Shortened adverse possession is reduced to ten years in the presence of good faith and a title abstractly suitable to transfer ownership, and to three years if the possessor acquired the item in good faith from a merchant. For registered movable property (motor vehicles, boats, aircraft), the rules on immovable property apply: ordinary adverse possession in twenty years, shortened adverse possession in ten years. Case law has extended the adverse possession of movable property also to uncertificated corporate shares, works of art, and assets of historical value, with appropriate specificities.

Movable property: 20 years ordinary — 10/3 years shortened depending on good faith and title
Legislative Decree no. 28/2010 — Mandatory Mediation

Mandatory Mediation in Adverse Possession Disputes

For disputes concerning rights in rem over immovable property — including the judicial declaration of adverse possession (accertamento dell’usucapione) — D.Lgs. 28/2010 (as amended by D.L. 69/2013 and the Cartabia Reform, D.Lgs. 149/2022) requires a mediation attempt as a condition precedent to the admissibility of the judicial claim (condizione di procedibilità). The mediation procedure must be initiated before a mediation body registered with the Ministry of Justice and must take place prior to filing the petition (ricorso) with the Court (Tribunale). Failure to carry out mediation renders the judicial claim inadmissible, which may also be raised by the court on its own motion (d’ufficio). However, mediation in adverse possession proceedings has a low success rate, as the formal owner rarely agrees to relinquish formal title without a court judgment.

Mandatory prior to legal proceedings — Inadmissibility raisable on the court’s own motion (d’ufficio)
Applicable legal categories

Which type of adverse possession (usucapione) applies to your case

The Italian Civil Code provides for various forms of adverse possession (usucapione), each with different requirements and time periods. Selecting the correct legal framework to assert in court is the primary strategic step: ordinary adverse possession is proven differently from shortened adverse possession, and the required evidence varies considerably.

▶ Ordinary Adverse Possession (20 years)

The most common form of adverse possession of immovable property. It requires twenty years of continuous, peaceful, open, and uninterrupted possession, exercised as the owner. Neither good faith nor formal title is required. It applies to agricultural land, urban properties, garages, commercial premises, courtyards, and common areas. The term may be combined with that of previous possessors (tacking of possession, accessione del possesso pursuant to art. 1146 c.c.). It is the pre-eminent legal category for factual situations established over several family generations.

▶ Shortened Adverse Possession (10 years)

Allows the acquisition of ownership in only ten years if the possessor is in good faith and holds an abstractly suitable title (void contract of sale, void deed of gift, inheritance with defects), duly registered in the land registry (trascritto). Common in cases of acquisition from a non-owner, sale of inherited real property prior to the registration of the succession, or deeds of purchase (rogito) entered into with a fraudulent owner. Good faith at the time possession was acquired is presumed pursuant to art. 1147 c.c. and is not undermined by the subsequent discovery of the defect.

▶ Adverse Possession of Mountain Rural Land

Special legal category pursuant to art. 1159-bis c.c. for rural land located in mountain or partially mountain municipalities. Reduced terms: fifteen years for ordinary, five years for shortened adverse possession. Particularly significant in Southern Italy and in the Apennine regions, where numerous agricultural plots were abandoned by emigrated owners and cultivated for decades by third parties. The status of a municipality as mountainous (ISTAT classification or DPR 601/1973) is an objective fact, verifiable through certification issued by the Municipality (Comune) or the Mountain Community (Comunità Montana).

▶ Adverse Possession of Movable Property

Applicable to unregistered movable property (machinery, equipment, works of art, jewellery) and registered movable property (motor vehicles, watercraft). For unregistered movable property: twenty years ordinary, ten years shortened with good faith and title, three years if purchased from a commercial merchant in good faith. Registered movable property follows the rules applicable to immovable property. Frequently used to regularise the ownership of vehicles lacking formal transfer with the public vehicle registry (PRA), vintage motor vehicles, or industrial machinery acquired without formal deeds of assignment.

▶ Adverse Possession of Easements

Apparent easements (servitù apparenti) — that is, those manifested by visible and permanent works (a road, gate, aqueduct, opening in a wall) — may be acquired by adverse possession pursuant to art. 1158 c.c. upon the lapse of twenty years of continuous and open exercise of the easement itself. Rights of way (servitù di passaggio) are the most common: pathways, inter-farm roads, vehicular access routes exercised for decades without formal title. Non-apparent easements (e.g., negative easements not to build, servitù non aedificandi) cannot be acquired by adverse possession, but only by contract or by deed of destination (atto di destinazione).

▶ Judicial Declaration with Land Registry Registration

A judgment declaring adverse possession has constitutive effect: it establishes ownership in favour of the adverse possessor. It must be registered in the land registries (registri immobiliari) pursuant to art. 2651 c.c. to be enforceable against third parties. Registration of the judicial claim (domanda giudiziale) during the proceedings pursuant to art. 2652 c.c. (no. 7) is essential to prevent the formal owner from transferring the property to third parties in good faith prior to the judgment. A property acquired through adverse possession that has not been registered cannot be mortgaged, sold by notarial deed, or used as bank collateral: judicial declaration and land registry registration are two indivisible steps.

How we work

The six strategies to obtain a judicial declaration of adverse possession

Every adverse possession claim is built on evidence: witness, documentary, and technical. The defense strategy must anticipate the formal owner’s objections, which will almost invariably dispute the continuity or the nature of the possession. Here are the six strategies we systematically implement.

1. Proof of possession exceeding twenty years: systematic collection

The core of every adverse possession dispute is the proof of possession: its duration, its continuity, and its quality. We work to build a robust evidentiary file even before commencing the mediation procedure. Sources of evidence include: tax returns listing the asset within one’s personal estate, utility bills in the possessor’s name, payment receipts for local property taxes (ICI/IMU), historical photographs, historical cadastral certificates (visure catastali), lease agreements leasing the property to third parties, and cadastral variation certificates requested by the possessor. Case law has also recognised full evidentiary value in local tax payments made by the adverse possessor as an indicator of the continuous exercise of possession.

2. Witness testimonies and oral evidence: selection and preparation

Testimonies of neighbours, relatives, farmers working the land, tradespeople who performed work on the property, and representatives of adjoining local authorities are often the primary evidence of adverse possession, because they establish the public perception of the possession over time. We carefully select witnesses, verify their credibility, and formulate the evidentiary articles (capitoli di prova) to cover all relevant elements: commencement of possession, continuity, absence of interruptions, nature of possession (animus domini versus mere detention, detenzione), and absence of challenges by the formal owner. The testimony of a single witness is sufficient if consistent with documentary evidence.

3. Interruption of third-party adverse possession: acting promptly

A formal owner of an asset possessed by third parties may interrupt the running of adverse possession through precise legal acts: service of an out-of-court formal notice of default (costituzione in mora) pursuant to art. 2943 c.c., initiation of a legal action to recover possession (azione di rivendica) pursuant to art. 948 c.c., or any act that deprives the possessor of factual control over the asset. If the owner allowed the statutory period to elapse without taking action, adverse possession has already matured and can no longer be interrupted retroactively. We assist formal owners in promptly identifying situations of unlawful possession and taking interruptive measures before the time period matures, preserving formal ownership against the risk of adverse possession.

4. Mandatory mediation: strategic management of the procedure

Mediation pursuant to D.Lgs. 28/2010 is not a mere formal step to be carried out to satisfy the procedural admissibility of the judicial claim: handled correctly, it can be a tool to achieve favourable solutions without a trial. In mediation, the formal owner may transfer title in exchange for nominal consideration, agree to regularisation by notarial deed, or agree to a split of cadastral and notarial costs. We handle mediation with a direct approach: we present the gathered documentary evidence to the mediator and the opposing party, demonstrating the strength of our client’s position, and assess settlement proposals in real time.

5. Registration of the judicial claim pursuant to art. 2652 c.c.

As soon as the judicial proceeding for adverse possession (usucapione) is commenced, it is essential to immediately register the claim (trascrivere la domanda) in the land registers pursuant to art. 2652, no. 7, c.c. This registration has a fundamental protective effect: it makes the declaratory judgment enforceable against third parties who have acquired rights over the property after the registration of the claim. Without registration, the formal owner could sell the asset to a good-faith third party during the proceedings, and the judgment establishing adverse possession (usucapione) could not be enforced against that purchaser. We personally handle the registration of the claim in the registers of the competent land registry office (Conservatoria dei Registri Immobiliari) within the first few days of commencing the proceedings.

6. Shortened adverse possession (usucapione abbreviata): leveraging title and good faith

When the possessor holds a defective formal title (void deed of sale - rogito, invalid donation, irregular succession), the strategy is to qualify the case as shortened adverse possession (usucapione abbreviata) pursuant to art. 1159 c.c.: the ten-year period is significantly shorter, and proof of good faith — presumed pursuant to art. 1147 c.c. — considerably simplifies the burden of proof. We analyze the formal title defect by defect to verify its abstract suitability, verify the date and regularity of the registration (trascrizione), and calculate the ten-year period from the moment of registration. A lawsuit that would appear to require twenty years of proof can often be won in ten years, halving the duration of the dispute and simplifying the evidence to be gathered.

The procedure step by step

From the initial consultation to the registration of the judgment

  • 1

    Case analysis and gathering of historical documentation

    Verification of the cadastral and mortgage status of the property, identification of the formal owner, analysis of deeds of title (atti di provenienza). Collection of initial evidentiary documentation: historical cadastral records (visure catastali storiche), cadastral map excerpts (estratti mappa), tax returns, IMU/ICI receipts, utility contracts. Calculation of the statutory period for adverse possession (usucapione) and verification of the applicable legal regime (ordinary, shortened, mountainous agricultural land).

  • 2

    Mandatory mediation attempt (mediazione obbligatoria)

    Filing of the mediation request (istanza di mediazione) with an accredited mediation body. Service on the formal owner. First mediation session: presentation of evidentiary documentation and the applicant's position. Assessment of a potential settlement agreement via notarial deed of voluntary transfer (a faster alternative to judicial declaration).

  • 3

    Filing of the claim/writ of summons (ricorso/atto di citazione) with the Court (Tribunale)

    In the event of failure to reach an agreement in mediation, filing of the originating pleading (writ of summons - atto di citazione, or petition - ricorso, depending on the applicable procedure). Identification of the territorially competent Court (Tribunale) (where the property is located). Formulation of the relief sought: declaration of the acquired adverse possession (usucapione) with an order to register the judgment.

  • 4

    Registration of the judicial claim (trascrizione della domanda giudiziale)

    Immediately following filing, registration of the judicial claim pursuant to art. 2652, no. 7, c.c. at the competent land registry office (Conservatoria dei Registri Immobiliari). This registration makes the future judgment enforceable against subsequent third-party purchasers, blocking any attempts by the formal owner to dispose of the property during the proceedings.

  • 5

    Evidentiary phase (istruttoria): documentary and witness evidence

    Filing of historical documentary evidence, possible request for admission of witness testimony on specific articles of proof (capitoli di prova). Possible court-appointed expert examination (CTU) for the description of the property, identification of boundaries, planimetric survey, and verification of cadastral correspondence. Acquisition of historical aerial photographs (historical Google Earth, IGM) as evidence of the possession status over time.

  • 6

    Judgment and final registration (trascrizione)

    The judgment declaring adverse possession (usucapione) has retroactive declaratory effect as of the date the statutory period was completed. Once the judgment becomes final and unappealable (passata in giudicato), it is registered in the land registers pursuant to art. 2651 c.c.: from that moment, the adverse possessor is the full legal owner, entitled to sell, mortgage, gift, and execute any act of disposition over the property.

Results achieved

5 cases of adverse possession (usucapione) resolved

Case no. 1

Twenty-year adverse possession (usucapione) of agricultural land — Farmer family against absent heirs

Property: 3,2-hectare agricultural land — Location: province of Caserta — Tribunale di Santa Maria Capua Vetere

A family of farmers had cultivated an agricultural property of approximately 3.2 hectares for over thirty years, paying the relevant municipal taxes and cadastral taxes (tributi catastali), and using the land for the production of tomatoes and vegetables without ever having received any objection. The original formal owner had passed away in the nineteen-eighties without leaving heirs who showed any interest in the land; only following a purchase offer from an agricultural company interested in the area did heirs residing abroad appear, claiming ownership of the land and demanding disproportionate compensation.

The firm initiated the mandatory mediation procedure (mediazione obbligatoria) pursuant to D.Lgs. 28/2010, during which the heirs refused any reasonable agreement, demanding sums incompatible with the cadastral value of the land. Following the unsuccessful outcome of the mediation, the petition (ricorso) was filed before the Court (Tribunale), with the concurrent registration of the judicial claim (trascrizione della domanda giudiziale). The evidentiary file proved to be particularly solid: twenty-seven years of invoices for agricultural supplies billed to the property, ICI/IMU tax returns from 2003 onwards, IGM aerial photographs from the 1990s showing cultivation, and witness testimonies from five neighbors attesting to the family’s exclusive and uninterrupted possession since the 1980s. The court-appointed technical appraisal (CTU) confirmed the correspondence between the possessed property and the indicated cadastral parcels. The Court (Tribunale) upheld the claim, establishing that adverse possession (usucapione) had occurred pursuant to art. 1158 c.c. The judgment (sentenza) was officially registered, making the family the owner to all legal effects of an agricultural property with an estimated value of approximately 180,000 euros.

Adverse possession established — Ownership consolidated with a value of € 180.000
Case no. 2

Adverse possession (usucapione) of urban property — Apartment occupied for over thirty years

Property: 95 sq. m apartment — Location: Napoli (Vomero district) — Tribunale di Napoli

A seventy-four-year-old lady had occupied a third-floor apartment in a condominium building in Vomero for thirty-two years, having purchased it verbally from the prior occupant — a distant relative who had emigrated to Argentina — by paying him a sum of money without entering into any notarial deed. Over the years, she had carried out renovation works, registered all utilities (electricity, gas, water) in her own name, paid condominium expenses and local taxes, and used the apartment as her primary residence without ever encountering disputes. The issue arose when the children of the original formal owner, who was now elderly and facing financial hardship, claimed ownership of the property in order to resell it.

The evidentiary situation was complex because the elderly lady had not kept any documentation of the payments made decades earlier, and the emigrated relative had difficulty testifying from Argentina. The firm based the defence on continuous documentary evidence: thirty-two years of utility bills in her name, condominium fees paid regularly to the property manager (amministratore), income tax returns indicating the property's tax code as her primary residence, continuous municipal property tax (IMU) returns since 2012, building permits relating to renovation works carried out in the 1990s, and the testimony of six condominium residents. The Court of Naples (Tribunale di Napoli) recognized the twenty-year adverse possession (usucapione) pursuant to art. 1158 c.c., holding that public, peaceful, exclusive, and uninterrupted possession had been amply proven. The property, valued at 280.000 euro, is now formally registered in our client's name.

Ownership established — Apartment valued at € 280,000
Case no. 3

Shortened ten-year adverse possession (usucapione abbreviata) — Purchase from a non-owner by notarial deed (rogito)

Asset: villa with 180 sq m garden — Location: Pozzuoli — Tribunale di Napoli

In 2011, a couple had purchased a villa with a garden in Pozzuoli for € 310,000, executing a standard notarial deed (rogito notarile) and registering a bank mortgage for the loan. Only thirteen years later, in 2024, during a cadastral search for a new mortgage, it emerged that the seller was not the true owner of the property: the asset was formally registered in the name of the heir of a prior owner who had passed away in 2003, who had signed the 2011 deed despite never having formalized their purchase through a declaration of succession and cadastral transfer (voltura catastale). The deed was technically void due to lack of title/legitimation of the seller, and the true formal owner — the nephew of the original title holder — was demanding the return of the property.

The firm immediately classified the situation as a case of shortened adverse possession (usucapione abbreviata) pursuant to art. 1159 c.c.: the spouses had acted in absolute good faith (no defect had emerged in the notarial checks carried out in 2011) and held a formal title — the notarial deed (rogito) — abstractly capable of transferring ownership, recorded in the land registers from the date of the deed itself. Between 2011 and 2024, thirteen years had elapsed, amply sufficient to complete the ten-year adverse possession period. The proceedings concluded at the hearing for the specification of final pleadings, following the filing of a comprehensive brief under art. 183, para. 6, c.p.c. substantiating good faith, registered title, and the expiry of the ten-year period. The Court established the shortened adverse possession, dismissing the formal owner's claim for recovery of property (azione di rivendicazione) and declaring the spouses full owners of the property for all legal purposes.

Shortened adverse possession established — Villa valued at € 310,000 preserved
Case no. 4

Successful opposition to claim of adverse possession — Interruption of possession demonstrated

Asset: 1,200 sq m building plot — Location: Afragola — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

The client — the formal owner of a building plot in Afragola — was summoned as defendant in adverse possession proceedings by a neighbour who claimed to have possessed the land for over twenty-five years, requesting a ruling establishing twenty-year adverse possession. The land had a market value of approximately 420,000 euros by virtue of its building potential (a lot included in an approved subdivision plan). The claimant had produced some testimonies from neighbours stating that they had seen him working on the land, along with several undated photographs.

The defence was structured across three levels. First: we demonstrated that the formal owner had interrupted the statutory limitation period for adverse possession through three documented legal acts: a registered letter with advice of receipt sent in 2007 warning the neighbour against occupying the land, a criminal complaint for unlawful land invasion under art. 637 c.p. filed in 2012 (subsequently time-barred, but relevant for the purposes of interruption), and an expert technical valuation commissioned in 2015 to appraise the plot in view of a sale. Second: we demonstrated that the alleged possession was discontinuous, limited to occasional seasonal maintenance work, and that the claimant occupied the land as a mere permissive holder (detentore autorizzato), not as a possessor with the intention to hold as owner (animus domini). Third: we produced historical satellite photographs (Google Earth) showing that the land was uncultivated and unfenced, which was inconsistent with the claimant's allegations. The Court dismissed the adverse possession claim, declaring that adverse possession had not occurred due to the absence of the legal requirements of possession and as a result of the documented interruptions. The formal owner retained ownership of the building plot.

Adverse possession claim dismissed — Ownership of the € 420,000 plot preserved
Case no. 5

Adverse possession of right of way (servitù di passaggio) — Vehicular access exercised for forty years

Asset: vehicular right of way (servitù di passaggio carraio) over landlocked property — Location: Frattamaggiore — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

An agricultural business operating in the Campanian plain had used for over forty years a driveway crossing the neighboring land to reach the public road, as its own land was landlocked and lacked any other viable access. The access consisted of a dirt road of about 80 meters, with a metal gate that the business had installed at its own expense in the eighties. The owner of the servient estate (fondo servente) had always tolerated the use without ever formalizing any written agreement. The conflict arose when the owner of the servient estate (fondo servente) sold the land to a real estate developer who intended to close the passage to build a residential complex, ordering the business to cease access immediately.

The firm acted on two fronts simultaneously: on the one hand, it filed an urgent application for interim relief (ricorso cautelare d’urgenza) pursuant to art. 700 c.p.c. to preserve the right of way pending the proceedings on the merits (giudizio di merito), obtaining an injunction (provvedimento di inibitoria) that prevented the developer from closing the access during the proceedings; on the other hand, it initiated proceedings on the merits to establish the acquisition by adverse possession (usucapione) of the easement of way (servitù di passaggio) pursuant to art. 1158 c.c. The evidence was particularly solid: the metal gate installed in the 1980s was a visible and permanent structure that qualified the easement as “apparent” pursuant to art. 1061 c.c. (an indispensable prerequisite for the acquisition of easements by adverse possession, usucapione), aerial photographs showed the road since the 1970s, and historical corporate financial statements indicated that access as the sole route to the land. The Court (Tribunale) confirmed the adverse possession of the easement of way, ordering the developer to keep the access clear and unobstructed. The agricultural enterprise was able to continue operating without interruption.

Right of way established — Vehicular access definitively secured
