Regulatory framework

Family law in Italy

Civil Code — Artt. 149–158

Personal separation of spouses

Personal separation is governed by artt. 149–158 of the Civil Code (Codice Civile). It does not dissolve the marital bond, but suspends its effects: the spouses cease to be subject to the duties of cohabitation and fidelity, and their mutual financial relations and the exercise of parental responsibility are regulated. Separation may be consensual (separazione consensuale) — when there is agreement on all terms — or judicial (separazione giudiziale), when no agreement is reached and court intervention (Tribunale) is required.

Legal Separation (separazione): Artt. 149–158 Civil Code (Codice Civile)
Law 898/1970 as amended by L. 55/2015

Divorce and the «short divorce» law

Divorce definitively dissolves the marital bond. It is governed by L. 1° dicembre 1970, n. 898, substantially reformed in 2015 by the so-called short divorce (divorzio breve) law (L. 55/2015), which drastically reduced the waiting periods: 6 months from the appearance of the spouses before the President of the Court (Presidente del Tribunale) in the case of consensual separation (separazione consensuale), 12 months in the case of judicial separation (separazione giudiziale). Prior to the reform, the periods were 3 years and 3 years, respectively. Divorce maintenance (assegno divorzile) is governed by art. 5 of the law, with criteria established by the landmark ruling of the Sezioni Unite della Cassazione n. 18287/2018.

Divorce: L. 898/1970 — Short divorce: L. 55/2015
D.L. 132/2014 — Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita)

The out-of-court procedure for consensual separations

Decree-Law (D.L.) of 12 September 2014, no. 132 (converted into Law 162/2014) introduced assisted negotiation in family matters (negoziazione assistita), allowing consensual separations and divorces to be formalized without the need to appear before the Court (Tribunale), provided there are no minor children, adult children with severe disabilities, or economically non-self-sufficient children. The parties' lawyers enter into an agreement directly, which is submitted for verification to the Public Prosecutor (Procuratore della Repubblica) and then to the civil registrar (ufficiale di stato civile) for registration. The procedure is faster, less costly, and less stressful than ordinary court proceedings.

Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita): D.L. 132/2014
2023 Updates

Cartabia Reform (D.Lgs. 149/2022). The civil procedure reform that entered into force on 28 February 2023 also deeply affected family proceedings. It introduced the unitary proceeding for separation, divorce, and proceedings concerning children of unmarried parents (pursuant to art. 473-bis c.p.c.), unifying previously distinct procedural tracks. It reinforced instruments for the protection of minors, introduced heightened information requirements, and broadened access to urgent interim measures. For proceedings pending as of 28 February 2023, the transitional rules provided for by D.Lgs. 149/2022 apply.

Fault-based separation (Addebito della separazione). The Court (Tribunale) may rule that the separation is attributable to one of the spouses (addebito) when the marital breakdown is causally linked to conduct in breach of spousal duties (fidelity, assistance, contribution to family needs). Fault allocation has significant financial consequences: the spouse found at fault loses the right to a maintenance allowance (assegno di mantenimento), without prejudice to the right to basic support (alimenti), and forfeits inheritance rights. A declaration of fault requires strict proof: the Court of Cassation (Cassazione, Sez. I, no. 4175/2021) reaffirmed that merely leaving the marital home is not sufficient; a causal link between the conduct and the breakdown of the marriage must be established.

Which path is right for you

Available procedures

There is no single path. The choice between consensual separation, judicial separation, joint divorce, contentious divorce, or assisted negotiation depends on the specific circumstances: presence of children, matrimonial property regime, extent of the assets, level of conflict, and urgency. Our law firm examines each case and identifies the most effective and least stressful approach.

Agreed procedure

Consensual separation

The spouses reach an agreement on all terms: children, maintenance, marital home, division of assets. The agreement is endorsed (omologato) by the Court (Tribunale, art. 158 c.c.), without a hearing on the merits. It is the fastest and least costly procedure. The Court verifies of its own motion the protection of the best interests of minor children and may refuse to approve terms detrimental to them.

Average duration: 3–6 months from the filing of the petition (ricorso)
Contentious procedure

Judicial separation

When the spouses cannot reach an agreement, one of them applies to the Court (Tribunale) with a petition (ricorso) pursuant to art. 706 c.p.c. (now art. 473-bis.12 c.p.c. following the Cartabia Reform). The President adopts interim measures in the interests of the parties and the children; subsequently, the investigating judge (giudice istruttore) conducts the proceedings on the merits. It is possible to request fault attribution (addebito) against the other spouse.

Average duration: 1–3 years (varies by judicial venue and caseload)
Consensual dissolution

Joint divorce (divorzio congiunto)

Once the 6-month period from the appearance in consensual separation proceedings (or 12 months in the case of judicial separation) has elapsed, the spouses may jointly request the dissolution of the marriage pursuant to Law (L.) 898/1970. If there is agreement on all conditions, the procedure concludes swiftly with a court decree (decreto del Tribunale) or with an assisted negotiation agreement (negoziazione assistita).

Average duration: 4–8 months from the filing of the petition (ricorso)
Contentious dissolution

Judicial divorce (divorzio giudiziale)

In the absence of an agreement on the terms of the divorce — divorce maintenance allowance (assegno divorzile), marital home, child custody — the Court decides on each disputed matter by judgment (sentenza). The proceedings are governed by the unified procedure pursuant to Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 149/2022. The judgment may be appealed before the Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello) and, on grounds of law, before the Court of Cassation (Corte di Cassazione).

Average duration: 2–4 years at first instance
Out-of-court alternative

Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita)

The most streamlined procedure for consensual separations and divorces without minor or incapacitated children (Decree-Law / D.L. 132/2014). The parties' lawyers formalise the agreement, which receives clearance (nulla osta) from the Public Prosecutor (PM) and is forwarded to the civil registrar (ufficiale di stato civile). No hearing, no court decree. Reduced costs, short timeframes, utmost confidentiality.

Average duration: 1–3 months from the initial agreement
Modification of conditions

Revision of conditions

The conditions of separation and divorce are not immutable. In the presence of subsequent justified reasons (giustificati motivi) — job loss, remarriage, changes in the children's needs, relocation abroad — either spouse may request a revision of the maintenance allowance, the custody arrangements, or the terms of allocation of the family home. The proceedings are conducted in chambers (procedimento camerale) and can also be concluded swiftly.

Average duration: 6–18 months (urgent: orders within 30–60 days)

How we work

The aspects we handle with the greatest care

Every separation is a story of its own. However, there are issues that recur prominently in almost every case file: asset protection, spousal maintenance, the family home, and the children. On each of these matters, our firm adopts a structured approach, grounded in the most recent case law and knowledge of the specific aspects of the Neapolitan area.

I.

Asset protection and matrimonial property regime

The property regime chosen at the time of marriage — statutory community of property (comunione legale) or separation of property (separazione dei beni) — fundamentally determines how assets are divided in the event of separation. Under the statutory community of property, all assets acquired during the marriage are shared equally; under separation of property, each spouse retains exclusive ownership of their purchases. We verify the nature of each asset (personal or joint), identify any simulated transactions or fictitious registrations, and formulate the most advantageous strategy for the division of marital property.

II.

Maintenance allowance (Cass. SS.UU. 18287/2018)

Following the landmark ruling of the Joint Sections (Sezioni Unite) of 2018 (No. 18287), the criteria for determining the divorce allowance (assegno divorzile) were fundamentally overhauled: the court must assess not only the standard of living during the marriage, but also each spouse's current earning capacity, the contribution made to family life and to the other spouse's career, and the «compensatory-equalising» function of the allowance. We draft substantiated briefs with analyses of historical contributions and income prospects to obtain or challenge the allowance in its exact amount.

III.

Allocation of the marital home

The family home is allocated to the spouse with whom minor children or adult children who are not financially self-sufficient reside, regardless of the ownership of the property (art. 337-sexies c.c.). The Court of Cassation (Sez. I, No. 9143/2020) reaffirmed that allocation is independent of legal ownership and aims exclusively to protect the children's family environment. We assist both those seeking allocation and those who — as sole owner or co-owner — intend to regain possession when conditions change.

IV.

Child custody and residence (affidamento e collocamento)

Shared custody (affidamento condiviso) is the general rule in the Italian legal system (art. 337-ter c.c.): both parents exercise parental responsibility. Primary residence (collocamento prevalente) — that is, with which parent the child primarily lives — is instead a distinct and often crucial matter, as it affects the allocation of the home and the amount of maintenance. Sole custody (affidamento esclusivo) is permitted only in serious cases of incapacity or detriment to the minor. We attend with care to every aspect concerning children, including disputes over education, residence, and major decisions of significant interest.

V.

Fast-track divorce (divorzio breve): taking advantage of reduced timeframes

Under Law (L.) 55/2015, the waiting periods to file for divorce were reduced to 6 months (consensual separation) and 12 months (judicial separation), running from the appearance of the spouses before the President of the Court (Presidente del Tribunale). It is essential to calculate the dies a quo correctly: the Court of Cassation clarified that the time limit runs from the first presidential hearing, even if the proceedings were already pending prior to the reform. We monitor deadlines and file the divorce petition (ricorso per divorzio) as soon as the statutory time requirement has elapsed, avoiding any unnecessary extension of separated status.

VI.

Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) and out-of-court agreements

When both parties are open to dialogue, assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) allows an agreement to be reached quickly and at reasonable cost, without the protracted delays of court proceedings. Our law firm is able to assist both as counsel for the applicant and as coordinator of the procedure, managing negotiations professionally to ensure an agreement that is fair and lasting over time. A poorly negotiated agreement is worse than a trial: every clause must be drafted with precision to prevent future disputes regarding interpretation.

Child maintenance is not negotiated downwards. The obligation of child maintenance is a right of the minor, not of the parent. The Court of Cassation (Sez. I, No. 4811/2023) reaffirmed that the judge may depart from the parties' requests and set ex officio an amount consistent with the best interests of the child. Agreements that penalise the children are not approved (omologati) by the Court. We assist our clients to ensure that every agreement respects the rights of minors and remains, at the same time, sustainable for the paying parent.

5 cases handled and resolved

Case no. 1

Contentious divorce with hidden assets — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli, First Civil Section — 2023 — Statutory community of property regime

A woman consulted the firm in the midst of a contentious divorce from her husband, an entrepreneur in the food distribution sector, who claimed to have very modest income and virtually no assets, having, in the weeks prior to filing the petition (ricorso), transferred his corporate shares to a nominee and shifted liquidity to bank accounts held by family members. The client, left without employment after years dedicated to the family and her husband's business, risked receiving a negligible allowance despite an affluent marital standard of living.

Our firm conducted an in-depth asset search, obtaining Chamber of Commerce records, company register extracts, notarial deeds, and bank statements through an application to the Tribunal pursuant to art. 492-bis c.p.c. We proved that the transfers were sham transactions (simulazione) carried out to the detriment of the wife, requesting and obtaining from the Judge the revocation of the acts pursuant to art. 2901 c.c. (clawback action / azione revocatoria). The Tribunal then determined the divorce allowance (assegno divorzile) based on the husband's actual income, reconstructed also through an expert accounting appraisal ordered by the court (CTU contabile), and awarded our client 50% of the value of the fictitiously transferred company shares, in addition to a monthly divorce allowance reflecting the true standard of living of the family unit.

The case required two years of evidentiary proceedings but concluded with a favourable ruling on all counts: division of the actual estate, adequate allowance, assignment of the family home. The husband waived his right to appeal during negotiations following the first-instance judgment.

Hidden assets reconstructed — Full allowance obtained — Family home assigned
Case no. 2

Review of maintenance allowance after job loss — Corte d’Appello di Napoli

Corte d’Appello di Napoli, Family Section — 2024 — Modification of divorce terms

One of our clients, an engineer employed by a construction company, had been ordered in contentious divorce proceedings to pay his ex-wife a divorce allowance of € 1,400 per month, determined on the basis of his gross annual income of approximately € 65,000. Three years after the judgment, the employing company was declared bankrupt and our client became unemployed. With only unemployment benefits (NASpI) as his sole income, the allowance had become unsustainable for him: he was accumulating mounting debt toward his former spouse and faced enforcement measures against his bank account.

We filed an application (ricorso) for the modification of the divorce conditions pursuant to art. 9 Law 898/1970, precisely documenting the unforeseen occurrence of job loss, the overall debt situation, and the unsuccessful searches for new employment. On an urgent basis, we obtained from the Tribunal the temporary stay of the payment obligation pending proceedings. On the merits, the judge reduced the allowance to € 400 per month for 18 months, with provision for automatic reassessment upon re-entering the workforce. The former wife challenged the order before the Corte d’Appello, which fully upheld the first-instance decision.

The outcome allowed our client to preserve his financial stability during his period of unemployment, preventing the accumulation of unsustainable debt and the related enforcement consequences. The reassessment provision ensured fairness for both parties upon his resumption of employment.

Allowance reduced from € 1,400 to € 400 — Upheld on Appeal
Case no. 3

Assignment of the family home to the non-owning spouse — Tribunale di Roma

Tribunale di Roma, Ninth Civil Section — 2023 — Judicial separation

One of our clients was facing a particularly contentious judicial separation from her husband, who was the sole owner of the apartment where the family had lived for twelve years. The husband, supported by legal pleadings emphasizing his exclusive ownership of the property, demanded the immediate vacation of the premises, arguing that the wife and the two minor children could relocate to the maternal grandparents' residence located in a different municipality, resulting in the children changing schools and disrupting all their established social relationships.

We structured the application for the assignment of the family home by thoroughly documenting the minors' educational and social integration in the neighborhood, their relationships with peers and their pediatrician, their enrollment in sports activities, and the therapy program of one of the children guided by the school psychologist. We cited established case law of the Court of Cassation (Sez. I, no. 9143/2020; no. 25604/2022), which reiterates that the assignment of the family home is a measure intended to protect the best interests of the child, entirely independent of the ownership of the property right.

The President of the Court, at the presidential hearing, granted the marital home to the wife as an urgent measure in the best interests of the children. The husband insisted on a judicial separation (separazione giudiziale), but the investigating judge confirmed the assignment in the final judgment, further ordering that the husband continue to bear the ordinary condominium charges in consideration of his significantly higher income.

Marital home assigned to the mother — The best interests of the children prevailed over property ownership
Case no. 4

Consensual separation and fast-track divorce in 9 months — Tribunale di Napoli

Tribunale di Napoli — 2024 — Consensual separation + assisted negotiation for divorce

A childless couple, married for seven years under the separate property regime, had reached a mutual decision to separate and wished to conclude the matter as quickly and with as little distress as possible. Both had clear asset positions — each owning their own real estate property and a bank account held exclusively in their own name — and the only unresolved matter was a limited liability company (società a responsabilità limitata) incorporated during the marriage with equal shares, which one spouse wished to acquire in full in order to continue the entrepreneurial activity.

We prepared a consensual separation agreement that included a clause for the purchase of the company shares by the husband at a price determined by an appraisal agreed upon by the parties, thereby preventing a separate corporate dispute. The petition for consensual separation (ricorso per separazione consensuale) was filed, and the Court's homologation decree was issued in less than three months. Once the six months from the presidential appearance had elapsed — the minimum statutory period under Law 55/2015 — we initiated the assisted negotiation procedure (negoziazione assistita) for the divorce.

In the absence of minor children, assisted negotiation was the optimal route: no court hearings, no judicial decree, merely an agreement negotiated by the parties' counsel, forwarded to the Public Prosecutor (Procuratore della Repubblica) for clearance (nulla osta), and then to the civil registrar for transcription. The entire process — from the initial separation to the transcription of the divorce — was completed in less than nine months overall.

Separation + divorce in 9 months — No contentious hearings — Zero conflict
Case no. 5

Sole custody granted due to severe parental neglect — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Tribunale di Napoli Nord, Family Division — 2024 — Judicial separation involving minor children

A mother of two children aged 5 and 8 sought assistance from our firm in an emergency situation: her husband, with whom she was undergoing judicial separation proceedings, had severe, diagnosed but untreated alcohol addiction issues, had been involved in two incidents of driving under the influence with the children in the car, and had exhibited aggressive behaviour in their presence. Despite this, the interim regime ordered at the presidential hearing provided for joint custody with unsupervised weekend visitation for the father. The mother feared for the children's safety and sought an urgent modification of the arrangements.

We filed an urgent petition pursuant to art. 473-bis.15 c.p.c. (as amended by the Cartabia Reform) for the modification of the interim measures, documenting the two driving-under-the-influence incidents with certified traffic police reports, the family doctor's report certifying the diagnosis of alcohol dependency and non-compliance with the recommended treatment plan, and a report from the municipal Social Services already in the case file. We sought and obtained the suspension of unsupervised visits and their conversion into supervised visits at the municipality's neutral visitation centre.

On the merits of the case, the court ordered a court-appointed psychological expert assessment (CTU) on the minors and the father. The expert confirmed that the children exhibited symptoms of separation anxiety attributable to the witnessed incidents. In the final judgment, the Court decreed the separation, granting sole custody of the children to the mother with supervised visits for the father, conditional upon adherence to a certified treatment programme for addiction subject to six-monthly reviews. It further attributed fault for the separation (addebito) to the husband for breach of his parental duties of care and protection towards the children.

Sole custody granted — Separation attributed to the father's fault — Children protected
