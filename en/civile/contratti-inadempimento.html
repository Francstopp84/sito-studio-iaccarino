Arts. 1218–1229 c.c. — Contractual Liability

A contract is a mutual commitment.
When the other party fails to fulfill their obligations, you have the right to take legal action.

Every contract generates obligations: whoever fails to perform them is liable with their own assets. Breach of contract is one of the most widespread occurrences in the Italian legal landscape: unreturned deposits, works contracts only partially executed, real estate purchase agreements not finalized, services not rendered or rendered with serious non-conformities, supplies failing to meet agreed specifications, leases terminated unlawfully. The law provides a broad range of remedies: contract termination for breach (risoluzione del contratto), an action for specific performance, compensation for actual loss (danno emergente) and loss of profits (lucro cessante), and the defense of non-performance (eccezione di inadempimento) allowing one party to suspend their own performance in response to the other party's default. Understanding these instruments and knowing which one to choose in your specific circumstances is the starting point for obtaining justice.

Regulatory framework

The provisions protecting the performing party

The Italian system of contractual liability is founded upon a core set of provisions of the civil code governing non-performance, available remedies, and compensation for damages. Each remedy requires distinct legal grounds and prerequisites: an incorrect choice can compromise the entire defense strategy.

Civil Code

Art. 1218 c.c. — Debtor’s Liability

A debtor who does not accurately render the performance due is liable for damages, unless they prove that the non-performance or delay was caused by impossibility of performance arising from a cause not attributable to them. Art. 1218 c.c. establishes a presumption of fault against the defaulting debtor: it is the debtor who must prove they are exempt from liability (fortuitous event, force majeure, act of the creditor), whereas the creditor need only prove the existence of the contract and the breach. Covid-19 gave rise to extensive debate regarding the applicability of the defense of fortuitous event, with prevailing case law ruling against any automatic exemption from contractual liability (Cass. Sez. VI, no. 21700/2023).

Presumption of fault of the debtor — The debtor proves the fortuitous event
Civil Code

Art. 1223 c.c. — Compensation for Damages

Compensation for damage due to breach of contract encompasses both the loss suffered by the creditor (damnum emergens) and the lost profits (lucrum cessans), insofar as they are an immediate and direct consequence thereof. Art. 1223 c.c. establishes the so-called “adequate causation link”: only damages that, according to the ordinary course of events, are a consequence of the non-performance are compensable. Case law has clarified that lost profits must be proven with reasonable certainty and cannot be merely hypothetical, but may also be proven by simple presumptions (Cass. Sez. Unite, no. 576/2008, concerning damages for loss of chance). Non-pecuniary damage arising from breach of contract is compensable only where the prejudice affects constitutionally protected interests.

Actual loss + lost profits — Direct and immediate causal link
Civil Code

Art. 1453 c.c. — Termination for Breach of Contract (Risoluzione per Inadempimento)

In contracts with reciprocal obligations, when one of the contracting parties fails to perform their obligations, the other party may choose between termination of the contract (risoluzione del contratto) and performance, without prejudice, in any case, to the right to damages. Termination may also be sought when legal proceedings have been initiated to obtain performance, but performance may no longer be claimed once termination has been requested. Once termination is pronounced, the defaulting party may no longer perform their obligation (art. 1453, para. 3, c.c.). The non-performance that justifies termination must not be “of minor importance having regard to the interest of the other party” (art. 1455 c.c.): case law has developed both objective criteria (extent of the unperformed obligation) and subjective criteria (the creditor's interest in performance).

Choice between termination or performance — Breach not of minor importance
Civil Code

Art. 1454 c.c. — Notice to Perform (Diffida ad Adempiere)

The notice to perform (diffida ad adempiere) is a unilateral out-of-court act whereby the creditor demands that the defaulting debtor perform within a reasonable time limit, not less than fifteen days, stating that, failing this, the contract will be deemed terminated as of right. If the debtor does not perform within the time limit, the contract is terminated as a matter of law (without the need for a judicial ruling), and the creditor may act directly for damages. The notice must be sent by means that ensure certainty of date and receipt (registered letter with return receipt, served process, certified electronic mail - PEC). The time limit must be “reasonable”: case law has considered a time limit of less than fifteen days to be unreasonable for obligations of a certain complexity; shorter periods may be justified by urgency.

Minimum 15-day time limit — Termination as of right in the event of failure to perform within the time limit
Civil Code

Art. 1460 c.c. — Plea of Non-Performance (eccezione di inadempimento) (exceptio non adimpleti contractus)

In contracts with reciprocal obligations, each contracting party may refuse to perform their obligation if the other party does not perform or does not offer to perform their own simultaneously, unless different terms for performance have been agreed upon by the parties or result from the nature of the contract. The plea of non-performance (eccezione di inadempimento) is a powerful defensive tool: it allows one to legitimately suspend one's own performance without incurring liability for breach. The limitation: the plea cannot be raised when the other party's breach is of minor significance (the suspension would be disproportionate) or when the performances are not simultaneous. Case law has recognized the lawfulness of the plea of non-performance even in the event of partial non-performance by the other party, provided that the suspension is proportionate to the seriousness of the breach (Cass. Sez. II, no. 25644/2022).

Lawful suspension of one's own performance — Proportionality required
Civil Code

Art. 1382 c.c. — Penalty Clause (Clausola Penale)

The penalty clause (clausola penale) is the agreement whereby the parties conventionally determine, at the time the contract is concluded, the amount of compensation due in the event of non-performance or delay in performance. The penalty clause serves both a compensatory function (it liquidates damages in advance) and a sanctioning function (contractual deterrent). Its enforcement does not require proof of actual damage: proof of non-performance is sufficient. The court may equitably reduce the penalty if it is manifestly excessive in relation to the interest the creditor had in performance (art. 1384 c.c.), but cannot increase it. In real estate transactions, the confirmatory deposit (caparra confirmatoria) under art. 1385 c.c. performs a similar function: in the event of breach by the prospective buyer, the prospective seller may withdraw from the contract and retain the deposit; in the event of breach by the prospective seller, the prospective buyer may withdraw from the contract and demand double the deposit paid.

No proof of damage required — The court may reduce the penalty if excessive
Available remedies

Which action is right for your contract

In the face of a breach of contract, the civil code provides several concurrent remedies. The choice depends on the objective to be achieved: to be released from the contract or to obtain its performance? To recover the damages suffered or to obtain the penalty? To suspend one's own performance or to take immediate legal action?

▶ Termination for Breach of Contract

The primary remedy for anyone seeking release from a contract breached by the other party: termination ends the contractual bond, obliges the parties to return what has already been performed (retroactive effect), and entitles the creditor to seek damages. It may be pronounced by the court or operate as a matter of law following a formal notice to perform (diffida ad adempiere, art. 1454 c.c.) or an express termination clause (clausola risolutiva espressa, art. 1456 c.c.). The breach must be material (art. 1455 c.c.): not of minor importance having regard to the creditor's interest.

▶ Compelled Performance

An alternative to termination: the creditor may request the court to order the debtor to perform the unfulfilled obligation (art. 1453, para. 1, c.c.). The action for performance is feasible when performance is still possible and the creditor still retains an interest in its execution (e.g., delivery of the purchased good, execution of works, transfer of ownership of real property). In certain cases, the judgment ordering performance may itself produce in rem effects: the judgment enforcing the obligation to conclude a final contract pursuant to art. 2932 c.c. produces the same effects as the contract not concluded.

▶ Contractual Damages

Compensation for damage may be sought jointly with termination or with the action for performance. It encompasses actual loss (the direct losses resulting from the breach: expenses incurred, value of the unreceived performance) and lost profits (the earnings that the creditor would have made if the contract had been performed). The assessment of damages requires proof of quantum: documentation of costs incurred, expert appraisal reports, analysis of lost revenue. In commercial contracts, statutory late payment interest pursuant to D.Lgs. 231/2002 is also added.

▶ Plea of Non-Performance

A classic defensive tool for those who have already received a demand for performance from the defaulting counterparty: one opposes the other party's demand for performance by pleading that counterparty's own failure to perform. “You have not performed your obligation, therefore you are not entitled to demand mine.” It enables one to lawfully suspend one's own obligation without incurring liability, paralyzing the opposing party's legal action. Proportionality between the other party's non-performance and one's own suspension is a fundamental requirement assessed by the court.

▶ Penalty Clause and Confirmatory Deposit

When the contract provides for a penalty clause, the creditor may directly enforce the penalty without having to prove actual damage. In real estate sale and purchase agreements featuring a confirmatory deposit (caparra confirmatoria), breach by the seller triggers the buyer's right to demand twice the amount of the deposit; breach by the buyer entitles the seller to retain the deposit. A swift and effective instrument: it avoids the lengthy evidentiary process of ordinary damages and crystalizes the damage at the time the contract is entered into.

▶ Termination for Excessive Onerousness (risoluzione per eccessiva onerosità)

An exceptional remedy pursuant to art. 1467 c.c.: in contracts with continuous or periodic performance, if the performance of one of the parties has become excessively onerous due to the occurrence of extraordinary and unpredictable events, the party owing such performance may petition for the termination of the contract (risoluzione del contratto). The other party may avoid termination by offering to equitably modify the terms of the contract. Frequently applied in long-term contracts during economic crises, energy shocks, or pandemics, with variable outcomes in case law depending on the foreseeability of the events.

How we work

The six strategies to protect the performing party

Every contractual dispute is unique: the contract, the parties, the breach, and the available evidence determine the optimal strategy. There is no single remedy for all cases of non-performance. Here is how we systematically address each situation.

1. Formal notice to perform (diffida ad adempiere): immediate effect, minimal cost

A formal notice to perform (diffida ad adempiere) pursuant to art. 1454 c.c. is often the first and most effective tool in managing contractual non-performance. A formal notice demanding performance within a time limit of not less than fifteen days, with the declaration that, failing such performance, the contract shall be deemed terminated, produces two immediate effects: it urges the debtor to perform (the prospect of termination is often sufficient to unblock stalemates) and, in the event of failure to perform within the deadline, it terminates the contract by operation of law without the need to apply to court. We oversee the drafting of the notice with close attention to detail: precise identification of the unperformed obligations, adequacy of the deadline, and statement of the consequences. We always serve it via certified e-mail (PEC) or registered mail with return receipt (raccomandata A/R), to ensure proof of date and receipt.

2. Action for termination (azione di risoluzione): dissolution of an unwanted contract

When the formal notice produces no effect and the breach is material, a judicial action for termination (azione di risoluzione) pursuant to art. 1453 c.c. is the instrument to be released from the contract and recover what has already been performed. We build the action by preliminarily verifying three elements: the gravity of the non-performance pursuant to art. 1455 c.c. (not of minor importance), the breach attributable to the counterparty (without causes of exemption pursuant to art. 1218 c.c.), and the current interest in termination (not having already demanded performance, since once an action for performance has been brought it can no longer be converted into termination pursuant to art. 1453, co. 2, c.c.). We systematically seek restitution of what has already been performed and compensation for damages cumulatively with termination.

3. Action for performance (azione di adempimento): obtaining what was promised

When the creditor still desires performance — the delivery of the purchased asset, the conclusion of the final contract following a preliminary agreement (contratto preliminare), the execution of the agreed works — the action for performance pursuant to art. 1453 c.c. is the judicial route. For contracts with deferred real effects (preliminary purchase and sale agreements), a judgment pursuant to art. 2932 c.c. producing the effects of the uncompleted contract is particularly effective: the judge, having established the creditor's right, renders a judgment directly transferring ownership of the property, without the need for the defaulting debtor's consent. This provision has been applied extensively by case law to all obligations to conclude contracts (Cass. Sez. II, n. 9260/2022).

4. Conservatory seizure (sequestro conservativo): protecting assets prior to judgment

In cases where non-performance concerns significant sums and there is a concrete risk that the debtor will dissipate their assets pending trial (sale of real estate, emptying of bank accounts, transfer of business), conservatory seizure (sequestro conservativo) pursuant to art. 671 c.p.c. allows for the preventive freezing of the debtor's assets. The order is issued inaudita altera parte where there is fumus boni iuris and periculum in mora, and converts automatically into an attachment (pignoramento) when the judgment of order becomes final (passaggio in giudicato). In contract matters, the fumus is readily demonstrated by submitting the contract and evidence of the breach; the periculum is proven through concrete elements of the debtor's financial risk.

5. Proof of breach: building an unassailable case file

In contractual matters, evidence is everything. The creditor must prove the contract and the breach; the debtor must prove exact performance or the cause of exemption. We systematically organize the available evidence before undertaking any action: the original contract (in writing or reconstructible in writing), communications between the parties (PEC, email, WhatsApp — recognized by case law as documentary evidence), delivery reports, invoices, technical reports on non-conformities of performance, and witness statements from third parties. We identify the critical points of the evidentiary file and take steps to remedy them prior to filing court pleadings, avoiding surprises during the evidentiary phase that could jeopardize the outcome of the proceedings.

6. Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita): resolving without a trial

Litigation is often the last option, not the first. Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) pursuant to D.L. 132/2014 is mandatory for contractual disputes up to 50,000 euros as a procedural condition precedent to a judicial claim, but it is also a useful tool for higher amounts: it allows parties to reach binding agreements more swiftly than in ordinary proceedings, with the possibility of structuring creative solutions (installment payments, set-offs in kind, modifications to the original contract). We manage negotiations with a clear strategy: we come to the table with a comprehensive evidentiary dossier that demonstrates the solidity of our position, and we use the leverage of potential litigation to obtain settlements favorable to the client.

The procedure step by step

From formal notice to judgment

  • 1

    Contract analysis and assessment of non-performance

    Full review of the contract, identification of unperformed obligations, assessment of the gravity of the breach pursuant to art. 1455 c.c. Collection of evidentiary documentation: contract, communications, invoices, reports, photographs. Assessment of the debtor's asset risk and the appropriateness of preventive interim measures. Choice of remedy: termination (risoluzione), performance (adempimento), defense of non-performance (eccezione di inadempimento).

  • 2

    Formal notice to perform (diffida ad adempiere) pursuant to art. 1454 c.c.

    Service of the formal notice via certified e-mail (PEC) or registered mail with return receipt (raccomandata A/R): precise identification of the unperformed obligations, adequate time limit (not less than 15 days), statement of automatic termination in the event of non-performance within the deadline. Concurrent assessment of an application for conservatory seizure (sequestro conservativo) where there is a risk of dissipation of the debtor's assets.

  • 3

    Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) or mediation (mediazione)

    For claims up to € 50,000: mandatory assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) pursuant to D.L. 132/2014. For higher amounts or for matters subject to mandatory mediation (works contracts [appalto], real estate sales): mediation pursuant to D.Lgs. 28/2010. Strategic management of out-of-court proceedings as negotiating leverage. Possible settlement agreement (accordo transattivo) with enforceable effect.

  • 4

    Initiation of proceedings on the merits

    In the absence of an agreement, filing of the originating pleading (writ of summons [citazione] or petition [ricorso]) with formulation of specific prayers for relief: termination of the contract, order to perform, compensation for actual loss (danno emergente) and lost profits (lucro cessante), any contractual penalty. Request for an interim provisional payment (provvisionale) pursuant to art. 278 c.p.c. if the damage can be partially liquidated immediately.

  • 5

    Evidentiary phase: evidence and court-appointed expert witness (CTU)

    Filing of written pleadings pursuant to art. 171-ter c.p.c. Admission of documentary and witness evidence. Eventual technical court-appointed expert consultancy (consulenza tecnica d'ufficio - CTU) (to verify conformity of performance: defects in works, defects in the property) or accounting consultancy (to liquidate damages: lost revenues, additional costs incurred). The evidentiary phase is when the factual basis of the decision is consolidated.

  • 6

    Judgment and enforcement

    The judgment may declare the termination of the contract (risoluzione del contratto) with an order for restitution and compensation for damages, order specific performance (adempimento specifico) or the monetary equivalent, determine the contractual penalty, or produce proprietary effects pursuant to art. 2932 c.c. for preliminary contracts. In the event of an order to pay, enforcement takes place through attachment (pignoramento) of the debtor's assets.

Results achieved

5 breach of contract cases resolved

Case no. 1

Termination of preliminary contract — Double the deposit and compensation for damages

Subject: preliminary contract for real estate purchase and sale (contratto preliminare di compravendita immobiliare) — Deposit: € 40.000 — Tribunale di Napoli

A couple had entered into a preliminary contract of sale (contratto preliminare di compravendita) for the purchase of an apartment in Naples (Fuorigrotta district) for a total price of € 320.000, paying a confirmatory deposit (caparra confirmatoria) of € 40.000 and agreeing on the execution of the final notarial deed (rogito definitivo) within six months. In the meantime, the seller had received a higher offer from another buyer and had informed our clients that he was no longer available to sell, alleging non-existent bureaucratic problems regarding the property's building permits to justify his withdrawal. The couple risked losing not only the deposit, but also the costs incurred for the application process of the bank mortgage that had already been approved and the moving expenses that had already been booked.

The firm acted on two fronts. Principally, it filed an application (ricorso) pursuant to art. 2932 c.c. for specific performance (esecuzione in forma specifica) of the preliminary contract: the judgment would have automatically transferred ownership of the apartment to our clients without the consent of the defaulting seller. In the alternative (in the event that termination was preferred to the claim for performance), it requested an order for payment of double the confirmatory deposit pursuant to art. 1385, para. 2, c.c. (€ 80.000), plus compensation for further damages: agency commission (euro 9.600), bank loan processing fees (euro 1.800), costs of the canceled move (euro 2.400), and damage from lost bank savings during the waiting period (euro 4.200). During the proceedings, the seller — aware of the strength of the claim under art. 2932 c.c. — proposed a settlement (transazione): he paid double the deposit (€ 80.000) plus € 18.000 by way of damages, for a total of € 98.000, in addition to legal fees. The couple fully recovered the loss suffered and acquired sufficient liquidity for a new purchase.

Settlement € 98.000 — Double deposit + full compensation
Case no. 2

Breach of works contract — Unfinished works and defects in the work

Subject: building renovation contract (appalto) — Contract value: € 95.000 — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

A private individual had entrusted a construction company with the complete renovation of his apartment for a price of € 95.000, paying a deposit of € 38.000. The company had performed approximately 40% of the works and then abandoned the construction site, citing liquidity issues and requesting a further advance not contractually agreed upon. The works performed presented numerous defects: workmanship not in accordance with standard practice (a regola d’arte), misaligned tiling, leaking plumbing system, and unstable false ceilings. The client had attempted to resolve the situation amicably for weeks, but to no avail. Meanwhile, the apartment was uninhabitable and the client was incurring rental costs for temporary accommodation.

The firm drafted a notice to perform (diffida ad adempiere) pursuant to art. 1454 c.c., formally demanding that the company resume the works within fifteen days, with the declaration that, failing this, the contract would be deemed terminated by operation of law. The company did not comply with the notice: the contract was automatically terminated. An ex parte technical expert report (perizia tecnica di parte) was immediately commissioned, documenting the state of the works performed, the discrepancies with respect to the contract, and the defects in the work. The proceedings on the merits saw the admission of a court-appointed technical expert report (consulenza tecnica d’ufficio - CTU) which confirmed the defects and quantified the cost of the works necessary for compliance and the completion of the renovation: € 67.400. The court ordered the company to refund the down payment (€ 38.000), to pay damages for the completion and remediation works (€ 67.400), and to reimburse the costs of temporary rental accommodation incurred for fourteen months (€ 15.400), for a total of € 120.800 plus legal fees.

Order to pay € 120.800 — Down payment + remediation of defects + rental costs
Case no. 3

Defense of non-performance — Supplier sues for unpaid consideration

Subject: management software supply contract — Disputed amount: € 28.600 — Tribunale di Napoli

A food distribution company had been summoned to court by a management software supplier for the payment of € 28.600 in overdue fees. The company had suspended payments, arguing that the software delivered suffered from serious malfunctions that made warehouse management and automated invoicing impossible: errors in inventory synchronization, frequent system crashes during peak operational hours, and lack of integration with the cash register system already in use. The supplier, for its part, maintained that the problems were attributable to the client's hardware and network infrastructure, not to the software.

The defense was built entirely on the defense of non-performance (eccezione di inadempimento) pursuant to art. 1460 c.c.: the company had legitimately refused to pay the fees because the supplier had not delivered functional software as contractually agreed. We documented the supplier's breach through: bug report emails sent from the very first days following installation, technical support tickets opened and left unresolved, formal certified e-mail (PEC) notifications sent over the months, and the report of an independent IT expert certifying the software's incompatibility with the contractually guaranteed technical specifications. We also brought a counterclaim (domanda riconvenzionale) for compensation for the damages suffered by the company: lost revenue from documented operational inefficiencies, and costs of implementing alternative emergency solutions. The court dismissed the supplier's claim (recognizing the legitimacy of the defense of non-performance) and partially granted the counterclaim, awarding € 14.200 by way of damages in favor of the company.

Defense of non-performance upheld — Counterclaim: € 14.200 recovered
Case no. 4

Contractual penalty clause — Delay in delivery of industrial machinery

Subject: contract for the purchase and sale of industrial machinery — Contractual penalty: € 52.000 — Tribunale di Napoli

A manufacturing company had purchased specialized industrial machinery for € 260.000, under a contract providing for delivery and commissioning within ninety days of signing. The contract included a penalty clause (clausola penale) of € 2.000 for each day of delay in delivery, up to a maximum of € 60.000. The supplier had delivered the machinery with a delay of one hundred and thirty days compared to the agreed date, citing difficulties in the component supply chain. The delay made it impossible for the company to meet delivery deadlines with its own customers, resulting in the application of contractual penalties by the latter for a total of € 38.000.

The firm precisely reconstructed the delay: one hundred and twenty-six certified days between the agreed delivery date and the actual one, documented by transport documents and installation reports. The applicable contractual penalty was therefore € 2,000 x 126 days = € 252,000, but was contractually capped at € 60,000. The supplier had objected, pleading that the penalty clause was manifestly excessive compared to the actual damage and requesting its reduction pursuant to art. 1384 c.c. We argued that the penalty had been agreed by commercial parties in equal bargaining positions and that the actual loss suffered by the company (€ 38,000 in penalties paid to clients, plus commercial losses arising from the delay) was fully proportionate to the agreed penalty. The Tribunal ordered the supplier to pay the penalty in its maximum contractually agreed amount of € 60,000, ruling out excessiveness and rejecting the request for reduction pursuant to art. 1384 c.c., plus € 8,000 in legal costs.

Maximum contractual penalty obtained — € 60,000 + legal costs
Case no. 5

Termination for excessive onerousness rejected — Commercial lease agreement defended

Subject matter: 6+6 commercial lease agreement — Annual rent: € 48,000 — Tribunale di Napoli

The owner of a commercial property in the centre of Naples consulted the firm to defend their commercial lease agreement after the tenant — a restaurant chain — had sued the landlord claiming that the contract had terminated for excessive onerousness (risoluzione per eccessiva onerosità) pursuant to art. 1467 c.c., allegedly caused by the Covid-19 pandemic emergency and the consequent reduction in turnover. The tenant had unilaterally stopped paying rent from March 2020, had vacated the premises in September 2021 without formal handover, and was seeking in court a declaration of termination of the lease for excessive onerousness and the return of twelve monthly installments already paid, for a total of € 48,000.

The defence was founded on three main arguments. First: termination for excessive onerousness pursuant to art. 1467 c.c. requires that the supervening event be extraordinary and unpredictable, but the restaurant sector is notoriously exposed to the risks of economic downturns and potential administrative restrictions; furthermore, the pandemic had affected the sector unevenly, and the restaurant chain had maintained delivery and takeaway operations, generating revenue even during the lockdown period. Second: the prevailing case law, including from the Court of Cassation (Cass. Sez. III, no. 12154/2022), excluded that the Covid-19 pandemic constituted grounds for automatic termination of commercial leases for excessive onerousness, at most recognizing the possibility of renegotiating contractual terms. Third: the tenant had not offered an equitable modification of the contract as provided for by art. 1467, para. 3, c.c., but had directly ceased payments and abandoned the premises. The Tribunal dismissed the claim for termination for excessive onerousness, declared the tenant's withdrawal unlawful, and ordered the restaurant chain to pay overdue rent for eighteen months (€ 72,000), plus statutory interest and legal costs.

Termination for excessive onerousness dismissed — Tenant ordered to pay € 72,000
