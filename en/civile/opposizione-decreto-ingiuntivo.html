Artt. 645–653 c.p.c. — Opposition Proceeding

Have you received an injunction order (decreto ingiuntivo)?
You have 40 days. Every day that passes is a right you lose.

An injunction order (decreto ingiuntivo) is not a final judgment: it is an order issued by the court without hearing you, solely based on the creditor's claims. The law grants you the right to lodge an opposition and assert your arguments before the court in ordinary full-merits proceedings. However, the deadline is mandatory: forty days from notification. If you allow this deadline to lapse without acting, the order becomes definitive and immediately enforceable, and the creditor may proceed with attachment (pignoramento) of bank accounts, wages, or real property. Formal defects in service, statute of limitations of the debt, set-off with counterclaims, disputing the very existence of the debt, excessive interest: these are all valid grounds to lodge an opposition. The first step is to verify the date of service and contact a lawyer immediately.

Regulatory framework

The rules governing opposition to injunction orders (decreto ingiuntivo)

Mandatory deadline: 40 days from notification. The time limit for lodging an opposition runs from the date of service of the order, not from the date you receive it. If the order is provisionally enforceable and you do not suspend enforcement within the time limits, the creditor may proceed immediately with attachment (pignoramento). The proceeding for opposition to an injunction order (decreto ingiuntivo) is governed by artt. 645–653 c.p.c. and forms part of the broader summary payment procedure (procedimento monitorio, artt. 633–656 c.p.c.). The Cartabia Reform (D.Lgs. 149/2022) introduced significant amendments to the applicable procedure, making legal assistance crucial right from the outset.

Code of Civil Procedure

Art. 645 c.p.c. — Opposition: 40-Day Time Limit

Art. 645 c.p.c. provides that the opposition must be lodged by summons (atto di citazione) within the mandatory deadline of forty days from service of the order. The time limit is mandatory: non-compliance results in the definitive invalidity of the opposition and the declaration of enforceability of the order pursuant to art. 647 c.p.c. The time limit is calculated from the date of service (not from receipt), but certain causes for suspension may apply: judicial recess (sospensione feriale) under L. 742/1969 (from 1 to 31 August), void or non-existent service. The Cartabia Reform (D.Lgs. 149/2022) amended the rules governing the procedure applicable to the opposition, aligning it with the new ordinary civil proceeding featuring an initial hearing and an exchange of written pre-evidentiary briefs.

Time limit: 40 days from service — Mandatory — Judicial recess: 1-31 August
Code of Civil Procedure

Art. 648 c.p.c. — Provisional Enforcement of the Order

The court may grant provisional enforceability to the injunction order in two circumstances: ex officio, when the claim is based on a bill of exchange, cheque, bank liquidity certificate, or a document executed by a notary; on application by the creditor, in any other case where delay might cause serious harm. Provisional enforcement makes the order immediately actionable: the creditor may serve a formal notice to pay (atto di precetto) and proceed with attachment (pignoramento) without awaiting the conclusion of the opposition proceedings. The declaration of provisional enforceability must be specifically challenged via an application for suspension pursuant to art. 649 c.p.c.; failing this, attachment may take place before the opposition proceedings have even concluded.

Provisional enforcement: ex officio for negotiable instruments — On application in other cases
Code of Civil Procedure

Art. 649 c.p.c. — Suspension of Provisional Enforcement

When the order has been declared provisionally enforceable, the opposing party may request the court to suspend enforcement. The court may suspend it if there are serious grounds, assessed by balancing the prejudice resulting from immediate enforcement (for the debtor) against the prejudice resulting from delay in recovery (for the creditor). The application under art. 649 c.p.c. must be filed concurrently with or immediately following the opposition brief. If enforcement has already commenced (formal notice to pay served, attachment registered), the suspension does not retroactively cancel the enforcement act already carried out, but stays its continuation. The Cartabia Reform eliminated the panel hearing, assigning jurisdiction to the single judge.

Serious grounds required — Application concurrent with the opposition — Decided by the single judge
Code of Civil Procedure

Artt. 633–656 c.p.c. — Summary Payment Proceedings: Defects in the Injunction Phase

Defects in the summary payment proceeding (procedimento monitorio) — the phase in which the order is issued — may be raised within the opposition. In particular: lack of jurisdiction of the court that issued the order (art. 637 c.p.c.: jurisdiction lies with the Court of the place where the defendant has their domicile), lack of admissibility requirements (claim not certain, liquidated, and due; lack of written evidence), formal irregularities in the service of the order. Nullity of service of the order renders the entire injunction proceeding ineffective and may be raised by the court ex officio. Null service reopens the deadlines for lodging an opposition even if the forty days have apparently lapsed.

Nullity of service: may be raised ex officio — Reopens the deadlines for opposition
D.Lgs. 149/2022 — Cartabia Reform

Cartabia Reform: Impact on Opposition Proceedings

The Cartabia Reform (D.Lgs. 149/2022, in force since 28 February 2023) significantly reformed civil proceedings, with direct consequences on oppositions to injunction orders. Among the most relevant changes: the rules governing the ordinary full-merits proceeding, the system of supplementary briefs pursuant to art. 171-ter c.p.c. (replacing the previous briefs pursuant to art. 183 c.p.c.), the timely statement of defence (comparsa di risposta), and the mandatory assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) as a condition of admissibility for claims up to 50,000 euros. Furthermore, for opposition proceedings, the Reform clarified that the court must rule on the application for suspension pursuant to art. 649 c.p.c. at the first hearing, eliminating dilatory adjournments that previously exposed the opposing debtor to the risk of attachment while proceedings were pending.

New briefs under art. 171-ter c.p.c. — Suspension under art. 649: at the first hearing
Available remedies

Which type of opposition applies to your case

There is no single form of opposition: depending on the grounds underlying the challenge, the defence strategy changes radically. An opposition based on formal defects is constructed differently from an opposition on the merits; an application for suspension has its own timing and procedures. Here are the main forms.

▶ Opposition for Formal Defects

Challenge to the regularity of the summary order procedure (procedura monitoria): lack of jurisdiction of the issuing judge, nullity or non-existence of the service of the injunction, failure to comply with appearance deadlines, lack of admissibility requirements for the summary order procedure. If the service is null and void, the forty-day deadline has never started to run: the opposition (opposizione) is always admissible and the order must be declared ineffective. It requires an immediate technical analysis of the service documents.

▶ Opposition for Lack of Debt

Challenge on the merits to the existence or amount of the debt claimed by the applicant: the debt does not exist, has already been paid (in whole or in part), is of an amount different from that ordered, or the performance to which the debt relates was never carried out. In proceedings, the opposing party must prove payment or the grounds for non-liability; the opposing creditor must prove the existence of the debt within the limits of the documentary evidence produced during the summary order stage.

▶ Opposition for Statute of Limitations

The claim is time-barred: the creditor has allowed the limitation period to expire (ordinary: 10 years pursuant to art. 2946 c.c.; short: 5 years for professional claims pursuant to art. 2949 c.c.; 3 years for insurance policies; 1 year for carriers and hoteliers) without interrupting acts. The statute of limitations (prescrizione) is an affirmative defense that must be expressly raised by the opposing party in the writ of summons for opposition (citazione di opposizione): if it is not raised in a timely manner, it can no longer be asserted. The analysis of the dates of accrual of the claim and of the interrupting acts produced is an essential preliminary step.

▶ Provisional Suspension of Enforceability (Art. 649)

A fundamental interim measure within the proceedings when the order is provisionally enforceable. The petition for suspension must be filed concurrently with the opposition or immediately thereafter. The judge assesses the prima facie merits (fumus boni iuris) of the opposition and the risk of prejudice from delay (periculum in mora) arising from immediate enforcement (loss of employment due to wage garnishment, loss of home due to real estate foreclosure). The suspension halts the attachment pending the judgment on the merits.

▶ Late Opposition (Art. 650 c.p.c.)

When the forty days have expired but the opposing party was not aware of the order due to irregularities in service, fortuitous event, or force majeure, art. 650 c.p.c. permits a late opposition (opposizione tardiva). The time limit for late opposition runs from the moment the opposing party obtained actual knowledge of the order, and in any case no later than six months from the filing of the service in the judge's court registry (cancelleria). Late opposition is an extraordinary remedy that requires rigorous proof of lack of knowledge of the order within the ordinary time limit.

▶ Stay of Enforcement on Appeal

When the court of first instance rejects the petition for suspension pursuant to art. 649 c.p.c. and the debtor has already filed an opposition, it is possible to lodge an appeal against the decree of dismissal and concurrently request a stay of execution (inibitoria) from the Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello) pursuant to art. 351 c.p.c. The Court of Appeal may suspend enforcement by urgent order pending the decision on the appeal. A residual but fundamental instrument in cases of unjustified rejection of the suspension at first instance.

How we work

The six strategies for an effective opposition

A successful opposition to an injunction (decreto ingiuntivo) is not built in a few minutes: it requires an in-depth technical analysis of the service document, of the documents produced by the creditor, and of the possible grounds for opposition. Here is the method we follow systematically.

1. Immediate verification of deadlines: 40 days will not wait

The first step we take is the precise verification of the deadline for lodging an opposition: date of service (not of receipt), any court recess period (sospensione feriale, August 1°–31), and verification of the formal regularity of the service itself. Service carried out by deposit in the court registry (service due to untraceability) causes the time limits to run from a different date compared to personal service or service to a family member. If the service is null and void (e.g.: deposit in a place other than that of residence, missing proof of service, service on an unauthorized individual), the time limit does not run at all and the opposition is always timely. This check is always the first step, even when the client contacts us just a few days before the apparent deadline.

2. Procedural objections: defects that overturn the entire order

Procedural objections concern the regularity of the summary order procedure: lack of territorial or subject-matter jurisdiction of the issuing judge (art. 637 c.p.c.), lack of standing to sue of the applicant (creditor acting without holding the claim), lack of admissibility prerequisites for the summary order procedure (debt not certain, not quantified, not due and payable), non-existence or nullity of the service. These defects, if well-founded, lead to the declaration of ineffectiveness of the entire order, regardless of the existence of the claim on the merits. They are often combined with substantive objections, building a multi-layered defense.

3. Substantive objections: statute of limitations, set-off, breach of contract

Objections on the merits attack the existence, validity, or amount of the ordered debt. The most frequent: statute of limitations of the claim (an objection to be raised expressly in the writ of summons for opposition, never ex officio); full or partial payment of the debt (to be proven with receipts, bank statements, wire transfers); set-off with a counterclaim held by the opposing party against the creditor (art. 1241 c.c.); challenge to the amount of the debt due to incorrect calculation of interest, application of compound interest (anatocismo) or usurious rates, or unauthorized charges. Each objection requires the specific submission of supporting documentation.

4. Petition for suspension pursuant to art. 649 c.p.c.: halting the attachment

When the order is provisionally enforceable, the absolute priority is to obtain a suspension of enforcement to prevent the creditor from proceeding with attachment pending the judgment on the merits. The petition must be filed concurrently with the writ of opposition and must illustrate the serious grounds: the prima facie merits (fumus boni iuris) of the opposition (plausibility of the grounds for opposition) and the risk of irreparable harm (periculum in mora) resulting from immediate enforcement (such as the loss of one's home or sole source of wage income). We draft the petition with the utmost argumentative rigor, documenting both requirements with precision, to maximize the likelihood of suspension at the first hearing.

5. Counterclaim: turning defense into offense

In many cases, the ordered debtor holds, in turn, a claim against the petitioning creditor: damages arising from breach of contract, sums already paid as an advance for work not performed, or compensation for unlawful pre-contractual or contractual conduct. In opposition proceedings, it is possible to file a counterclaim (domanda riconvenzionale) that also introduces the opposing party's counterclaim into the proceedings (art. 36 c.p.c.). If the counterclaim exceeds the ordered claim, the counterclaim may lead not only to the rejection of the order but also to an order directing the creditor to pay the difference to the opposing party, radically altering the outcome of the proceedings.

6. Mediation and settlement agreement: the fastest path

In many cases, an opposition to an injunction is an opportunity to renegotiate the entire contractual relationship between the parties and reach a settlement agreement that reduces the debt, reschedules its payment, or provides for a set-off with obligations of a different nature. Mediation (mediazione) is mandatory in the matters specified by D.Lgs. 28/2010 (including contractual disputes involving amounts exceeding 50,000 euros); for lower amounts, assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) is a condition of admissibility. We manage mediation as a strategic instrument, not as a formal procedural step: the procedural pressure arising from the opposition is often the leverage that leads the creditor to reduce its claims.

The procedure step by step

From the injunction to the opposition judgment

  • 1

    Urgent analysis of the order and verification of deadlines

    Reading of the injunction, verification of the service date, calculation of the forty-two day time limit (40 days + 2 for any judicial recess suspension if served close to August). Verification of the formal regularity of service. Analysis of the documentation attached to the summary application (ricorso monitorio). Assessment of viable grounds of opposition (procedural and substantive).

  • 2

    Filing of the writ of summons in opposition

    Drafting and filing of the writ of summons in opposition (atto di citazione in opposizione) within the mandatory deadline. The writ must contain all grounds of opposition (procedural and substantive), substantive defences, any counterclaim, and the petition for stay under art. 649 c.p.c. if the injunction is provisionally enforceable. Under the new procedure (post-Cartabia Reform), the writ of summons is filed electronically.

  • 3

    First hearing: stay and organisation of the proceedings

    At the first hearing, the judge decides on the application for a stay under art. 649 c.p.c. and organises the substantive proceedings (admission of evidence, scheduling of subsequent hearings). If the stay is granted, any enforcement already initiated is halted. If it is denied, it is possible to apply to the Court of Appeal for an injunction order under art. 351 c.p.c.

  • 4

    Supplemental pleadings under art. 171-ter c.p.c.

    After the first hearing, the parties file three written pleadings: the first to amend claims and objections, the second to reply to the other party's arguments and submit evidentiary requests, the third to specify the evidentiary issues. This stage is crucial: objections not raised in the pleadings may be declared time-barred. We handle every pleading with the utmost technical care.

  • 5

    Evidentiary phase and gathering of evidence

    Evidentiary hearing for the admission of evidence: documentary evidence, witness evidence, any accounting or technical court-appointed expert assessment (CTU). In many oppositions, documentary evidence is sufficient (settled payments, receipts, bank statements, contracts). The evidentiary phase is when the factual basis of the judgment is built.

  • 6

    Judgment: revocation or confirmation of the order for payment

    The judge decides by judgment which may revoke the order for payment (if the opposition is well-founded) or confirm it (if the opposition is dismissed). In the event of partial revocation, the debt is reduced to the amount actually owed. The judgment may be appealed within thirty days of service, or six months from filing with the court registry. In the event of an adverse ruling, the advisability of an appeal is always evaluated.

Results achieved

5 oppositions to an injunction order (opposizione a decreto ingiuntivo) resolved

Case no. 1

Opposition due to nullity of service — Order revoked for procedural defect

Injunction amount: € 48,700 — Sector: commercial supplies — Tribunale di Napoli

A craftsman had received a phone call from his accountant informing him that a creditor had obtained an injunction order (decreto ingiuntivo) for € 48,700 regarding material supplies never ordered by the client, and that the order had been declared enforceable. The craftsman was certain he had never received service of the original order, and the forty-day time limit appeared to have well and truly expired. During the initial consultation, we carefully examined the certificate of service (relata di notifica): it emerged that the order had been served by filing with the court registry due to the untraceability of the recipient, but the bailiff (ufficiale giudiziario) had not properly verified the domicile and had recorded an incorrect address as the registered office of the firm — commercial premises transferred three years earlier, without any verification having been carried out with the Chamber of Commerce (Camera di Commercio).

We challenged the service as null and void due to the non-existence of the place of delivery, with the consequent legal non-existence of the service itself and the non-commencement of the forty-day time limit. Having filed the late opposition (opposizione tardiva) pursuant to art. 650 c.p.c. (the craftsman having acquired knowledge of the order only through communication from his accountant), the judge upheld the objection of nullity of service, declared the opposition timely, and opened the proceedings on the merits. On the merits, we demonstrated that the claim asserted by the supplier referred to goods delivered to a different company with a similar name: the craftsman had never ordered or received the goods in question. The Court revoked the injunction order (decreto ingiuntivo), ordering the creditor to pay the costs of both phases of the proceedings (summary and opposition).

Order revoked due to nullity of service — Claim declared non-existent
Case no. 2

Urgent stay of execution pursuant to art. 649 and successful opposition — Private loan not due

Injunction amount: € 31,500 — Sector: private relationship — Tribunale di Torre Annunziata

A woman had received a provisionally enforceable order for payment (decreto ingiuntivo provvisoriamente esecutivo) for € 31,500, filed by a former partner who claimed to have loaned her that sum in cash during their relationship. The order had been issued with provisional enforcement on the basis of a private agreement (scrittura privata) produced by the petitioner, which the woman did not recall ever signing. The creditor had already served the notice to pay (atto di precetto) and was preparing to proceed with the attachment (pignoramento) of her bank account into which her salary as a public employee was credited. The woman contacted the firm three days before the deadline for filing an opposition expired.

We acted on two fronts in parallel. First, an extremely urgent action: filing the statement of opposition (atto di opposizione) together with an application for suspension pursuant to art. 649 c.p.c., in which we documented both the likelihood of success on the merits (fumus boni iuris)—the private agreement showed suspicious graphological features and the funds had never passed through bank accounts—and the risk of irreparable harm in delay (periculum in mora), namely the attachment of the client’s sole source of income. The judge suspended provisional enforcement at the first hearing. Second action: in the proceedings on the merits, we obtained court approval for an expert handwriting analysis (consulenza tecnica grafologica) on the private agreement, which concluded that our client’s signature was forged. The Court (Tribunale) revoked the order and forwarded a copy of the judgment to the Public Prosecutor’s Office (Procura della Repubblica) for appropriate assessment regarding the crime of forgery of a private document.

Suspension obtained at the first hearing — Order revoked — Forgery detected
Case no. 3

Opposition based on the five-year statute of limitations — Belated professional fee claim

Amount of the order for payment: € 22,800 — Sector: professional fees — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

A building contractor had received an order for payment (decreto ingiuntivo) for € 22,800, filed by an engineer who claimed to have performed design and site supervision services between 2015 and 2017. The order had been served in March 2024, approximately seven years after the services were completed. The contractor recalled having had a working relationship with the professional, but was convinced he had paid a substantial part of the invoices: some by bank transfer, others in cash as an advance payment. The question was whether the claim was indeed time-barred by the statute of limitations and whether evidence existed of the payments made.

The analysis immediately highlighted the issue of limitation periods: claims for fees of engineers and architects are barred by a five-year statute of limitations pursuant to art. 2949 c.c. (professional fees). The last service dated back to late 2017; the order had been served in 2024: seven years later, well beyond the five-year term. We verified whether the creditor had produced any acts interrupting the statute of limitations: no registered letter, no certified e-mail (PEC), no service of judicial acts. The opposition was founded solely on the objection of the five-year statute of limitations pursuant to art. 2949 c.c., documented with precise chronological records. In parallel, the contractor produced bank statements proving three bank transfers as partial payments for a total of € 14,200. The Court (Tribunale) upheld the statute of limitations defense, revoked the order for payment in its entirety, and ordered the professional to pay the costs of the proceedings.

Order revoked due to statute of limitations — € 22,800 not due
Case no. 4

Opposition with counterclaim — From judgment debtor to creditor

Amount of the order for payment: € 15,200 — Counterclaim: € 38,600 — Tribunale di Napoli

A decorative craftsman had received an order for payment (decreto ingiuntivo) for € 15,200, filed by a client who claimed that the decoration work carried out in the apartment did not conform to the contract, and that he was entitled to a refund of the advance payments made. In reality, the works had been completed according to the agreed design, but the client had rejected them citing pretextual aesthetic discrepancies in order to avoid paying the second installment of the contract price (precisely € 15,200). In addition to the disputed debt, the client had in the meantime entrusted another craftsman with the finishing works that the original craftsman was supposed to carry out as part of the overall contract, causing him damage for loss of profit.

The strategy involved an opposition structured on two levels. On the merits of the order for payment: the works performed conformed to the design approved in writing, as demonstrated by photographs taken during execution and by the technical report of an expert witness for the party. The counterclaim (domanda riconvenzionale) brought the craftsman’s counter-credit into the proceedings: € 15,200 for the balance of the unpaid contract price, plus € 23,400 as damages for early termination of the contract (lost profits on finishing works not executed for reasons attributable to the client). Having ordered a court-appointed technical expert appraisal (CTU) which confirmed the conformity of the works, the Court (Tribunale) dismissed the client’s petition for an order for payment, upheld the craftsman’s counterclaim, and ordered the client to pay € 38,600 plus legal costs. The judgment debtor became the judgment creditor.

Order revoked — Client ordered to pay € 38,600 on counterclaim
Case no. 5

Opposition by a company — Reduction of bank debt due to compound interest

Amount of the order for payment: € 187,000 — Sector: bank credit — Tribunale di Napoli

A small transport business had received an order for payment (decreto ingiuntivo) from a bank for € 187,000, as the debit balance of a commercial current account with a credit facility. The company was in financial difficulty, but the business owner was convinced that the balance charged by the bank was higher than what was actually due as a result of the application of compound interest (anatocismo), overdraft fees not agreed in the contract, and variable rates applied without the mandatory periodic notices. The order was provisionally enforceable and the bank had already served the notice to pay (atto di precetto); the risk was the attachment (pignoramento) of the company’s vehicles and vehicle fleet.

We filed the statement of opposition and immediately requested a suspension pursuant to art. 649 c.p.c., which was granted by the judge at the first hearing on the grounds of likelihood of success arising from the technical challenge to the rates applied. During the proceedings, a court-appointed accounting expert appraisal (CTU) was admitted, which analyzed the entire bank statement from the inception of the credit facility. The CTU determined: quarterly capitalization of debit interest in breach of the prohibition of compound interest (art. 1283 c.c. and CICR resolution of 2000, in the period prior to the 2014 regulatory amendment); application of overdraft fees not agreed contractually for € 18,700; and variable rates applied in excess of the contractual terms due to a calculation error regarding the Euribor benchmark. The balance adjusted by the CTU was € 112,400, compared to the € 187,000 claimed. The Court (Tribunale) revoked the order for the excess amount, upholding it for € 112,400 and ordering the bank to pay costs in proportion to its unsuccessful claims. The company was able to agree on a repayment plan with the bank for the adjusted balance, avoiding the attachment of its corporate vehicles.

Debt reduced from € 187,000 to € 112,400 — Savings of € 74,600
