Regulatory framework

The law on your side

Main reference provision

Artt. 1490–1497 c.c. — Warranty for defects in the sold item

The Italian Civil Code imposes on the seller a statutory warranty for defects that render the asset unfit for its intended use or that significantly diminish its value. The warranty applies even if the seller was unaware of the defects, unless expressly agreed otherwise. A purchaser who discovers latent defects has two alternative remedies: the redhibitory action (azione redibitoria - termination of the contract and refund of the price) and the estimatory action or actio quanti minoris (azione estimatoria - proportional reduction of the price). The limitation period (prescrizione) is one year from handover; notice of the defect must be given within eight days of discovery, upon pain of forfeiture (decadenza).

Limitation period (prescrizione): 1 year from handover — Notice of defect (denuncia): 8 days from discovery
  • Artt. 1490–1497 c.c.
  • Art. 1669 c.c. (10 years)
  • Art. 1667 c.c. (works contract - appalto)
  • Art. 1495 c.c. (notice of defect - denuncia)
  • D.Lgs. 122/2005

Art. 1669 c.c. provides even stronger protection for major defects and total or partial collapse of the building: the builder is liable for ten years from the completion of the work for total or partial collapse and for defects that compromise structural stability and safety. This liability is mandatory and cannot be waived, and it also extends to subsequent purchasers. Furthermore, D.Lgs. 122/2005 protects those who purchase a building under construction from a developer, requiring a bank guarantee (fideiussione bancaria) and a ten-year post-construction insurance policy at the builder's expense.

Art. 1667 c.c., in works contracts (appalto), requires the principal to give notice of defects within sixty days of discovery; the warranty is time-barred after two years from the acceptance of the work. The distinction between sale and works contract is of decisive importance for the litigation strategy: determining the correct classification of the contract is the first step of the legal defence analysis.

Case Law and Scenarios

The most frequent types

Each category of defect is subject to a different legal regime, different notice deadlines, and specific litigation strategies. Correct qualification is the prerequisite for any effective legal protection.

🔒

Latent Defects

Defects not detectable with ordinary diligence at the time of the notarial deed of sale (rogito): dampness, structural mould, non-compliant systems, latent water infiltration. Regime: Arts. 1490-1495 c.c. Notice within 8 days of discovery; one-year limitation period.

🏠

Structural Defects

Cracks in load-bearing walls, foundation subsidence, partial collapses, defects compromising the building's stability. Privileged regime: art. 1669 c.c. — mandatory ten-year liability of the builder. 10-year limitation period, notice within 1 year of discovery.

📄

Urban Planning and Building Non-Conformities

Unremedied building irregularities, discrepancies between cadastral floor plans and actual conditions, lack of building permit (permesso di costruire), unfiled variations. They can render the property unmarketable or severely affect its value. Actionable as delivery of a different good (aliud pro alio) or as defects pursuant to art. 1490 c.c.

💧

Water Infiltration

Infiltration from the roof, terraces, common parts, flat roofs. Relevant both in relations between seller and buyer (Arts. 1490 et seq. c.c.) and in condominium relations (art. 1117 c.c. and art. 2051 c.c. — liability for custody). Often requires a preliminary technical appraisal (accertamento tecnico preventivo).

🛠

Defects in Works Contracts

Non-conformities and defects in the works entrusted to the contractor: substandard materials, defective workmanship, failure to comply with specifications. Regime pursuant to art. 1667 c.c. — notice within 60 days, warranty for 2 years from acceptance. Strict liability for intentional latent defects.

⚠

Lack of Certifications

Absence or falsity of the certificate of habitability/fitness for use (certificato di agibilità), system conformity certification (L. 37/2008), energy performance certificate (attestato di prestazione energetica - APE), seismic compliance. They may constitute cases of delivery of a different good (aliud pro alio) allowing termination of the contract irrespective of the ordinary warranty terms.

How we protect your rights

Our strategies

Effective legal protection regarding property defects is built on six pillars: from timely notification to technical evidence, up to the choice of the most advantageous remedy for the client.

01

Timely Notice (8 days / 60 days)

The first defensive act is the most urgent: written notice to the seller or contractor within the mandatory statutory deadlines. We take care of drafting a comprehensive technical and legal notice describing the defects accurately and preserving all client rights, avoiding forfeitures that would deprive even the most grounded case of protection.

02

Action for Termination (Azione Redibitoria)

When defects are serious and make the property unfit for use, the action for termination pursuant to art. 1492 c.c. allows the client to obtain contract termination, refund of the purchase price, reimbursement of expenses incurred, and compensation for damages if the seller acted in bad faith. It is the most decisive remedy when the purchase should never have taken place under those conditions.

03

Action for Price Reduction (Azione Estimatoria)

The action for price reduction (quanti minoris) pursuant to art. 1492 c.c. allows the client to retain ownership of the property while obtaining a price reduction proportional to the extent of the defect. It is the ideal choice when the client wishes to keep the property, but it is fair that the price reflects the real conditions at the time of sale.

04

Ten-Year Liability of the Builder

Art. 1669 c.c. is a powerful and often underutilised instrument: the builder remains liable for ten years for serious defects, including towards subsequent purchasers. The Court of Cassation (Corte di Cassazione) has extended this protection beyond direct ownership, further strengthening the position of the buyer who discovers structural defects even years after the notarial deed (rogito).

05

Preliminary Technical Assessment pursuant to art. 696-bis c.p.c.

The preliminary technical appraisal during proceedings (accertamento tecnico preventivo) is the instrument that secures evidence before defects can be concealed or the property is altered. The court-appointed expert witness (CTU) ascertains and documents the state of the premises with full evidentiary value. It often leads to conciliation prior to trial, saving time and costs.

06

Post-Completion Guarantee (D.Lgs. 122/2005)

For purchases from builders of properties to be constructed or under construction, D.Lgs. 122/2005 imposes a bank guarantee covering amounts paid and a ten-year insurance policy for construction defects. We verify compliance with these obligations and activate the guarantees on behalf of our client.

Case Law and Scenarios

Five resolved cases

Every case is different. These examples show how the same technical competence produces concrete results in various situations, from the timely notice through to trial.

Case Study — 01

Latent moisture in a newly renovated apartment

Real estate purchase — Naples, Vomero District — Amount in dispute: 38,000 euros

A client had purchased an apartment presented as “fully renovated” in the Vomero neighbourhood. A few months after the notarial deed of sale (rogito), the walls of the living room and master bedroom began to show evident patches of rising damp, which subsequently spread to wall coverings, parquet floors, and furnishings. The property had appeared dry and devoid of visible defects at the time of viewings and the signing of the definitive contract.

The firm intervened immediately: we drafted a written notice of defects to the seller within five days of discovery, preserving against forfeiture under art. 1495 c.c., and simultaneously filed a petition (ricorso) for preventive technical assessment (accertamento tecnico preventivo — ATP) pursuant to art. 696-bis c.p.c. The court-appointed expert (CTU) appointed by the Tribunal established the presence of a dilapidated plumbing system, concealed beneath the new finishes, which caused chronic water infiltration. The expert quantified the damages at 38,000 euros, encompassing remediation works, complete overhaul of the systems, replacement of furnishings, and property devaluation. Faced with the unassailable technical report, the seller accepted a settlement agreement (transazione) granting the client full compensation for the damage, including legal and expert fees, without the need for court proceedings.

✓ Settlement agreement: 38,000 euros awarded
Case Study — 02

Structural defect in a newly constructed building

Contractor liability pursuant to art. 1669 c.c. — Caserta — Amount in dispute: 95,000 euros

A family had purchased an apartment in a newly constructed condominium building. Three years after the notarial deed of sale (rogito), during routine maintenance works, a technician detected through-cracks in the ground floor load-bearing columns, an indication of differential settlement of the foundations. The structural analysis revealed a severe error in design and execution: the concrete used exhibited a water/cement ratio exceeding regulatory limits, resulting in a reduction of characteristic strength below design specifications.

The procedural strategy centred on art. 1669 c.c.: the contractor's ten-year liability for defects that compromise the stability of the building is mandatory (inderogabile) and prevails over any contractual waiver clause. We filed a petition for preventive technical assessment (ATP) involving not only the contractor but also the construction supervisor (direttore dei lavori) and the testing engineer (collaudatore), establishing the joint and several liability of all parties. The proceedings concluded with a judgment jointly and severally ordering the contractor and the construction supervisor to pay 95,000 euros for structural consolidation, remediation of the affected apartments, and compensation for damages due to loss of commercial value.

✓ Judgment: 95,000 euros + costs
Case Study — 03

Undeclared cadastral and urban planning non-compliance

Aliud pro alio and defects pursuant to art. 1490 c.c. — Napoli Nord — Termination of contract

A client had purchased commercial premises by notarial deed (rogito notarile). Following the execution, in the context of an application for renovation works, a technician discovered that the cadastral plan attached to the deed did not correspond to the actual state of the property: approximately 40 square metres had been constructed in non-compliance with the building permit, without any planning regularisation (sanatoria). Part of the acquired space was unlawful (abusiva), and the premises could not obtain commercial occupancy certification (agibilità) in their current state. The client had paid a price calculated on an authorised surface area that did not in fact exist.

We framed the case as the delivery of aliud pro alio, a legal category developed by the jurisprudence of the Court of Cassation (Corte di Cassazione) for cases where the asset delivered is ontologically different from the one agreed upon. In this event, ordinary forfeiture time-limits do not apply, and the action for termination of the contract can be brought within the ordinary ten-year prescription period. The Tribunal granted the claim, ruling the termination of the contract and ordering the seller to refund the purchase price, reimburse notarial expenses, and provide full compensation for damages, including lost profits for the period during which the premises remained unusable.

✓ Contract termination + full refund + damages
Case Study — 04

Water infiltration from common condominium parts

Art. 2051 c.c. — liability for custody — Napoli — Condominium and seller

A fifth-floor apartment suffered ceiling infiltration originating from the condominium flat roof (lastrico solare), which was not designated for exclusive use. The owner had unsuccessfully demanded action from the condominium for over a year, receiving only unfulfilled promises of intervention. The situation had caused plaster to detach, mould to form on furniture and walls, and the temporary uninhabitable state of a bedroom. Restoration costs were estimated at over 18,000 euros.

The strategy operated on two parallel fronts. Against the condominium, we invoked liability for custody under art. 2051 c.c.: the condominium is the custodian of the common parts and is liable for damages caused to individual unit owners regardless of fault, barring an unforeseeable event or force majeure (caso fortuito), which was absent in this case. Against the original seller, we examined prior deeds of conveyance to verify whether the infiltration pre-existed the transfer. The dual legal action made it possible to obtain a joint and several order against both the condominium — on grounds of custodial liability — and the seller — under the warranty pursuant to art. 1490 c.c. — with full recovery of remediation expenses and compensation for temporary loss of use.

✓ Joint and several order against condominium + seller: damage fully compensated
Case Study — 05

Defects in works under complex renovation contract

Art. 1667 c.c. — private works contract (appalto) — Napoli — Amount: 62,000 euros

A client had engaged a construction company to carry out the complete renovation of a historic apartment in the centre of Naples for a price of 120,000 euros. The completed work presented severe defects: out-of-plumb flooring, plaster detaching only months after application, door and window frames installed without ensuring watertightness, and an electrical system executed without complying with CEI standards and lacking a certificate of conformity. The contractor refused to intervene, claiming that the issues were attributable to the age of the building.

We promptly drafted a formal notice of defects pursuant to art. 1667 c.c. within sixty days of discovery, simultaneously filing a petition for preventive technical assessment (ATP) to establish the state of the premises before any intervention by the contractor. The court-appointed expert (CTU) dismantled the contractor's defence: the works had been executed using inadequate materials and contrary to standard trade practices (regole dell’arte), regardless of the building's age. The expert appraisal estimated the remediation costs at 62,000 euros. In light of this valuation, the Tribunal ordered the company to pay full compensation, also awarding damages for the inability to reside in the property during the remediation works.

✓ Judgment: 62,000 euros + damages for loss of use
