Artt. 633–656 c.p.c. — Enforcement Proceedings

Do you have an unpaid debt?
Every day of delay is one less day to recover it.

An uncollected debt is not merely a financial loss: it is a breach of an obligation that the law entitles you to enforce with the coercive power of the State. Unpaid invoices, unpaid professional services, defaulted loans, overdue rent: there are precise and rapid procedural instruments to recover what is owed to you. An order for payment (decreto ingiuntivo), attachment, and conservative attachment are not remote options: they are legal rights. Knowing how and when to use them is what distinguishes those who recover their debt from those who lose it due to statutory limitation or debtor inaction.

Regulatory framework

The provisions protecting your claim

Judicial debt collection is founded on a comprehensive regulatory framework that allows creditors, in the presence of a certain, liquidated, and enforceable debt, to obtain an enforceable title and proceed with enforcement proceedings against the debtor's assets. Accurate knowledge of these instruments — and of the time limits within which they can be exercised — forms the basis of every effective recovery action.

Code of Civil Procedure

Artt. 633–656 c.p.c. — Summary Payment Order Proceedings (Procedimento per Decreto Ingiuntivo)

The order for payment (decreto ingiuntivo) is the primary instrument for recovering sums of money, quantities of fungible goods, or specific movable property. The judge issues the order inaudita altera parte on the basis of documentation produced by the creditor (invoices, contracts, bank statements, fee notes). Where privileged written evidence is available (cheques, promissory notes, bank statements), the order is issued with provisional enforceability. The debtor has 40 days to file an opposition pursuant to art. 645 c.p.c.; failing this, the order becomes final and enforceable.

Required proof: written evidence of the debt — Debtor's opposition deadline: 40 days
Code of Civil Procedure

Artt. 474–632 c.p.c. — Forced Enforcement (Espropriazione Forzata)

Once an enforceable title is obtained (final order for payment, judgment, notarial deed), the creditor may proceed with forced enforcement on the debtor's assets. The procedural code governs three main forms: attachment of movable property (physical goods), attachment of immovable property (registered real estate), and third-party attachment (claims of the debtor against third parties, such as employers or banking institutions). Enforcement requires the service of a notice to comply (atto di precetto) at least ten days prior to the commencement of the attachment.

Enforceable title required — Notice to comply (precetto): at least 10 days before attachment
Code of Civil Procedure

Art. 543 c.p.c. — Third-Party Attachment (Bank Account and Salary)

Third-party attachment is the most incisive instrument for debt recovery, as it directly targets the debtor's liquidity: the bank account balance, wages, or pension paid by the employer or social security entity. The garnishee third party (bank or employer) is required to make the statutory declaration under art. 547 c.p.c. and, absent dispute, pay the indicated sums over to the creditor. For salaries and pensions, the law establishes limits of attachability (one-fifth of the net monthly amount for ordinary debts; different ratios for maintenance or tax debts).

Current account: entire balance attachable — Salary/pension: limit of 1/5 of the net amount
Civil Code

Art. 2740 c.c. — General Patrimonial Liability

The general principle of general patrimonial guarantee: the debtor is liable for the performance of obligations with all of their present and future assets. This article forms the basis of enforcement action: all assets registered in the debtor's name at the time of enforcement may be seized, with the sole exception of assets expressly declared non-attachable by law. The rule also justifies conservatory actions (conservatory seizure, clawback action / revocatory action) aimed at preserving the patrimonial guarantee before the debtor dissipates it through fraudulent disposal transactions.

Cornerstone principle: all present and future assets of the debtor serve as guarantee
D.L. 132/2014 converted into L. 162/2014

Assisted Negotiation for Debt Recovery

Law Decree no. 132 of 2014, converted into Law no. 162 of 2014, introduced mandatory assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) for disputes concerning the payment of sums of money up to EUR 50,000, as a condition of admissibility (condizione di procedibilità) for bringing a court action. The procedure makes it possible to reach binding out-of-court settlements in shorter timeframes compared to ordinary proceedings, with the possibility of crystallising the agreement into an enforceable title (titolo esecutivo). In many cases, merely serving the invitation to assisted negotiation induces the debtor to perform voluntarily, avoiding the costs of court litigation.

Mandatory for claims up to € 50,000 — Agreement reached: enforceable title
Available remedies

Which procedure is right for your case

There is no single solution for all debts. The choice of instrument depends on the amount, the presence of written documentation, the debtor's financial and asset status, and the urgency of the recovery. Here are the procedures we use.

▶ Injunction Order (Decreto Ingiuntivo)

The summary debt collection procedure (procedimento monitorio) par excellence. On the basis of documentary evidence (invoices, contracts, bank statements, correspondence), the judge issues an order for payment (ingiunzione di pagamento) inaudita altera parte (ex parte). Average timeframes: 2–6 weeks. With immediate provisional enforceability in the presence of bills of exchange or bank documents. The instrument of choice for commercial, professional, and condominium debts.

▶ Notice to Comply (Atto di Precetto)

A formal notice served on the debtor, who is already subject to an enforceable title, requiring compliance within a period of not less than 10 days, failing which enforcement proceedings will commence. It also serves as a formal demand: often the service of the notice to comply (precetto) is sufficient to induce voluntary payment, avoiding the costs of actual enforcement.

▶ Attachment of Movable Property (Pignoramento Mobiliare)

The court bailiff (ufficiale giudiziario) attends the debtor's residence or registered office and places seals on seizable movable assets (vehicles, machinery, equipment, company furnishings). The assets are subsequently sold at auction with distribution of the proceeds. A procedure less effective for debtors with no assets of value; optimal for commercial businesses equipped with machinery or inventory.

▶ Attachment of Immovable Property (Pignoramento Immobiliare)

The most complex procedure, but also the most secure for substantial claims: real estate registered in the debtor's name is attached. The attachment is recorded in the land registry, appraised by a court-appointed expert (CTU), and sold via electronic auction. Timeframes are longer (2–5 years on average), but it allows the recovery of even very high debts against solid assets.

▶ Third-Party Attachment (Bank Accounts & Salary / Pignoramento presso Terzi)

The most direct and immediate instrument: claims that the debtor holds against third parties are attached. The bank account balance is frozen and transferred to the creditor; salary or pension is deducted up to one-fifth by the employer or by INPS and paid monthly until satisfaction of the debt. A rapid procedure with a high success rate.

▶ Conservatory Seizure (Sequestro Conservativo)

An interim precautionary measure available pre-action (ante causam) or during proceedings: it allows the debtor's assets to be frozen in advance to prevent their dissipation through disposal transactions (sales, gifts, transfers to family members). It is obtained ex parte in the presence of a prima facie case (fumus boni iuris) and risk of irreparable harm in delay (periculum in mora). Automatically converted into an attachment (pignoramento) upon the final judgment of condemnation becoming res judicata.

How we work

The six recovery strategies

An effective debt collection action is not limited to serving a notice to comply (atto di precetto). It requires preliminary asset investigations, the choice of the most suitable procedural instrument, and the ability to anticipate the debtor's defensive steps. Here are the six strategies we systematically employ.

1. Collection and strengthening of evidence of the claim

Prior to any court action, we organise all documentation supporting the claim: contracts, purchase orders, delivery notes, invoices, correspondence, bank statements, partial payment receipts. Well-organised documentation is essential to obtain an order for payment with provisional enforceability and to withstand any opposition by the debtor. We also assess the probative value of communications via email, WhatsApp, and certified email (PEC), now recognised as written evidence by established case law (Cass. Sez. VI, n. 14818/2023).

2. Provisionally enforceable injunction order (Decreto ingiuntivo provvisoriamente esecutivo)

Where the documentation permits, we systematically request provisional enforceability pursuant to art. 642 c.p.c. — particularly where cheques, promissory notes, or authenticated private deeds are present — so as to proceed immediately with attachment without having to wait the forty days granted to the debtor to file opposition. Where the claim is founded on negotiable instruments (cheques or promissory notes), the order is issued with provisional enforceability as of right. This substantially reduces actual recovery times.

3. Targeted attachment of the most reachable asset

Not all attachments are equal. The choice of asset to attach determines the effectiveness of the entire procedure. We prioritise third-party attachment on bank accounts or salaries due to its speed and low procedural cost. Real estate attachment is initiated for substantial claims against debtors with consolidated property holdings. Movable attachment of motor vehicles, via the public vehicle register (PRA), is effective against debtors without bank liquidity but with vehicles registered in their name. The attachment strategy is designed on the basis of preliminary asset investigations.

4. Preliminary asset investigations

Before initiating enforcement, we verify the debtor's attachable assets: real estate through land registry (visure catastali) and mortgage registry searches, vehicles through the PRA, corporate shareholdings, and bank accounts. For commercial claims, analysing the debtor company's financial statements makes it possible to identify attachable receivables (customer receivables, cash, available bank balances). Asset investigations avoid initiating costly enforcement procedures destined to fail against insolvent or assetless debtors, focusing resources on the most promising avenues.

5. Debt negotiation and settlement agreement

In many cases, initiating the summary payment order procedure is sufficient to bring the debtor to the negotiation table. We manage the negotiation phase with a precise strategy: we maintain procedural pressure (service of the notice to comply, registration of the attachment) as negotiation leverage, and seek settlement agreements that allow the creditor to recover a certain and immediate portion of the claim, avoiding the time and uncertainties of litigation. The agreement may take the form of a settlement agreement (transazione) pursuant to art. 1965 c.c., an instalment repayment plan with security, or assisted negotiation pursuant to D.L. 132/2014, with immediate enforceable title status.

6. Clawback action (Azione revocatoria) pursuant to art. 2901 c.c.

When the debtor has attempted to remove their assets from enforcement through disposal transactions (sham sales to family members, gifts, creation of family patrimonial funds, trusts, nominee arrangements), the ordinary clawback action (azione revocatoria ordinaria) pursuant to art. 2901 c.c. makes it possible to render such acts ineffective with respect to the creditor. The case law of the Court of Cassation has established precedents favourable to creditors: for transactions entered into without consideration (a titolo gratuito), it is sufficient to prove the debtor's mere awareness of the prejudice (scientia damni) at the time of the transaction (Cass. Sez. III, n. 29742/2022). The action is subject to a limitation period of five years from the contested transaction.

The procedure step by step

From petition to recovery

  • 1

    Analysis of the claim and document collection

    Verification of the existence, liquidity, and due status (esigibilità) of the claim. Collection of evidentiary documentation (contract, invoices, reminders, correspondence). Assessment of merits and risks. Verification of statutes of limitations (ordinary: 10 years pursuant to art. 2946 c.c.; short: 5 years for professional fees pursuant to art. 2949 c.c.).

  • 2

    Out-of-court formal notice (diffida stragiudiziale) and assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita)

    Service of a formal notice of default via certified e-mail (PEC) or registered mail. If the claim is below € 50,000, commencement of mandatory assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) pursuant to D.L. 132/2014 as a condition of procedibility. Attempt at a settlement agreement prior to court action.

  • 3

    Petition for an injunction to pay (ricorso per decreto ingiuntivo)

    Filing of the summary petition (ricorso monitorio) with the competent Court (Tribunale) (or with the Justice of the Peace (Giudice di Pace) for claims up to € 10,000). Application for provisional enforceability pursuant to art. 642 c.p.c. The decree is issued by the judge in chambers, ex parte, in 2–6 weeks.

  • 4

    Service of the injunction and writ of execution (atto di precetto)

    The injunction to pay (decreto ingiuntivo) is served on the debtor. The 40-day time limit for lodging an opposition (opposizione a decreto ingiuntivo) begins to run (art. 645 c.p.c.). If provisional enforceability has been granted, service of the writ of execution (atto di precetto) proceeds immediately (minimum time limit: 10 days). If the debtor does not pay and does not lodge an opposition, the decree becomes final.

  • 5

    Asset searches and choice of attachment (pignoramento)

    Land registry, mortgage registry, public vehicle register (PRA) searches, company shareholding checks, and INPS searches. Identification of the most attachable assets. Selection of the most effective form of attachment (third-party garnishment, movable property attachment, or real estate foreclosure) based on the debtor's assets.

  • 6

    Enforcement proceedings (esecuzione forzata) and recovery

    Service of the notice of attachment (atto di pignoramento) on the debtor and, in the case of third-party garnishment (pignoramento presso terzi), on the third party (bank, employer). Hearing for the assignment of sums pursuant to art. 553 c.p.c. Distribution of proceeds to the creditor. Issuance of the assignment order or, for real estate, online judicial auction and distribution plan.

Results achieved

5 resolved debt recovery cases

Case no. 1

Commercial credit from supplies — Order for payment with provisional enforcement

Amount: € 78,400 — Sector: industrial supplies — Tribunale di Napoli

A company supplying industrial components turned to the firm after more than a year of unsuccessful out-of-court attempts: the debtor, a limited liability company operating in the mechanical engineering sector, had failed to pay fourteen invoices totaling € 78,400, citing generic complaints regarding the quality of the supply that were never formalized in writing. The concrete risk was the five-year statute of limitations for commercial debts and the gradual erosion of the debtor’s asset guarantees, as it had begun transferring machinery to another company within the same corporate group.

The firm acted on two simultaneous fronts: it filed an application (ricorso) for an order for payment (decreto ingiuntivo) requesting provisional enforcement pursuant to art. 642 c.p.c. — granted on the basis of regularly registered invoices and signed transport documents — and concurrently submitted an application for a conservatory arrest (sequestro conservativo) on the machinery subject to the suspicious transfer. The order was issued in three weeks. The service of the notice to comply (precetto) and the attachment (pignoramento) of the company's bank accounts prompted the debtor to enter negotiations. The settlement agreement enabled the full recovery of the principal (euro 78.400) plus late payment interest pursuant to D.Lgs. 231/2002 and legal fees, for a total sum exceeding € 91.000, in a single payment within forty-five days of service of the order.

Debt fully recovered — € 91.000 in 45 days
Case no. 2

Attachment (pignoramento) of bank account — Unpaid professional fee claim

Amount: € 22.600 — Sector: professional services — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

A professional registered with a professional body had assisted a business consulting firm for eighteen months, issuing regular fee invoices (parcelle) totaling € 22.600. Upon termination of the relationship, the client refused to pay the last five invoices, claiming that the services did not correspond to what was agreed. The professional had attempted mandatory assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) pursuant to D.L. 132/2014 without success, as the debtor failed to respond to the negotiation agreement within the statutory deadlines. The fee invoices had been issued with a revenue stamp and formally recorded, and the activities performed were documented by reports delivered to the client via certified email (PEC).

Having obtained the order for payment (decreto ingiuntivo) with provisional enforcement — the professional claim being supported by privileged written evidence pursuant to art. 636 c.p.c. for professionals —, the firm served the notice to comply (precetto) and proceeded with a third-party attachment (pignoramento presso terzi) pursuant to art. 543 c.p.c., targeting the debtor company's main bank, identified through the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate). The bank declared an available balance exceeding the claim. The enforcement judge issued the assignment order (ordinanza di assegnazione) pursuant to art. 553 c.p.c. The sums were assigned in full to the professional, including default interest and legal costs assessed by the judge. The entire procedure, from the commencement of the summary proceedings to the assignment of funds, was concluded in six months.

Sums assigned in full — Procedure completed in 6 months
Case no. 3

Pre-action conservatory arrest (sequestro conservativo ante causam) — Absconding debtor

Amount: € 145.000 — Sector: construction contract claim — Tribunale di Napoli

A construction company had carried out renovation and expansion works for a private individual, receiving an advance payment of € 55.000 on a contractual price of € 200.000. Once the works were completed and inspected, the client refused to settle the remaining balance of € 145.000, claiming construction defects that were never specified, and had in the meantime sold the renovated property and transferred the proceeds to foreign accounts, presumably in an attempt to render any future enforcement action ineffective. The concrete risk was that, by the time a judgment was rendered, there would no longer be any assets to seize in Italy.

The firm acted with utmost urgency: it filed an application (ricorso) for pre-action conservatory arrest pursuant to art. 671 c.p.c. ante causam, enclosing documentary evidence of the claim (signed construction contract, bill of quantities, acceptance report, and receipts of advance payments) and evidence of periculum in mora (deed of sale of the property and wire transfers abroad extracted from the conveyance records). The judge granted the application inaudita altera parte within forty-eight hours, ordering the conservatory arrest (sequestro conservativo) on an active Italian bank account and on a corporate shareholding in a second company owned by the debtor. The measure froze assets valued at € 160.000, exceeding the claimed amount, thereby ensuring sufficient assets to cover interest and costs as well. During the proceedings on the merits, the debtor accepted a settlement for € 138.000, avoiding the continuation of the trial. The conservatory arrest was converted into an attachment (pignoramento) and the funds were assigned to the creditor.

Conservatory arrest obtained in 48 hours — Settlement € 138.000
Case no. 4

Recovery of unpaid professional fees — Lawyer against former client

Amount: € 34.800 — Sector: legal services — Corte d’Appello di Napoli

A lawyer had represented a client in a complex civil dispute lasting over four years, achieving significant results. At the conclusion of the mandate, the client paid only part of the fees, refusing to pay the remaining € 34.800 and alleging that the legal representation had been deficient. The lawyer filed an application for an order for payment (ricorso per decreto ingiuntivo) with the competent Bar Council (Consiglio dell’Ordine) pursuant to art. 636 c.p.c. The client lodged an opposition, challenging both the determination of the fees (pleading the absence of a written estimate pursuant to Law 247/2012) and the evidentiary value of the fee statements (parcelle). At first instance, the court reduced the claim to € 18.000, deeming the activity performed at the hearings insufficiently documented.

The firm handled the appeal, systematically reconstructing the legal activities through: court minutes obtained from the court registry (cancelleria), statements of costs (note spese) filed over the years, certified email (PEC) communications with the client proving the updates and instructions received, and the judicial assessment of costs in the judgments obtained. The Corte d’Appello, applying the forensic fee parameters of Ministerial Decree (D.M.) 55/2014 as updated by Ministerial Decree (D.M.) 147/2022, overturned the first-instance judgment, recognizing a claim of € 31.500 plus interest and costs for both instances, for a total awarded sum of € 38.200. The funds were recovered through an attachment (pignoramento) of the client's bank account, identified through access to the records of the civil proceedings won by the same party for which the compensation had been sought.

Appeal granted — € 38.200 fully recovered
Case no. 5

Debt recovery through real estate attachment (pignoramento immobiliare) — Defaulted private loan

Amount: € 210.000 — Sector: private loan — Tribunale di Nola

An entrepreneur had granted a private loan of € 210.000 to a business partner, formalized by an authenticated private deed (scrittura privata autenticata), with a repayment plan in thirty-six monthly installments and a mortgage guarantee (ipoteca) on a residential property owned by the debtor. After regularly paying the first eight installments, the debtor ceased all payments, claiming the contract was void due to the alleged usurious nature of the interest (set at 6% per annum, well below the legal usury threshold), and concurrently entered negotiations to sell the mortgaged property to a buyer in good faith, in an attempt to release the asset from the mortgage before the creditor could initiate enforcement proceedings.

The firm acted with the utmost timeliness: it served the notice to comply (precetto) on the basis of the enforceable title (titolo esecutivo — authenticated private deed having enforceable status pursuant to art. 474 c.p.c.) and filed the real estate attachment (pignoramento immobiliare) transcribed in the land registry before the scheduled deed of sale (rogito). The transcription of the attachment blocked the sale, rendering the transfer unenforceable against the attaching creditor. During the enforcement proceedings, the debtor — aware of the inevitable loss of the property at auction — entered a settlement agreement: he paid € 195.000 in a single lump sum (the outstanding balance was € 178.200 plus interest), thereby obtaining the discharge of the attachment and the mortgage. The creditor fully recovered his investment along with an additional return derived from default interest accrued during the proceedings.

Debt fully recovered — Settlement € 195.000
