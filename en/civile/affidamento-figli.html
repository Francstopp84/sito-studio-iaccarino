Regulatory framework

The rules governing child custody

The matter is governed by an organic body of rules recognizing the minor's right to a stable and continuous relationship with both parental figures, except in cases of proven prejudice to their psychophysical development.

Art. 337-ter c.c. — Joint custody

Establishes joint custody as the general rule. Each parent must maintain a balanced and continuous relationship with the child, participating in decisions of greatest importance. Primary residential placement does not imply a reduction of parental authority.

Art. 337-quater c.c. — Sole custody

Represents the exception: it is ordered only when joint custody would run contrary to the best interests of the minor. The court must provide specific reasoning for the inadequacy of the non-custodial parent. The excluded parent retains the right and duty of supervision.

Arts. 337-bis / 337-octies c.c.

Define the scope of application of custody rules (legal separation, divorce, termination of cohabitation) and govern the judge's powers: the court may ex officio take any necessary measure in the interest of the minor, including hearing the child and appointing a court-appointed expert witness (CTU).

Law 54/2006 — Custody reform

Introduced into our legal system the principle of shared parenting (bigenitorialità) as a right of the child, not of the parent. It established direct maintenance as the preferred rule and periodic maintenance payments as a residual mechanism to balance the economic capacities of the parents.

Hague Convention of 1980

Governs the return of children wrongfully removed or unlawfully retained in a foreign State. It provides for expedited return procedures (within 6 weeks) and cooperation mechanisms between the Central Authorities of the contracting States. Italy implemented the Convention with Law 64/1994.

Art. 337-octies c.c. — Hearing of the minor

The judge orders the hearing of the minor who has reached 12 years of age, or younger if capable of discernment. The hearing may be delegated to an expert appointed by the court. The minor's statements must be evaluated taking into account their maturity and the family context.

The types

Forms of custody and associated rights

Every family situation is unique. The choice of the most appropriate custody arrangement depends on specific parental dynamics, the history of the family unit, and above all the concrete best interest of the child in their living context.

01

Joint custody

Ordinary regime provided for by art. 337-ter c.c. Both parents jointly exercise parental responsibility for decisions of greatest importance (education, health, residence). Day-to-day decisions are taken by the cohabiting parent.

02

Sole custody

Ordered when the other parent is unsuitable or potentially detrimental to the minor. The court must provide adequate reasoning for departing from shared parenting. The non-custodial parent retains visitation rights and the duty to oversee education.

03

Primary residential placement

Within joint custody (affidamento condiviso), the child resides permanently with one parent (residential parent, or genitore collocatario) and spends time with the other according to a schedule agreed upon by the parties or ordered by the court. Primary residence (collocamento prevalente) does not alter the equality of parental responsibility (autorità genitoriale).

04

Access rights

The non-cohabiting parent has both the right and the duty to maintain regular relations with the child. The court may regulate days, times, holidays, and school breaks. The systematic obstruction of access rights (diritto di visita) may lead to the adoption of sanctions against the obstructing parent.

05

Modification of custody and access arrangements

Custody arrangements may be amended at any time upon the occurrence of a material change of circumstances (relocation, new partner, change of employment, deterioration of living conditions, harmful conduct). The court evaluates the prevailing best interests of the child.

06

International child abduction

An urgent procedure initiated when a parent unlawfully removes or retains a minor child abroad. The 1980 Hague Convention and EC Regulation 2201/2003 apply. Jurisdiction lies with the country of the child's habitual residence prior to the abduction.

The Firm's Approach

The defence strategies we adopt

In custody matters, there is no one-size-fits-all solution. Each strategy is developed on the specific circumstances of the case, with the constant objective of safeguarding the child's well-being and protecting the rights of the client parent.

⚖️

Joint custody as the general rule

We advocate for the application of the principle of shared parenting (bigenitorialità) even in cases where the other parent attempts to obtain sole control over the child's upbringing. Documenting parental fitness, active involvement in the child's life, and cooperation represent the main pillars of our strategy.

📋

Proof of parental fitness

We compile a comprehensive documentary file: school records, extracurricular activities, medical appointments, communications with teachers, qualified witness statements. Every element demonstrating the active and responsible presence of the parent throughout the child's development is strategically highlighted.

🔬

Court-appointed psychological expert evaluation

The Court-Appointed Expert Evaluation (Consulenza Tecnica d'Ufficio - CTU) is frequently the decisive instrument. We monitor the entire expert evaluation process: the selection of the expert, the formulation of questions, the execution of the expert operations, and the assessment of the draft report. When necessary, we appoint an ex parte technical advisor (consulente tecnico di parte) to challenge the findings of the court's expert.

🎤

Hearing of the child

We handle the hearing of the child pursuant to art. 337-octies c.c. with particular care. We prepare the client to understand the significance and procedural modalities of this step, ensure that the child is not subjected to undue pressure, and assess whether to request the presence of a qualified expert auxiliary.

🔄

Modification based on changed circumstances

When the factual circumstances change significantly, we promptly initiate proceedings for the variation of existing orders. From changes in income to relocation, from the introduction of a new partner to the emergence of prejudicial conduct: any material change may justify a revision of the original order.

🤝

Family mediation

In situations of moderate conflict, mediation may lead to solutions that are more lasting and less traumatic for the children than contentious court proceedings. We cooperate with accredited family mediators and, where mediation achieves a positive outcome, we draft the agreement into a legally enforceable text suitable for court approval (omologazione).

Matters Handled

Five significant cases

The cases below illustrate concretely the most frequent procedural dynamics in custody and access matters. Identifying details have been altered to protect the confidentiality of the parties.

Case 1 — Sole custody ordered for parental alienation

Favourable outcome
Court: Tribunale di Napoli, Family Division
Duration: 18 months
Key legal instrument: Psychodiagnostic CTU (Consulenza Tecnica d'Ufficio)

A mother contacted the Firm after her twelve-year-old son progressively ceased all contact with his father, refusing the access visits established in the legal separation order. The situation had deteriorated over more than a year: the minor displayed systematic and groundless rejection toward the father, employing adult and stereotyped language that defence consultants had already flagged as indicative of potential psychological manipulation. The father, assisted by the Firm, filed an application (ricorso) pursuant to art. 709-ter c.p.c. for breach of the provisions governing the exercise of parental responsibility. Following an in-depth evidentiary phase and a psychodiagnostic court-appointed expert evaluation (CTU) conducted by Prof. R.M. of the Università Federico II, the Tribunale di Napoli established the existence of parental alienation orchestrated by the mother through systematic efforts to undermine the father's role. The court's expert identified severe and ongoing emotional interference that had caused a traumatic rupture of the child's emotional bond with his father.

Outcome: Sole custody granted to the father. Primary residence transferred. Mother directed to undergo psychological support. Father-son relationship restored with the support of a psychologist.

Case 2 — Variation of primary residence

Favourable outcome
Court: Tribunale di Salerno, Civil Division I
Duration: 14 months
Key legal instrument: Witness testimonies and school reports

A separated father sought the variation of the primary residence (collocamento prevalente) of his two minor daughters (aged 8 and 11), originally established with the mother under the separation order approved by the court (omologato) four years earlier. Living circumstances had changed significantly: the mother had lost her employment, entered into a relationship with an individual with criminal convictions for violent offences against the person, and relocated the children to a suburban residence lacking adequate sanitary conditions. The daughters, interviewed by the social worker of the Municipality of Salerno, expressed distress and discomfort. The father, a public employee with a stable income and a suitable home in the city centre near the children's schools, filed an application (ricorso) for the modification of residence pursuant to art. 337-quinquies c.c. The evidentiary proceedings involved the headmistresses of the two schools attended by the children, who confirmed a decline in attendance and academic performance in recent months. The social services submitted a report highlighting the deficiencies of the maternal environment. The Tribunale di Salerno, invoking the "best interest of the child" principle and giving decisive weight to the environmental and emotional stability offered by the father, ordered the transfer of primary residence with visitation rights granted to the mother on alternating weekends.

Outcome: Primary residence transferred to the father. Access rights regulated for the mother. Referral to child welfare authorities for ongoing monitoring of the living environment.

Case 3 — International child abduction

Repatriation obtained
Court: Corte d'Appello di Napoli (Central Authority)
Duration: 7 months
Key legal instrument: 1980 Hague Convention + EC Reg. 2201/2003

An Italian father contacted the Firm as a matter of utmost urgency: the mother, a Romanian citizen, had taken their five-year-old son to Romania without his consent during an agreed summer holiday period and had not returned by the scheduled date. The child's habitual residence was in Naples, where the parents had lived together until their recent de facto separation. Custody was shared (affidamento condiviso) and no court order had been issued authorising the relocation of the minor's residence abroad. The Firm immediately initiated the procedure provided for by the Hague Convention of 25 October 1980 by lodging an application (ricorso) with the Italian Central Authority (Department for Juvenile Justice) along with a concurrent request for international cooperation. In parallel, an appeal (ricorso) was filed before the Corte d'Appello di Napoli, which has jurisdiction over international child abduction, seeking the issuance of a return order. The Romanian court of first instance — seised of the matter through the Romanian Central Authority — ordered the return of the minor within forty-two days. Romania, as a signatory State to the Convention, enforced the order. The child returned to Italy accompanied by a social worker appointed by the Romanian judge.

Outcome: Return of the minor ordered by the Romanian court pursuant to the Hague Convention. Child returned to Italy within 7 months of the abduction. Custody proceedings reopened before the Tribunale di Napoli.

Case 4 — Modification of visitation rights

Expanded rights
Court: Tribunale di Caserta, Family Division
Duration: 8 months
Key instrument: Police report and expert appraisal

A father, subject to a supervised visitation regime (meetings at a family home shelter, once a week, for two hours) provisionally ordered by the Tribunale di Caserta following accusations of aggressive behaviour made by his former partner, turned to the Firm to obtain a modification of the conditions and the reinstatement of a standard visitation regime. The mother's allegations, unsupported by any final judicial ruling, had resulted in a provisional presidential order significantly restricting father-son contact. The Firm reconstructed the matter from the outset: the allegations had been contested in criminal proceedings, which concluded with a dismissal (archiviazione) on the grounds of unfounded notice of crime. In the meantime, the father had undertaken a voluntary course of psychotherapy, the results of which were documented by the psychologist. Witness statements from the paternal grandparents, teachers, and other family members regarding the quality of the father-son relationship during periods when visits had occurred normally were also produced. The Tribunale di Caserta, citing the principle whereby the restriction of visitation rights must be based on certain evidence and not on mere allegations, ordered the gradual elimination of the supervised visitation regime, with a phased transition towards unsupervised visits and ultimately alternate overnight stays, subject to a six-monthly report from the family counselling centre (consultorio familiare).

Outcome: Termination of the supervised visitation regime. Progressive reinstatement of unsupervised visits with the father. Alternate overnight stays obtained after six months. Criminal dismissal leveraged as rebuttal evidence.

Case 5 — Shared custody with detailed arrangements

Endorsed agreement
Court: Tribunale di Napoli Nord (Aversa)
Duration: 5 months
Key instrument: Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) pursuant to Law 162/2014

A couple with three children (aged 7, 10, and 14) separated after twenty years of marriage. The initial situation was highly contentious: both parents sought primary residence (collocamento prevalente) of the children, maintaining apparently irreconcilable positions. An agreement seemed impossible after six months of unsuccessful direct communications. The Firm accepted the mandate with the objective of reaching an agreement through assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita), avoiding court litigation that would have exposed the three children to months of court-appointed expert appraisals (CTU), protected hearings, and inevitable instrumentalisation. The negotiation required four intensive sessions: during the first phase, efforts focused on separating the parents' personal demands from the actual needs of the children; during the second, a detailed parenting plan was established, including differentiated weekly schedules for each child, the management of summer holidays by alternating destination areas, digital communication arrangements during periods spent with the other parent, and the management of extraordinary expenses with monthly accountings. The final document, drafted in forty-two clauses, received clearance from the Juvenile Public Prosecutor and judicial endorsement (omologazione) from the Tribunale di Napoli Nord within fifty-two days of the joint petition (ricorso congiunto) being filed.

Outcome: Endorsement of assisted negotiation agreement. Detailed parenting plan for three children. Conflict resolved without court-appointed expert appraisal (CTU) and without adversarial litigation. Estimated savings of 24 months of court proceedings.
