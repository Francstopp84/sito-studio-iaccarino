Regulatory framework

The rules governing condominiums

Italian condominium law is regulated by a stratified body of legislation, substantially reformed by Law no. 220 of 11 December 2012, in force since 18 June 2013. Understanding the applicable provisions — and the strict deadlines they impose — is the first step towards effectively safeguarding your rights in condominium matters.

Civil Code — Book III, Title VII

Arts. 1117–1139 c.c. — Condominium in buildings (Reform Law 220/2012)

The core of condominium regulations is contained in Articles 1117–1139 of the Civil Code, thoroughly revised by Law 220/2012. Art. 1117 lists the common parts of the building (soil, foundations, main walls, roofs, stairs, entrance gates, courtyards, hallways, washhouses, wells, cisterns, sewers, drain pipes, systems for the distribution of water, gas, electricity, central heating), with a presumption of co-ownership in the absence of a title to the contrary. The reform introduced fundamental innovations regarding the mandatory building manager (amministratore) (over 8 unit owners), the condominium financial statement (rendiconto condominiale), the general assembly and voting quorums, video surveillance, supercondominiums, and minimal condominiums.

Comprehensive reform: Law of 11 December 2012 no. 220
Civil Code

Art. 1137 c.c. — Challenging assembly resolutions: the 30-day time limit

Art. 1137 c.c. governs the challenge of condominium assembly resolutions. The provision distinguishes between void resolutions (delibere nulle) (challengeable without time limits: resolutions with an unlawful or impossible object, affecting the individual rights of unit owners, or completely lacking a quorum) and voidable resolutions (delibere annullabili) (challengeable within the mandatory time limit of 30 days from the date of the resolution for absent unit owners, and from communication of the minutes for dissenting or abstaining unit owners). Failure to comply with the 30-day time limit renders the resolution definitively unchallengeable. Before initiating legal proceedings, it is mandatory to attempt mediation pursuant to art. 71-quater disp. att. c.c.

Mandatory time limit: 30 days — non-extendable

Pay attention to the 30-day time limit: The time limit to challenge a voidable condominium resolution runs from the date of the meeting for attending and dissenting unit owners, and from communication of the minutes for absent owners. It is a forfeiture deadline (termine di decadenza): once expired, the resolution becomes definitively unchallengeable. If you have received meeting minutes that you believe to be unlawful, do not wait — contact us immediately.

Civil Code

Art. 1123 c.c. — Apportionment of condominium expenses

Art. 1123 c.c. establishes the fundamental criteria for apportioning condominium expenses. Expenses for the preservation and enjoyment of the common parts of the building, for the provision of services in the common interest, and for innovations resolved by the majority are apportioned among the unit owners in proportion to the value of each owner’s property (in millesimal units). If items are intended to serve unit owners to a different extent, expenses are apportioned in proportion to the use each can make of them. If a building has multiple staircases, courtyards, flat roofs (lastrici solari), works, or systems intended to serve a part of the entire building, the expenses relating to their maintenance are borne by the group of unit owners who derive benefit from them. Contractual derogations from statutory millesimal tables are permitted only with the unanimous consent of all unit owners.

Principle: proportionality to the millesimal value
Civil Code

Art. 1130 c.c. — Powers and duties of the building manager

Art. 1130 c.c. (as amended by Law 220/2012) exhaustively lists the duties of the building manager: to execute assembly resolutions; to regulate the use of common areas and the enjoyment of services in the common interest; to collect contributions and pay expenses necessary for the routine maintenance of common parts; to perform acts of preservation concerning the common parts; to fulfill tax obligations. The building manager must maintain the condominium registry (registro di anagrafe condominiale), the register of assembly minutes, the register of appointment and revocation of the manager, and the accounting register. They must prepare the budget estimate and the annual condominium financial statement. Serious irregularities in management entitle any unit owner to petition for judicial revocation (revoca giudiziale) pursuant to art. 1129, paragraph 11, c.c.

Judicial revocation for serious irregularities — art. 1129 c.c.
Implementing Provisions of the Italian Civil Code

Art. 71-quater disp. att. c.c. — Mandatory condominium mediation

Art. 71-quater of the implementing provisions of the Italian Civil Code (introduced by Law 220/2012) establishes that for condominium disputes, the attempt at mediation is a condition precedent to legal action (condizione di procedibilità). The claim is inadmissible if brought without first having attempted mediation (mandatory pursuant to art. 5 of Legislative Decree 28/2010). Condominium mediation is governed by arts. 1117 et seq. c.c. The building manager must be authorized by the assembly to participate in mediation and to sign any settlement agreement with the quorums required by art. 1136 c.c. Failure to participate in mediation without justified reason may be assessed by the court for the purpose of legal costs.

Mandatory condition precedent before legal proceedings
What we handle for you

The most frequent condominium disputes

Condominium litigation covers a broad spectrum of disputes. Our firm assists both individual unit owners and condominiums as a whole in all primary types of condominium dispute.

⚖

Challenging an assembly resolution

Resolutions adopted without the prescribed quorum, in violation of condominium bylaws or the individual rights of unit owners. Distinction between nullity (not subject to time limits) and voidability (30 days). Stay of execution pursuant to art. 1137 c.c.

💵

Apportionment of expenses

Challenging the allocation of extraordinary expenses, maintenance of systems, elevators, roofs, and facades. Verification of the proper application of criteria pursuant to art. 1123 c.c. and applicable millesimal tables.

📈

Millesimal tables

Judicial revision of millesimal tables that are erroneous or no longer correspond to the actual condition of the building pursuant to art. 69 disp. att. c.c. Consensual amendment (unanimity) or judicial amendment in the event of substantial error (exceeding one-fifth).

🏠

Use of common parts

Disputes over the right to use common parts pursuant to art. 1102 c.c. (each owner may use common property, provided they do not alter its intended purpose or prevent others from making equal use of it). Super-elevations, prohibited alterations, and unlawful occupation.

🔊

Noise and nuisances (immissioni)

Protection against intolerable disturbances caused by noise, smoke, fumes, and vibrations from neighboring properties pursuant to art. 844 c.c. Injunctive relief, compensation for physical/psychological damage (danno biologico) and non-pecuniary damage (danno morale), and preliminary technical assessment (accertamento tecnico preventivo).

🚧

Defects in common parts

Liability of the condominium and the building manager for defects in common parts causing damage to unit owners or third parties. Water infiltration, falling plaster, and structural subsidence. Claims for damages pursuant to arts. 2043 and 2051 c.c.

Mandatory procedure

Condominium mediation

Art. 71-quater disp. att. c.c. — A mandatory step that can become an opportunity

Before bringing any condominium dispute before the Court (Tribunale), the law requires prior resort to mediation. This is not merely a bureaucratic formality: when handled strategically, mediation allows the dispute to be resolved much more quickly (generally 3-4 months compared to 5-7 years for ordinary court proceedings), at significantly lower costs, and with the opportunity to reach tailored agreements that a judge could never impose.

Please note: the building manager must be authorized in advance by the assembly to participate in mediation and to enter into settlement agreements. The omission of such authorization invalidates the condominium’s participation in the mediation proceedings.

4

Settlement agreement or report of unsuccessful mediation → access to court proceedings

How we assist you

Our firm’s strategies

Every condominium dispute has its own specific complexity. Our firm combines thorough knowledge of condominium law with the most recent case law to devise the most effective strategy for your case.

Strategy 01

Challenging resolutions: nullity vs. voidability

The distinction between void resolutions (which may be challenged without time limits) and voidable resolutions (subject to a 30-day time limit) is fundamental. Void resolutions include those with an unlawful or impossible subject matter or that infringe upon the individual rights of unit owners (use of common parts, change of designated use, impact on exclusive ownership). Identifying the correct category makes it possible to select the most effective course of action.

Strategy 02

Strategic condominium mediation

Mandatory mediation under art. 71-quater disp. att. c.c. is not merely a procedural hurdle: it is a powerful instrument when managed by a professional. We prepare a well-structured conciliation proposal, supported by technical and legal documentation, which maximizes the chances of an agreement and, in the event mediation fails, strengthens the client’s procedural position in court. An unjustified failure of the opposing party to participate is relevant for the allocation of legal costs pursuant to art. 8 D.Lgs. 28/2010.

Strategy 03

Revision of millesimal tables

Millesimal tables (tabelle millesimali) may be amended through judicial proceedings when they are affected by an error exceeding one fifth of the value assigned to the property unit (art. 69, no. 1, disp. att. c.c.) or when the condition of the building has changed substantially over time. We coordinate the technical appraisal of an expert surveyor or engineer to quantify the error and structure the legal action.

Strategy 04

Action against unlawful emissions pursuant to art. 844 c.c.

Art. 844 c.c. prohibits emissions of noise, smoke, fumes, vibrations, and similar disturbances that exceed normal tolerability, taking into account the condition of the premises and the balance between competing interests. In a condominium context, this provision is the primary legal remedy against nuisance neighbors. We file for preventive technical inquiry (accertamento tecnico preventivo - ATP) pursuant to art. 696 c.p.c. to document the emissions in an admissible evidentiary manner, obtain urgent injunctive relief, and take legal action to claim compensation for biological damage (danno biologico) and moral damage.

Strategy 05

Liability and removal of the building manager

Art. 1129, paragraph 11, c.c. lists the serious irregularities that justify the judicial removal of the building manager (amministratore): failure to convene the annual assembly, failure to render accounts, accounting irregularities, personal use of the condominium bank account, refusal to allow inspection of condominium documents. When the assembly fails to resolve on the removal, any unit owner may apply to the judicial authority. We also handle compensation claims against the former manager for damages caused by mismanagement.

Strategy 06

Condominium debt collection

Art. 63 disp. att. c.c. allows the building manager to take enforcement proceedings against defaulting unit owners without having to first enforce against owners who are up to date with payments. An injunction (decreto ingiuntivo) issued for condominium contributions is immediately enforceable. We assist both condominiums in debt recovery actions for unpaid fees, and individual unit owners in opposing injunctions issued for contributions disputed in their existence or quantification.

Case No. 1 — Condominium

Resolution annulled for notice defect — Tribunale di Napoli

Action: challenge of resolution pursuant to art. 1137 c.c. — Defect: failure to provide notice with reasonable advance notice and failure to specify the agenda

A condominium owner in the historic centre of Naples contacted the firm to challenge a condominium meeting resolution (delibera assembleare) by which, unbeknownst to her, the co-owners had approved the replacement of the building's roof with expenses divided among all owners for a total amount exceeding 180,000 euros, with a share charged to her of approximately 12,000 euros. Although listed in the condominium registry, the client had not received the notice of call due to an error in the address used by the building administrator (amministratore di condominio), who had sent the notice to the address of the real estate unit (located on the fifth floor, actually unoccupied and lacking a working mailbox) rather than to the registered residence address of the client, who resided elsewhere.

The defence proved, by means of an official extract from the Civil Registry (Anagrafe) of the Municipality of Naples, that the client's residence differed from the address of the real estate unit, and submitted the correspondence sent by the administrator to the wrong address. Since the owner was absent from the meeting (not having received notice), the 30-day statutory time limit ran from the date of formal notification of the minutes, which had not been served in accordance with statutory requirements. The action was brought following the mandatory condominium mediation procedure, which concluded unsuccessfully due to the administrator's refusal to submit the matter to the general meeting. The Tribunale di Napoli granted the application, declaring the resolution voidable for defective notice and ordering the condominium to reimburse legal costs.

Resolution annulled — Expenses waived and reimbursed
Case No. 2 — Condominium

Apportionment of extraordinary expenses challenged — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Action: challenge of resolution + ascertainment of correct apportionment criteria pursuant to art. 1123 c.c. — Contested amount: € 24,500

A condominium owner from Giugliano in Campania received a payment demand for 24,500 euros as his share of the extraordinary refurbishment of the flat roof (lastrico solare) covering exclusively one wing of the building, which served as roofing only for the real estate units beneath it. The general meeting had erroneously apportioned the expenses among all owners of the entire building in proportion to the general millesimal tables, failing to apply the statutory criterion set out in art. 1126 c.c. for flat roofs under exclusive use, which provides for an apportionment of one-third charged to the owner holding exclusive use and two-thirds charged to the owners of the units beneath.

The defence first verified the legal nature of the flat roof, establishing through land registry plans and statements by the administrator that it was a flat roof under the exclusive use of a single condominium owner. It then reconstructed the proper apportionment criterion pursuant to art. 1126 c.c. supported by an expert technical report that quantified the share legally owed by our client at under 4,000 euros, compared to the 24,500 demanded. The challenge to the resolution, brought after mandatory mediation concluded unsuccessfully, was upheld by the Court, which declared the apportionment criterion unlawful and ordered the condominium to refund the sums unduly collected.

Refund obtained — Share reduced from € 24,500 to € 3,850
Case No. 3 — Condominium

Compensation awarded for intolerable noise — Tribunale di Napoli, Sezione III Civile

Action: injunctive relief + claim for damages pursuant to arts. 844 and 2043 c.c. — Biological and non-pecuniary damage established

A couple residing in a second-floor flat of a condominium building in the Vomero district of Naples had been enduring for over two years acoustic emissions caused by an auto repair garage unlawfully established on the ground floor. In breach of the designated property use (originally commercial premises designated for shop use), it had been converted into a garage-workshop operating from 7:30 to 21:00, including Saturdays. The clients suffered from the noise of compressors, metallic hammering, and fumes from fuel and solvents, which made it impossible to open windows and caused serious disruption to their rest and domestic life.

The defence initially initiated a preliminary technical assessment (accertamento tecnico preventivo) pursuant to art. 696-bis c.p.c., which documented, through phonometric measurements, that the threshold of normal tolerability was exceeded (DPCM 14/11/1997 and WHO guidelines), with noise peaks exceeding the permissible threshold for residential areas by more than 15 dB. The medical records obtained proved the causal link between the emissions and an anxiety syndrome with sleep disorders diagnosed in the client. The judge ordered the immediate cessation of the activity via an urgent injunction (ordine inibitorio) pursuant to art. 700 c.p.c. and, in the proceedings on the merits, awarded biological damage (danno biologico) and non-pecuniary damage (danno morale) for a total of 38,000 euros, plus legal costs.

Cessation of emissions + Compensation of € 38,000
Case No. 4 — Condominium

Building administrator revoked for serious irregularities — Tribunale di Napoli

Action: judicial revocation pursuant to art. 1129 c.c. + ascertainment of liability — Deficit: € 67,000

A group of seven condominium owners (out of a total of twelve) of a residential building in the Fuorigrotta district approached the firm after discovering serious irregularities in the management of their condominium. The administrator, in office for more than eight years, had persistently refused to provide the condominium financial statements for the previous three years despite repeated requests by the owners; had never convened the annual ordinary meeting for the approval of the financial accounts, systematically breaching the obligation under art. 1130-bis c.c.; held the condominium bank account in his personal name rather than in his capacity as condominium administrator; and had made payments to members of his family for alleged maintenance works that were never carried out. The group of owners, despite representing a majority in terms of millesimal shares, was unable to pass a resolution of revocation due to the administrator's obstruction.

The defence filed an application (ricorso) for judicial revocation pursuant to art. 1129, paragraph 11, c.c., documenting each irregularity in detail through condominium records (obtained via court order in interim proceedings), bank statements of the condominium account, and witness statements of the owners. The Tribunale di Napoli granted the application ex parte (inaudita altera parte), immediately appointing a court administrator (amministratore giudiziale). In the subsequent proceedings on the merits, the former administrator was found liable for a deficit of 67,000 euros and ordered to repay the full amount, together with statutory interest and costs.

Immediate revocation + Restitution of € 67,000
Case No. 5 — Condominium

Millesimal tables judicially revised — Tribunale di Napoli

Action: revision of millesimal tables pursuant to art. 69 disp. att. c.c. — Established error: 28% in property unit value

The owner of a penthouse flat in a condominium on Via Petrarca in Naples contacted the firm because his real estate unit, featuring a large panoramic terrace overlooking the gulf, was valued in the millesimal tables in force at approximately 28% less than its actual value. This resulted in an under-allocation of condominium expenses to his detriment relative to the real value of his property. The other owners, who benefited from this inaccurate valuation, opposed any consensual modification of the tables.

The court-appointed technical expert (consulente tecnico) appointed by the Court upon the proposal of the defense proceeded with the valuation of the entire building using the comparative market method, establishing that the client’s apartment had an actual market value 28.3% higher than the value assigned in the original tables, which were drafted in the 1970s without considering the added value of the panoramic terraces. Since the error well exceeded the one-fifth threshold (20%) provided for by art. 69, n. 1, disp. att. c.c. as a condition for judicial revision, the Court (Tribunale) ordered the rectification of the millesimal tables (tabelle millesimali). The new apportionment increased the plaintiff’s millesimal share (quota millesimale), with retroactive effects also on the apportionment of expenses for the past five years, leading to a compensatory settlement in favour of the other condominium co-owners (condomini) calculated on the difference between what was actually paid and what they ought to have paid.

Revised tables — 28.3% error ascertained and corrected
