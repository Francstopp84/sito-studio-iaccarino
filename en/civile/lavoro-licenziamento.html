Have you been dismissed?
You have 60 days to take action.

Dismissal is one of the most traumatic events in an individual's working life. However, not every dismissal is lawful: the law imposes specific obligations of statement of reasons and procedure upon the employer, the violation of which paves the way for reinstatement in the workplace or compensation for damages. The time limit to challenge a dismissal is mandatory (peremptory): 60 days from receipt of the dismissal letter. Do not waste a single day.

Urgent Legal Consultation Resolved Cases

Attention — Mandatory time limit: The dismissal must be challenged by out-of-court written notice within 60 days of receipt (art. 6, paragraph 1, Law 604/1966). Within the subsequent 180 days, the application (ricorso) must be lodged before the Employment Tribunal (Tribunale del Lavoro). Failure to comply with even one of these deadlines leads to the irrevocable forfeiture of the right of action: the dismissal becomes final and unchallengeable.

Applicable Legislation

The legal framework of dismissal in Italy

The regulation of dismissals in Italy is stratified across multiple statutory sources that coordinate depending on the employee's hiring date and the employer's workforce size. Accurately determining which legal regime applies to your case is the first and fundamental step of any effective defence.

Law of 20 May 1970, no. 300

Workers' Statute — Art. 18 (Reinstatement in the workplace)

Art. 18 of the Workers' Statute (Statuto dei Lavoratori) is the cornerstone provision protecting unlawfully dismissed employees. In the version amended by the Fornero Reform (Law 92/2012), it distinguishes between full reinstatement protection (discriminatory dismissal or dismissal given orally), attenuated reinstatement protection (insubstantiality of the alleged facts underlying the disciplinary dismissal), strong indemnity protection (from 12 to 24 monthly salaries), and weak indemnity protection (from 6 to 12 monthly salaries).

Applies to employees hired before 7 March 2015 by employers with more than 15 employees in the production unit or 60 nationwide.

Reinstatement + compensation up to 12 months' salary
Legislative Decree of 4 March 2015, no. 23

Jobs Act — Contract with increasing protections

Applies to employees hired under open-ended contracts starting from 7 March 2015. It drastically limits the scope of reinstatement, which remains available only for discriminatory dismissals, dismissals for unlawful motives, and disciplinary dismissals in which the alleged conduct is found to be non-existent. In all other cases of unlawfulness, protection is exclusively financial, amounting to one month's salary of the last reference remuneration for each year of service (min. 3, max 27 monthly salaries).

Jobs Act — hired from 7/3/2015
Law of 15 July 1966, no. 604

Individual dismissal for just cause and justified reason

Regulates the substantive validity requirements for individual dismissals: requirement of just cause (art. 1) or justified reason (art. 3), requirement of written form with concurrent statement of reasons (art. 2). The well-known art. 6 establishes the forfeiture deadlines: 60 days for the out-of-court challenge and 180 days for lodging the application (ricorso) in court.

Challenge: 60 + 180 days
Art. 2119 of the Italian Civil Code

Just cause for dismissal and resignation

Defines just cause as a breach of contract so serious as to preclude the continuation, even temporary, of the employment relationship. The provision operates symmetrically: it may be invoked by the employer to justify summary dismissal without notice, but also by the employee seeking to resign without notice when the employer's breach is such as to render continuation of the relationship impossible (workplace harassment/mobbing, unpaid wages, demotion).

Just cause — without notice
Law of 23 July 1991, no. 223

Collective redundancies and Wage Guarantee Fund (C.I.G.)

Governs the procedure for collective redundancies (reduction of personnel affecting at least 5 employees within 120 days). The employer must initiate a trade union procedure (art. 4) lasting 45 days, followed by a joint examination with the comparatively representative trade union organisations. The statutory selection criteria for the employees to be dismissed (length of service, family dependants, technical-production requirements) must be observed. Violation of the procedure entails the application of protections under art. 18 of the Workers' Statute or Legislative Decree 23/2015 depending on the date of hiring.

Min. 5 redundancies in 120 days
Art. 7 Law 300/1970 (Workers' Statute)

Mandatory disciplinary procedure

Prior to imposing any disciplinary sanction, including disciplinary dismissal, the employer must: (1) serve written notice of the specific charge to the employee in a timely manner; (2) observe the minimum defence period of at least 5 days; (3) hear the employee upon their request. Any omission or procedural defect invalidates the dismissal regardless of whether the alleged facts occurred, resulting in the application of strong indemnity protection (art. 18, para. 6, Workers' Statute and art. 4 Legislative Decree 23/2015).

Disciplinary procedure — 5 days for defence
Legislative Decree of 6 September 2001, no. 368 / Law 183/2010

Fornero special procedure and summary proceedings

The special fast-track procedure for disputes concerning the challenge of dismissals under art. 18 of the Workers' Statute is laid down by art. 1, paragraphs 47-69, Law 92/2012 (so-called Fornero procedure): it consists of an urgent summary phase before the employment judge (similar to interim relief proceedings), followed by an opposition phase and a subsequent appeal stage. The Fornero procedure allows for the prompt granting of an enforceable provisional reinstatement order, without waiting for the ordinary timeframes of employment litigation.

Fornero procedure — urgent summary phase

The applicable protection regime depends on the hiring date and, in certain cases, on the size of the enterprise. This distinction is critical: it can mean the difference between reinstatement in the workplace and financial compensation.

Legal Categories

Types of dismissal

Each type of dismissal involves specific legal requirements, procedures, and consequences. Correctly identifying the legal category is the starting point for any defence strategy.

Dismissal for just cause

Summary dismissal, without notice, due to a very serious breach of duty by the employee that precludes even the temporary continuation of the employment relationship (theft, serious insubordination, fraudulent sick leave). The court assesses whether the sanction is proportionate to the conduct, taking into account the provisions of the applicable collective agreement. If the conduct is specified in the national collective bargaining agreement (CCNL) as punishable by a non-termination sanction, the dismissal is unlawful.

Art. 2119 Italian Civil Code — Immediate, without notice

Dismissal for subjective justified reason

Grounds based on a significant breach of contractual duties by the employee, of lesser severity than just cause. Notice is required. Examples: repeated unpunctuality, failure to comply with company directives, unjustified refusal to carry out assigned duties. The severity of the disciplinary sanction is always subject to judicial review: the court determines whether the national collective bargaining agreement (CCNL) provides for non-termination sanctions for the alleged misconduct.

Art. 3, Law 604/1966 — With notice

Dismissal for objective justified reason

Based on reasons inherent to production activity, work organization, and its regular functioning (suppression of the position, company crisis, staff reduction). The employer must prove the genuineness of the economic reasons and the impossibility of reassigning the employee to another available position. For companies with more than 15 employees, prior notification to the Territorial Labor Directorate (Direzione Territoriale del Lavoro - DTL) is required (art. 7 L. 604/66).

Art. 3, L. 604/1966 — Obligation of redeployment (obbligo di repechage)

Collective dismissal (licenziamento collettivo)

It involves at least 5 employees within 120 days in the same production unit or across several units within the same province. The employer must comply with the trade union procedure pursuant to art. 4 L. 223/1991 (prior notification, joint examination with trade unions, notification to the competent bodies) and the selection criteria (length of service, family responsibilities, technical-production needs) agreed with trade unions or provided by law. Any violation, even merely formal, is sanctioned with reinstatement (reintegra) or indemnity protection.

L. 223/1991 — Mandatory trade union procedure

Disciplinary dismissal (licenziamento disciplinare)

Imposed following specific unlawful conduct by the employee. It strictly requires compliance with the disciplinary procedure pursuant to art. 7 of the Workers' Statute (Statuto dei Lavoratori): specific and prompt written notice of charges, a defense period of at least 5 days, and a hearing of the employee upon request. The violation of even one of these procedural requirements renders the dismissal unlawful, leading to the application of strong indemnity protection. On the merits, the judge assesses the proportionality of the dismissal sanction in relation to the conduct.

Art. 7, L. 300/1970 — Mandatory procedure

Resignation for just cause (dimissioni per giusta causa)

The employee may resign without notice when the employer has committed a breach of contract so serious as to prevent even the temporary continuation of the employment relationship: non-payment or delayed payment of salary, unlawful demotion (demansionamento) (art. 2103 c.c.), systematic workplace bullying (mobbing), unlawful transfer, sexual or moral harassment. In this case, the employee is entitled to unemployment benefits (NASpI) and damages. The resignation must be submitted electronically through the ministerial platform.

Art. 2119 c.c. — Right to unemployment benefit (NASpI)

How we defend you

Defense strategies for the employee

Every employment matter is unique, but established defense approaches exist that our firm employs to build the most effective protection. Timely action is crucial: the 60-day deadline does not wait.

01

Out-of-court challenge of dismissal (impugnazione stragiudiziale del licenziamento)

First mandatory step: written communication to the employer (registered letter with acknowledgment of receipt or certified email (PEC)) within 60 days of receiving the dismissal letter, expressing the intention to challenge the dismissal measure. The challenge prevents forfeiture (decadenza) and opens the 180-day time window for filing the petition (ricorso) with the Court (Tribunale). The content of the challenge must be drafted with precision so as not to preclude grounds that may be asserted in legal proceedings.

02

Reinstatement under art. 18 of the Workers' Statute (Statuto dei Lavoratori) and the Jobs Act

Where dismissal is discriminatory, ordered for an unlawful motive, based on a materially non-existent fact (in disciplinary matters), or lacks written form, the employee may obtain reinstatement (reintegra) to the workplace with immediate effect, including by urgent proceedings. Through the Fornero Procedure (Rito Fornero) (summary phase), it is possible to swiftly obtain a provisionally enforceable reinstatement order without awaiting the outcome of the proceedings on the merits. The employer is required to pay the lost salary from the date of dismissal.

03

Indemnity protection and compensation for damages

Where reinstatement is not applicable, or the employee prefers to opt for compensation in lieu of reinstatement (right of option under art. 18, para. 3, Workers' Statute (Statuto dei Lavoratori)), an indemnity is awarded commensurate with length of service and the type of flaw identified. Under the Jobs Act, the indemnity is equal to one month of the last reference salary for each year of service (minimum 3, maximum 27 monthly salaries).

04

Mandatory conciliation attempt (justified objective reason - GMO)

For dismissal on justified objective grounds (giustificato motivo oggettivo) ordered by employers with more than 15 employees, art. 7 L. 604/1966 provides for a mandatory preliminary conciliation attempt before the Territorial Labor Directorate (Direzione Territoriale del Lavoro - DTL). The failure to summon the party to the DTL hearing or the breach of the relevant procedure may constitute an independent ground of unlawfulness of the dismissal. In conciliation proceedings, our firm negotiates the terms of the settlement agreement to maximize the client's entitlements.

05

Action for workplace bullying (mobbing) and straining damage

Mobbing is the systematic and prolonged conduct of the employer (or colleagues with the employer's consent) aimed at marginalizing, isolating, or psychologically harming the employee. Compensation for damage from mobbing includes personal injury damage (danno biologico) (documented by medical certification), moral damage (danno morale), existential damage (danno esistenziale), and professional damage from loss of chance (perdita di chance). An employee who suffers mobbing may resign for just cause, retaining the right to unemployment benefit (NASpI) and full compensation for damages.

06

Disciplinary notice of charges and procedural defects

A thorough analysis of the disciplinary notice of charges often reveals formal and substantive defects that invalidate the dismissal: vague or untimely notice, failure to comply with the 5 days for defense, refusal to hear the employee, violation of the guarantees provided by the national collective labor agreement (CCNL). On the merits, the defense verifies whether the contested conduct is designated by the collective agreement as subject only to conservative sanctions (reprimand, fine, suspension), rendering the dismissal disproportionate and therefore unlawful due to the legal non-existence of the grounds.

Results achieved

5 resolved cases for unlawfully dismissed employees

The following cases illustrate actual matters handled by our firm (with all identifying data anonymised in compliance with privacy regulations). Each case demonstrates how timely and rigorous technical analysis can reverse situations that appeared conclusively compromised.

Case no. 1

Dismissal for just cause annulled — Alleged facts non-existent — Tribunale di Napoli

Sector: large-scale retail — Role: cashier — Applicable regime: art. 18 St. Lav.

An employee with twelve years of service at a supermarket chain had been dismissed for just cause on charges of having stolen goods from the shelves without paying, based on footage recorded by the internal video surveillance system. The employer had served the formal disciplinary charge and imposed dismissal within seven days, without allowing the employee to be heard in person, the company claiming it had not received his written request for a hearing.

Our firm challenged the dismissal within the 60-day statutory period and filed an application (ricorso) pursuant to art. 1, paragraph 47, Law 92/2012 (Fornero Procedure). During the preliminary phase, the defence proved through certified email (PEC) transmission records that the request for a hearing had been duly sent and received by the employer, who had ignored the communication in violation of art. 7 St. Lav. On the merits, a technical expert analysis of the video recordings established that the footage merely showed the employee restacking items mistakenly misplaced by a colleague, and not an act of theft. The Labour Court declared the dismissal unlawful due to the non-existence of the alleged facts, ordering immediate reinstatement to the position and payment of all accrued back pay, calculated at approximately 14 months' salary.

Immediate reinstatement — 14 months of back pay awarded
Case no. 2

Dismissal for objective justified reason unlawful due to breach of redeployment duty — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Sector: IT services — Role: systems engineer — Applicable regime: D.Lgs. 23/2015 (Jobs Act)

An IT services company based in Caserta had dismissed a systems engineer hired in 2018, invoking the suppression of his position following an organisational restructuring and the loss of a major commercial contract. The employee received written notification of dismissal for objective justified reason (giustificato motivo oggettivo) in formal compliance with the Territorial Labour Directorate (DTL) procedure; however, the dismissal letter contained no mention of any assessment of available alternative positions for the employee's internal redeployment (repechage).

Our firm immediately obtained an updated company search (visura camerale) and organisational chart, discovering that in the months following the dismissal, the company had posted a recruitment advertisement for the same technical role on an online job board. The defence also obtained an order for the disclosure of corporate commercial contracts during the evidentiary stage, proving that the loss of the contract cited as the ground for dismissal had occurred after the dismissal notice had been issued. The Court held that the alleged organisational grounds were not substantiated and found a breach of the duty of redeployment (repechage), ordering the employer to pay the maximum indemnity prescribed by the Jobs Act (27 months' salary) in consideration of the length of service and the gravity of the employer's conduct.

Maximum compensation awarded: 27 months' pay — Full victory
Case no. 3

Systematic workplace bullying (mobbing) with compensation for biological and non-pecuniary damage — Tribunale di Santa Maria Capua Vetere

Sector: public administration — Role: administrative officer — Applicable regime: art. 18 St. Lav.

A female public official with over twenty years of service consulted our firm after having been progressively stripped of all duties of responsibility, deprived of her office, relegated to a communal space without an individual workstation, and systematically excluded from staff meetings, all coinciding with a conflict that arose with the head of the office following the submission of a trade union petition. Her physician had certified an adjustment disorder with anxiety and depressive symptoms attributable to the work situation, which had forced the employee to take several periods of sick leave.

Our firm reconstructed the timeline of the employer's conduct through the systematic collection of emails, internal memos, service orders, and witness statements from colleagues, documenting the systematic and prolonged nature of the hostile actions. The forensic medical report quantified the permanent biological damage (danno biologico) at a significant percentage of permanent disability, resulting in financial loss due to reduced working capacity. The Court recognized the workplace harassment (mobbing) and awarded compensation encompassing biological damage, subjective moral damage, and professional damage arising from the loss of career opportunities (perdita di chance). The employee subsequently resigned for just cause, retaining her entitlement to unemployment benefit (NASpI) and to the full severance indemnity (trattamento di fine rapporto - TFR).

Mobbing established — Full compensation awarded — Unemployment benefit (NASpI) recognized
Case no. 4

Disciplinary dismissal annulled for procedural defect pursuant to art. 7 Workers' Statute (St. Lav.) — Tribunale di Napoli

Sector: catering and hospitality — Role: receptionist — Applicable regime: art. 18 Workers' Statute (St. Lav.)

An employee who had worked for eight years as a receptionist in a luxury hotel in the centre of Naples had received a written disciplinary notice in which he was accused of having abandoned his workstation during the night shift and of having engaged in prolonged private conversations with guests, in violation of company regulations. The disciplinary notice was dated a Friday evening. The worker, who had Saturday off and resumed duty on Monday, submitted his written justifications on Monday morning, within the statutory deadline of 5 days. The employer imposed disciplinary dismissal the following Saturday, just eight days after the notice.

The defence pleaded the violation of the time limit for defence under art. 7, paragraph 5, Workers' Statute (St. Lav.): the 5 days are calculated as days of actual opportunity to prepare the defence, and the Saturday rest day could not be included in the calculation. The applicable National Collective Labour Agreement (CCNL Turismo) explicitly provided that the time limit for defence was not to be calculated on scheduled rest days. The Labour Court upheld the procedural objection, declaring the dismissal unlawful due to a defect in the disciplinary procedure and ordering the employer to pay the indemnity provided for by art. 18, paragraph 6, Workers' Statute (St. Lav.) (weak indemnity protection: 10 months' compensation), thereby sparing the client the risk of having to return to work in an environment that was by then irremediably compromised.

Dismissal annulled — 10 months' indemnity awarded
Case no. 5

Resignation for just cause with recognition of unemployment benefit (NASpI) — Tribunale di Nola

Sector: retail trade — Role: store manager — Applicable regime: art. 18 Workers' Statute (St. Lav.)

A store manager with seven years of seniority consulted our firm in a situation of severe professional hardship: for approximately eight months she had not received her full salary, receiving reduced and irregular sums accompanied by verbal promises of regularisation; in the meantime, she had been unilaterally transferred to a branch located more than sixty kilometres from her residence, without compliance with the procedures set forth in art. 2103 of the Italian Civil Code (c.c.) and without the employer demonstrating proven technical, organisational, and productive reasons. The employee had reported the situation in writing on multiple occasions without receiving any reply. The situation of work-related stress and financial hardship had caused a significant deterioration in her psychophysical health, documented by her attending physician.

Our firm prepared a formal notice of default (diffida) to the employer, analytically documenting the failure to pay wages (with bank statements and incomplete payslips), the unlawfulness of the transfer (absence of documented organisational reasons, distance exceeding the threshold of reasonable burden), and the damage to health. The formal notice, which remained unanswered, enabled the employee to submit her resignation for just cause through the ministerial online platform, with immediate effect and without obligation of notice. The Labour Court, petitioned to obtain a judicial declaration of just cause and an order for payment of unpaid wages, severance indemnity (TFR), indemnity in lieu of notice, and compensation for biological damage (danno biologico), fully granted the claims. INPS recognized the entitlement to unemployment benefit (NASpI), granting the monthly unemployment allowance for the maximum statutory period.

Just cause established — Back pay + compensation + unemployment benefit (NASpI) recognized
