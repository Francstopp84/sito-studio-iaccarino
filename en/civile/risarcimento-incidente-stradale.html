Have you been the victim of a road traffic accident?
Compensation is not a favour — it is your right.

Every year in Italy tens of thousands of people fall victim to road traffic accidents. Many receive insufficient compensation, accepting settlement offers that do not account for the actual damage sustained. Biological damage (danno biologico), moral damage, permanent disability, loss of income: each head of damage deserves to be assessed and quantified rigorously. A lawyer can make the difference between what the insurance company offers and what is rightfully due to you.

Regulatory framework

The legal rules protecting the victim

Compensation for road traffic accidents does not stem from a single legal provision, but from a structured system of complementary sources: the general law of civil liability, the specific rules governing vehicular traffic, the private insurance code, and the actuarial tables for the quantification of biological damage.

Art. 2043 c.c. — Tort liability (Responsabilità aquiliana) The cornerstone provision of non-contractual civil liability: whoever causes unjust damage to another, intentionally or through negligence, is liable to pay damages. It forms the foundation of the right to compensation for every head of pecuniary and non-pecuniary damage arising from the accident.
Art. 2054 c.c. — Operation of vehicles Special provision governing road traffic liability. It establishes a presumption of fault against the driver: the driver is relieved of liability only by proving having done everything possible to avoid the damage. Paragraph 2 provides for equal liability in collisions between vehicles unless the exclusive fault of the other driver is proven. Paragraph 3 extends joint and several liability to the vehicle owner alongside the driver.
D.Lgs. 209/2005 — Private Insurance Code (art. 149 — Direct compensation) Regulates the direct compensation system (indennizzo diretto): the accident victim may claim compensation directly from their own insurer rather than the liable party's insurer, for accidents involving solely property damage or physical injuries classified as minor permanent injuries (micropermanenti, up to 9% permanent disability). The procedure is fast but often penalising: legal assistance is essential to prevent undervalued settlements.
D.P.R. 254/2006 — Direct compensation regulation Governs the deadlines and procedures for claims settlement under the direct compensation scheme. The insurer must present a reasoned settlement offer within 30 days (property damage) or 60 days (minor permanent injuries) of receiving the compensation claim accompanied by complete documentation. If these deadlines expire without an adequate response, the insurer incurs aggravated liability.
Milan Tables — Settlement of biological damage The Tables of the Court of Milan (Tribunale di Milano), widely used as the national reference standard, define the monetary values for the equitable assessment of permanent and temporary biological damage based on the victim's age and degree of disability. Each percentage point of permanent disability has a specific value, increasing progressively with the severity of the harm. Disregarding them means accepting lower settlements.
D.Lgs. 209/2005, art. 138-139 — Table for major and minor permanent injuries Article 138 governs so-called major permanent injuries (macropermanenti, disability exceeding 9%), providing for a single national table approved by ministerial decree (d.m.) (still awaiting definitive adoption, with supplementary reliance on the Milan Tables). Article 139 governs minor permanent injuries (micropermanenti, up to 9%), using a ministerial table with fixed values.

How the Milan Tables work

The Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano) provide for progressive settlement: the higher the degree of permanent disability and the younger the victim, the higher the compensation. By way of example, a 10% permanent disability awarded to a 40-year-old individual is worth approximately 35,000–45,000 euros under the 2024 Tables, to which customization of damages, temporary disability, and moral damages are added. A 30% disability can exceed 200,000 euros. Being aware of these parameters is the first step toward avoiding being undercompensated.

What you can claim

Compensable heads of damage

Compensation for a road traffic accident is not limited to immediate medical expenses. It encompasses every harm that the accident caused to your physical, psychological, emotional, and financial well-being. Each head of damage must be documented, assessed, and properly quantified.

●

Permanent biological damage

The permanent and definitive impairment of a person’s psycho-physical integrity, assessed in percentage terms by the medical-legal expert. Each point of permanent disability corresponds to an increasing tabular value (Milan Tables — Tabelle di Milano). It includes damage to health in all its dimensions: physical, relational, sexual, and aesthetic.

Articles 138-139 D.Lgs. 209/2005 — Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano)
●

Temporary biological damage

The impairment of health during the period of illness and convalescence following the accident, prior to the stabilization of sequelae. It is divided into total temporary disability (inabilità temporanea totale — ITT) and partial temporary disability (inabilità temporanea parziale — ITP). The Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano) provide a daily rate for each category, multiplied by the certified days of disability.

Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano) — ITT / ITP
●

Moral damages

Subjective non-pecuniary harm: inner suffering, anguish, and psychological distress caused by the injurious event and its sequelae.

Art. 2059 c.c.
●

Pecuniary damages — Loss of earnings

Loss of earnings caused by the accident: income lost during convalescence, reduction in future earning capacity as a result of permanent sequelae, and loss of professional opportunities. Quantification requires income documentation and, often, actuarial advice to project future losses.

Art. 2043 c.c. — Art. 1223 c.c.
●

Pecuniary damages — Out-of-pocket expenses

Expenses actually incurred as a result of the accident: medical and rehabilitation costs, purchase of assistive devices and orthopedic aids, home care, transport costs for treatment, and home adaptation. Each item must be documented with invoices and receipts, including foreseeable and quantifiable future expenses.

Art. 1223 c.c.
●

Fatal injury damages (iure proprio and iure hereditatis)

In the event of the victim’s death: family members are entitled to compensation iure proprio for loss of the family relationship (danno da perdita del rapporto parentale, encompassing existential and moral damages), varying according to the intensity of the emotional bond. Compensation iure hereditatis includes catastrophic damage suffered by the victim in the interval between the event and death, transmitted to the heirs. The 2024 Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano) provide indicative values for loss of family relationship damages.

Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano) (loss of family relationship damages)
●

Minor permanent injuries (< 9% disability)

The special regime under art. 139 D.Lgs. 209/2005 governs minor injuries with disability up to 9%. Whiplash typically falls into this category, with specific evidentiary requirements (mandatory visual verification of objective traumatic injuries).

Art. 139 D.Lgs. 209/2005

How we achieve full compensation

Strategies for your compensation

Obtaining full compensation is not automatic. It requires a structured strategy, from documenting the damages to selecting the appropriate compensation procedure, from negotiating with the insurance company to taking legal action when necessary.

01

Independent medical-legal assessment

An independent medical-legal expert appointed by the party is our primary tool for documenting and quantifying biological damages. Their report is essential to establish the causal link between the accident and the injuries, classify permanent sequelae with the correct percentage of disability, distinguish any pre-existing conditions, and produce an independent evaluation compared to the — often understated — assessment of the insurance company's expert. An accurate medical-legal assessment can mean tens of thousands of euros more in compensation.

02

Comprehensive tabular quantification

Quantifying damages according to the 2024 Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano), including every compensable head of damage, is the second pillar of the strategy. Many victims accept offers that overlook the customization of moral damages, days of temporary disability, or future loss of earnings. We prepare a comprehensive analytical calculation — item by item — to document the true amount of the compensation claim prior to any negotiation.

03

Direct compensation vs. ordinary legal action

The choice of compensation procedure is strategic. Direct compensation (indennizzo diretto under art. 149 D.Lgs. 209/2005) is fast but often insufficient for severe injuries, and it is not mandatory: in cases of disputed contributory negligence, injuries exceeding 9%, or uncooperative insurers, it may be more effective to proceed through ordinary legal action against the at-fault party’s insurance company. We assess case by case the most advantageous path for the victim.

04

Negotiation with the insurance company

Before initiating legal proceedings, out-of-court negotiation is often the swiftest and most efficient route. We send a documented formal claim for compensation and, if the offer is inadequate, we initiate the mandatory mediation procedure (where applicable) and steer negotiations toward a settlement that reflects the correct tabular values. A well-drafted formal notice (lettera di diffida), complete with an analytical calculation of damages, is often sufficient to obtain an appropriate offer.

05

Judicial action and court-appointed medical-legal expert (CTU)

When the insurance company does not make an equitable offer or denies liability, ordinary legal proceedings are the final instrument. In court, the court-appointed expert (Consulente Tecnico d’Ufficio — CTU) designated by the court carries out an impartial medical-legal assessment that binds the determination of damages. A robust litigation strategy — with well-organized evidentiary briefs, documentary proof, and witness testimony — maximizes the final compensation.

06

Differential damages

If the victim has already accepted a sum from the insurer or signed a discharge receipt (quietanza liberatoria), it is not necessarily too late. In certain cases, it is possible to take legal action for so-called differential damages (danno differenziale): the difference between what was received and what was actually owed, particularly where the receipt was signed without full awareness of the extent of the damage or before sequelae stabilized. We examine every signed document to assess whether this course of action is viable.

Results achieved

5 resolved compensation cases

The following cases illustrate the types of disputes handled and the tangible results achieved for our clients. The names of the parties and certain identifying details have been omitted for confidentiality reasons, in full compliance with legal professional privilege and personal data protection regulations.

1

Rear-end collision with whiplash — Contested minor permanent disability (micropermanente) — Tribunale di Napoli

Type: minor permanent injuries (art. 139 D.Lgs. 209/2005) — Permanent disability: 4% — Initial insurance offer: € 1,200

A 42-year-old teacher was rear-ended while stationary in a queue of traffic at a traffic light in eastern Naples by a passenger car traveling at low speed. Immediately following the impact, the woman did not exhibit obvious injuries; however, in the subsequent hours, she experienced acute neck pain, headaches, and paresthesias in the upper limbs. The emergency room diagnosed a grade I–II cervical sprain with post-traumatic cervicalgia. The liable party’s insurance company issued a settlement offer of € 1,200, deeming the injuries insufficiently documented from an instrumental diagnostic perspective, as required by art. 139 D.Lgs. 209/2005 following the amendments introduced by the 2017 Stability Law (Legge di stabilità 2017).

The firm arranged an expert medico-legal report for the party, which documented the causal link between the accident and the clinical symptoms by means of an MRI of the cervical spine performed 30 days after the event, confirming an objectively verifiable musculo-ligamentous injury. The expert report established a permanent disability of 4% alongside 30 days of total temporary disability and 20 days of partial disability at 50%. An assisted negotiation procedure (negoziazione assistita) was initiated. In response to the documentation produced, the company made a new offer of € 4,800, which was rejected. The Tribunale di Napoli, having obtained the court-appointed medico-legal expert report (CTU), awarded a total of € 7,400, including customized permanent biological damage, temporary disability, and documented medical expenses, plus statutory interest from the date of the accident.

Compensation obtained: € 7,400 (+518% compared to the initial offer)
2

Severe accident with 35% permanent disability — Corte d’Appello di Napoli

Type: major permanent injury (macropermanente) — Permanent disability: 35% — Initial insurance offer: € 85,000

A 38-year-old construction worker, riding his motorcycle, was struck by a car turning left without giving right of way at a signalized intersection in the municipality of Caserta. The impact caused extremely severe injuries: open fracture of the right femur, fractures of the tibia and fibula, and injury to the left brachial plexus with partial impairment of hand function. The rehabilitation process lasted eighteen months with numerous surgical interventions. Upon the stabilization of the sequelae, the insurance company’s medical examiner recognized a permanent disability of 22% and made an offer of € 85,000, covering all heads of damage. The client, unconvinced of the adequacy of the amount, consulted the firm.

The party’s expert medico-legal report established a permanent disability of 35%, analytically documenting the impairment of working capacity: as a construction worker, the partial loss of use of his left hand and limitations in ambulation resulted in a substantial inability to perform his specific job duties. The calculation of loss of earnings (lucro cessante), prepared with actuarial advice, projected on a statistical basis the future loss of income up to retirement age. In the first instance, the Tribunale di Napoli awarded € 220,000. The insurance company appealed. The Corte d’Appello confirmed the quantification, marginally reducing the loss of earnings to a total of € 195,000, comprising biological damage according to the 2023 Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano 2023), customized moral damage, existential damage resulting from the loss of specific working capacity, and capitalized future medical expenses.

Final compensation: € 195,000 (+129% compared to the initial offer)
3

Fatal accident — Compensation to family members in their own right (iure proprio) — Tribunale di Roma

Type: fatal injury — Injured parties: spouse and two minor children — Initial insurance offer: € 160,000 (entire family)

A 45-year-old man, father of two children aged 8 and 12, lost his life in a head-on road traffic accident on a provincial road in Lazio, caused by the opposing vehicle crossing into his lane. The dynamics of the accident were clearly attributable to the driver of the other vehicle, covered by a third-party liability policy (polizza RC). The insurance company made an overall offer for the spouse and children of € 160,000, an amount the widow rejected as insufficient, turning to the firm after having independently consulted the insurance adjuster without success for over six months after the event.

The firm structured the claim by distinguishing the damage suffered in their own right (iure proprio) by each surviving family member. For the widow: loss of the spousal relationship (moral component and existential component for the termination of shared family life), in addition to financial damage from the loss of her husband’s economic contribution, quantified on the basis of the man’s income and projected statistically up to retirement age. For each child: loss of the parental relationship, with specific consideration given to their young age and the deprivation of their father’s formative, educational, and emotional support. The criteria of the 2024 Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano 2024) for loss of next of kin were applied, which provide a settlement range for the spouse from € 168,000 to € 336,000 and for each minor child from € 126,000 to € 252,000. Following unsuccessful mediation, the Tribunale di Roma awarded a total of € 580,000 for all family members, which included the minimum table values adjusted upwards due to the intensity of the emotional bond documented through witness evidence, in addition to financial damage.

Total compensation: € 580,000 (+262% compared to the initial offer)
4

Accident with disputed contributory negligence — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Type: 18% permanent biological damage — Contributory negligence raised by the company: 50% attributed to the victim

A 55-year-old cyclist was struck by a passenger car in a residential area. The driver of the vehicle claimed that the cyclist suddenly entered the roadway from the cycle lane without signaling, thereby causing the collision. Acknowledging the disputed dynamics, the driver’s insurance company made an offer reduced by 50% by way of a causal contribution attributed to the victim, paying € 18,000 against a total damage that its own expert report assessed at € 36,000 (with permanent disability at 10%). The cyclist had suffered multiple rib fractures, pneumothorax, and liver contusion, requiring 90 days of hospital and rehabilitative care. The party’s expert report established a permanent disability of 18%.

The dispute over the accident dynamics required the involvement of an expert in accident reconstruction (consulente cinematico) instructed by the party, who analyzed the skid marks, footage from the surveillance camera of the commercial premises opposite (obtained by formal notice of demand [diffida] served on the operator), and the reports drawn up by the Carabinieri. The reconstruction demonstrated that the cyclist had already been on the roadway for at least 8 seconds prior to the collision, thus being visible to the driver with sufficient advance notice to stop or perform an emergency maneuver: the cause of the accident was the driver’s inattention, distracted by using a smartphone (substantiated by data traffic records from his telephone, admitted into the case files). The Tribunale di Napoli Nord excluded any contributory negligence on the part of the victim, awarding full compensation: € 98,000, including 18% permanent biological damage under the 2024 Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano 2024) for a 55-year-old individual, customized moral damage, 90 days of total temporary disability, medical expenses, and existential damage resulting from the inability to engage in amateur cycling.

Contributory negligence excluded — Full compensation: € 98,000
5

Medical malpractice post-accident — Aggravation of damage due to delayed diagnosis — Tribunale di Napoli

Type: cumulative liability (driver + healthcare facility) — Final permanent biological damage: 28%

A 26-year-old man sustained an injury to his cervical spine in a road traffic collision. In the emergency department of a hospital in Naples, healthcare staff performed a standard X-ray which was reported as negative, discharging the patient with a simple cervical collar. Two weeks later, due to a persistent motor deficit in the upper limbs, the patient visited a private clinic where an MRI of the cervical spine revealed a C5 burst fracture with spinal cord compression, requiring urgent neurosurgical intervention. The surgery, performed with a fourteen-day delay from the trauma, resulted in a less favourable surgical outcome than would have been possible with a timely diagnosis: the neurologist assessed that an immediate diagnosis would have allowed an 80% functional recovery, compared to the 40% actually achieved.

The firm structured the claim for damages on two cumulative grounds, involving concurrent joint and several liability among multiple parties: on the one hand, the driver of the vehicle responsible for the collision (along with their motor third-party liability insurer, RC auto) for the personal injury (danno biologico) resulting from the accident in its entirety, including the worsening attributable to the delayed diagnosis as a consequence of the original causal event; on the other hand, the hospital facility and the emergency department physicians for medical malpractice pursuant to art. 7 of the Gelli-Bianco Law (L. 24/2017), for failure to diagnose and delayed treatment. The party-appointed medico-legal expert assessment estimated the overall permanent biological impairment at 28%, and the portion causally attributable to medical negligence at 50%. Following mandatory mediation pursuant to art. 5 of D.Lgs. 28/2010, the liability insurer paid € 120,000 under a settlement agreement, while the proceedings against the hospital facility concluded with a judgment ordering the payment of a further € 65,000, for the differential of damage attributable to negligent medical conduct, in addition to legal costs.

Total compensation: € 185,000 (two distinct liable parties)
