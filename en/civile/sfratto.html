Eviction for arrears:
know your rights before it is too late.

Receiving an eviction notice (intimazione di sfratto) is one of the most destabilising events in a tenant's life: the home, the place where family life unfolds, is called into question by summary and expedited judicial proceedings. However, eviction proceedings are not a final condemnation: precise legal instruments exist — opposition, the grace period (termine di grazia), curing of the arrears (sanatoria della morosità) — that can halt or completely overturn the outcome. On the other hand, the landlord who is unable to recover rent and obtain repossession of the property has the right to act with all the instruments made available by law. In both cases, the promptness and competence of the lawyer make the difference.

Regulatory framework

The rules governing eviction

Eviction proceedings are governed by a stratified set of rules combining the Italian Code of Civil Procedure (codice di procedura civile), the Fair Rent Act (legge sull’equo canone), and the Residential Tenancies Act. The Cartabia Reform (D.Lgs. 149/2022) introduced significant amendments to tenancy proceedings (rito locatizio). Understanding the regulatory framework in its entirety is the first step towards building an effective defence or acting properly as a landlord.

Code of Civil Procedure

Artt. 657–669 c.p.c. — Eviction proceedings

Title V of Book IV of the procedural code governs the entire procedure for notice to quit (licenza) and eviction. Art. 657 c.p.c. provides that the landlord may serve notice to quit for termination of the lease (licenza per finita locazione) on the tenant prior to the expiry of the agreement. Art. 658 c.p.c. governs eviction for arrears (sfratto per morosità): a landlord wishing to seek termination of the contract for non-payment of rent may serve an eviction notice and, simultaneously, summon the tenant before the judge for confirmation (convalida). Art. 663 c.p.c. provides for the confirmation of the eviction in the event of failure to appear or failure to enter an opposition by the tenant. Art. 665 c.p.c. allows the judge, in the event of an opposition, to issue a provisional order for repossession (ordinanza provvisoria di rilascio) upon the landlord's request where the opposition is not based on written evidence or does not appear grounded. Art. 667 c.p.c. establishes the deadline for vacating the premises in the confirmation order.

Special summary proceeding with deferred adversarial hearing (a contraddittorio differito)
Law of 27 July 1978, no. 392

Art. 55 L. 392/1978 — Grace period (termine di grazia) and curing of arrears (sanatoria della morosità)

Art. 55 of the Fair Rent Act (legge sull’equo canone) is the primary defence instrument for the defaulting tenant. The judge, upon request of the tenant appearing at the confirmation hearing (udienza di convalida), may grant a grace period not exceeding ninety days for the payment of overdue rent, statutory interest, and procedural expenses. If the tenant performs in full within the assigned time limit, the eviction proceedings are extinguished and the lease agreement remains in force. As a rule, the grace period may be granted only once during the course of the same lease agreement. The rule applies to residential leases and, by extended case-law application, also to non-residential leases under specific circumstances.

Maximum time limit: 90 days — Effect: extinguishment of proceedings upon full performance
Law of 9 December 1998, no. 431

Art. 6 L. 431/1998 — Residential leases

Law 431/1998 governs leases of urban real estate for residential use, introducing the regime of freely negotiated rent contracts (4+4 years) and agreed-rate rent contracts (3+2 years). Art. 6 governs the landlord's termination and the grounds for denial of renewal upon the first expiry, including the landlord's need for themselves or their family unit, the intention to carry out complete renovation, or the sale of the property. Eviction proceedings for arrears involving residential contracts are governed in conjunction with this law and the procedural rules of the c.p.c. Unregistered contracts are penalised with nullity pursuant to art. 1, co. 4, L. 431/1998, with significant consequences for eviction proceedings: the tenant may plead the nullity of the contract and obtain restitution of rent paid in excess.

4+4 contract (freely negotiated) — 3+2 contract (agreed-rate)
Code of Civil Procedure

Art. 447-bis c.p.c. — Tenancy proceedings (controversie in materia di locazione)

Art. 447-bis c.p.c. extends to disputes concerning urban property leases, gratuitous loans for use (comodato), and business leases the rules of labour proceedings (artt. 409 ss. c.p.c.), introducing a proceeding characterised by concentration, immediacy, and orality. When the eviction is opposed and the proceeding converts into an ordinary proceeding on the merits, the trial is conducted according to tenancy proceedings (rito locatizio): a single hearing for discussion, concentrated evidentiary phase, and ex officio powers of the judge in collecting evidence. The Cartabia Reform (D.Lgs. 149/2022, entered into force on 28 February 2023 for proceedings instituted thereafter) affected tenancy proceedings, providing for the possibility of filing written notes in lieu of the hearing and modifying evidentiary preclusions. The introduction of the preliminary admissibility filter on appeal (art. 348-bis c.p.c.) is also relevant for judgments in tenancy matters.

Special labour procedure applied to tenancies — Cartabia Reform applicable from 28.02.2023

Cartabia Reform — Relevant procedural novelties for eviction

D.Lgs. 149/2022 (so-called Cartabia Reform), applicable to proceedings instituted after 28 February 2023, introduced amendments that directly affect tenancy proceedings:

  • Strengthened mandatory mediation: disputes concerning tenancies remain subject to mandatory mediation under art. 5, co. 1-bis, D.Lgs. 28/2010, now reinforced by the requirement of mandatory personal participation of the parties (not only of their counsel) and mandatory legal representation.
  • Hearing in written form (art. 127-ter c.p.c.): the judge may order that the hearing be held by filing written notes in lieu of an in-person hearing, with an impact on the timeframe and procedures for handling eviction cases converted into ordinary proceedings.
  • Simplified access to summary proceedings: the Reform has rationalised the system of summary proceedings, with potential effects on the overall duration of repossession proceedings and on the enforceability of eviction orders.
  • Amendments to forced execution: measures affecting enforcement proceedings that concern the procedures for repossession of real estate and the management of eviction orders in the compulsory enforcement phase.
Types

The main categories of eviction

Not all eviction proceedings (procedimenti di sfratto) are the same. The nature of the breach entitling the landlord to act, the type of contract, the designated use of the property, and the position of the tenant determine profoundly different procedures, time limits, and defence options. Correctly identifying the type of eviction is the first step towards an effective defence.

Art. 658 c.p.c. — Main

Eviction for Rent Arrears (Sfratto per Morosità)

The landlord may serve an eviction notice for non-payment of rent (sfratto per morosità) when the tenant fails to pay the rent or ancillary charges (oneri accessori). There is no statutory minimum number of unpaid rent instalments (unless stipulated by contract): even the non-payment of a single rent instalment, if overdue by at least twenty days, may entitle the landlord to serve the notice. The procedure is summary and concludes with an automatic order of validation (convalida) if the tenant fails to appear or does not raise an opposition. If the tenant appears and requests a grace period (termine di grazia, art. 55 L. 392/1978), the judge may grant up to ninety days to remedy the arrears (sanatoria della morosità).

Art. 657 c.p.c. — End of contract

Eviction for Expiry of Lease (Sfratto per Finita Locazione)

When the lease reaches its natural expiration and the tenant fails to vacate the property, the landlord may initiate the eviction proceeding for expiry of lease (sfratto per finita locazione). In this case, no grace period (termine di grazia) is available: the tenant cannot “remedy” the situation by paying arrears, because the right to occupy the property has ceased upon expiration of the contract. The landlord must have complied with the statutory notice periods for termination (disdetta) provided by law (generally six months prior to expiry for residential leases). If the landlord has not served timely notice of termination, the lease is tacitly renewed and eviction for expiry of lease cannot be brought before the subsequent expiration date.

Art. 657 c.p.c. — Prior to expiry

Notice to Quit Prior to Expiry (Licenza per Finita Locazione)

The notice to quit prior to expiry (licenza per finita locazione) is the legal instrument whereby the landlord orders the tenant to vacate the property prior to the expiration of the lease, in order to have it vacant at the time of expiry itself. Unlike an eviction notice, the notice to quit is served while the lease is still ongoing but approaching its end: the landlord thus seeks to obtain from the judge an enforceable title (titolo esecutivo) already ready for the date of expiry, without having to commence a new proceeding. The notice to quit is automatically converted into an eviction order upon expiration of the contractual term, if the tenant has not vacated the property voluntarily. This instrument is particularly useful when it is anticipated that the tenant will not vacate spontaneously at the end of the lease.

Art. 3, co. 1, L. 431/1998 — Qualified grounds

Eviction for Landlord’s Personal Use (Sfratto per Necessità)

The landlord may obtain early repossession of property leased for residential use at the first expiration date (after the first four years in a 4+4 contract, or after the first three years in a 3+2 contract) when one of the statutory grounds for refusal of renewal provided by Art. 3 of L. 431/1998 applies: residential need for oneself or for a family member within the second degree of kinship, sale of the vacant property, or comprehensive renovation. A tenant evicted on fictitious grounds of “need” — that is, when the landlord does not actually use the property in the manner declared — is entitled to compensation for damages under Art. 3, co. 3, L. 431/1998, equal to a minimum of thirty-six monthly instalments of the last rent paid.

Art. 663 co. 3 — Art. 665 c.p.c.

Opposition to Eviction (Opposizione allo Sfratto)

A tenant intending to challenge the merits of the eviction — due to defective service, non-existence of the claimed debt, irregularities in the contract, or substantive defences — must raise an opposition (opposizione) at the validation hearing (udienza di convalida) scheduled in the notice. The opposition has the effect of preventing automatic validation and converting the summary proceeding into ordinary proceedings on the merits under the procedural rules for tenancy disputes (rito locatizio, art. 447-bis c.p.c.). In the event of an opposition, the judge may issue a provisional order for repossession under Art. 665 c.p.c. if the opposition is not supported by written evidence or does not appear manifestly well-founded, with the consequent obligation for the tenant to vacate the property pending the judgment on the merits.

Pay attention to time limits: the validation hearing (udienza di convalida) scheduled in the eviction notice is the crucial moment. A tenant who fails to appear or does not raise an opposition at that hearing definitively forfeits the opportunity to challenge the eviction: the judge issues the validation order upon request of the landlord. Generally, between twenty and thirty days elapse from the date of receipt of the notice to the day of the hearing: it is essential to consult a lawyer immediately.

Defence Tools

Strategies for the Tenant and for the Landlord

Eviction proceedings provide specific legal instruments both to the tenant seeking to defend their home and to the landlord seeking to repossess the property as quickly as possible. Awareness of these tools — and promptness in their use — is decisive for the outcome of the proceeding.

Art. 55 L. 392/1978

Grace Period (Remedy of Arrears — Termine di Grazia)

The primary defence tool for a tenant in arrears. Upon request of the tenant appearing at the validation hearing, the judge grants a deadline of up to ninety days to pay all rent arrears, statutory interest, and procedural costs. Full payment within the assigned deadline extinguishes the eviction proceedings and keeps the lease agreement in force. As a rule, the grace period may be granted only once for the same lease.

Formal and Procedural Defects

Procedural Objections (Eccezioni Procedurali)

Eviction is a formal proceeding: defects in the notice (atto di intimazione) may lead to its nullity or preclude validation. Among the most effective objections: defective service of the notice (e.g. service upon an unauthorised person, incorrect location, untraceability not handled correctly); failure to state the statutory requirements in the writ (rent amount, expiration date, legal grounds); lack of subject-matter or territorial jurisdiction; lack of standing to sue of the landlord (e.g. co-ownership rules not complied with, lack of power of attorney). A defect in service raised within the applicable time limits can force the landlord to restart the proceeding from the beginning.

Art. 663 co. 3 c.p.c.

Opposition and Disputing the Claim (Opposizione e Contestazione del Credito)

The tenant may oppose the eviction by disputing the existence or amount of the debt claimed by the landlord: payments made but not credited, set-off against claims arising from works performed on the property (Art. 1576 c.c.), nullity of the contract due to formal or substantive defects, actual rent differing from that declared (undisclosed agreements / patti occulti), objections concerning ancillary charges not due or contested expenses. The opposition converts the summary proceeding into ordinary proceedings on the merits, reversing the burden of proof on specific issues and opening the statutory deadlines for evidentiary submissions.

D.Lgs. 28/2010 — Art. 5 co. 1-bis

Mandatory Mediation (Mediazione Obbligatoria)

Tenancy disputes are subject to a mandatory mediation attempt as a condition of admissibility (condizione di procedibilità) of the judicial claim. Mediation, administered by bodies accredited by the Ministry of Justice (Ministero della Giustizia), offers a concrete opportunity to reach out-of-court settlements: the tenant can negotiate a gradual repayment plan, and the landlord can obtain guarantees for voluntary repossession by a certain date without facing the lengthy duration of ordinary court proceedings. Failure to carry out the mandatory mediation attempt renders the claim inadmissible (improcedibile), which may also be raised by the court on its own motion (d’ufficio) no later than the first hearing.

Art. 1578 c.c. — Art. 1491 c.c.

Rent Reduction and Defence of Non-Performance (Eccezione di Inadempimento)

A tenant who has partially paid the rent due to flaws or defects in the property that reduce its suitability for the agreed use may raise the plea of non-performance (eccezione di inadempimento, art. 1460 c.c.) and claim a proportional reduction of the rent (art. 1578 c.c.). This strategy is particularly effective when the landlord has omitted extraordinary maintenance, the property presents documentable structural issues, or there are defects in the leased property that were known or discoverable. Case law has also recognised rent reduction in the event of malfunctions of condominium installations attributable to the landlord.

Out-of-court settlement (accordo stragiudiziale)

Settlement and Agreed Vacation of Property (transazione e rilascio concordato)

In many cases, the best solution is a negotiated one: the landlord obtains the certainty of regaining possession of the vacant property by an agreed date, without the risks and costs of enforcement proceedings; the tenant secures the necessary time to find new accommodation and, in some cases, a reduction or partial write-off of the accrued debt. The agreement may be formalised during mediation (with the enforceability of an executive title - titolo esecutivo) or before the court (conciliation minutes - verbale di conciliazione pursuant to art. 185 c.p.c.). Our firm routinely negotiates agreed vacation agreements that safeguard the needs of both parties, avoiding the enforcement phase.

Case no. 1

Eviction for non-payment of rent successfully challenged — Defective service — Tribunale di Napoli

Rent in dispute: € 14,400 (12 months × € 1,200) — Risk: immediate vacation of the family home

A family with three minor children, tenants of an apartment in the Fuorigrotta district, received an eviction notice for non-payment of rent (intimazione di sfratto per morosità) served by deposit of a copy at the town hall, pursuant to art. 143 c.p.c. (recipient temporarily untraceable), even though the tenant resided permanently in the property and was domiciled there. The landlord had instructed the judicial officer by falsely declaring that the tenant was untraceable, in order to expedite the summary proceedings and surprise the tenant at the validation hearing with a final order already obtained.

The tenant, having become aware of the validation order that had already been issued, contacted our firm. We immediately lodged an opposition to enforcement (opposizione all’esecuzione) pursuant to art. 615 c.p.c. and, at the same time, filed an application (ricorso) for a declaration of nullity of the service of the originating document, substantiating through a historical residence certificate, utility bills, the children's school enrolment, and regularly collected registered letters that the tenant was permanently living in the property. The Tribunale di Napoli declared the service null and void, with the consequent lack of due process (contraddittorio) and nullity of the validation order, ordering the renewal of the proceedings ab initio. At the new validation hearing, the tenant requested and obtained the grace period (termine di grazia) pursuant to art. 55 of L. 392/1978, proceeding with full payment of the rent arrears within the sixty-day time limit granted by the judge. The lease agreement remained in force and the family retained their home.

Validation declared null and void — Grace period granted — Lease maintained
Case no. 2

Grace period granted — Rent arrears due to Covid-19 — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Rent arrears: € 9,600 (8 months × € 1,200) — Lease: residential 4+4

A self-employed worker in the restaurant sector, renting an apartment in the municipality of Pozzuoli, found himself in serious financial difficulty following the forced closure of his business during the pandemic. He had accumulated eight months of rent arrears for a total of € 9,600. The landlord, having waited in vain for several months in the hope of a spontaneous resumption of payments, served an eviction notice for non-payment of rent. The tenant, in evident distress, appeared at the validation hearing without legal counsel, risking automatic validation due to lack of formal technical opposition.

Contacted by telephone by the tenant a few days before the hearing, our firm took on the mandate in extremis. At the validation hearing, counsel filed documentation proving the tenant's financial situation (tax returns from previous years, INPS documentation concerning the non-repayable grants received, bank statements showing a progressively recovering positive balance), the seriousness of the intention to remedy the default (a written agreement with a family member for the provision of the liquidity necessary to clear the debt), and the long tenancy relationship that had continued uninterrupted for seven previous years without any defaults. The judge, taking a positive view of the documentation produced and the tenant's past reliability, granted the maximum grace period (termine di grazia) of ninety days. The tenant paid the entire overdue amount, plus interest and costs, within the sixtieth day. The eviction proceedings were extinguished by operation of law.

90-day grace period — Full performance — Proceedings extinguished
Case no. 3

Eviction upon expiration of lease — Failure to give notice of termination — Tacit renewal — Tribunale di Napoli

Lease: residential 4+4 — Rent: € 900/month — Outcome: eviction declared inadmissible

A tenant in her seventies living in an apartment in the Vomero district, who had resided in the property for over eighteen years through successive lease renewals, received an eviction notice upon lease expiration (intimazione di sfratto per finita locazione) served by the new owner of the property, who had purchased it and intended to use it for personal purposes. The notice was served four months before the expiration of the lease, which was a 4+4 contract that had reached its second maturity date. The tenant contacted our firm in a state of severe distress, convinced that she would have to vacate the apartment within a few weeks.

Analysis of the lease file revealed a decisive circumstance: the original landlord, prior to the transfer of ownership, had not sent the notice of termination within the statutory time limit (six months prior to expiration), as required by art. 3 of L. 431/1998. The new owner had purchased the property when the lease had already been tacitly renewed for an additional period of four years, running from the original expiration date. Our firm filed an opposition to the eviction, documenting the failure to serve notice within the time limit and the consequent automatic renewal of the lease, submitting the late registered letter of termination, the registered envelopes, the postmark, and the text of the contract. The Tribunale di Napoli declared the eviction upon expiration of lease inadmissible, recognizing the tenant's right to remain in the property for the entire renewal period, protecting her right to housing for an additional four years.

Eviction declared inadmissible — Tacit renewal recognized — 4-year stay secured
Case no. 4

Eviction from commercial property — Landlord's breach of contract defence — Tribunale di Napoli

Property: ground-floor shop, historic centre — Rent: € 2,800/month — Arrears in dispute: € 33,600 (12 months)

The owner of a clothing store in the historic center of Naples, tenant for six years of commercial premises on the ground floor of a historic building, was served with an eviction notice for non-payment of rent (intimazione di sfratto per morosità) concerning twelve months of unpaid rent, for a total of € 33,600. The tenant had suspended payments following severe structural defects in the property: water infiltration from the ceiling that had damaged shelving and part of the inventory, as well as severe rising damp that rendered the environment unsanitary, prompting the competent local health authority (ASL) to issue a mandatory hygiene notice. The landlord had been repeatedly formally called upon (diffidato) to carry out the necessary extraordinary repairs, but had failed to act on the notices.

Our firm framed the defense around the defense of non-performance (eccezione di inadempimento under art. 1460 c.c.) and a counterclaim for rent reduction pursuant to art. 1578 c.c. and damages for deteriorated goods. We submitted into evidence: formal notices sent by the tenant via registered mail with return receipt (raccomandata a.r.), an expert report prepared by a qualified technician certifying the property's defects and their structural origin, the health authority (ASL) notice, photographs of the damaged goods, and purchase invoices for the damaged property. The judge, upholding the defense of non-performance, declared the eviction claim inadmissible pending determination of the claim asserted by the tenant, setting a mandatory deadline for the landlord to carry out remediation works. At the conclusion of the proceedings, the parties reached a settlement agreement (saldo e stralcio) on the rent arrears, granting the tenant a 40% reduction of the amount due for the twelve months of partial enjoyment of the property.

Defense of non-performance upheld — 40% rent reduction — Settlement agreement
Case no. 5

Opposition to eviction on serious grounds — Conversion into ordinary plenary proceedings — Tribunale di Salerno

Arrears: € 7,200 (6 months) — Property: residential — Situation: disabled tenant with caregiver

An elderly person with certified 100% disability, assisted by a live-in caregiver, tenant of a residential property in a municipality in the province of Salerno, was served with an eviction notice for non-payment of rent (intimazione di sfratto per morosità) regarding six months of arrears totaling € 7,200. The tenant had ceased payments following a dispute with the landlord regarding the latter's refusal to carry out necessary repairs on the heating system, which had become inoperative due to a breakdown and was essential given the tenant's health conditions. The live-in caregiver had met with the landlord to report the issue and request urgent repairs, but the landlord replied that the heating system was the tenant's responsibility. The tenant, elderly and unable to manage the proceedings without assistance, faced the risk of losing their home during the winter season.

Our firm immediately filed an opposition to eviction at the appearance hearing (udienza di convalida), raising: the landlord's failure to fulfill the statutory duty of extraordinary maintenance of the heating system (art. 1576 c.c.); the lawfulness of suspending rent payments in relation to the reduced suitability of the property for the agreed use; and the necessity of determining in full plenary proceedings the existence of an offsetting claim held by the tenant. We also filed medical records certifying the disability, an expert technical appraisal of the heating system establishing the breakdown and attributing responsibility to the masonry structure (extraordinary maintenance), as well as the formal warning letters sent to the landlord. Following the opposition, the judge dismissed the landlord's application for a provisional eviction order under art. 665 c.p.c., finding the opposition to be based on documentary evidence, and converted the summary proceedings into ordinary plenary proceedings (giudizio di cognizione piena). Pending trial, the landlord chose to enter into a settlement agreement: completing the works on the system, granting a 30% reduction on the rental arrears, and agreeing to an installment plan for the balance. The tenant retained their home and secured a heated living space for the winter season.

Opposition upheld — Provisional order under art. 665 dismissed — Settlement agreement with 30% reduction

Have you received an eviction notice (intimazione di sfratto)?

Acting immediately is essential: the time limits are very short.

Between the service of the eviction notice (intimazione di sfratto) and the confirmation hearing (udienza di convalida), twenty to thirty days generally elapse. Within that timeframe, it is necessary to gather documentation, assess the defence strategy, and appoint legal counsel. Those who delay risk forfeiting any opportunity for defence.
