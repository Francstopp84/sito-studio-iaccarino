L. 24/2017 Gelli-Bianco — Healthcare Liability

Have you suffered a medical error?
The healthcare system has obligations toward you.

A diagnostic error that delayed life-saving therapy, a surgical operation performed unskilfully, an infection contracted in a hospital, injury sustained during childbirth: these situations are not unavoidable fatalities. They are cases of healthcare liability governed by the Gelli-Bianco Law (L. 24/2017), which reshaped the entire system of patient protection in Italy. Compensation for personal injury (danno biologico), moral injury (danno morale), and loss of quality of life (danno esistenziale) is a right that must be asserted with technical competence and evidentiary rigor.

Relevant legal framework

The Gelli-Bianco Law and applicable statutory provisions

Medical liability is currently governed by a structured legal framework that draws a clear distinction between the liability of the healthcare facility and that of the individual healthcare professional. Law No. 24 of 8 March 2017 — the so-called Gelli-Bianco Law — introduced precise rules on the burden of proof, procedural deadlines, the mandatory conciliation attempt, and compulsory insurance coverage. Knowing precisely which provision applies to your case is the first step toward obtaining the compensation due to you.

Law No. 24 of 8 March 2017

L. 24/2017 — Gelli-Bianco Law (Healthcare liability)

The Gelli-Bianco Law is the primary source of healthcare liability law in Italy. It introduces accredited guidelines as the reference standard for assessing the healthcare professional's conduct, regulates the criminal liability of the medical practitioner (art. 590-sexies c.p.), and redefines the entire civil liability system. It provides for compulsory insurance for both facilities and healthcare professionals and establishes the Guarantee Fund for damages arising from healthcare activities.

Primary source — Healthcare liability
Art. 7, L. 24/2017

Art. 7 — Civil liability of the facility vs. liability of the medical practitioner

Art. 7 establishes a fundamental two-tier system: the healthcare facility is always liable in contract (art. 1218 c.c.), even when the injury is caused by an external practitioner whose services it uses. The individual healthcare professional is instead liable in tort (responsabilità extracontrattuale) pursuant to art. 2043 c.c., unless a contract was entered into directly with the patient. This distinction is of tremendous practical relevance: it changes the burden of proof, the limitation period (10 years for the facility, 5 years for the practitioner), and the procedural strategy to be adopted.

Two-tier system: contractual (facility) / non-contractual (practitioner)
Arts. 8-9, L. 24/2017

Arts. 8-9 — Mandatory conciliation attempt and ATP

Before initiating any judicial claim for healthcare liability, it is mandatory to undertake a conciliation attempt (art. 8) or, alternatively, proceed with a preventive technical assessment (accertamento tecnico preventivo - ATP) pursuant to art. 696-bis c.p.c. (art. 9). These are mandatory conditions precedent to litigation (condizioni di procedibilità): their omission renders any subsequent court proceeding inadmissible. The ATP, in particular, serves a dual purpose: it preserves the evidence before it is lost and frequently constitutes the stage at which the parties reach an out-of-court settlement, thereby avoiding ordinary trial proceedings.

Mandatory condition precedent to litigation
Art. 696-bis c.p.c.

Art. 696-bis c.p.c. — Preventive Technical Assessment (ATP)

The preventive technical assessment (accertamento tecnico preventivo - ATP) is the most effective procedural tool in healthcare liability. It allows for the appointment of a court-appointed expert witness (CTU) who examines the medical records, gathers reports, assesses the conduct of the healthcare practitioners, and quantifies the residual personal injury (danno biologico) even before the merits trial begins. The ATP interrupts the limitation period, secures the evidence irrevocably, and, in the vast majority of cases, leads to a settlement significantly higher than what would be obtained without the support of the expert's report. Properly formulating the questions to be submitted to the CTU is the most delicate phase of the entire procedure and requires in-depth medico-legal expertise.

Fundamental evidentiary tool
Civil Code

Arts. 2043 and 2236 c.c. — Non-contractual liability and complex technical performance

Art. 2043 c.c. forms the basis of non-contractual liability (responsabilità aquiliana) for private medical practitioners: it requires proof of the unlawful act, the damage, the causal link, and fault. Art. 2236 c.c., on the other hand, limits the liability of a professional providing services involving the resolution of technical matters of extraordinary difficulty solely to cases of intentional misconduct (dolo) or gross negligence (colpa grave); however, this provision does not exempt the practitioner from the duty to comply with accredited guidelines in situations not characterized by exceptional difficulty.

Art. 2043 + Art. 2236 c.c.
Quantification of damages

Milan Tables — Personal injury, moral injury, and loss of quality of life

The quantification of non-pecuniary loss in healthcare liability actions is governed by the Tables of the Court of Milan (Tabelle del Tribunale di Milano), used as the national benchmark. The tables distinguish personal injury (danno biologico, impairment of physical and psychological integrity), moral injury (danno morale, subjective inner suffering), and existential loss (danno esistenziale, disruption of one's personal life activities). To the non-pecuniary heads of loss is added pecuniary loss: incurred and future medical expenses, loss of earning capacity, and loss of future earnings. The accurate assessment and valuation of each head of damage requires medico-legal expertise and thorough knowledge of trial and appellate case law.

Milan Tables — National standard
Types of liability

When medical malpractice liability arises

Healthcare liability does not arise automatically from an unfavorable treatment outcome. The medical practitioner does not guarantee a cure, but is bound to adhere to the standards of the medical art. Liability arises when the negative outcome is causally linked to conduct that departs from accredited guidelines or from the standard of diligence required of a professional in the specific case.

📋

Diagnostic error

Failure to diagnose, delayed diagnosis, or incorrect diagnosis that delayed the initiation of appropriate treatment. It includes failure to identify unequivocal clinical signs, underestimation of diagnostic imaging and lab tests, and omission to prescribe examinations indicated by clinical guidelines. Diagnostic error is the most frequent cause of serious healthcare liability.

🔫

Surgical error

Technical deficiencies in the execution of the operation, injury to anatomical structures not involved in the surgery (nerves, vessels, adjacent organs), retained foreign bodies, incorrect selection of the surgical technique, or an intervention performed under inadequate anaesthesiological safety conditions.

💉

Therapeutic error

Prescription of contraindicated medications, incorrect dosages, failure to evaluate drug interactions, administration of therapy inappropriate for the diagnosis, failure to reassess therapy in the presence of new symptoms or complications, and improper management of postoperative care.

☣

Nosocomial infection

Infection contracted within the healthcare facility due to failure to comply with hygiene and prevention protocols. The liability of the facility is contractual in nature, and the patient must only prove the occurrence of the infection and its link to the hospital admission, without needing to establish specific negligent conduct.

📝

Lack of informed consent

The medical practitioner is obliged to inform the patient fully and comprehensibly regarding the risks of the intervention, therapeutic alternatives, and the consequences of refusing treatment. Failure to properly obtain informed consent constitutes an independent ground of liability, even where the medical procedure was carried out correctly.

👶

Birth injury

Errors in the management of childbirth causing permanent damage to the newborn (cerebral palsy, hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy) or to the mother. This represents the category with the highest compensation awards of all, often in the order of one million euros or more, given the severity of the impairments and their impact over the entire lifespan of the injured party.

The procedure

How the Preliminary Technical Assessment works

The Preliminary Technical Assessment (Accertamento Tecnico Preventivo – ATP) pursuant to art. 696-bis c.p.c. is the primary procedural avenue in medical malpractice cases. It is an atypical precautionary proceeding that makes it possible to obtain an expert technical report prior to the proceedings on the merits, interrupt the limitation period (prescrizione), and initiate settlement negotiations on objective grounds. In more than 60% of medical malpractice cases, the ATP concludes with a settlement agreement without the need to initiate ordinary court proceedings.

01
Petition to the Court

Filing of the petition (ricorso) pursuant to art. 696-bis c.p.c. setting out the facts, clinical records, and questions to the Court-Appointed Expert (CTU). The Court appoints the expert and schedules the hearing for the commencement of expert operations.

02
Appointment of the Court-Appointed Expert

The judge appoints the Court-Appointed Expert (Consulente Tecnico d’Ufficio – CTU), to whom the parties may add an ex-parte medical-legal expert (Consulente Tecnico di Parte – CTP). The choice of the CTP is strategically decisive.

03
Expert operations

The CTU acquires all medical documentation, examines the parties, interviews the healthcare personnel involved, and prepares the technical report answering the questions formulated by the judge upon proposal of the parties.

04
Filing of the report

The CTU files the report. The parties file their critical observations through their respective CTPs. The judge schedules the hearing for the conciliation attempt on the basis of the expert findings.

05
Settlement or court proceedings

If the parties reach an agreement, a conciliation record having the value of an enforceable title (titolo esecutivo) is drawn up. Failing an agreement, the file is used in the subsequent proceedings on the merits.

Pay attention to the limitation periods: The action against the healthcare facility is barred by the statute of limitations in 10 years from the awareness of the damage (contractual liability); the action against the individual doctor is barred in 5 years (non-contractual liability). Filing the petition for ATP interrupts both limitation periods. Do not wait: every day that passes without taking action may irreparably jeopardize your claim for compensation.

How we assist you

Strategies for obtaining compensation

A medical liability case requires the seamless integration of legal and medical-legal expertise. Our law firm coordinates every stage of the proceeding — from obtaining clinical documentation to drafting expert questions, from selecting the appropriate procedural instrument to the precise quantification of each head of damage — to maximize the compensation result in the shortest possible time.

1

Medical-legal technical advice

Before initiating any proceeding, we coordinate the analysis of clinical records with a trusted medical-legal expert. The preliminary assessment of the causal link and non-compliant conduct is essential to decide whether to proceed and with which strategy, avoiding actions bound for uncertain outcomes.

2

Preliminary Technical Assessment pursuant to art. 696-bis c.p.c.

The preliminary technical assessment (Accertamento Tecnico Preventivo – ATP) is our instrument of choice. We file the petition with technically precise questions, select the best CTP available for the pathology in question, and follow the expert operations at every hearing, submitting detailed technical observations on the CTU’s report.

3

Healthcare mediation

Where an ATP is not pursued, an attempt at mediation pursuant to art. 8 of Law 24/2017 before an authorized mediation body represents the alternative condition of procedural admissibility (condizione di procedibilità). We manage the procedure with the aim of reaching a fair settlement, or alternatively gathering elements useful for subsequent court proceedings.

4

Action against the healthcare facility

The hospital facility — whether public or private — is liable on a contractual basis (art. 7 of Law 24/2017 in conjunction with art. 1218 c.c.). The patient only needs to prove the injury and the causal link to the healthcare service; it is up to the facility to demonstrate that it performed with due diligence. Limitation period: 10 years. Practical advantage: the facility is insured with high coverage limits.

5

Action against the individual physician

The individual practitioner is liable pursuant to art. 2043 c.c. (non-contractual liability). The action against the physician may be brought independently or joined with that against the facility. It requires proof of specific fault (departure from recognized guidelines without justified reason) and is subject to a 5-year limitation period. The liability of an employed physician is limited to cases of intentional misconduct or gross negligence.

6

Quantification of damages

We calculate and document every item of compensation: temporary and permanent biological damage (Milan Tables), moral damage arising from subjective suffering, existential damage from impairment of personal and social life, past and future medical expenses, loss of earning capacity, loss of earnings (lucro cessante), and damage from loss of the parental relationship (iure proprio claims of family members). No item should be left to chance or to the discretion of the opposing party.

5 actual medical malpractice cases resolved

Case no. 1

Orthopaedic surgical error with iatrogenic injury to the sciatic nerve — Tribunale di Napoli

Case type: healthcare facility liability (art. 7 Law 24/2017) + non-contractual liability of the surgeon — Damage: permanent neurological deficit of the left lower limb

A fifty-two-year-old man, a skilled worker, underwent total left hip replacement surgery for severe coxarthrosis at a public hospital in Naples. In the immediate post-operative course, a motor and sensory deficit of the left lower limb with foot drop emerged, which was not present prior to the procedure. The clinical records confirmed an iatrogenic lesion of the common peroneal nerve (nervo sciatico popliteo esterno) sustained during the implant positioning manoeuvres.

The firm filed a petition (ricorso) pursuant to art. 696-bis of the Italian Code of Civil Procedure (c.p.c.) before the Tribunale di Napoli, appointing an orthopaedic party-appointed expert witness (CTP) and neurological CTP of the highest standing alongside the court-appointed expert witness (CTU). The expert report confirmed the iatrogenic lesion, tracing it to manoeuvres not compliant with AAOS (American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons) guidelines, which are adopted by Italian scientific literature as the benchmark standard. Permanent biological damage was quantified at 38% total disability, with loss of specific working capacity. During the conciliation attempt, the hospital facility and its insurance company agreed to a settlement of € 385.000 covering biological damage, moral damage, future medical expenses for neuromotor prostheses, and loss of income.

Compensation obtained — € 385.000 in preventive technical assessment (ATP) proceedings
Case no. 2

Diagnostic delay in colon carcinoma — Tribunale di Roma

Case type: contractual liability of the healthcare facility (ASL) + failure to prescribe diagnostic investigations — Damage: neoplastic progression from stage II to stage IV

A forty-eight-year-old woman who repeatedly attended the emergency room and visited her general practitioner over an eighteen-month period, complaining of irregular bowel habits, occasional rectal bleeding, and weight loss. On none of these occasions was a colonoscopy or other diagnostic imaging of the intestinal tract prescribed. The diagnosis of colon carcinoma was ultimately made following an emergency admission for bowel obstruction, by which time the disease had already reached stage IV with multiple liver metastases.

The petition for preventive technical appraisal (ricorso ATP) filed before the Tribunale di Roma involved three court-appointed technical experts (CTU): an oncologist, a gastroenterologist, and a legal medical doctor. The expert assessment established that the AIOM (Associazione Italiana di Oncologia Medica) guidelines and the recommendations of the Ministry of Health required the prescription of a colonoscopy as early as after the second visit, in the presence of the symptomatic picture described. With a timely diagnosis at stage II, 5-year survival is in the order of 85%; at stage IV it drops to 14%. The expert report (CTU) recognized the loss of chance of recovery as an autonomous head of compensable damage. In conciliation proceedings, the Local Health Authority (Azienda Sanitaria Locale) settled for € 520,000, encompassing biological damage (danno biologico), loss of chance damage, moral damage, and iure proprio compensation to cohabiting family members.

Compensation obtained — € 520,000 including loss of chance damage
Case no. 3

Multi-drug resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae infection contracted in surgical ward — Tribunale di Milano

Case type: contractual liability of a private healthcare facility for hospital-acquired infection — Damage: sepsis, chronic renal failure, 22% permanent disability

A sixty-four-year-old man admitted to a private clinic in Milan for a laparoscopic cholecystectomy, a routine procedure with an expected hospital course of 24-48 hours. On the fourth post-operative day, he developed high fever and signs of sepsis. Culture tests isolated a strain of carbapenemase-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae (KPC), a bacterium highly resistant to common antibiotics. The patient remained hospitalized for forty-two days in intensive care, sustaining chronic renal failure requiring thrice-weekly dialysis.

The petition for preventive technical appraisal (ricorso ATP) documented, through the report of the infectious disease court-appointed technical expert (CTU), that the facility had not implemented the screening protocols for multidrug-resistant microorganisms recommended by the national plan against antimicrobial resistance (PNCAR), and that the traceability of medical devices was severely deficient. The clinic was held liable regardless of specific proof of individual negligent conduct, proof of the infection and of the temporal link to the hospitalization being sufficient. Following the filing of the CTU report, the facility offered a settlement of € 310,000 accepted by the client, including permanent biological damage (danno biologico), capitalized future dialysis costs, and damage from partial loss of working capacity.

Compensation obtained — € 310,000 in out-of-court settlement during preventive technical appraisal (ATP)
Case no. 4

Birth injury — Obstetric brachial plexus palsy — Tribunale di Napoli

Case type: liability of public healthcare facility and gynecologist — Damage: permanent paralysis of the newborn's right upper limb, 45% disability

Newborn with obstetric right brachial plexus palsy (Erb-Duchenne lesion) resulting from incorrect obstetric maneuvers during a vaginal delivery complicated by shoulder dystocia. Shoulder dystocia was a foreseeable complication documented in the medical records (fetal macrosomia estimated by ultrasound at 38 weeks, birth weight 4,650 grams), but no specific management protocol had been planned. The maneuvers performed by the gynecologist on duty did not comply with the sequence recommended by the RCOG (Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists) and SIGO guidelines, which provide in sequence for the McRoberts maneuver, suprapubic pressure, internal Wood maneuvers, and, where necessary, symphysiotomy.

The court-appointed technical expert (CTU), a renowned obstetrician-gynecologist appointed by the Tribunale di Napoli, established that the maneuvers performed had been excessive and uncoordinated, causing traumatic traction of the brachial plexus. The permanent biological damage (danno biologico) of the minor, calculated with a lifetime projection according to the Milan Tables (Tabelle di Milano), was quantified at € 780,000 for non-pecuniary damage alone, to which are added the expenses for physiotherapy and future neuromotor treatments, as well as damage for the loss of parental relationship in favor of the parents iure proprio. The proceedings on the merits concluded with a judgment of the Tribunale di Napoli ordering the hospital facility to pay a total of € 920,000.

Court award — € 920,000 for birth injury
Case no. 5

Anesthesiological error resulting in post-operative coma — Tribunale di Roma

Case type: contractual liability of the facility + tort liability of the anesthesiologist — Damage: anoxic encephalopathy, minimally conscious vegetative state, 85% disability

A thirty-nine-year-old woman, in good health, underwent scheduled total thyroidectomy surgery at a private clinic in Rome. During the induction of anesthesia, an episode of esophageal intubation occurred that was not promptly recognized, resulting in cerebral hypoxia lasting approximately eleven minutes. The patient was transferred to intensive care and sustained severe anoxic encephalopathy with transition toward a minimally conscious state.

The reconstruction of the events took place through the analysis of the anesthesia records and capnography tracings, which documented a delay of approximately four minutes between the first monitoring alarm signal and the initial corrective maneuvers. The guidelines of SIAARTI (Società Italiana di Anestesia Analgesia Rianimazione e Terapia Intensiva) mandate the compulsory use and continuous monitoring of capnography during every endotracheal intubation: the anomalous signal had been ignored for a duration incompatible with any standard of diligence. The court-appointed technical expert (CTU) appointed by the Tribunale di Roma concluded for the exclusive liability of the anesthesiologist and the facility. Following the filing of the expert report, the clinic's insurance company settled for € 1,450,000, encompassing all non-pecuniary damages, the costs of future continuous care capitalized on an actuarial projection, pecuniary damage for the total loss of working capacity, and iure proprio compensation for the spouse and the two minor children.

Compensation obtained — € 1,450,000 during preventive technical appraisal (ATP)
