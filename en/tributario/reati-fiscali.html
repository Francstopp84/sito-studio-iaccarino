D.Lgs. 74/2000  •  Criminal Tax Law  •  Art. 13 Non-Punishability

Under investigation or committed for trial for a tax crime?
Criminal tax defence requires specific and thorough expertise. Time is the critical factor.

Tax offences under Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 74/2000 — from fraudulent tax returns to non-payment of VAT — carry penalties ranging from six months to eight years of imprisonment and extremely severe consequences for the defendant's personal assets: preventive seizure (*sequestro preventivo*) aimed at confiscation by equivalent (*confisca per equivalente*), corporate liability pursuant to Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 231/2001, disqualification from holding public office. Article 13 of D.Lgs. 74/2000 provides for significant grounds for exemption from punishment (*cause di non punibilità*) and mitigating circumstances linked to the settlement of the tax debt: an experienced technical defence can employ them strategically to avoid conviction or obtain reduced penalties. Every day of delay reduces the available defence options.

Regulatory framework

Legislative Decree (D.Lgs.) 74/2000: the tax crimes code

Art. 2 D.Lgs. 10 March 2000, n. 74 — Threshold € 100.000

Fraudulent tax return through the use of invoices for non-existent transactions

Art. 2 D.Lgs. 74/2000 punishes with imprisonment from four to eight years (in its basic form) anyone who, in order to evade income taxes or value added tax, by using invoices or other documents for non-existent transactions, records fictitious liabilities in one of the annual tax returns. The offence is intentional in nature and requires the specific intent to evade tax. There are no minimum prosecution thresholds when the conduct involves invoices for objectively non-existent transactions (the party never received the invoiced goods or services). The offence is also punishable when the evaded tax exceeds the threshold of 100.000 euros in the aggravated version (penalty of five to eight years). Confiscation by equivalent (*confisca per equivalente*) of the proceeds of the crime may be ordered in an amount corresponding to the evaded tax.

Art. 2 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Imprisonment 4–8 years
Art. 4 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Threshold € 100.000 / € 2.000.000

Inaccurate tax return (*dichiarazione infedele*): understated active elements or overstated liabilities

Art. 4 D.Lgs. 74/2000, as amended by D.Lgs. 158/2015, punishes with imprisonment from two to four years and six months anyone who, in order to evade income taxes or VAT, states in one of the annual tax returns active elements in an amount lower than the actual amount or non-existent liabilities, when jointly the tax evaded exceeds 100.000 euros and the total amount of active elements concealed from taxation exceeds 10% of the total amount of active elements stated in the tax return, or in any event exceeds 2.000.000 euros. D.Lgs. 158/2015 excluded from criminal liability appraisal estimates that, individually considered, differ by less than 10% from the actual value; it also excluded criminal liability for applying tax rules in accordance with the position adopted by the Revenue Agency (*Agenzia delle Entrate*) in response to a tax ruling request (*interpello*).

Art. 4 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Imprisonment 2–4 years and 6 months
Art. 5 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Threshold € 50.000

Failure to file a tax return (*omessa dichiarazione*): failure to submit above the threshold

Art. 5 D.Lgs. 74/2000 punishes with imprisonment from two to five years anyone who, for the purpose of evading income taxes or VAT (IVA), fails to submit, being obliged to do so, one or more of the annual returns relating to said taxes, when the evaded tax exceeds, with reference to any of the individual taxes, 50,000 euros. The offense is consummated upon the expiration of the deadline for submitting the omitted return (30 November for income tax returns). The provision also applies to the tax withholding agent (sostituto d’imposta) who fails to submit the withholding agent return (modello 770) when the amount of unpaid withholdings exceeds 50,000 euros. The required mens rea is general intent (dolo generico): the conscious will not to file the return is sufficient, without requiring a further purpose of evasion; the threshold, however, is a constituent element of the offense to be proven by the public prosecutor (pubblico ministero).

Art. 5 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Imprisonment 2–5 years
Art. 10-bis D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Threshold € 250.000

Failure to pay VAT (*omesso versamento IVA*): the most common offence among business owners

Art. 10-bis D.Lgs. 74/2000 punishes with imprisonment from six months to two years anyone who does not pay, within the deadline for the payment of the advance for the following tax period, the value added tax due on the basis of the annual return, for an amount exceeding 250.000 euros for each tax period. The offence is consummated on 27 December of the year following the tax year (deadline for the payment of the VAT advance). The grounds for exemption from punishment (*causa di non punibilità*) under Art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000 applies if the debt is discharged, including through ongoing instalment payments, prior to the formal declaration opening the trial of first instance (*apertura del dibattimento di primo grado*).

Art. 10-bis D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Imprisonment 6 months – 2 years
Art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Grounds for exemption from punishment

Settlement of the tax debt excludes criminal liability

Art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000, as rewritten by D.Lgs. 158/2015, provides for an exemption from punishment (*causa di non punibilità*) for the offences under Articles 10-bis, 10-ter, and 10-quater, para. 1 (omitted payments and unlawful set-off using undue tax credits) when, before the declaration opening the trial of first instance (*apertura del dibattimento di primo grado*), the tax debts, including administrative penalties and interest, have been extinguished through full payment of the amounts due, including following special conciliation procedures or tax assessment settlements (*adesione all’accertamento*) provided for by tax regulations. For the offences under Articles 4 and 5 (inaccurate tax return and failure to file a tax return), full payment of the tax debt is instead a special mitigating circumstance that entails a reduction of the penalty by up to half and the non-applicability of ancillary penalties (*pene accessorie*). Article 13-bis provides for a further special mitigating circumstance for anyone who has endeavoured to make full payment of the tax debt and has cooperated with the investigating authorities.

Art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Exemption from punishment and special mitigating circumstance

Warning: corporate liability under D.Lgs. 231/2001. The tax offences under Articles 2, 3, 8, 10, 11, and 12 of D.Lgs. 74/2000 were added to the catalogue of predicate offences (*reati presupposto*) for the administrative liability of entities pursuant to D.Lgs. 231/2001, by Art. 39 of Law Decree (D.L.) 124/2019, converted into Law (L.) 157/2019. This means that, where tax offences are committed in its interest or to its advantage by senior managers or subordinate persons, the legal entity is subject to financial penalties of up to 774 quotas (equivalent to approximately 1.3 million euros) and disqualifying sanctions (*sanzioni interdittive*). The defence of the entity requires an urgent review of the adopted Organisation and Management Model (*Modello Organizzativo 231*) and of its effectiveness.

Criminal offences

The six most frequent tax offences

D.Lgs. 74/2000 provides a catalogue of tax offences categorized by type of conduct, required subjective element (*mens rea*), and prosecution thresholds. The correct qualification of the charged offence is the essential first step of an effective defence, as statutory limitation periods (*prescrizione*), applicable grounds for exemption from punishment, and the optimal procedural strategy all depend upon it.

Art. 2 D.Lgs. 74/2000

Fraudulent tax return (false invoices)

Use in a tax return of invoices for objectively or subjectively non-existent transactions, in order to reduce taxable income or VAT payable. It is the most serious offence in the catalogue, with no minimum prosecution threshold in its basic form. The defence requires an assessment of the economic reality of the disputed transactions, the actual awareness of the defendant, and the specific intent to evade tax.

Penalty: imprisonment 4–8 years (5–8 if tax > € 100.000)
Art. 4 D.Lgs. 74/2000

Inaccurate tax return (dichiarazione infedele)

Indicating revenues lower than the actual amount or fictitious or inflated costs in the tax return, exceeding the thresholds for criminal liability (€ 100.000 of evaded tax and 10% of active assets or € 2.000.000). D.Lgs. 158/2015 excluded the punishable nature of non-congruent valuations by less than 10%: a fundamental defense argument for valuation disputes (goodwill, shareholdings, real estate).

Penalty: imprisonment for 2–4 years and 6 months
Art. 5 D.Lgs. 74/2000

Omitted tax return (omessa dichiarazione)

Failure to submit the annual IRPEF, IRES, or VAT (IVA) tax return, with evaded tax exceeding 50.000 euros. The offense is completed upon expiry of the ordinary deadline for filing. The defense must verify whether the threshold is actually exceeded (the Public Prosecutor — PM — must prove it), whether force majeure or technical impediments exist, and whether the omitted tax return was filed late prior to the trial stage (*dibattimento*).

Penalty: imprisonment for 2–5 years
Art. 10-bis D.Lgs. 74/2000

Failure to pay VAT (*omesso versamento IVA*)

Failure to pay, by 27 December of the following year, declared VAT (*IVA*) exceeding 250.000 euros per year. It is statistically the most frequent tax offense, often committed by business owners facing a liquidity crisis. The defense of a non-culpable liquidity crisis is viable but requires rigorous proof. Extinction of the tax debt prior to the trial stage (*dibattimento*) results in non-punishability under art. 13.

Penalty: imprisonment for 6 months – 2 years
Art. 10-ter D.Lgs. 74/2000

Failure to pay certified withholding tax (*omesso versamento ritenute certificate*)

Failure to pay withholding taxes deducted and certified to the withholdees, in an amount exceeding 150.000 euros per year. The prerequisite is that the withholding agent (*sostituto d’imposta*) actually withheld the taxes but did not remit them to the Revenue (*Erario*). The defense must verify the match between the single withholding tax certifications issued (*Certificazioni Uniche*) and the amounts actually withheld, as well as available liquidity and any corporate crisis.

Penalty: imprisonment for 6 months – 2 years
Art. 10 D.Lgs. 74/2000

Concealment or destruction of accounting documents

Destruction, concealment, or falsification, in whole or in part, of accounting records or mandatory documents in order to evade taxes or enable third parties to evade them, resulting in the impossibility of reconstructing business turnover or income. The offense is standalone and does not depend on actual tax evasion. It is frequently charged alongside offenses of fraudulent tax return during complex tax audits.

Penalty: imprisonment for 6 months – 5 years

Our method

The six defense strategies in criminal tax matters

Criminal tax defense is multidimensional: it encompasses criminal proceedings, tax proceedings, corporate administrative liability, and the management of preventive seizure (*sequestro preventivo*). The strategy must be designed from the very outset of the investigation, while it is still possible to take advantage of grounds for non-punishability related to the payment of the debt.

I.

Verification of thresholds and legal classification of the facts

Technical analysis of the charges to verify whether the liability thresholds are actually exceeded, whether the legal classification of the facts adopted by the prosecution is correct, whether there are constituent elements of the offense that can be qualified differently (subjectively vs. objectively non-existent transactions; appraisal valuations vs. factual manipulations), and whether the specific criminal intent (*dolo specifico*) required for specific intent offenses can be proven or challenged.

II.

Grounds for non-punishability pursuant to art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000

For offenses of failure to pay (artt. 10-bis and 10-ter), full payment of the tax debt — including tax, administrative penalties, and interest — before the formal opening of the first-instance trial (*dibattimento*) results in non-punishability. The strategy involves financial planning for payment (including installment plans with the tax authorities) coordinated with the timeline of criminal proceedings, in order to make use of the window available before the trial hearing.

III.

Non-culpable liquidity crisis

For offenses of failure to pay, the defense of a liquidity crisis aims to demonstrate that the business owner was in a situation of objective and non-culpable impossibility to make payment: lack of liquid funds despite diligent corporate management, refusal of bank loans, inability to access installment plans. Rigorous evidence requires banking documentation, financial statements, bank statements, and correspondence with credit institutions.

IV.

Release from seizure and opposition to confiscation

Preventive seizure aimed at equivalent confiscation (*confisca per equivalente*) is often the most immediately devastating measure for the defendant and their family. The defense must act promptly by seeking review before the Review Court (*Tribunale del Riesame*) and, if necessary, an appeal to the Court of Cassation (*Corte di Cassazione*), challenging the prima facie case (*fumus commissi delicti*) of the predicate offense, the proportionality of the seizure, and the correct identification of the assets to be placed under precautionary attachment (*misura cautelare reale*).

V.

Plea bargaining (*patteggiamento*) and special mitigating circumstances

For offenses under artt. 4 and 5 D.Lgs. 74/2000 (unfaithful tax return and omitted tax return), payment of the tax debt prior to the trial stage (*dibattimento*) does not preclude liability but constitutes a special mitigating circumstance that reduces the penalty by up to one half. In this perspective, plea bargaining for penalty application pursuant to art. 444 c.p.p. (*patteggiamento*) — negotiated with the Public Prosecutor (*PM*) based on the penalty reduced for the mitigating circumstance — can be the most advantageous solution, avoiding trial and obtaining a suspended sentence (*sospensione condizionale*).

VI.

Corporate defense under D.Lgs. 231/2001

When a tax offense is committed in the interest or to the advantage of a legal entity, the company is subject to independent proceedings pursuant to D.Lgs. 231/2001. The defense of the entity aims to demonstrate the adoption and effective implementation of an Organizational Model (*Modello Organizzativo*) suitable for preventing the offense, the absence of advantage for the entity, or the commission of the offense solely in the personal interest of the perpetrator. The 231 proceedings are conducted in parallel with criminal proceedings but require a separate technical defense.

Tangible results

Five resolved cases in criminal tax law

The following cases illustrate our firm’s actual defense work in criminal proceedings concerning tax offenses. Identifying details have been anonymised in compliance with client confidentiality and legal professional privilege.

Case No. 1 — Art. 10-bis of Legislative Decree no. 74/2000

Failure to pay VAT: non-punishability for € 680,000 through pre-trial instalment plan

Tribunale di Napoli, Criminal Division — Year 2024

An entrepreneur in the manufacturing sector had been indicted for failure to pay VAT (omesso versamento IVA) relating to the 2020 and 2021 tax years, with an aggregate tax debt (tax, penalties, and interest) of approximately 680,000 euros. Sector liquidity constraints during the pandemic had made timely payment impossible. The client approached our firm six months before the opening of the trial hearing. The defense strategy involved the immediate submission of an application for an extraordinary instalment plan to the Revenue Agency – Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione), pursuant to art. 19 of Presidential Decree no. 602/1973 (extension of payment plans for proven financial hardship), securing a 72-month instalment plan. Over the subsequent months, the entrepreneur paid the first twelve instalments, placing the tax liability into the status of being “under settlement” pursuant to an instalment plan formally granted by the tax authorities. Before the formal opening of the trial proceedings, defense counsel filed documentation certifying the full scheduled repayment plan and the regularity of the payments made. The Tribunale di Napoli, recognising the applicability of art. 13 of Legislative Decree no. 74/2000 in the version applicable ratione temporis, issued a judgment of dismissal (sentenza di non doversi procedere) due to the occurrence of a statutory bar to punishment.

Non-punishability established — criminal conviction avoided
Case No. 2 — Art. 2 of Legislative Decree no. 74/2000

Fraudulent tax return: acquittal due to lack of specific intent

Tribunale di Napoli Nord — Year 2023

A motor vehicle dealer was charged with fraudulent tax return filing through the use of invoices for subjectively non-existent transactions (so-called “carousel fraud”) relating to the 2018 and 2019 tax years, involving contested evaded tax of approximately 190,000 euros. The prosecution alleged that the defendant had knowingly purchased vehicles from intermediary paper companies (società cartiere), unlawfully claiming VAT credits. The defense systematically established that the commercial transactions were genuine — the vehicles had actually been purchased, delivered, and resold — and that the defendant was unaware of the fraudulent mechanism orchestrated upstream in the distribution chain by the scheme's organisers. All transport documents, purchase agreements, supplier correspondence, and vehicle valuation reports proving market-price consistency were entered into evidence. The Court acquitted the defendant on the merits with the formula “because the fact does not exist” (perché il fatto non sussiste), holding that the specific intent (dolo specifico) to evade taxes was not proven.

Acquitted — «the fact does not exist»
Case No. 3 — Art. 4 of Legislative Decree no. 74/2000

Inaccurate tax return: plea bargain with mitigating circumstances, conditionally suspended sentence

Tribunale di Napoli — Year 2024

A healthcare professional was under investigation for filing inaccurate tax returns (dichiarazione infedele) for the years 2019, 2020, and 2021, with total evaded taxes of approximately 215,000 euros. The Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) had issued analytical assessment notices based on inductive income reconstruction through the synthetic wealth assessment tool (redditometro) and bank financial records obtained pursuant to art. 32 of Presidential Decree no. 600/1973. The Public Prosecutor's Office had entered the individual into the register of investigated persons (registro degli indagati) pursuant to art. 335 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, and the preliminary investigation concluded with a request for indictment (richiesta di rinvio a giudizio). The strategy entailed initiating a simultaneous tax settlement procedure (accertamento con adesione) before the Revenue Agency office, during which the assessed tax was reduced to 142,000 euros, paid in full (including reduced penalties and interest) prior to the preliminary hearing. This made it possible to invoke the statutory mitigating circumstance under art. 13, para. 2 of Legislative Decree no. 74/2000, which reduces the sentence by up to one-half. The sentence agreed upon via plea bargain (patteggiamento) was one year and four months of imprisonment, conditionally suspended, thereby precluding any custodial detention as well as ancillary penalties (such as disqualification from public office).

Plea bargain — suspended sentence — custodial detention precluded
Case No. 4 — Legislative Decree no. 231/2001 + Art. 2 of Legislative Decree no. 74/2000

Corporate liability of the entity: release of € 920,000 from seizure and dismissal under Decree 231

Tribunale del Riesame of Naples / GUP Tribunale di Napoli — Years 2023–2024

A limited liability company (società a responsabilità limitata) operating in the food distribution sector had been subjected to preventive seizure aimed at equivalent confiscation (sequestro preventivo finalizzato alla confisca per equivalente) for an amount of 920,000 euros, in relation to an alleged fraudulent tax return through the use of invoices for non-existent transactions (dichiarazione fraudolenta mediante uso di fatture per operazioni inesistenti) charged against the sole director (amministratore unico). In parallel, the company had been registered in the proceedings pursuant to D.Lgs. 231/2001. The seizure had frozen the company bank accounts and registered movable assets owned by the company, causing the operational paralysis of the business and a real risk of insolvency and employee dismissals. The defence immediately lodged an application for review (riesame) before the Tribunale del Riesame di Napoli, arguing: (a) the lack of fumus commissi delicti, as the disputed transactions had been established as subjectively non-existent without proof of the company’s awareness; (b) the disproportion of the seizure compared to the profit of the offence (the disputed costs were partially deductible for direct tax purposes, reducing the actual profit); (c) the existence of an Organisational Model 231 (Modello Organizzativo 231) adopted prior to the events, which excluded the liability of the entity. The Tribunale del Riesame granted the application for release from seizure (dissequestro) for 620,000 euros, reducing the measure to 300,000 euros corresponding solely to the net profit. Subsequently, at the 231 preliminary hearing (udienza preliminare), the Judge for Preliminary Hearings (GUP) ruled that there were no grounds to proceed (non luogo a procedere) against the company, deeming the adopted Organisational Model effective.

Partial release from seizure (dissequestro) € 620,000 — entity cleared
Case No. 5 — Art. 5 D.Lgs. 74/2000

Failure to file a tax return (omessa dichiarazione): proof of tax below the threshold and acquittal

Tribunale di Napoli — Year 2024

A Neapolitan craftsman had been committed for trial (rinviato a giudizio) for failure to file VAT and IRPEF tax returns (omessa presentazione della dichiarazione) for the years 2019 and 2020. The prosecution quantified the evaded tax at approximately 82,000 euros, calculated on a presumptive basis using sector profitability coefficients (studi di settore). The defendant had not filed the returns due to severe family difficulties (serious illness of his spouse) that had rendered him unable to manage the business accounts for over a year. The defence based its strategy on two distinct fronts. On the evidential level, it appointed an independent expert witness (consulente tecnico di parte) to reconstruct the defendant’s actual income for the two disputed years through the analysis of bank transactions, invoices issued and received, supply contracts, and customer payment receipts. The analytical reconstruction proved that the tax actually due for both years did not exceed a total of 38,000 euros, well below the threshold of criminal liability (soglia di punibilità) of 50,000 euros for each tax. In terms of factual circumstances, medical records concerning the spouse’s illness were submitted as evidence demonstrating the non-intentional nature of the omission. The Court acquitted the defendant because the fact does not exist (perché il fatto non sussiste), as the threshold of criminal liability provided by law had not been reached.

Acquitted — threshold not reached — «the fact does not exist» (il fatto non sussiste)
