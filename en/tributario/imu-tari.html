Have you received an IMU or TARI assessment from the Municipality?
Many are unlawful. You have 60 days to file an appeal (ricorso).

IMU and TARI are the primary local taxes levied on real estate owners and possessors in Italy. Municipalities (Comuni) assess and collect them with increasing aggressiveness, often applying tax rates incorrectly, denying exemptions and reliefs to which the taxpayer is entitled, or calculating TARI on surface areas that the law excludes from taxation. An unlawful IMU or TARI tax assessment notice (avviso di accertamento) can be challenged before the Tax Justice Court (Giudice Tributario) within 60 days of service. Our law firm has significant experience in defending taxpayers against claims brought by Municipalities in Campania, with a high success rate in appeals concerning local taxes.

Regulatory framework

The Regulation of IMU and TARI

D.Lgs. 30 December 1992, no. 504 — Original IMU

The Municipal Property Tax: Origins and Structure

IMU (Imposta Municipale Propria – Municipal Property Tax) in its current form derives from ICI (Imposta Comunale sugli Immobili) introduced by D.Lgs. 504/1992. The taxable event is the ownership or possession of buildings, building plots, and agricultural land within the municipal territory. The tax base consists of the cadastral value of the property, obtained by multiplying the cadastral yield (rendita catastale), revalued by 5%, by a coefficient that varies according to the property's cadastral category. Municipalities have the authority to vary the baseline rate within the limits set by national law and to introduce additional tax reliefs beyond the mandatory ones provided for by national legislation. This very municipal discretion generates extensive litigation, as Municipalities do not always apply their own resolutions correctly and sometimes demand amounts that are not owed.

D.Lgs. 504/1992 — Cadastral Tax Base
Law of 27 December 2019, no. 160 — New municipal property tax (Nuova IMU)

The 2020 IMU Reform: Exemptions and Controversial Cases

L. 160/2019 (2020 Budget Law) reorganized the IMU framework, repealing TASI and incorporating its revenue into IMU, with effect from 1 January 2020. The reform confirmed the complete exemption for the primary residence (abitazione principale), defined as the property in which the possessor and their family unit habitually dwell and maintain their registered residence, but introduced a controversial provision: when members of the family unit dwell in different properties located in the same Municipality, the exemption applies to only one property chosen by the unit. This provision generated widespread litigation because Municipalities interpreted it restrictively, denying the exemption to separated or divorced spouses residing in different Municipalities.

L. 160/2019
Law no. 147 of 27 December 2013 — waste tax (TARI)

The Waste Tax: Taxable Event and Taxable Surfaces

TARI (Tassa sui Rifiuti – Waste Tax) was introduced by L. 147/2013 with effect from 1 January 2014, replacing the previous TARES. The taxable event is the ownership or possession, under any title, of premises or uncovered areas for any use, capable of producing municipal waste. The statutory rule expressly excludes from the taxable surface area uncovered appurtenant or accessory areas to taxable premises, as well as common condominium areas that are not held or occupied exclusively. Art. 1, paragraph 649, of L. 147/2013 provides that for real estate units with standard (residential) designation, the taxable surface area equals 80% of the cadastral surface area. For properties not registered in the land registry (catasto), the Municipality must measure the actual surface area. Surface measurement is a frequent source of errors and disputes: Municipalities often levy TARI on surface areas greater than those actually taxable.

L. 147/2013 — Art. 1 paragraphs 641–668

This means that notices concerning tax years dating back more than five years, for which no valid interrupting acts were served, are voidable due to limitation before even examining any other substantive flaw of the assessment. Verifying the statute of limitations is the first check our law firm conducts on every tax assessment notice concerning local taxes.

The most common cases

When IMU and TARI Can Be Challenged

IMU and TARI assessment notices present a wide variety of defects that can render the Municipality's claim partially or entirely unlawful. Challenging them requires in-depth knowledge of both national legislation and municipal regulations.

IMU

IMU Tax Assessment

The Municipality challenges the non-payment or underpayment of IMU. The most frequent grounds of unlawfulness include: application of incorrect or outdated tax rates, miscalculation of the revalued cadastral yield, incorrect multiplying coefficient, failure to grant tax reliefs enacted by the Municipality itself, and assessments issued beyond forfeiture time limits (decadenza, i.e., 31 December of the fifth year following that in which the tax return was filed).

Reference: D.Lgs. 504/1992 — L. 160/2019
TARI

TARI Tax Assessment

The Municipality levies TARI on incorrect surfaces or surfaces exceeding those actually taxable, on premises producing special waste that cannot be assimilated to municipal waste (productive categories), on unused premises lacking utility connections, or on non-taxable appurtenant areas. TARI may also be reduced for users who demonstrate that they have produced smaller quantities of waste compared to the category average.

Reference: L. 147/2013 — Art. 1 c. 649
Exemption

Primary Residence IMU Exemption

The Municipality denies the IMU exemption for the primary residence, disputing registered residence, habitual dwelling, or the fact that the family unit resides in another property.

Reference: Art. 1 c. 741 L. 160/2019
TARI

Non-Taxable Surface Areas

TARI does not apply to portions of properties used for the storage of special waste assimilated to municipal waste where the producer handles disposal independently and provides proof thereof to the Municipality. Uncovered appurtenant areas, condominium surfaces not in exclusive use, premises lacking utility connections (electricity, water, gas) indicating non-use, and premises in an objective state of unusable dilapidation are not taxable.

Reference: Art. 1, cc. 641-645, L. 147/2013
IMU

Agricultural Land

Agricultural land managed by direct farmers (coltivatori diretti) and professional agricultural entrepreneurs registered with the agricultural social security scheme enjoys an IMU exemption or relief. Land situated in Municipalities classified as partially mountainous or hilly benefits from preferential regimes. The Municipality may have denied these benefits by challenging the operator's professional qualification or the territorial classification, in both cases offering significant grounds for defense.

Reference: Art. 1 c. 758 L. 160/2019
Refund

Refund of Excess IMU/TARI Paid

A taxpayer who has paid IMU or TARI in excess of the amount owed may submit a refund application (istanza di rimborso) to the Municipality within five years of payment. If the Municipality fails to respond or rejects the application, an appeal (ricorso) may be lodged with the Tax Justice Court (Giudice Tributario). The most frequent cases include: payments made without legal basis on properties subsequently found to be exempt, payments on surfaces subsequently recalculated, or duplicate payments made due to taxpayer error.

Reference: Art. 1 c. 164 L. 296/2006

Our method

The six defence strategies

Defending against IMU and TARI assessment notices requires a systematic examination of all possible defects, both procedural (statement of reasons, statutory time limits, jurisdiction) and substantive (tax base, tax rates, exemptions). The strategy varies significantly depending on the predominant type of defect.

I.

Verification of Statute of Limitations and Forfeiture

Systematic verification of compliance with forfeiture deadlines for the notification of the notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento) (December 31 of the fifth year following that in which the tax return was filed or, failing that, in which it should have been filed) and with the five-year statute of limitations of the tax claim. A late notice is voidable regardless of the merits of the claim.

II.

Analysis of tax base and rates

Recalculation of the IMU tax base (cadastral income [rendita catastale] × revaluation coefficient × multiplier) and verification of the applied rate against the municipal resolutions in force in the reference year. The tax rate resolution must be published on the Portale del Federalismo Fiscale within the statutory deadlines: failing that, the basic rate set by national law applies.

III.

Recognition of exemptions and tax relief

Verification of all applicable exemption and relief regimes: primary residence (prima casa), inventory properties of construction companies, instrumental rural buildings, agricultural land of direct farmers, properties of non-commercial entities, uninhabitable properties. Many assessments are annulled because the Municipality failed to consider tax reliefs mandatory by law or adopted by the Municipality itself.

IV.

Challenging TARI surface areas

Measurement and documentation of the surface areas actually taxable for TARI purposes, excluding areas exempt by law (appurtenances, condominium common areas, unused premises, areas allocated to special waste disposed of independently). Request for reduction of the surface area declared to the Municipality with the submission of cadastral floor plans and technical documentation certifying the actual intended use of the premises.

V.

Administrative complaint-mediation (reclamo-mediazione) and self-redress (autotutela)

For disputes with a value of less than € 50,000, mandatory complaint-mediation (reclamo-mediazione) pursuant to art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992, with the possibility of an automatic reduction of penalties to 35% in the event of an agreement. For manifest flaws, an application for self-redress (autotutela) directly to the Municipality's tax office, which can cancel the assessment without the need for court proceedings, saving time and litigation costs.

VI.

Appeal (ricorso) to the CGT and refund

Appeal (ricorso) to the Tax Justice Court (Giudice Tributario) for the annulment of the unlawful notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento), with a concurrent request for refund of any amounts already paid in excess. In the event of a judgment against the Municipality, an order for costs can also be obtained, making the appeal financially viable even for relatively modest amounts.

Tangible results

Five resolved cases

The following cases illustrate our law firm's practical defense work regarding municipal property tax (IMU) and waste tax (TARI). Identifying details have been anonymized in accordance with client confidentiality.

Case no. 1 — CGT of First Instance

Denied primary residence exemption: IMU assessment annulled for € 11,400

Giudice Tributario di I grado di Napoli — Year 2024

A legally separated married couple each received an IMU tax assessment notice (avviso di accertamento IMU) from the Municipality of Naples for tax years 2021 and 2022, concerning respectively the apartment where the husband lived (Naples) and the apartment where the wife lived (Naples, different neighborhood). The Municipality had denied the exemption for both properties, deeming applicable the rule that limits the primary residence exemption to only one property per family unit even when spouses reside in different properties within the same Municipality. The total assessment amounted to € 11,400 between the two spouses. The appeal (ricorso) claimed the exemption for separated spouses who habitually reside and have their registered residence in different properties, a situation in which actual separation was demonstrated by distinct registered residences and by court-approved legal separation (separazione personale omologata). The First-Instance Court of Tax Justice (CGT di I grado) of Naples fully upheld both appeals, recognizing the exemption for both properties and annulling the respective assessment notices, ordering the Municipality to pay legal costs.

Total annulment — € 11,400
Case no. 2 — Administrative self-redress (Autotutela)

Waste tax (TARI) on non-taxable surface areas: € 4,800 refund via administrative self-redress (autotutela)

Comune di Napoli — Tax administrative self-redress (Autotutela) — Year 2023

A ceramic artisan workshop received waste tax notices (avvisi TARI) for three consecutive tax years (2020–2022) calculated on an overall surface area of 380 sq. m. The examination of the cadastral floor plans and lease agreements revealed that the Municipality had included in the taxable surface calculation: a 45 sq. m storage area used exclusively for storing scrap materials (special waste not assimilable to municipal waste, for which the workshop held a contract with an authorized company for collection and disposal) and an uncovered appurtenance of 35 sq. m, consisting of an unused ancillary courtyard. Our law firm prepared an application for administrative review (istanza di autotutela) accompanied by floor plans, the special waste disposal contract, photographic documentation, and the refund calculation. The Municipality of Naples acknowledged the defect under administrative review (autotutela), adjusted the taxable surface areas for all three tax years, and refunded € 4,800 by offsetting it against future taxes.

Refund of € 4,800 under administrative review (autotutela)
Case no. 3 — First-Instance Tax Court (CGT I grado)

Incorrect municipal property tax rate: assessment against an entrepreneur annulled for € 22,700

Giudice Tributario di I grado di Caserta — Year 2024

A building contractor received a municipal property tax assessment notice (avviso di accertamento IMU) from the Municipality of Caserta for the years 2020 and 2021, for a total of € 22,700, regarding an unoccupied apartment he owned. The Municipality had applied the maximum tax rate of 1.06% provided for unleased properties, whereas a 2019 municipal resolution — duly published on the Fiscal Federalism Portal (Portale del Federalismo Fiscale) within the statutory deadlines — had reduced the tax rate on unleased properties to 0.86%, with regular application for subsequent years. The municipal tax office had simply ignored its own updated resolution, applying the previous, superseded tax rate. Our defense analytically documented the discrepancy between the applied tax rate and the one in force pursuant to the resolution published on the Portal, attaching a printout of the Fiscal Federalism webpage. The First-Instance Tax Court (CGT di I grado) of Caserta upheld the appeal (ricorso), redetermining the property tax (IMU) due at € 6,200 (annulling the difference of € 16,500) and ordering the Municipality to pay legal costs. The remaining tax assessment was regularized through an installment plan.

Reduction from € 22,700 to € 6,200
Case no. 4 — First-Instance CGT

Agricultural land: exemption granted — Municipal property tax (IMU) annulled for € 9,350

Giudice Tributario di I grado di Salerno — Year 2023

A retired direct farmer (coltivatore diretto) received municipal property tax assessment notices (avvisi di accertamento IMU) from the Municipality of Battipaglia for agricultural land he farmed for the years 2018, 2019, and 2020, for a total amount of € 9,350. The Municipality had subjected the land to municipal property tax (IMU), denying the exemption provided for direct farmers and professional agricultural entrepreneurs, on the grounds that the taxpayer had reached retirement age and was no longer registered with the agricultural social security administration as a direct farmer. Our law firm established that the law does not require active social security registration to benefit from the municipal property tax (IMU) exemption on agricultural land, as the status of direct farmer under art. 2083 of the Italian Civil Code is sufficient and is not forfeited upon reaching retirement age where agricultural activity continues to be carried out on an ongoing and predominant basis. The First-Instance Tax Court (CGT di I grado) of Salerno upheld the appeal (ricorso) and fully annulled the three assessment notices, ordering the Municipality to pay legal costs in the amount of € 1,200.

Exemption granted — € 9,350 annulled
Case no. 5 — Tax Refund + First-Instance Tax Court (CGT)

Municipal property tax (IMU) overpaid for 4 years: € 7,200 refund obtained in court

CGT I grado di Napoli — Year 2024

The owner of an apartment categorized as B/2 (luxury residential dwelling) had paid municipal property tax (IMU) for the years 2018–2021 by applying the multiplier coefficient of 160 (applicable to ordinary residential dwellings in category A), whereas the correct coefficient for cadastral category B/2 is 140, as clearly set out in art. 13 of Decree-Law 201/2011. The error in calculating the multiplier coefficient resulted in an overpayment of approximately € 1,800 for each tax year, totaling € 7,200 over the four-year period. The taxpayer filed a refund claim (istanza di rimborso) with the Municipality of Naples within five years from the date of payment, but the Municipality failed to reply within the ninety-day statutory deadline, resulting in an implied rejection (silenzio-rifiuto) that can be challenged before the Tax Court (Giudice Tributario). The appeal against the implied rejection sought a refund of the full overpaid amount, plus statutory interest accruing from the date of each payment. The First-Instance Tax Court (CGT di I grado) of Naples upheld the appeal, ordering the Municipality to refund € 7,200 plus interest and legal costs. The refund was executed within four months of the judgment.

Refund of € 7,200 + interest
