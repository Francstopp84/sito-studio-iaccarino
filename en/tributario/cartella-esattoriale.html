Have you received a tax bill (cartella esattoriale)?
It may be unlawful. You have 60 days to act.

Thousands of tax bills (cartelle esattoriali) issued by the revenue collection agency (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) contain defects that render them voidable: improper service, time-barred claims, expiration of statutory deadlines, errors in the calculation of amounts, lack of statement of reasons. A tax lawyer can identify these defects and challenge the tax bill before the Tax Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria) before the measure becomes final and no longer contestable. The deadline is mandatory: 60 days from service, with no possibility of extension.

Regulatory framework

The rules governing tax collection

Presidential Decree of 29 September 1973, no. 602

The payment demand (cartella di pagamento): nature, content, and time limits

The tax bill (cartella esattoriale) is the enforceable title through which the Collection Agent (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) demands payment of a tax or social security debt enrolled into the tax roll (iscritto a ruolo). It is governed by Presidential Decree 602/1973, Art. 25 of which sets the time limit within which the bill must be served under penalty of forfeiture (decadenza): for direct taxes (IRPEF, IRES) by 31 December of the third year following that in which the assessment became final. Once this period has elapsed without service, the right of collection is definitively forfeited and any tax bill subsequently served is unlawful. Art. 26 governs the methods of serving the bill, non-compliance with which may lead to the nullity or legal non-existence of the act.

Presidential Decree 602/1973 — Arts. 25 and 26
Legislative Decree of 31 December 1992, no. 546 — Arts. 19–21

The appeal before the Tax Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria)

The appeal (ricorso) against the tax bill (cartella esattoriale) is lodged before the Tax Court of First Instance (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di primo grado, formerly Commissione Tributaria Provinciale) within 60 days of service of the bill, pursuant to Art. 21 of Legislative Decree 546/1992. The deadline is mandatory: failure to comply results in the act becoming permanently non-appealable. Art. 19 identifies the challengeable acts, including the payment demand (cartella di pagamento), administrative vehicle immobilisation (fermo amministrativo), tax mortgage (ipoteca esattoriale), and notice to pay (intimazione di pagamento). Art. 47 allows for a concurrent request for precautionary suspension of collection, which the court may grant where there is a prima facie case (fumus boni iuris) and a risk of irreparable harm from delay (periculum in mora, namely the risk of serious and irreparable damage arising from the collection).

Legislative Decree 546/1992 — Arts. 19, 21, and 47
Art. 2948 Civil Code

The five-year limitation period for periodic taxes

Art. 2948, no. 4, of the Civil Code provides that rights that must be exercised periodically each year or within shorter terms are subject to a five-year statute of limitations (prescrizione). Under this rule, taxes that accrue annually — municipal taxes such as IMU and TARI, road tax (bollo auto), INPS social security contributions — expire under the five-year limitation period. This means that if more than five years have passed since the last validly effected service without any tolling acts, the claim is extinguished by statute of limitations.

Art. 2948 Civil Code

Attention to mandatory administrative claim-mediation. For disputes involving amounts up to 50,000 euros, Art. 17-bis of Legislative Decree 546/1992 requires filing a preliminary claim-mediation petition (reclamo-mediazione) with the authority that issued the tax bill. Only after 90 days from the submission of the claim (or after a formal notice of rejection) may the appeal (ricorso) be filed with the court. The 60-day period for lodging the claim runs from service of the tax bill, but the time limit for filing the appeal in court is suspended during the 90 days of the out-of-court phase. Failure to comply with the mandatory claim-mediation requirement results in the inadmissibility of the appeal. The claim is not required when the tax bill is issued by INPS or other social security bodies for social security disputes.

The most common cases

When a tax bill can be challenged

Not all tax bills are lawful. There are recurring categories of defects that allow for the total or partial cancellation of the act. Identifying the correct defect is the critical first step in the defence.

Statute of limitations (prescrizione)

Time-barred tax bill (cartella prescritta)

The tax or social security claim asserted by the Authority has been extinguished by the expiry of the limitation period (five years for periodic taxes, ten years for IRPEF/VAT) without any valid tolling acts having been performed. The tax bill, even if formally compliant, is substantively unlawful.

Reference: Art. 2948 c.c.
Formal defect

Defective service (vizio di notifica)

The service of the tax bill occurred irregularly: to an individual not authorised to receive it, at the wrong address, through methods not permitted by law, without a certificate of service (relata di notifica), or with an incomplete certificate. Legally non-existent service produces no legal effects; void service can only be cured if the statutory requirements for remedying procedural defects under Art. 156 of the Code of Civil Procedure are met.

Reference: Art. 26 Presidential Decree 602/1973
Forfeiture (decadenza)

Expiration of statutory time limits (decadenza dei termini)

The creditor authority served the tax bill beyond the statutory forfeiture deadline (termine di decadenza). For income taxes, the deadline is set by Art. 25 of Presidential Decree 602/1973; specific deadlines apply to local taxes and VAT. Forfeiture can be raised by the court on its own motion (d'ufficio) and cannot be cured.

Reference: Art. 25 Presidential Decree 602/1973
Clerical error

Calculation error

The sums indicated in the tax bill do not correspond to the actual debt: interest calculated on an incorrect basis, penalties applied exceeding the statutory maximum, amounts already paid and not deducted, or duplication of items enrolled into the tax roll. A calculation error may permit the partial or total cancellation of the tax bill.

Reference: Art. 36 Presidential Decree 602/1973
Substantive defect

Lack of statement of reasons (mancata motivazione)

The tax bill does not state clearly and comprehensibly the grounds for the tax claim, the calculation of the amounts, or the prerequisite underlying decisions on which it is based. The obligation to provide a statement of reasons for a tax measure derives from Art. 7 of Law 212/2000 (Taxpayer's Rights Charter) and Art. 42 of Presidential Decree 600/1973; failure to comply renders the tax bill null and void.

Reference: Art. 7 L. 212/2000
Non-existence of the legal basis

Tax bill lacking a prerequisite act (cartella senza atto presupposto)

The tax collection notice (cartella esattoriale) was issued without the taxpayer ever having received the underlying assessment act (notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento), INPS debit notice (avviso di addebito), final judgment (sentenza passata in giudicato)) justifying the entry on the tax roll (iscrizione a ruolo). In this case, the tax collection notice is fundamentally unlawful and the appeal (ricorso) may also extend to the unserved underlying act.

Reference: Art. 19 D.Lgs. 546/1992

Our method

The six defence strategies

Defense against a tax collection notice (cartella esattoriale) is not one-dimensional. Our firm develops a comprehensive strategy combining multiple instruments, from verifying formal defects to challenging the merits, from urgent suspension to facilitated settlement (definizione agevolata) when advantageous.

I.

Verification of the statute of limitations (prescrizione)

Rigorous calculation of the limitation periods (five-year period for IMU, TARI, vehicle tax, INPS contributions; ten-year period for IRPEF, VAT (IVA), IRES) and verification of the validity of any acts interrupting the statute of limitations served in the meantime. The statute of limitations extinguishes the claim regardless of its merits.

II.

Plea of non-existent or null service (notifica)

Analysis of the certificate of service (relata di notifica) and delivery methods to identify defects causing the nullity or legal non-existence of the service itself. Non-existent service does not trigger the running of time limits; null service may be pleaded without time limits if the taxpayer had no knowledge of the act.

III.

Internal administrative review (autotutela)

Application to the taxing authority (ente impositore) or the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) for the administrative annulment of the tax collection notice when the defect is manifest and indisputable. Internal administrative review (autotutela) is the swiftest and most cost-effective remedy when the defect is patent; it does not stay the time limits for an appeal (ricorso) and must therefore be pursued concurrently with or following judicial appeal.

IV.

Complaint-mediation procedure (reclamo-mediazione)

For disputes up to 50,000 euros, the mandatory complaint-mediation procedure (reclamo-mediazione) pursuant to Art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992 allows for an automatic reduction of penalties to 35% in the event of an agreement. It is an out-of-court phase preliminary to the appeal (ricorso) that can lead to a favorable resolution without going to court, saving significant time and costs.

V.

Suspension of tax collection

Urgent application for interim suspension pursuant to Art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992 to stay enforcement proceedings (attachment, administrative seizure (fermo), mortgage) pending proceedings on the merits. The suspension is granted by the tax judge when prima facie justification (fumus boni iuris) and urgency (periculum in mora) are present, namely the risk of grave and irreparable harm resulting from enforcement.

VI.

Appeal before the Tax Court of First and Second Instance (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria - CGT)

Challenging the tax collection notice before the First-Instance Tax Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado) within 60 days of service, with potential appeal to the Second-Instance Tax Court (CGT di II grado) and, in cases involving questions of law only, appeal to the Court of Cassation (Corte di Cassazione). The appeal (ricorso) must be technically sound and based on specific, documented grounds of nullity, avoiding generic objections.

Five resolved cases

The following cases illustrate the actual defence work carried out by our law firm before the Tax Justice Courts (Corti di Giustizia Tributaria). Identifying details have been anonymised in accordance with client confidentiality.

Case no. 1 — CGT of First Instance

Five-year statute of limitations: INPS tax demand notice cancelled for € 38,400

Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado di Napoli — Year 2024

A craftsman from Naples received a tax demand notice (cartella esattoriale) from Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione for unpaid INPS social security contributions for the years 2016 and 2017, plus interest and penalties, for a total amount of 38,400 euros. The tax demand notice had been served in May 2024, more than seven years after the contribution liabilities had accrued. The defence promptly raised the plea of the five-year statute of limitations for social security claims pursuant to art. 3, paragraph 9, L. 335/1995 and art. 2948 of the Italian Civil Code (c.c.). The tax collection agent produced no act interrupting the limitation period served within the preceding five-year period. The Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado di Napoli, by judgment filed in September 2024, upheld the appeal (ricorso) in full, declared the claim time-barred, and ordered INPS to pay legal costs.

Total annulment — € 38,400
Case no. 2 — CGT of Second Instance

Legally non-existent service: VAT tax demand notice of € 61,200 declared null and void

Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di II grado della Campania — Year 2024

A limited liability company (società a responsabilità limitata) received a tax payment demand (cartella esattoriale) for alleged VAT (IVA) omissions for the years 2017 and 2018, for a total of 61.200 euros. Analysis of the documentation revealed that the payment demand had been served exclusively in a sealed envelope to a person (a security guard of the building) completely lacking any relationship with the company, neither as an employee nor as a cohabitant of the director. The proof of service (relata di notifica) was deficient and contradictory on several points. The defence filed an appeal (ricorso) before the First-Instance Tax Court of Justice (CGT di I grado), arguing the absolute nullity of the service for non-compliance with the formalities required by art. 26 D.P.R. 602/1973 and art. 60 D.P.R. 600/1973, with the resulting lack of legal knowledge of the deed by the company. The first-instance judge dismissed the appeal on procedural grounds. On appeal, the Corte di Giustizia Tributaria della Campania overturned the decision, declaring the service legally non-existent, rendering the payment demand as never having been validly brought to the taxpayer's knowledge and therefore not yet final. The tax authority's claim was dismissed in its entirety.

Tax bill (cartella) annulled on appeal — € 61.200
Case no. 3 — Administrative review (autotutela) + First-Instance CGT

Double payment: refund obtained for € 14.750

Revenue Agency–Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) / CGT I grado di Napoli — Year 2023

A professional (chartered accountant / commercialista) received a tax payment demand (cartella esattoriale) regarding personal income tax (IRPEF) for 2019 for 14.750 euros, inclusive of penalties and interest. Analysis of the client's tax documentation revealed that the debt had already been paid in full following a previous instalment plan, with payment receipts duly retained. It was therefore a duplicate entry in the tax rolls (iscrizione a ruolo), likely caused by an IT error in allocating the payments received. Our firm first submitted a petition for administrative review (istanza di autotutela) to the Agenzia delle Entrate, attaching documentation of the payments made and banking correspondence proving the wire transfers. When the statutory deadlines expired without a response, and after the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) had in the meantime served a payment reminder, an appeal (ricorso) was filed before the First-Instance Tax Court of Justice (CGT di I grado). During the precautionary hearing, the Authority acknowledged the error, proceeded with the annulment under administrative review (annullamento in autotutela) of the tax bill, and refunded any sums already withheld as down payments towards enforcement. Legal costs were offset.

Tax bill (cartella) annulled + refund — € 14.750
Case no. 4 — First-Instance CGT

Tax bill (cartella) without underlying act: annulment for failure to serve the notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento)

Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado di Salerno — Year 2023

A taxpayer unexpectedly received a tax payment demand (cartella esattoriale) for personal income tax (IRPEF) for the years 2018 and 2019, for the total amount of 27.600 euros, without ever having previously received any notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento) justifying registration in the tax rolls (iscrizione a ruolo). Analysis of the client's tax position revealed that the Agenzia delle Entrate had issued an analytical assessment notice for those years, but service had been attempted at the taxpayer's former residential address, which he had vacated and duly communicated to the Civil Registry (Anagrafe) in 2016. The notice had never reached the rightful addressee. The appeal (ricorso) argued the nullity of the service of the underlying act, with the resulting unlawfulness of the entry in the tax rolls and of the tax bill issued on the basis of an act never validly brought to the taxpayer's knowledge. Concurrently, an application for interim suspension (sospensiva cautelare) was filed and promptly granted by the judge. The First-Instance Tax Court of Justice of Salerno (CGT di I grado di Salerno) upheld the appeal on the merits, declaring the unlawfulness of the tax bill due to the lack of legal knowledge of the underlying act and cancelling the claim in its entirety.

Total annulment — € 27.600
Case no. 5 — Complaint-mediation (reclamo-mediazione)

Lack of statement of reasons: 80% reduction through tax mediation

Claim-mediation (reclamo-mediazione) pursuant to art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Year 2024

A small commercial business owner received a tax payment demand (cartella esattoriale) relating to unpaid waste tax (TARI) and municipal surcharge for the years 2019, 2020, and 2021, for a total amount of 9.800 euros, of which over 4.000 euros consisted of penalties and interest. The tax bill contained no statement of reasons regarding the calculation method of the applied TARI, the criteria for determining the taxable area, or the rates applied for each year. It therefore constituted a manifest breach of the duty to state reasons provided for by art. 7 of L. 212/2000 (Statute of the Taxpayer / Statuto del Contribuente), which requires the taxing authority (Ente impositore) to specify the factual grounds and legal reasons for the claim. Since the value was below 50.000 euros, the complaint-mediation procedure (reclamo-mediazione) was initiated with the Municipality. During the mediation phase, the Authority acknowledged the defects of the tax bill and proposed a facilitated settlement with the removal of penalties and late payment interest, limiting the claim solely to the originally due tax of 1.900 euros, resulting in a net saving of 80% compared to the original claim. The client accepted the mediation proposal, settling the dispute out of court.

Mediation: 80% saving — from € 9.800 to € 1.900
