Frozen account, garnished salary, property at risk.
Tax enforcement attachment is not always lawful. Defend your rights.

Tax enforcement attachment (pignoramento esattoriale) is the instrument through which the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) forcibly recovers unpaid taxes, drawing directly upon the debtor's assets: bank accounts, salaries, pensions, real estate, receivables from third parties. Unlike ordinary enforcement, tax attachment takes place without prior authorization from the judge: it is an autonomous administrative procedure governed by special rules laid down by D.P.R. 602/1973. However, mandatory statutory limits, essential procedural requirements, and defects exist that render the attachment unlawful. A tax lawyer can halt enforcement, obtain an interim stay, and, in the most severe cases, have the entire procedure declared unlawful with restitution of sums already collected.

Regulatory framework

The legal framework of tax enforcement attachment

Presidential Decree (D.P.R.) 29 September 1973, no. 602 — Art. 72-bis

Third-party attachment: bank accounts and claims

Art. 72-bis of D.P.R. 602/1973 governs tax collection third-party garnishment (pignoramento presso terzi esattoriale), the most frequent and immediate instrument used by the Revenue Collection Agency (Agente della Riscossione). By means of this act, the Revenue Collection Agency directly serves upon the credit institution (or another third-party debtor of the taxpayer) the order to freeze the funds lying in bank accounts and to pay them over, within 60 days, to the Revenue Collection Agency itself. For bank accounts, service upon the credit institution results in the immediate freezing of the funds. Art. 72-bis provides for certain safeguards: service upon the debtor (taxpayer) must take place at the same time as or immediately following service upon the third party. The absence of such service or its irregularity results in the nullity of the garnishment. For bank accounts into which salaries or pensions are paid, the quantitative limits laid down by art. 72-ter apply and, for pensions, the thresholds of exemption from attachment set out in art. 545, para. 4, c.p.c.

Art. 72-bis D.P.R. 602/1973 — Third-party garnishment
Presidential Decree (D.P.R.) of 29 September 1973, no. 602 — Art. 72-ter

Limits on the garnishment of salaries and pensions

Art. 72-ter of D.P.R. 602/1973 establishes mandatory quantitative limits on the attachment of wages, salaries, pensions, and other indemnities arising from an employment relationship. Sums due by way of salary or equivalent may be attached to the extent of one-tenth (10%) for net amounts up to 2,500 euros, one-seventh (approximately 14.3%) for net amounts from 2,500 to 5,000 euros, and one-fifth (20%) for net amounts exceeding 5,000 euros. For pensions, the limits are more restrictive: art. 545, para. 4, c.p.c. provides that a pension cannot be attached for the portion corresponding to the social allowance (assegno sociale) increased by one half (currently approximately 780 euros per month). Exceeding these quantitative limits by the Revenue Collection Agency (Agente della Riscossione) renders the attachment unlawful for the exceeding portion, and the taxpayer is entitled to the release of the sums attached in excess. Verifying compliance with these limits is one of the first steps that legal counsel must carry out.

Art. 72-ter D.P.R. 602/1973 — Salary/pension limits
Presidential Decree (D.P.R.) of 29 September 1973, no. 602 — Art. 76

Real estate foreclosure: the 120,000-euro threshold and the primary residence

Art. 76 of D.P.R. 602/1973, in the text currently in force following the amendments introduced by art. 52, para. 1, of D.L. 69/2013 (converted into Law 98/2013), establishes important limits on tax collection real estate foreclosure (pignoramento immobiliare esattoriale). In the first place, the Revenue Collection Agency (Agente della Riscossione) cannot proceed with real estate foreclosure where the debt enrolled in the tax roll (iscritto a ruolo) is less than 120,000 euros, and this condition must be assessed with reference to the sole real property owned by the debtor. Secondly, foreclosure cannot involve the sole real property owned by a natural person debtor, used for residential purposes and in which the debtor has established their registered civil residence (residenza anagrafica), provided that the property is not a luxury property (cadastral categories A/1, A/7, A/8, and A/9). Where these prerequisites are met, the Revenue Collection Agency may register a mortgage (ipoteca) but cannot proceed with foreclosure. A breach of these limits renders the real estate foreclosure radically null and void.

Art. 76 D.P.R. 602/1973 — Real estate and primary residence
Art. 615 c.p.c. — Opposition to execution

Opposition to execution: the primary judicial remedy

Art. 615 c.p.c. governs the opposition to enforcement (opposizione all’esecuzione), the judicial remedy by which the debtor may challenge the creditor's right to proceed with forced enforcement. In the case of tax collection enforcement, the opposition is filed before the Tax Justice Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria) (when the existence or validity of the tax claim is contested: statute of limitations, forfeiture, payment, defects of the underlying act) or before the ordinary enforcement judge (giudice dell’esecuzione) (when the form and modalities of enforcement are contested: attachment exceeding statutory limits, lack of prior notice, breach of exemption from attachment). An opposition pursuant to art. 615, para. 2, c.p.c. lodged in the course of enforcement may be accompanied by an application for interim suspension pursuant to art. 624 c.p.c., which the judge may grant where there is a prima facie case (fumus boni iuris) and danger in delay (periculum in mora).

Art. 615 c.p.c. — Opposition to execution

Prior notice of administrative immobilization: an instrument frequently used abusively. Prior to proceeding with real estate foreclosure, the Revenue Collection Agency (Agente della Riscossione) is required to serve the notice of demand for payment (avviso di intimazione di pagamento) pursuant to art. 50, para. 2, of D.P.R. 602/1973. If more than 365 days have elapsed since service of the tax bill (cartella esattoriale), the Revenue Collection Agency must serve such notice before proceeding with enforcement. Failure to serve the demand notice, in these cases, renders the subsequent attachment unlawful. Administrative immobilization of vehicles (fermo amministrativo), on the other hand, may be registered without prior demand for debts exceeding 1,000 euros, but immobilization is also subject to limits and may be challenged where the vehicle is essential to work activities.

The factual circumstances

Types of tax collection attachment

Tax collection enforcement may target different assets, each subject to its own rules, specific limits, and distinct legal remedies. Knowing the type of attachment is essential for determining the correct defense strategy.

Art. 72-bis

Bank account garnishment

The bank account is frozen from the date of service on the credit institution. Within 60 days, the bank must remit the funds to the Revenue Collection Agency. Sums credited after the attachment remain unencumbered (the garnishment freezes the balance as of the service date). Garnishment is partially restricted if the account is the one into which salary is paid.

Reference: Art. 72-bis D.P.R. 602/1973
Art. 72-ter

Garnishment of salary and pension

The employer (or social security entity) is served with the garnishment order and must withhold the attached portion monthly. Mandatory limits: 1/10, 1/7, or 1/5 depending on the net amount of the salary. For pensions, the non-attachable portion corresponds to the social allowance increased by 50%. Non-compliance with these limits gives the right to have the excess released.

Reference: Art. 72-ter D.P.R. 602/1973; art. 545 c.p.c.
Art. 76

Real estate foreclosure

Real estate expropriation is the most severe enforcement remedy. It cannot be carried out if the debt is less than 120,000 euros or if the property is the primary non-luxury place of residence. Prior to real estate foreclosure, the Revenue Collection Agency registers a mortgage: this mortgage may also be challenged when statutory prerequisites are not met or when the underlying debt is extinguished.

Reference: Art. 76 D.P.R. 602/1973
Art. 72-bis

Third-party garnishment

In addition to bank accounts, third-party garnishment may target claims of the debtor against third parties: rental income, fees for professional services, corporate dividends, and tax refunds. The third party garnishee (the tenant, the client, the investee company) becomes legally bound to pay the Revenue Collection Agency instead of the debtor.

Reference: Art. 72-bis D.P.R. 602/1973
Art. 86

Administrative seizure (fermo amministrativo)

The administrative immobilization of a vehicle (fermo amministrativo) is technically a pre-enforcement precautionary measure, not an attachment in the technical sense, but it produces similar effects: the vehicle may not be driven, sold, or exported. It can be challenged if the debt is less than 1,000 euros, if it was improperly served, if the vehicle is essential for work activity, or if it is registered in the name of a person other than the debtor.

Reference: Art. 86 D.P.R. 602/1973
Art. 77

Tax mortgage (ipoteca esattoriale)

A tax lien (ipoteca esattoriale) is registered on the debtor’s real estate assets to secure the tax claim (it does not require the immediate commencement of foreclosure proceedings). It may be registered when the debt exceeds 20,000 euros. If the claim is extinguished (by statute of limitations, payment, or cancellation), the lien must be cancelled. Failure to cancel the lien despite the extinction of the debt justifies an appeal (ricorso) for compulsory cancellation.

Reference: Art. 77 D.P.R. 602/1973

Our method

The six defence strategies

Defence against tax enforcement attachment (pignoramento esattoriale) requires promptness and a comprehensive approach. The primary objective is the immediate suspension of enforcement; the ultimate objective is the cancellation of the debt or the release of the seized assets.

I.

Urgent interim suspension (sospensiva cautelare)

First step to take in the event of an ongoing attachment: request for an interim suspension of enforcement before the Tax Justice Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria, pursuant to art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992) or before the ordinary enforcement judge (pursuant to art. 624 c.p.c.), depending on the nature of the procedural defect raised. If granted, the stay immediately halts the transfer of the seized sums to the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) and may lead to the unfreezing of the blocked bank account. Deadlines are extremely tight: the interim hearing is usually held within 7-15 days of the filing of the appeal (ricorso).

II.

Verification of quantitative statutory limits

For the attachment of salaries and pensions, immediate verification of compliance with the statutory limits under art. 72-ter D.P.R. 602/1973 and art. 545, c. 4, c.p.c. If the Collection Agent has attached amounts exceeding the statutory limits, the taxpayer is entitled to the release of the excess without having to prove defects going to the merits of the underlying debt. For bank accounts into which salaries are deposited, verification of the non-attachable threshold corresponding to three times the social allowance (assegno sociale).

III.

Plea of statute of limitations or forfeiture (prescrizione o decadenza)

Tax attachment is unlawful when the underlying claim is extinguished due to the statute of limitations (prescrizione) or forfeiture (decadenza). This plea must be raised before the Tax Justice Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria) by lodging an appeal (ricorso) challenging, as an incidental matter, the tax bill (cartella esattoriale) underlying the attachment, pleading the limitation period accrued since the last valid service of process. If upheld, the attachment is declared null and void ab origine and any sums already collected must be reimbursed.

IV.

Opposition for violation of real estate enforcement limits

For real estate foreclosures, verification of compliance with the 120,000 euro debt threshold and the prohibition against seizing a non-luxury primary residence. If the Collection Agent has initiated foreclosure in breach of these limits, an opposition under art. 615 c.p.c. before the enforcement judge is the remedy to declare the invalidity of the real estate expropriation. A tax lien registered in breach of these statutory limits may likewise be challenged.

V.

Challenge based on procedural defects

Tax attachment must comply with strict procedural requirements: the prior payment notice (intimazione di pagamento) under art. 50, c. 2, D.P.R. 602/1973 must precede enforcement if more than 365 days have elapsed since the tax bill (cartella esattoriale); the attachment order must be served on the debtor concurrently; the third-party garnishee (terzo pignorato) cannot make payment before the deadline for lodging an opposition has expired. Violation of these formalities entails the invalidity of the attachment, irrespective of the merits of the underlying tax debt.

VI.

Instalment plans and facilitated settlement as an exit strategy

In cases where the underlying debt is valid and there are no actionable grounds on the merits, paying the debt in instalments under art. 19 D.P.R. 602/1973 or adhering to the facilitated tax settlement (rottamazione-quater, where still accessible) allows for the immediate suspension of the ongoing attachment. A debtor who obtains an instalment plan is entitled to the suspension of enforcement proceedings pending the payment of instalments. Submitting an application for an instalment plan stays enforcement activity by operation of law.

Case no. 1 — Tax Court of First Instance (CGT I grado) and enforcement opposition, Naples

Bank account unblocked due to statute of limitations: € 28,400 returned

Tax Court of First Instance (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado) of Naples / Court of Naples — Year 2024

A Neapolitan artisan discovered that his bank account had been blocked by the Revenue Agency-Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione) through third-party garnishment pursuant to art. 72-bis D.P.R. 602/1973, for an amount of 28,400 euros referring to INPS contributions for the years 2015 and 2016. The client contacted our firm on the same day he discovered the block. Immediate analysis of the available documentation (role extract plan, notification extracts of the acts) revealed that the original tax bill (cartella esattoriale) had been notified in 2017 and that no other act from the Collection Agent had been notified to the taxpayer in the seven subsequent years. The contribution credit, subject to the five-year statute of limitations pursuant to art. 3, paragraph 9, L. 335/1995, had therefore expired in 2022. The 2024 garnishment had taken place on a credit that was already extinguished. Our firm filed an urgent appeal to the Tax Court of First Instance (CGT di I grado) of Naples, pleading the statute of limitations of the credit and requesting a precautionary suspension of the garnishment. The tax judge granted the suspension within 10 days. During the merits proceedings, the Tax Court (CGT) upheld the appeal, declared the credit time-barred, and ordered the release of the sums blocked in the bank account (approximately 14,200 euros not yet transferred to the Agent) and the return of those already withdrawn, for a total amount of 28,400 euros.

Account unblocked + refund € 28,400
Case no. 2 — Enforcement opposition, Naples

Salary garnishment beyond limits: immediate release of the excess

Court of Naples — Enforcement Section — Year 2024

An employee of a private Neapolitan company found himself with a deduction on his monthly salary made by his employer on the order of the Revenue Agency-Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione). The worker's net monthly salary was 2,100 euros. The garnishment ordered by the Collection Agent required the employer to withhold and pay to the Treasury the monthly sum of 420 euros, equal to one-fifth of the net salary. This share was, however, higher than the legal limits: for a net salary of less than 2,500 euros, art. 72-ter D.P.R. 602/1973 sets the maximum limit at one-tenth (1/10) of the salary, which in the specific case corresponded to 210 euros per month. The Collection Agent was therefore withholding double what was permitted by law. Our firm filed an opposition to enforcement acts pursuant to art. 617 c.p.c. before the Court of Naples, enforcement section, requesting the immediate reduction of the deduction to legal limits and the return of the amounts withheld in excess in the previous months. The Court upheld the opposition, ordering the reduction of the deduction to 210 euros per month and the return of the excess amounts withheld for a total amount of 2,730 euros (13 months of excess). The debtor recovered the sums withheld in excess and saw his monthly deduction halved.

Deduction halved + refund € 2,730
Case no. 3 — Tax Court of First Instance (CGT I grado), Naples

Primary residence: blocking of unlawful real estate garnishment — property released

Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado di Napoli — Year 2023

A retired woman from Naples was served with a real estate foreclosure order (pignoramento immobiliare) by the Revenue Agency–Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) concerning her sole residence, an 80 sq. m. apartment in cadastral category A/3, where she had been officially resident for over 20 years. The total debt entered on the tax roll (iscritto a ruolo) amounted to 86,000 euros, relating to personal income tax (IRPEF) and social security contributions. The foreclosure was doubly unlawful: firstly, the debt was below the 120,000-euro threshold provided for by art. 76, c. 2, lett. a), D.P.R. 602/1973 as a prerequisite for real estate foreclosure; secondly, the property was the taxpayer’s sole home, used as a primary residence and classified under a non-luxury cadastral category, thereby falling under the protection of art. 76, c. 1, D.P.R. 602/1973. The foreclosure was therefore null and void due to a dual violation of the law. Our firm urgently filed an appeal (ricorso) with a simultaneous application for interim suspension (istanza di sospensiva cautelare) before the First-Instance Tax Justice Court (CGT di I grado) of Naples. The suspension was granted ex parte (inaudita altera parte) within 48 hours, immediately staying the expropriation proceedings. On the merits, the CGT subsequently upheld the appeal and declared the real estate foreclosure null and void, ordering the cancellation of the lien from the land registries.

Real estate foreclosure annulled — primary residence released
Case No. 4 — Enforcement opposition (opposizione all’esecuzione), Salerno

Administrative vehicle immobilisation (fermo amministrativo) on a commercial vehicle: cancellation and damages

Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado di Salerno — Year 2023

A plumber who is the sole proprietor of an individual business located in the province of Salerno was served with notice of an administrative immobilisation order (fermo amministrativo) pursuant to art. 86 D.P.R. 602/1973 on the only van registered to his business, which was essential for conducting his work (transporting tools, reaching client work sites). The debt for which the order had been registered amounted to 3,400 euros, relating to INPS social security contributions for the years 2019-2020. The measure made it impossible to use the van, forcing the professional to rent a replacement vehicle at his own expense. An analysis of the situation identified two grounds of illegality: first, INPS had not preceded the immobilisation order with the required service of a debit notice (avviso di addebito), thereby omitting a mandatory procedural step; second, the case law of the Court of Cassation (Cass., Sez. Trib., n. 14701/2019) has clarified that an immobilisation order on assets essential to a business activity (beni strumentali) may be challenged on grounds of disproportion to the collection objective, whenever the damage inflicted on the economic activity outweighs the benefit to the Treasury (Erario). The appeal (ricorso) before the CGT secured an interim suspension of the immobilisation order pending the merits and, in the final judgment, its full cancellation. The taxpayer was also awarded reimbursement of the rental costs for the replacement vehicle as damages arising from an unlawful restriction.

Vehicle immobilisation cancelled + reimbursement of rental costs
Case No. 5 — First-Instance Tax Justice Court (CGT I grado), Naples

Garnishment without prior notice: procedural nullity and refund of € 18,600

Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado di Napoli — Year 2024

A commercial company based in Naples discovered that the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) had garnished the sum of 18,600 euros from a company bank account, without any formal payment notice (avviso di intimazione di pagamento) having previously been served on the company. The original tax bill (cartella esattoriale) had been served in 2021, and the garnishment occurred in 2024, more than 365 days after service of the original bill. Pursuant to art. 50, c. 2, D.P.R. 602/1973, if more than one year has elapsed since the last valid service, the Collection Agent must precede enforcement action by serving a formal payment notice. The omission of this instrument renders the subsequent garnishment null and void for failure to comply with the legally prescribed procedure. Our firm lodged an appeal (ricorso) before the First-Instance Tax Justice Court (CGT di I grado) of Naples, pleading as the primary ground the nullity of the garnishment due to lack of prior notice pursuant to art. 50, c. 2, D.P.R. 602/1973. The judge granted an interim stay during the preliminary hearing, freezing the transfer of the funds still held in the account. On the merits, the CGT declared the garnishment null and void and ordered the restitution of the entire sum of 18,600 euros that had already been transferred by the bank to the Collection Agent prior to the suspension, as well as legal costs. The Collection Agent may repeat the enforcement procedure, this time in full compliance with statutory requirements.

Garnishment void due to procedural defect — € 18,600 refunded
