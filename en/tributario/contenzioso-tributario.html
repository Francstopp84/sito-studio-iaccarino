D.Lgs. 546/1992  •  L. 130/2022  •  CGT Reform

Have you received a notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento) or an assessment act?
You have 60 days to appeal. Tax defense begins immediately.

Tax litigation encompasses the judicial and out-of-court procedures through which a taxpayer defends against unlawful assessment acts issued by the tax authorities: IRPEF, IRES, and VAT (IVA) assessments, INPS debit notices (avvisi di addebito), tax collection notices (cartelle esattoriali), settlement notices (avvisi di liquidazione), administrative seizures (fermi amministrativi), and tax mortgages. Tax proceedings are governed by D.Lgs. 546/1992, substantially reformed by Law 130/2022 (Tax Justice Reform), which introduced significant innovations: written witness evidence, professional tax judiciary, and preliminary references on points of law to the Court of Cassation (giurisprudenza consolidata). The appeal (ricorso) must be filed within 60 days of service of the challenged act before the territorially competent First-Instance Tax Court (Giudice Tributario di I grado). For disputes up to 50,000 euros, the complaint-mediation procedure (reclamo-mediazione) under Art. 17-bis is a mandatory preliminary step. A tax lawyer analyzes the defects of the challenged act, assesses opportunities for facilitated settlement (definizione agevolata), and develops the most effective litigation strategy for your case.

Regulatory framework

Tax proceedings: fundamental provisions

D.Lgs. 31 December 1992, no. 546 — Code of Tax Procedure

Structure of tax proceedings: jurisdiction, parties, and procedure

D.Lgs. 546/1992 governs tax proceedings in all their aspects: the jurisdiction of the Tax Courts (Giudici Tributari, formerly Commissioni Tributarie Provinciali e Regionali), appealable acts (Art. 19: notices of assessment, tax collection notices (cartelle esattoriali), settlement notices, denials of refund, administrative seizures and tax mortgages), time limits for appeal (Art. 21: 60 days from service of the act), formal requirements of the petition (Art. 18), appearance of the parties (Arts. 22–23), conduct of the hearing, and gathering of evidence. Tax proceedings are fundamentally documentary: oral witness testimony was traditionally excluded, but the 2022 Reform introduced written witness testimony for relevant issues of fact. The tax judge rules solely on the legality of the challenged act and, on the merits, on the amount of the tax liability, except in cases of full jurisdiction. Orders for legal costs are governed by Art. 15: the judge must order the losing party to pay costs, unless offset for justified grounds.

D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Code of Tax Procedure
L. 31 August 2022, no. 130 — Tax Justice Reform

Key innovations of the 2022 Reform: professional judiciary and witness evidence

Law 130/2022 has implemented the most profound reform of the tax process since the Nineties. The main innovations introduced are: (1) the professional tax judiciary (magistratura tributaria professionale), with the provision for full-time tax judges (so-called «togati»), progressively intended to replace the non-professional component; (2) the written witness testimony (prova testimoniale scritta) (art. 7, co. 4, D.Lgs. 546/1992, as amended), which allows the parties to request the taking of evidence in writing regarding relevant disputed facts, significantly extending the evidentiary means available in the tax process; (3) the preliminary ruling before the Court of Cassation (rinvio pregiudiziale in giurisprudenza di legittimita'), which allows the Tax Courts (CGT) to refer to the Court of Cassation legal issues relevant to the resolution of the case, shortening the time for the formation of established case law (diritto vivente); (4) the single judge (giudice monocratico) for disputes with a value of less than 3,000 euros, with simplified proceedings; (5) the strengthening of judicial conciliation (conciliazione giudiziale), with an expansion of the cases in which it is also admissible on appeal.

L. 130/2022 — Tax Justice Reform
Art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Disputes up to € 50,000

Mandatory complaint-mediation (reclamo-mediazione): procedure and benefits

Art. 17-bis of D.Lgs. 546/1992 provides that, for tax disputes of a value not exceeding 50,000 euros, the appeal (ricorso) also produces the effects of an administrative complaint-mediation (reclamo-mediazione) vis-à-vis the tax authority (Ente impositore). Within 90 days of filing the complaint, the Tax Office may grant it, propose mediation, or reject it. Only after the 90 days have elapsed (or in the event of a negative response) may the appeal be filed with the court. Facilitated mediation provides for an automatic reduction of penalties to 35% of the statutory minimum in the event of an agreement, a significant advantage compared to the 40% reduction applicable in the settlement of pending disputes. The value of the dispute is determined by the individual challenged deed, net of penalties and interest. In the event of failure to file a complaint for disputes under 50,000 euros, the appeal is inadmissible.

Art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Claim-mediation (reclamo-mediazione)
Art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Interim suspension (sospensione cautelare)

The suspension of enforcement of the challenged tax assessment

Art. 47 of D.Lgs. 546/1992 allows the appellant to request, concurrently with the appeal (ricorso) (or separately, by specific application), the suspension of enforcement of the challenged act, when enforcement may cause serious and irreparable harm. Granting the stay of enforcement requires two conditions: fumus boni iuris (probable merits of the appeal) and periculum in mora (risk of serious and irreparable harm resulting from the immediate enforcement of the act). The judge may rule on the interim application even before considering the merits, in chambers (camera di consiglio), and the stay remains effective until the first-instance judgment (unless renewed on appeal). In cases of extreme urgency, it is possible to request a suspension «inaudita altera parte» (ex parte), by an order issued even before notification to the opposing parties.

Art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Interim relief (tutela cautelare)
Art. 62 D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Appeal to the second-instance CGT

The appeal to the Second-Instance Tax Court of Justice (Giudice Tributario di II grado)

Art. 62 of D.Lgs. 546/1992 provides that judgments of the first-instance Tax Court of Justice (CGT di I grado) may be appealed before the second-instance Tax Court of Justice (Giudice Tributario di II grado, formerly Commissione Tributaria Regionale) within 60 days of their service (notificazione) or, in the absence of service, within six months of their publication. The appeal must contain specific grounds of challenge: the introduction of new matters not raised in the first instance is not permitted, except for those that may be raised by the court of its own motion (d’ufficio). The 2022 Reform introduced the possibility of a conditional cross-appeal (appello incidentale condizionato), enabling the party that prevailed at first instance to lodge an appeal subject to the main appeal being upheld. Judicial conciliation (conciliazione giudiziale) is permitted on appeal (art. 48 of D.Lgs. 546/1992), with a reduction of penalties to 50% of the statutory minimum. Judgments of the second-instance CGT may be appealed before the Court of Cassation (giurisprudenza consolidata) pursuant to art. 360 c.p.c. on grounds of law only.

Art. 62 D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Appeal

Suspension of tax collection pending proceedings. Pursuant to art. 15 of D.Lgs. 546/1992, the amounts claimed under an enforceable notice of tax assessment (avviso di accertamento esecutivo) are collectible to the extent of one-third during first-instance proceedings and in full only after a judgment by the second-instance CGT in favour of the Revenue. This means that, during first-instance proceedings, the taxpayer is required to pay only one-third of the disputed taxes, being able to challenge the act without having to pay the entire amount immediately. The stay of enforcement pursuant to art. 47 also suspends this third, allowing for the complete suspension of tax collection pending a ruling on the merits.

The procedural stages

The stages of tax litigation

Stage 1 — Within 60 days of service

Lodging of the appeal (or claim)

The appeal (ricorso) (which for disputes ≤ 50,000 euros also produces the effects of a complaint) is brought before the territorially competent first-instance Tax Court of Justice (CGT di I grado). It must contain the details of the parties, the details of the challenged act, the subject matter of the claim, and specific grounds of appeal. It is served on the tax authority (Ente impositore) and then filed with the court within 30 days of service.

Stage 2 — Within 90 days of the claim (only for ≤ 50,000 euros)

Mandatory mediation stage

The Office examines the claim and may accept it, propose mediation, or reject it. If an agreement is reached, penalties are reduced to 35%. After 90 days have elapsed without a response or in the event of a refusal, the appeal is filed with the court and the judicial proceedings commence.

Stage 3 — After filing

Potential interim relief and evidentiary phase

If requested, the court rules on the application for suspension of enforcement. The parties file briefs, counterarguments, documents, and expert reports. The 2022 Reform allows written witness evidence on disputed facts relevant to the decision.

Stage 4 — Hearing and decision

Hearing on the merits and judgment of the first-instance CGT

The hearing on the merits may take place in open court (upon request of a party) or in chambers (camera di consiglio). The judge decides by judgment (sentenza), which is published and capable of being served. Legal costs are borne by the unsuccessful party, unless offset between the parties (compensazione) on just grounds. The judgment is provisionally enforceable.

Stage 5 — Within 60 days of service of the judgment

Potential appeal to the second-instance CGT

If the judgment is unfavourable (wholly or partially), an appeal may be lodged before the second-instance CGT within 60 days of service. On appeal, new documents are admissible and it is possible to apply for a stay of execution of the appealed judgment under art. 52 D.Lgs. 546/1992.

Types of proceedings

Which instrument is suitable for your case

Tax litigation does not end with court appeals. Out-of-court mechanisms exist — mediation, conciliation, agreed tax assessment (accertamento con adesione) — which in many cases deliver better outcomes with lower costs and shorter timeframes. The choice of the most appropriate mechanism depends on the value of the dispute, the strength of the grounds of illegality, and the taxpayer's risk appetite.

Art. 21 D.Lgs. 546/1992

Appeal to the first-instance CGT (Ricorso CGT I grado)

The primary remedy: challenging the tax act before the First-Instance Tax Court of Justice (Giudice Tributario di I grado) where the assessing authority is based. Time limits: 60 days from service of the act (or from its actual knowledge). Mandatory content: identification of the parties, challenged act, specific grounds, relief sought. Production of documents is permitted up to 20 days prior to the hearing. The first-instance CGT decides by judgment in a collegiate panel (three judges) or by a single judge (≤ 3,000 euros).

Reference: Artt. 18–21 D.Lgs. 546/1992
Art. 62 D.Lgs. 546/1992

Appeal to the second-instance CGT (Appello CGT II grado)

Challenging the first-instance judgment: lodged before the regional second-instance Tax Court of Justice (CGT di II grado) within 60 days of service of the judgment (or six months from publication). The appeal must contain specific grounds for criticising the challenged judgment. Neither new claims nor exceptions not raised at first instance are admissible (except for those that may be raised by the court of its own motion). On appeal, a cross-appeal by the prevailing party and judicial conciliation (conciliazione giudiziale) are also available.

Reference: Artt. 62–66 D.Lgs. 546/1992
Art. 360 c.p.c.

Appeal to the Court of Cassation (impugnazione in grado di appello)

Challenging the second-instance judgment on grounds of law only (breach of law, lack of or defective reasoning, procedural error / error in procedendo): an appeal to the Court of Cassation (impugnazione in grado di appello) in tax matters does not allow for a review of the merits. The time limits are 60 days from service of the second-instance judgment. The 2022 Reform introduced the preliminary reference from the CGT to the Court of Cassation, which can significantly shorten the time needed to resolve questions of law. Before the Court of Cassation, the taxpayer must be represented by a lawyer admitted to the special bar of cassazionisti.

Reference: Art. 62, co. 2, D.Lgs. 546/1992
Art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992

Claim-mediation (≤ € 50,000) (Reclamo-mediazione)

For disputes up to 50,000 euros, the administrative complaint-mediation (reclamo-mediazione) is mandatory and triggers a 90-day out-of-court phase during which the Tax Office may grant the complaint, propose mediation, or reject it. In the event of a mediation agreement, penalties are reduced to 35% of the statutory minimum. Failure to comply with this requirement results in the appeal being inadmissible. It is a strategically vital mechanism to reduce penalties and avoid judicial proceedings.

Reference: Art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992
Art. 48 D.Lgs. 546/1992

Judicial conciliation (Conciliazione giudiziale)

Conciliation (conciliazione) can be proposed by either party at any stage and instance of the proceedings, including on appeal. If accepted, the proceedings are extinguished and penalties are reduced to 40% of the statutory minimum (in first instance) or to 50% (on appeal). Conciliation is particularly advantageous when the appeal (ricorso) is supported by solid arguments but the outcome of the proceedings is uncertain: it allows for a definite reduction of penalties without facing the risk of litigation, while also avoiding the costs and duration of the trial.

Reference: Articles 48 and 48-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992
Art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992

Interim suspension (sospensiva cautelare)

Application for the suspension of the enforcement of the contested measure (istanza di sospensione), which may be submitted alongside the appeal (ricorso) or independently. It stays the collection even of the one-third amount collectable pending judgment. It requires a prima facie legal basis (fumus boni iuris) and danger in delay (periculum in mora). In cases of absolute urgency (imminent enforcement, administrative seizure, legal mortgage), it is possible to obtain an urgent suspension ex parte («inaudita altera parte»), through an order issued by the president of the judicial division prior to the hearing.

Reference: Art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992

Our method

The six tax litigation strategies

Effective tax defense is not limited to filing an appeal (ricorso). It requires the prior analysis of the contested measure, a comparative assessment of the available legal instruments, management of the interim stage, and planning for any potential appeal phase. The optimal strategy always depends on the specific circumstances of the individual case.

I.

Preliminary analysis of the contestable measure

Systematic examination of the tax measure to identify all defects: formal (lack of reasoning, lack of competence of the issuing office, breach of mandatory prior consultation under art. 12 L. 212/2000, improper service), substantive (errors in tax calculation, unfounded presumptions, improper reversal of the burden of proof), and procedural (forfeiture of the assessment power, violation of mandatory deadlines). Each defect must form the basis of a specific and independent ground of appeal.

II.

Strategic choice between appeal, mediation, and settlement

Comparative evaluation of the available options prior to filing an appeal (ricorso): tax settlement proposal (accertamento con adesione) (reduction of penalties to 1/3, rapid settlement), administrative complaint-mediation (reclamo-mediazione) (reduction of penalties to 35%, out-of-court phase), judicial conciliation (conciliazione giudiziale) (reduction to 40%), appeal with application for stay of enforcement (sospensiva) (preservation of the possession of assets pending judgment). The choice must take into account the strength of the defense arguments, the taxpayer's risk propensity, and their liquidity position.

III.

Urgent interim relief

When the contested measure is about to be enforced (impending attachment, served vehicle immobilization notice, registered tax mortgage), applying for an urgent stay of enforcement (sospensiva) is the absolute priority. In cases of extreme urgency, the president of the Tax Court of First Instance (Giudice Tributario) may grant the suspension ex parte («inaudita altera parte»), staying enforcement immediately upon request, even before the opposing party has been notified of the appeal. Every hour of delay may render the consequences of forced recovery irreversible.

IV.

Gathering and building documentary evidence

Tax proceedings are fundamentally documentary: evidence is formed through documents submitted by the parties. The defense must collect, organize, and substantiate all useful documentation: contracts, invoices, bank statements, correspondence with suppliers and clients, expert party opinions, and technical consultancy reports. The 2022 Reform introduced written witness testimony for disputed facts, broadening the evidentiary scope where documentation alone is insufficient to prove the defense position.

V.

Management of the mediation phase

For disputes up to 50,000 euros, the tax complaint-mediation phase (reclamo-mediazione) is fundamental and is not a mere formality. A well-structured mediation application, with solid legal arguments and appropriate documentation, significantly increases the likelihood of reaching a favorable agreement with a reduction of penalties to 35%. The Tax Authority is required to assess the complaint and respond within 90 days: silence amounts to a refusal and allows the formal lodging of the appeal (ricorso).

VI.

Management of the appeal and proceedings before the Court of Cassation

When the first-instance judgment is partially or totally unfavorable, evaluating the appropriateness of an appeal requires an objective analysis: which grounds of appeal have a real foundation in second instance, what is the risk of an adverse ruling increasing legal costs, and whether it is more advantageous to resolve the dispute through conciliation or partial acceptance (acquiescenza parziale).

Tangible results

Five cases resolved in tax litigation

The following cases illustrate the practical defence work of our firm before the First- and Second-Instance Tax Courts of Justice (Giudici Tributari di I e II grado) of Campania. Identifying details have been anonymised to protect client confidentiality.

Case no. 1 — First-Instance CGT + Urgent suspension

IRPEF assessment for € 214.000: suspension granted in 48 hours, appeal upheld in full

Giudice Tributario di I grado di Napoli — Year 2024

A commercial entrepreneur had received a personal income tax (IRPEF) notice of assessment («avviso di accertamento») for undeclared income for the year 2020, with a total claim (tax, 100% penalties, and interest) of 214.000 euros. The Italian Revenue Agency («Agenzia delle Entrate») had based the assessment on an inductive reconstruction of income founded exclusively on the incoming entries of the taxpayer’s personal bank account, without distinguishing between commercial turnover and personal deposits (withdrawals from savings, intra-family wire transfers, expense reimbursements). The notice of assessment had become enforceable, and the Tax Collection Agent («Agente della Riscossione») had served a payment notice («intimazione di pagamento») for one-third (71.333 euros), threatening the attachment of the bank account within thirty days. Our firm, instructed on an urgent basis, immediately filed an appeal («ricorso») with an urgent application for an ex parte stay of execution («istanza di sospensiva inaudita altera parte») pursuant to art. 47, co. 3, D.Lgs. 546/1992. The President of the Court of Tax Justice («Giudice Tributario» - CGT) granted the stay by presidential decree within 48 hours of the request, halting the imminent attachment. On the merits, the defence filed comprehensive documentation (annotated bank statements, wire transfers with payment references, bank correspondence), demonstrating that over 60% of the disputed transactions did not constitute income but deposits of a personal nature. At the hearing on the merits, the First-Instance CGT fully upheld the appeal, established the lack of adequate reasoning in the assessment based on unqualified presumptions, completely annulled the notice, and ordered the Agenzia delle Entrate to pay the legal costs.

Assessment annulled — € 214.000 — costs awarded against the Revenue (Erario)
Case no. 2 — Complaint-mediation (reclamo-mediazione)

VAT (IVA) assessment for € 38.500: mediation with penalties reduced to 35%, saving of € 22.000

Complaint-mediation procedure (reclamo-mediazione) pursuant to art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Year 2024

A small artisanal construction enterprise had received a VAT notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento IVA) for the year 2021, claiming 38,500 euros, of which 22,000 euros in administrative penalties. The Office contested the non-application of VAT to certain supplies of services that the business owner deemed to qualify as exempt transactions pursuant to art. 10 D.P.R. 633/1972. The examination of the assessment revealed grounds for defense on the merits (the genuinely exempt nature of some transactions) as well as certain areas of interpretive uncertainty. Since the dispute had a value below 50,000 euros, the tax complaint-mediation procedure (reclamo-mediazione) was mandatory. Our law firm structured the complaint from a technical standpoint, distinguishing the unquestionably exempt transactions (for which the claim was manifestly unfounded) from those with ambiguous classification. The mediation proposal drafted by the firm proposed the payment of tax solely on the transactions of uncertain classification (16,500 euros in tax) with an automatic reduction of penalties to 35% of the statutory minimum (7,700 euros in penalties instead of 22,000 euros). Within 60 days of filing the complaint, the Office accepted the mediation proposal. The taxpayer paid a total of 24,200 euros (tax + reduced penalties + interest), saving 14,300 euros compared to the original claim and, above all, avoiding the time and costs of court litigation.

Mediation concluded — savings of € 14,300 — penalties reduced to 35%
Case no. 3 — CGT II grado (Appeal)

Corporate income tax (IRES) assessment: overturned on appeal for lack of mandatory prior adversary hearing

Giudice Tributario di II grado della Campania — Year 2023

A limited liability company operating in the transport sector had challenged in the first instance a corporate income tax (IRES) notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento IRES) for the year 2019, concerning an overall claim of 127,000 euros. The Tax Court of First Instance (CGT di I grado) had dismissed the appeal (ricorso), upholding the lawfulness of the Office's action. Our firm, having taken on the mandate for the appellate stage, analyzed the assessment procedure in detail and identified a fundamental defect: the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) had conducted a desk audit (accertamento a tavolino, without physical entry into the company premises) reconstructing corporate income through synthetic methods, without previously serving a questionnaire or an invitation to a preliminary adversary hearing upon the taxpayer, as required by the case law of the Court of Cassation for so-called «desk» assessments. The Court of Tax Justice of Second Instance (CGT di II grado), overturning the first-instance judgment, upheld the appeal and annulled the assessment for breach of the adversary principle, ordering the Agenzia delle Entrate to pay the legal costs of both instances of proceedings.

Overturned on appeal — assessment annulled — € 127,000
Case no. 4 — Judicial conciliation in first instance

Cadastral valuation dispute on commercial property: 65% reduction via conciliation

CGT I grado di Napoli — Judicial conciliation pursuant to art. 48 D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Year 2024

An owner of commercial real estate in the center of Naples had received a municipal property tax (IMU) notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento IMU) for the years 2019, 2020, and 2021, totaling 92,000 euros across tax, penalties, and interest. The Municipality disputed the declared cadastral income (rendita catastale), deeming it undervalued compared to the property's market value, and had unilaterally redetermined the cadastral value by applying revaluation coefficients not compliant with cadastral standards. The appeal (ricorso) was filed before the First-Instance Court of Tax Justice (CGT di I grado), pleading the unlawfulness of the official cadastral revaluation carried out ex officio by the Municipality (a procedure on which conflicting judicial authorities existed) and the lack of adequate statement of reasons in quantifying the new cadastral value. During the proceedings, in light of the procedural uncertainty arising from conflicting local case law on the issue, our firm proposed a judicial conciliation (conciliazione giudiziale) pursuant to art. 48 D.Lgs. 546/1992. The agreed proposal provided for the recognition of an intermediate cadastral value, with the payment of 32,200 euros (redetermined tax) and penalties set at 40% of the statutory minimum (instead of 100%), resulting in a total outlay of 39,500 euros against an initial assessment of 92,000 euros: a 57% saving compared to the initial claim and the final resolution of the dispute without awaiting the ruling and the risk of an appeal.

Conciliation — 57% savings — dispute settled
Case no. 5 — CGT I grado + Partial appeal

VAT assessment on cross-border transactions: partial annulment and residual conciliation

CGT I grado di Napoli / CGT II grado Campania — Year 2023–2024

An import company operating in the fashion industry had received a VAT notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento IVA) relating to the years 2018 and 2019, for an overall claim of 318,000 euros. The Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) alleged that certain imports of goods from abroad, formally treated as VAT-exempt intra-Community acquisitions, were in fact purchases subject to tax in Italy due to the absence of subjective requirements of the foreign supplier (registration in the VIES database). The defense in the first instance demonstrated that a portion of the transactions (approximately 40% of the contested taxable base) had been carried out with suppliers regularly registered in VIES at the time of the transactions and subsequently deregistered, substantiated by specific documentary evidence (VIES registration certificates at the time of the transactions, correspondence with suppliers, international transport documentation). The First-Instance Court of Tax Justice (CGT di I grado) partially upheld the appeal (ricorso), annulling the assessment for the 40% share relating to transactions with suppliers actually registered in VIES, and upholding the claim for the remaining 60%. On this remaining share (approximately 190,000 euros), our firm lodged a cross-appeal concurrently with our appearance in the appeal brought by the Agenzia delle Entrate, which had appealed against the partial granting of the first-instance petition. In the appeal proceedings, the parties reached a judicial conciliation agreement (conciliazione giudiziale) pursuant to art. 48-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992, with penalties reduced to 50% of the statutory minimum and payment of tax on a negotiated lower taxable base, resulting in a final outlay of 98,000 euros compared to an original claim of 318,000 euros.

From € 318,000 to € 98,000 — 69% savings

The deadline is mandatory

You have 60 days to challenge the tax measure. After that, it becomes final and unappealable.

The deadline to appeal against a notice of tax assessment (avviso di accertamento), a tax payment demand (cartella esattoriale), or any other tax act is mandatory: 60 days from service. Once this period expires, the act becomes final and can no longer be challenged in any forum.
