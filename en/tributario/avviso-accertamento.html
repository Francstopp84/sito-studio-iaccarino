Have you received an assessment notice?
The claim of the Tax Authorities is not always founded. You have 60 days to defend yourself.

The assessment notice (avviso di accertamento) is the formal act through which the Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) alleges higher taxable income against the taxpayer and demands payment of additional taxes, penalties, and interest. It may be based on widely differing methodologies — from analytical assessment of individual income components, to synthetic assessment based on standard of living and the synthetic income assessment tool (redditometro), up to inductive assessment reconstructing income through statistical methods. Each type has its own rules and specific defects that may result in the total or partial annulment of the tax claim before the Tax Justice Court (Giudice Tributario). The time limit for filing an appeal is 60 days from service: a mandatory deadline that admits no exceptions.

Regulatory framework

The legal framework governing tax assessments

Presidential Decree (D.P.R.) 29 September 1973, no. 600 — Art. 38

Synthetic assessment and synthetic income assessment tool (redditometro)

Art. 38 D.P.R. 600/1973 governs the synthetic assessment of the total income of natural persons. Paragraphs 4 and 5 govern the so-called redditometro: the Revenue Agency may synthetically determine the taxpayer’s income on the basis of elements indicative of tax-bearing capacity (ownership of assets, standard of living, expenses incurred), applying the coefficients established by ministerial decree. The taxpayer has the right to defend themselves by proving that the disputed expenditures were financed through tax-exempt income, through the liquidation of savings accumulated in prior years, through funds received from third parties for any reason, or that the expense items indicated by the Tax Authorities are erroneous or overstated. The burden of proof to the contrary lies with the taxpayer, but the Administration must first precisely demonstrate the disputed synthetic calculation. An effective defence requires the systematic collection of bank statements, deeds of gift, third-party statements, and documentation of the liquidated investments.

Art. 38 D.P.R. 600/1973 — Redditometro
Presidential Decree (D.P.R.) of 29 September 1973, no. 600 — Art. 39

Analytical and inductive assessment for business income holders

Art. 39 D.P.R. 600/1973 governs the assessment of business income. Paragraph 1 regulates analytical assessment (accertamento analitico), applicable when accounting records exist and are deemed unreliable on specific points: the Tax Authority amends individual income components on the basis of certain and verifiable elements. Paragraph 2 instead provides for inductive or non-accounting assessment (accertamento induttivo o extra-contabile), which may only be used in the exhaustive cases established by the statute — failure to file a tax return, refusal to exhibit accounting records, overall unreliability of the accounts — and permits the Tax Authority to disregard accounting records entirely, reconstructing income through presumptive methods (sector studies [studi di settore], industry averages, transactions carried out with counterparties). Pure inductive assessment is an exceptional instrument that case law interprets restrictively: the legal prerequisites justifying it must be proven by the Tax Authority with analytical precision.

Art. 39 D.P.R. 600/1973 — Analytical and Inductive
D.P.R. 29 September 1973, n. 600 — Articles 42 and 43

Statement of reasons of the act and forfeiture time limits

Art. 42 D.P.R. 600/1973, read in conjunction with art. 7 Law 212/2000 (Taxpayer’s Statute), requires the notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento) to contain a complete and specific statement of reasons: it must state the factual grounds, the legal reasoning, the calculation of taxes, penalties, and interest, as well as the criteria for synthetic or inductive determination when applied. An insufficient statement of reasons or one provided by reference (per relationem, referring to other documents that are not attached) entails the nullity of the notice. Art. 43 establishes forfeiture time limits: the notice must be served by 31 December of the fifth year following that in which the tax return was filed (or of the seventh year in case of failure to file). A notice served beyond these time limits is null and void due to forfeiture (decadenza) and must be annulled.

Arts. 42 and 43 D.P.R. 600/1973 — Statement of Reasons and Forfeiture

Settlement with acceptance and suspension of time limits. A taxpayer who receives a notice of assessment may file an application for tax settlement (istanza di accertamento con adesione) pursuant to D.Lgs. 218/1997 within 60 days of service. Filing the application automatically suspends the time limit for filing an appeal (ricorso) for an additional 90 days, bringing the total time limit to 150 days. The settlement procedure allows penalties to be reduced to one-third of the statutory minimum and, where applicable, allows for a reduction of the disputed taxes. If the settlement is not achieved, the taxpayer may always appeal to the Tax Court of Justice (Giudice Tributario) within the suspended time limits. The choice between settlement and direct appeal must be evaluated carefully: an assessment based on weak presumptions is often more advantageous to challenge in court than to settle through acceptance.

The factual circumstances

Types of notices of assessment

Each type of assessment has different qualifying prerequisites, specific defects, and its own defence strategies. Identifying the type is the essential first step in tax defence.

Art. 39 para. 1

Analytical Assessment (Accertamento Analitico)

Analytical adjustment of individual items of business or self-employment income based on certain elements derived from the accounting records. The Tax Authority must specify precisely which items are challenged and why. Typical defects: lack of certain proof, presumptions treated as established facts, incorrect temporal allocation of items.

Reference: Art. 39, c. 1, D.P.R. 600/1973
Art. 38 paras. 4-5

Synthetic Assessment — “Redditometro”

Reconstruction of the natural person’s total income based on standard of living, expenditures incurred, and ownership of assets. The taxpayer may always prove an alternative source of the funds (prior savings, exempt income, gifts). The permissible contrary evidence is broad and not restricted solely to documents.

Reference: Art. 38, paragraphs 4-5, D.P.R. 600/1973
Art. 39 para. 2

Inductive Assessment (extra-contabile)

Reconstruction of income disregarding accounting records entirely, using presumptive and statistical methods. Applicable solely in the exhaustive cases set out in art. 39, para. 2. The prerequisites for its application must be strictly proven: their absence entails the illegitimacy of the method and the nullity of the act.

Reference: Art. 39, c. 2, D.P.R. 600/1973
Art. 41

Ex Officio Assessment (Accertamento d’ufficio)

Issued in the event of failure to file a tax return. The Tax Authority determines the taxable income using any available element, without methodological restrictions. Defence focuses on verifying the actual failure to file, the reliability of the elements relied upon, and the correct quantification of taxes.

Reference: Art. 41 D.P.R. 600/1973
Art. 41-bis

Partial Assessment (Accertamento parziale)

Assessment limited to specific items of income resulting from reports, cross-checks, or communications from third parties, without carrying out a general review of the tax position. A more streamlined procedure offering fewer procedural safeguards: the taxpayer may face multiple partial assessments for the same tax year, each concerning different items.

Reference: Art. 41-bis D.P.R. 600/1973
D.Lgs. 218/1997

With Acceptance (Accertamento con adesione)

Consensual procedure with the Tax Administration enabling the resolution of the dispute before trial, with a reduction of penalties to one-third of the statutory minimum. The application suspends the time limit for an appeal by 90 days. A useful tool when the claim is partially substantiated and litigation presents risks.

Reference: D.Lgs. 19 June 1997, n. 218

Our method

The six defence strategies

Defending against a notice of assessment requires a multi-level approach: from raising formal defects to disputing the substantive merits of the claim, from the pre-litigation administrative phase up to an appeal before the Tax Court of Justice (Giudice Tributario) and, on questions of pure law, before the Court of Cassation (giurisprudenza consolidata).

I.

Verification of forfeiture

The first objection to be raised: rigorous verification of compliance with forfeiture time limits under art. 43 D.P.R. 600/1973. If the notice was served after 31 December of the fifth year following the filing of the tax return (seventh in case of failure to file or fraud), it is radically null. Forfeiture may be noted ex officio by the court and does not even require a formal objection by the party. It is the most severe and incurable defect.

II.

Objection of insufficient statement of reasons

The notice of assessment must contain a specific and comprehensible statement of reasons pursuant to art. 42 D.P.R. 600/1973 and art. 7 Law 212/2000. A statement of reasons by reference (per relationem, referring to unattached documents) is invalid if the cited document was not made known to the taxpayer. An act that lacks reasons or has insufficient reasons is null and cannot be remedied during proceedings by the Tax Authority through supplementary briefs.

III.

Refutation of presumptions

Synthetic and inductive assessments are founded on presumptions. The defence may challenge the presumptive weight of the elements relied upon (simple presumption: it must be serious, precise, and concordant pursuant to art. 2729 of the Italian Civil Code), submit documented contrary evidence, and demonstrate that the challenged sources of income have another lawful origin.

IV.

Assessment of settlement options

In parallel with the analysis of procedural defects, we consistently evaluate the opportunity of filing an application for tax settlement (istanza di accertamento con adesione) pursuant to D.Lgs. 218/1997. The procedure suspends the deadline for filing an appeal by 90 days, reduces penalties to one-third if an agreement is reached, and frequently makes it possible to obtain a reduction of the disputed taxes as well. The choice between settlement and an appeal depends on the strength of the tax claim and the available defence documentation.

V.

Tax complaint-mediation

For disputes up to 50,000 euros, art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992 requires filing a tax complaint-mediation application (istanza di reclamo-mediazione) prior to an appeal. If concluded with an agreement, the procedure automatically reduces penalties to 35% of the statutory minimum. The complaint must be technically drafted as a full-fledged legal appeal (ricorso), setting out an analytical statement of defects and evidence.

VI.

Appeal to the CGT and stay of execution

When tax settlement (accertamento con adesione) yields no results or when defects make legal action advisable, an appeal (ricorso) to the First-Instance Court of Tax Justice (Giudice Tributario di I grado) is the natural forum. Concurrently with the appeal, it is possible to request the precautionary suspension of collection pursuant to art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992 to prevent the Revenue Agency–Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) from proceeding with enforcement during the proceedings. Precautionary suspension is granted when there is a reasonable legal basis (fumus boni iuris) and a risk of irreparable harm (periculum in mora).

Tangible results

Five resolved cases

The following cases illustrate the practical defense work carried out by our law firm before the Tax Justice Courts (Giudici Tributari) and in tax settlement procedures (accertamento con adesione). Identifying details have been anonymised in accordance with client confidentiality.

Case No. 1 — CGT of first instance, Naples

Synthetic tax assessment through expenditure-based assessment (redditometro): annulled by contrary evidence — € 87,400

Giudice Tributario di I grado di Napoli — Year 2024

A sole proprietor from Naples operating in the restaurant sector received a notice of synthetic tax assessment (avviso di accertamento sintetico) pursuant to art. 38, paras. 4-5, D.P.R. 600/1973 for the tax years 2019 and 2020, based on the expenditure-based assessment tool (redditometro). The Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) alleged a synthetic income of approximately 210,000 euros per year, inferred from the purchase of a luxury residence, the maintenance of two high-end motor vehicles, and travel expenses documented by credit card statements. The overall tax claim, including additional personal income tax (IRPEF), regional and municipal surtaxes, penalties, and interest, amounted to 87,400 euros. The defense analysis highlighted that the residence had been purchased using proceeds from a previous real estate sale and with the contribution of an inheritance received in 2018, while the vehicles were in mixed use (promiscuous use) for the business. The notarial deed of sale for the previous property, the deed of acceptance of inheritance, bank statements from previous years demonstrating the accumulation of funds, and corporate records concerning the business use of the vehicles were produced in evidence. The Giudice Tributario di I grado di Napoli upheld the appeal (ricorso) in full, ruling that the contrary evidence provided by the taxpayer was sufficient to rebut the legal presumptions of the redditometro and that the Revenue Office had failed to adequately refute the documentary justifications submitted. The claim of 87,400 euros was completely annulled.

Total annulment — € 87,400
Case No. 2 — Settlement, Agenzia Entrate Naples

Inductive tax assessment: 80% reduction for lack of legal prerequisites

Tax assessment with settlement (accertamento con adesione) pursuant to D.Lgs. 218/1997 — Year 2024

A limited liability company (società a responsabilità limitata) operating in retail clothing received a notice of pure inductive tax assessment (accertamento induttivo puro) pursuant to art. 39, para. 2, D.P.R. 600/1973, for the years 2020 and 2021, with a total claim of 142,000 euros across corporate income tax (IRES), VAT (IVA), penalties, and interest. The Revenue Office had justified the use of the inductive method by claiming an alleged overall unreliability of the accounting records, based on certain anomalies found in the daily receipts register (registro dei corrispettivi). The defense analysis immediately established that the accounting records were formally regular and that the anomalies in the receipts register were due to clerical recording errors that were documented and verifiable. The statutory prerequisites for applying the pure inductive method were therefore lacking: the overall unreliability of accounting records requires significantly graver evidentiary grounds than minor anomalies. Following the filing of a formal application for settlement (istanza di accertamento con adesione), the Revenue Office, presented with the documentation produced and the detailed legal challenge, agreed to drastically reduce the claim, bringing it from 142,000 to 28,600 euros, eliminating the maximum penalties and applying the statutory minimums. The company finalized the settlement with a net saving of over 113,000 euros.

80% reduction through settlement — from € 142,000 to € 28,600
Case No. 3 — CGT of first instance, Caserta

Analytical tax assessment: partial annulment for incorrect temporal allocation of revenue

Giudice Tributario di I grado di Caserta — Year 2023

A self-employed professional (freelance engineer) received a notice of analytical tax assessment (avviso di accertamento analitico) for the 2019 tax year, relating to 48,600 euros in allegedly undeclared professional fees, inferred from VAT data communications and cross-checks on issued invoices. The analysis of the accounting and tax documentation revealed that almost all of the contested fees — amounting to 41,200 euros — had already been properly declared by the professional in the 2020 tax year, since the fees had been collected in that year rather than in 2019 when the invoices were issued. For self-employed professionals, the cash accounting principle (principio di cassa) dictates that fees contribute to taxable income in the year of actual receipt, not in the year of invoice issuance. The remaining 7,400 euros related to fees for which the professional lacked sufficient documentation. The appeal (ricorso) challenged the incorrect temporal allocation of the fees, producing bank statements showing the credit dates and the 2020 tax return in which those fees had been duly reported. The CGT di I grado di Caserta partially upheld the appeal, annulling the claim for 41,200 euros and upholding only the lesser claim of 7,400 euros, with a corresponding reduction in penalties.

Partial annulment — saving of € 41,200
Case No. 4 — CGT of first instance, Naples

Forfeiture of statutory time limits: notice annulled due to late service — € 53,800

Giudice Tributario di I grado di Napoli — Year 2023

In December 2023, a small artisan enterprise received a notice of analytical tax assessment relating to the 2017 tax year for unrecorded higher revenues, with a total claim of 53,800 euros across personal income tax (IRPEF), VAT (IVA), regional production tax (IRAP), penalties, and interest. The taxpayer had duly filed the tax return for the year 2017 within the standard statutory deadlines. Pursuant to art. 43 D.P.R. 600/1973, in the absence of a fraudulent return, the notice of assessment for the year 2017 had to be served (notificato) by December 31 of the fifth year following the filing of the return, namely by December 31, 2022. The notice was instead served in December 2023, approximately one year after the forfeiture deadline (termine di decadenza). The appeal raised, as a preliminary and dispositive plea, the statutory forfeiture of the tax authority's power of assessment (decadenza del potere accertativo). The Giudice Tributario di I grado di Napoli upheld the appeal, finding that the statutory period had lapsed and annulling the notice in its entirety, ordering the Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) to pay the legal costs. The forfeiture, which may also be raised by the court on its own motion (d’ufficio), superseded any further substantive issues.

Total annulment due to statutory forfeiture — € 53,800
Case No. 5 — Administrative claim-mediation, Naples

Invalid motivation by reference: simplified tax settlement with reduction of penalties

Claim-mediation (reclamo-mediazione) pursuant to art. 17-bis D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Year 2024

A freelance contractor in the IT services sector received a notice of partial tax assessment (avviso di accertamento parziale) pursuant to art. 41-bis D.P.R. 600/1973 for the 2020 tax year, concerning alleged unbilled professional fees amounting to 38,400 euros, identified in a tax audit report (processo verbale di constatazione) by the Guardia di Finanza. The notice merely referred to the tax audit report of the GdF without appending it or reproducing its essential content, thereby constituting an unlawful motivation by reference (motivazione per relationem) under art. 7 L. 212/2000, which requires the attachment of referenced documents not already known to the taxpayer. As the dispute involved an amount below 50,000 euros, a detailed claim-mediation petition (istanza di reclamo-mediazione) was filed, raising the procedural defect of motivation by reference, the failure to attach the tax audit report (PVC), and challenging the substantive quantification of the alleged fees. During the mediation procedure, the Revenue Office, acknowledging the merits of the formal procedural objection, proposed a simplified settlement with a reduction of penalties to 35% and partial acceptance of the documentation submitted, capping the claim at 9,800 euros. The taxpayer accepted, settling the dispute with an overall saving of over 28,600 euros compared to the original claim.

Mediation: 74% saving — from € 38,400 to € 9,800
