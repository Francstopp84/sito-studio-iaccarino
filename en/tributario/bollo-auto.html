Relevant legal provision

The regulatory framework for car tax

D.P.R. 39/1953 — Art. 5 D.L. 953/1982 — Regional laws (L.R.)

Car Tax: the regional motor vehicle tax

Car tax (technically tassa automobilistica) is a regional tax established by D.P.R. 5 February 1953, no. 39. Beginning in 1998, competence was transferred to the Regions, which govern assessment and collection procedures through their own regional laws. Art. 5, paragraph 51, of D.L. 30 December 1982, no. 953 (converted into Law 28 February 1983, no. 53) expressly establishes the three-year statute of limitations: the right to collect car tax expires three years from the payment due date.

Regions may assess and collect their motor vehicle taxes through the Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione or via their own administrative offices. In any case, the three-year statute of limitations applies to all Regions, except where express exemptions are provided by regional statute (e.g. Campania Region, Lazio Region).

An administrative vehicle impoundment (fermo amministrativo) is governed by art. 86 of D.P.R. 602/1973 and may be registered for debts exceeding € 1,000. It is not an unappealable order: it may be suspended and revoked through self-rectification (autotutela) or by order of the Court of Tax Justice.

Statute of limitations: 3 years from due date — Art. 5 para. 51 D.L. 953/1982
Exemptions provided by law

Who is exempt from car tax

Persons with disabilities and adapted vehicles: vehicles owned by persons with disabilities presenting reduced or impaired motor skills, adapted pursuant to art. 8, L. 449/1997, are exempt from car tax. The exemption also applies to tax dependents. Threshold: one vehicle per beneficiary, engine displacement not exceeding 2,000 cc (petrol) or 2,800 cc (diesel) or 150 kW (electric).

Historic vehicles: vehicles of historic and collector interest over 30 years old benefit from total exemption; between 20 and 30 years of age, a reduced flat rate is due. Regulations vary by Region.

Deregistered vehicles: for vehicles deregistered from the Public Vehicle Registry (PRA), car tax is not owed from the date of deregistration. Any tax bills relating to periods following deregistration are unlawful.

Total exemption: persons with disabilities, historic vehicles >30 years, deregistered vehicles
Types of dispute

Main cases we handle

Time-Barred Car Tax

The most frequent scenario: tax bills served for car taxes relating to 4, 5, or even 10 years earlier. Under the three-year statute of limitations, the claim is extinguished by law. An appeal is brought before the CGT to obtain a formal declaration of extinguishment.

Tax Collection Bill for Car Tax

Payment notices (cartella esattoriale) served by the Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione or regional offices. We verify whether service was proper, whether the contested periods are time-barred, and whether the amount is calculated accurately with lawful penalties and interest.

Administrative Impoundment for Car Tax

An administrative impoundment (fermo amministrativo) prevents the use of the vehicle and can also affect vehicles used for work purposes. A precautionary suspension is sought pending appeal (art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992) to restore the right to drive immediately.

Exemption for Vehicles of Disabled Persons

Disputes regarding the denial or revocation of the exemption. The denial decision is challenged by presenting disability certification (L. 104/1992) and evidence of the vehicle's compliance with statutory requirements.

Exemption for Historic Vehicles

Disputes regarding the non-recognition of historic vehicle status. ASI/FIVA certification or documentation from the Historical Brand Register is produced, demonstrating compliance with age and collector interest requirements.

Car Tax on Deregistered Vehicles

Tax bills issued for years following deregistration from the Public Vehicle Registry (PRA). The PRA deregistration document constitutes sufficient proof of the absence of the taxable event. Relief is pursued via an appeal or an administrative review request (autotutela).

How we defend you

Six defense strategies for car tax

Every car tax matter is unique. The optimal strategy depends on the year in dispute, the vehicle type, the relevant Region, and the stage of proceedings. Our law firm examines all available legal remedies.

Strategy 01

Defense of the Three-Year Statute of Limitations

The most effective and immediate remedy. Pursuant to art. 5, co. 51, D.L. 953/1982, the motor vehicle tax (tassa automobilistica) is time-barred after 3 years from its due date. If the payment notice (cartella esattoriale) is served after this deadline without valid acts interrupting the statute of limitations, the debt is extinguished by law and an appeal to the Court of Tax Justice (ricorso CGT) obtains full annulment.

Strategy 02

Defense of PRA Deregistration

If the vehicle was deregistered from the Public Vehicle Registry (PRA), the taxable event (ownership and operation of the vehicle) ceases to exist. The deregistration certificate is submitted and cancellation of all tax bills relating to periods after deregistration is requested.

Strategy 03

Recognition of Exemptions

Verification of eligibility for exemptions for persons with disabilities, historic vehicles, electric or hybrid vehicles (regional incentives). Many taxpayers are unaware of their entitlement to exemptions and pay undue taxes for years. Refund requests are filed for non-time-barred tax years.

Strategy 04

Regional Administrative Review (Autotutela)

Before appealing to the CGT, an application for administrative review (autotutela) to the competent regional office (or to the Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione for affiliated Regions) is evaluated. Administrative review is free, fast, and, if granted, cancels the tax bill without litigation costs.

Strategy 05

Appeal to the First-Instance CGT

When the internal administrative review (autotutela) is not granted or deadlines are tight, an appeal (ricorso) is lodged before the Tax Court of First Instance (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di primo grado). The tax bill (cartella di pagamento) or the notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento) is challenged on grounds of statute of limitations (prescrizione), defective service, calculation errors, or the absence of the taxable event.

Strategy 06

Suspension of the Administrative Vehicle Immobilisation (Fermo Amministrativo)

In the event of an administrative immobilisation (fermo) registered against the vehicle, an application for precautionary suspension pursuant to Art. 47 of Legislative Decree 546/1992 is filed concurrently with the appeal (ricorso). The CGT, assessing the prima facie case (fumus boni iuris) and the risk of irreparable harm from delay (periculum in mora, such as the impossibility of working with the vehicle), can suspend the immobilisation within very short timeframes.

5 resolved cases regarding motor vehicle tax (bollo auto)

Case no. 1

Three-year statute of limitations on 7 years of motor vehicle tax — Campania Region

Contested amount: € 4,240 + penalties + interest — Risk: imminent administrative vehicle immobilisation (fermo amministrativo)

A craftsman from Naples had received a tax demand notice (cartella esattoriale) from Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione for seven years of unpaid car tax (bollo auto), for a total amount of € 4,240 plus penalties and interest, amounting to € 6,800. At the same time, a notice of administrative seizure (preavviso di fermo amministrativo) had been issued on his business van, essential for his work. The client came to our office convinced that he would have to pay the entire amount.

An examination of the file revealed that four of the seven contested tax years were time-barred well beyond the statute of limitations: the first dated back eight years prior to the service of the tax demand notice. For the remaining three years, the regularity of the acts interrupting the statute of limitations sent by Regione Campania was examined. The appeal (ricorso) before the CGT di Napoli pleaded the statute of limitations for four years. The Court, having established the absence of valid acts interrupting the statute of limitations within the prescribed deadlines, cancelled the tax demand notice for those four tax years and concurrently suspended the administrative seizure by way of interim relief.

Final outcome: debt reduced from € 6,800 to € 1,950, van immediately released, business activity uninterrupted.

Tax demand notice reduced by 71% — Administrative seizure revoked
Case no. 2

Disability exemption not recognised — Regione Lazio

Contested amount: € 3,600 over 6 years — Risk: full payment plus penalties

A family from Rome had purchased in 2018 a motor vehicle adapted for the transportation of a child with severe motor disability, certified pursuant to art. 3 co. 3 L. 104/1992. Despite the vehicle modification and the medical certification, Regione Lazio had failed to record the exemption correctly in its system and had continued to issue payment notices for six consecutive years, for a total amount of € 3,600. The family, believing they were in the wrong, had paid the first two years before consulting our firm.

The application for administrative review (istanza di autotutela) submitted to the regional offices, accompanied by the disability certification, the deed of purchase of the adapted vehicle, and the documentation proving compliance with legal requirements (engine displacement within statutory limits, single vehicle per beneficiary, child listed as a tax dependent), was upheld in full. The Region ex officio cancelled all payment notices and issued a refund for the two years already unduly paid, increased by statutory interest.

The entire matter was resolved through administrative channels without the need to lodge an appeal before the CGT, with a full refund of the sums paid and the cancellation of all future liabilities.

Exemption recognized — Refund obtained — Liabilities cancelled
Case no. 3

Car tax on deregistered vehicle — Competent Court in Campania

Contested amount: € 2,900 — Vehicle deregistered 5 years prior to the tax demand notice

An entrepreneur had transferred and deregistered a company vehicle from the Public Vehicle Registry (PRA) in 2017. Five years later, in 2022, he received a tax demand notice (cartella esattoriale) for car tax relating to tax years 2018-2021, for an amount of € 2,900. Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione had issued the tax demand notice because its records still showed the vehicle registered under the entrepreneur's name, due to a discrepancy between the PRA databases and the Tax Register (Anagrafe Tributaria).

An application for administrative review (istanza di autotutela) with supporting documents was filed, attaching the PRA deregistration certificate issued in 2017, the bill of sale, the transfer of ownership deed, and the corresponding registration. The office acknowledged the error, which was attributable exclusively to the discrepancy between public databases, and granted a full discharge of the tax debt (sgravio) within 45 days. Concurrently, data alignment procedures between the PRA and Agenzia delle Entrate were initiated to prevent a recurrence of the issue.

No court appeal required. Full discharge obtained through administrative proceedings within 45 days, with no legal costs for the client.

Tax demand notice cancelled — Full debt discharge — Administrative procedure
Case no. 4

Historic vehicle: exemption denied and refund of prior years — CGT Milano

Recovered amount: € 5,100 — 1972 Alfa Romeo 2000 vehicle

A collector from Milan owned a historic 1972 Alfa Romeo 2000 Berlina, duly registered with the Alfa Romeo Historical Register with ASI certification. Although the vehicle was nearly fifty years old and clearly qualified as of historical and collector interest pursuant to L. 342/2000 and Lombardy regional legislation, Regione Lombardia had continued to demand the payment of standard car tax for six years, disregarding the taxpayer's repeated notifications.

An appeal (ricorso) was filed before the CGT di Milano challenging the payment notices and simultaneously seeking a refund of the amounts paid for the six previous years, as they were not yet time-barred. The CGT, having examined the ASI certification and the vehicle's historical documentation, acknowledged its status as a vehicle of historical and collector interest and upheld the appeal in full, ordering Regione Lombardia to refund € 5,100 plus statutory interest.

The judgment also clarified that for vehicles with ASI certification, the recognition of the exemption is automatic and does not require any further administrative investigation by the regional office, establishing a legal principle applicable to future tax periods as well.

Refund of € 5,100 + interest — Permanent exemption recognised
Case no. 5

Administrative seizure on camper van — Defective service + statute of limitations — CGT Salerno

Contested amount: € 8,400 — Administrative seizure on a camper van valued at € 65,000

A commercial agent from Salerno discovered by chance, while attempting to sell his camper van valued at € 65,000, that the vehicle was encumbered by an administrative seizure (fermo amministrativo) registered by Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione for non-payment of car tax across eleven tax years, for a total amount of € 8,400 including penalties and interest. The client had never received any prior notification or the tax demand notice (cartella esattoriale).

The legal analysis revealed two procedural defects, each independently sufficient to overturn the entire claim: first, defective service of the tax demand notice that formed the legal basis of the seizure (served at the wrong address); second, the statute of limitations barring seven of the eleven contested years, for which no validly served acts interrupting the limitation period existed. An urgent appeal pursuant to art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992 was filed, concurrently requesting the suspension of the administrative seizure and the cancellation of the tax demand notice.

The CGT di Salerno suspended the administrative seizure within 15 days of the filing of the appeal and, at the hearing on the merits, annulled the entire tax demand notice due to defective service, ordering the tax authority to pay legal costs. The client was able to sell his camper van free of any encumbrance.

Tax demand notice fully annulled — Administrative seizure revoked within 15 days
