Have you failed to make or delayed a tax payment?
Voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso) reduces penalties down to 1.67%. Prompt action is advantageous.

Voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso) is the legal instrument provided to taxpayers to spontaneously remedy their tax omissions or errors — late payments, inaccurate returns, omitted communications — while benefiting from a substantial reduction in ordinary penalties. The sooner the regularisation takes place, the lower the applicable penalty: ranging from 1/10 of the statutory minimum for sprint regularisation up to merely double the reduced ordinary penalty for very long regularisation. Governed by Art. 13 of D.Lgs. 472/1997 and substantially reformed by Law 190/2014 (2015 Stability Law) and D.Lgs. 158/2015, this procedure applies to nearly all national and local taxes, provided that the taxpayer acts before the Tax Administration (Amministrazione finanziaria) has initiated site inspections, checks, audits or served formal tax assessment notices for the same violation. A tax lawyer verifies the applicable type of regularisation, accurately calculates the amounts due, and prevents errors that could invalidate the procedure.

Regulatory framework

The legal framework of voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso)

Art. 13 D.Lgs. 18 December 1997, no. 472

Voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso): prerequisites and conditions of applicability

Art. 13 of D.Lgs. 472/1997 is the cornerstone provision of voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso): it allows taxpayers to spontaneously remedy committed tax violations (omitted or late payments, inaccurate or omitted returns, formal breaches) while benefiting from significantly lower penalties than those ordinarily applicable. The mechanism rests on the principle that spontaneous and prompt compliance merits reduced sanctions. The fundamental condition of applicability is that the violation has not already been formally recorded in an official tax audit report (processo verbale di constatazione) served on the taxpayer, and that no notices of settlement, assessment notices, or invitations to administrative discussions (inviti al contraddittorio) concerning the tax and tax period in question have been served. The reform introduced by L. 190/2014 substantially broadened this mechanism, adding long and very long regularisation categories and permitting regularisation even while tax audits are ongoing, provided they have not yet resulted in served formal notices.

Art. 13 D.Lgs. 472/1997 — as amended by L. 190/2014
D.Lgs. 24 September 2015, no. 158

The revision of the tax penalty system

D.Lgs. 158/2015 carried out a comprehensive overhaul of the administrative and criminal tax penalty system, introducing significant amendments to voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso). The decree recalibrated the baseline penalties applicable to principal tax infringements — omitted payment, inaccurate return, omitted return — and introduced proportionality criteria for determining penalties, which directly impact the calculation of reduced penalties under voluntary regularisation. Specifically, for omitted payments (Art. 13 D.Lgs. 471/1997), the ordinary penalty was maintained at 30% of the unpaid tax, with the possibility of reduction to 15% for payments made within a delay not exceeding ninety days, and a further reduction to 1% for each day of delay during the first fifteen days. These baseline rates serve as the starting point for calculating the reduced penalty under voluntary regularisation, which is obtained by multiplying the base rate by the reduction coefficient established for the selected form of regularisation.

D.Lgs. 158/2015 — Tax penalty system
Law of 23 December 2014, no. 190 — Stability Law 2015 (Legge di Stabilità 2015)

Expansion of the procedure: long and very long regularisation

Law 190/2014 (2015 Stability Law) introduced more favourable categories of voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso), significantly expanding the time frame within which taxpayers may settle their position with the benefit of reduced penalties. Prior to the reform, regularisation was largely restricted to the calendar year in which the infringement occurred; following the reform, regularisation is permitted beyond one year from the violation (long regularisation), beyond two years (very long regularisation), and even up to the forfeiture deadline of the Tax Administration's assessment powers (multi-year regularisation). The statute also introduced partial regularisation, allowing taxpayers to remedy only a portion of the infringement while retaining the option to complete the settlement at later stages, with penalties reduced in proportion to the amounts paid.

L. 190/2014 — Stability Law 2015

Voluntary regularisation and ongoing tax audits. One of the most significant changes introduced by L. 190/2014 is the possibility of using voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso) even when a tax audit by the Guardia di Finanza or the Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) is underway, provided that an official tax audit report (processo verbale di constatazione - PVC) or other formal act barring regularisation has not yet been served. At this stage, spontaneous regularisation during an audit can produce mitigating effects under criminal tax law (Art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000) as well as administrative penalty law, constituting a primary defence strategy when infringements emerge during an inspection.

The types

Which regularisation applies to your case

The choice of the correct type of voluntary repentance (ravvedimento operoso) determines the amount of the reduced penalty to be paid. Timeliness is the most relevant factor: every day of delay in regularization can increase the applicable penalty. Legal assistance ensures the precise calculation of amounts and the correct completion of F24 forms with the updated tax codes.

Within 14 days

Sprint Regularisation (Ravvedimento Sprint)

For payments made with a delay of no more than fourteen days from the ordinary deadline. The penalty is equal to 1% of the unpaid tax for each day of delay (within the first fourteen days). With a 7-day delay, the penalty is 0.7%; with 14 days, 1.4%. It is the most advantageous form of voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso) and applies primarily to the failure to pay periodic taxes (late F24 payment forms).

Reference: Art. 13, para. 1, letter a) D.Lgs. 472/1997
Within 30 days

Short Voluntary Regularisation (Ravvedimento Breve)

For late payments made between the fifteenth and thirtieth day from the deadline. The penalty is fixed at 3% of the unpaid tax (1/10 of the ordinary 30%). Late-payment interest is calculated at the statutory rate in force for the actual days of delay. It is the type most frequently used for omitted payments of periodic taxes (quarterly VAT, withholding taxes, INPS contributions for self-employed professionals).

Reference: Art. 13, co. 1, lett. a-bis) D.Lgs. 472/1997
Within 90 days

Intermediate Voluntary Regularisation (Ravvedimento Intermedio)

For late payments between the thirtieth and ninetieth day from the deadline. The penalty is equal to 3.33% of the tax (1/9 of 30%). It also applies to the correction of errors in annual tax returns if the supplementary return is submitted within ninety days of the original deadline. It is particularly used for the regularisation of monthly VAT payments or withholding taxes.

Reference: Art. 13, co. 1, lett. b) D.Lgs. 472/1997
Within 1 year

Long Voluntary Regularisation (Ravvedimento Lungo)

For violations regularised within one year from the commission or from the expiry of the filing deadline for the tax return to which the violation refers. The penalty is 3.75% (1/8 of 30%). It applies to the failure to file an income tax return if cured within one year, to omitted payments of annual amounts, and to formal violations in bookkeeping.

Reference: Art. 13, co. 1, lett. b-bis) D.Lgs. 472/1997
Over 1 year

Extended Voluntary Regularisation (Ravvedimento Lunghissimo)

Introduced by L. 190/2014, it applies to violations regularised between one and two years from their commission (penalty of 4.29%, i.e., 1/7 of 30%) and beyond two years from their commission but within the assessment forfeiture deadline (penalty of 5%, i.e., 1/6 of 30%). It allows the regularisation of tax violations dating back several years, avoiding the consequences of a potential tax assessment.

Reference: Art. 13, para. 1, lett. b-ter) and b-quater) D.Lgs. 472/1997
Post-tax audit report (processo verbale di constatazione - PVC)

Special Voluntary Regularisation (Ravvedimento Speciale)

It permits regularisation following the service of an official tax audit report (processo verbale di constatazione - PVC) drawn up by the Guardia di Finanza or the Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate), but before the notice of assessment (avviso di accertamento) has been served. The penalty is 6% (1/5 of 30%). In this context, voluntary regularisation also produces effects under criminal tax law, as it may contribute to an exclusion of criminal liability pursuant to art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000 if the tax debt is settled prior to the opening of the trial.

Reference: Art. 13, co. 1, lett. b-sexies) D.Lgs. 472/1997

Our method

The six operational strategies

Legal assistance in voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso) is not limited to calculating amounts. A strategic approach also assesses the advisability of regularisation, its interaction with potential criminal proceedings, coordination with the tax consultant, and the documentation to be retained to prove regularisation.

I.

Analysis of the violation and selection of the type of regularisation

Verification of the nature of the violation (omitted payment, inaccurate tax return, omitted tax return, formal violation), the time of commission, the exact amounts due, and the applicable type of voluntary regularisation. An error in classifying the violation may render the regularisation ineffective or lead to the calculation of incorrect amounts, exposing the taxpayer to subsequent challenges.

II.

Precise calculation of tax, penalty, and interest

Rigorous calculation of the amounts: tax originally due, reduced penalty at the correct percentage for the chosen type of regularisation, late-payment interest at the daily statutory rate for the actual days of delay. Identification of the specific F24 tax codes for each item (tax, penalty, interest), as an incorrect indication may compromise payment allocation and invalidate the voluntary regularisation.

III.

Partial regularisation and instalment payments

When the taxpayer lacks sufficient liquidity to regularise the entire position, partial voluntary regularisation allows for the payment of a portion of the amounts due, with a proportional reduction of the penalty, leaving open the possibility of completing regularisation at a later stage. The strategy of partial voluntary regularisation is particularly useful for substantial amounts and requires careful planning of staggered payments.

IV.

Coordination with criminal tax defence

When the violation exceeds the criminal liability thresholds set forth in D.Lgs. 74/2000 (e.g. € 50,000 for omitted tax return, € 100,000 for inaccurate tax return), voluntary regularisation also carries criminal law implications. Art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000 provides for an exemption from punishment or a mitigating circumstance for the payment of the tax debt prior to the opening of the trial. Coordination between the tax strategy and the criminal defence strategy is essential.

V.

Voluntary regularisation during a tax audit

L. 190/2014 permits voluntary regularisation even while a tax audit is ongoing, provided that the official tax audit report (processo verbale di constatazione) has not yet been served. At this stage, spontaneous regularisation of violations identified during the on-site inspection produces a reduction in penalties and may favourably guide the auditing authorities toward more limited penalties for violations not yet regularised.

VI.

Documentation and proof of regularisation

Preparation of all documentation substantiating regularisation: F24 payment receipts, filed supplementary tax returns, calculation of amounts with methodology, electronic filing date. This documentation is essential in the event of subsequent challenges by the financial administration and must be methodically preserved throughout the limitation period for tax assessment.

Case No. 1 — Long Voluntary Regularisation (Ravvedimento Lungo)

Omitted VAT payments: penalties reduced from € 48,000 to € 6,000

Sole proprietor, construction sector — Year 2024

A sole proprietor in the construction sector had failed to make quarterly VAT settlement payments for the entire year 2022, accumulating an overall tax debt of 160,000 euros in unpaid principal. Standard statutory penalties for omitted payment, pursuant to art. 13 D.Lgs. 471/1997, amounted to 30% of the tax, equal to 48,000 euros, in addition to statutory late-payment interest. The client consulted our firm in July 2024 — between one and two years after the violations were committed — upon receiving initial payment reminders from the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate), without any formal tax assessment yet having been initiated. Since the violations had not yet been formally established by a formal tax notice, it was still possible to access the extended voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento lunghissimo: 1/7 of the minimum, penalty at 4.29% of the tax for violations between one and two years) and the ultra-annual regularisation (ravvedimento ultra-annuale: 1/6 of the minimum, penalty at 5%) for violations exceeding two years. Our firm devised a regularisation plan structured into three monthly instalments, using separate F24 payment forms (modello F24) for tax, penalty, and interest, applying the specific tax codes for each quarter. The total penalty actually paid amounted to 6,860 euros, resulting in a net saving compared to the standard penalty of over 41,000 euros, corresponding to 85% of the penalty originally applicable.

Penalty reduced from € 48,000 to € 6,860 — 85% savings
Case No. 2 — Special Voluntary Regularisation post-PVC

Inaccurate tax return: extinguishment of the tax crime through voluntary regularisation during tax audit

Limited liability company (S.r.l.), commerce sector — Year 2023

A limited liability company (società a responsabilità limitata) in the retail and commercial sector was undergoing a tax audit by the tax police (Guardia di Finanza) for the tax years 2020 and 2021. During the on-site inspection, invoices for non-existent transactions had emerged, which had been used to artificially reduce taxable income, generating a tax saving of approximately 120,000 euros for each year. In abstract terms, these violations constituted the criminal offence of fraudulent tax return through the use of invoices for non-existent transactions (art. 2 D.Lgs. 74/2000). The company partners contacted our firm during the audit, prior to the service of the official tax audit report (processo verbale di constatazione - PVC). The strategy adopted involved filing for special voluntary regularisation (ravvedimento operoso speciale), completed before the conclusion of the audit and prior to the formal service of the tax audit report (PVC). This special regularisation (penalty set at 6% of 30%, equal to 1.5% of the unpaid tax) enabled the settlement of approximately 20,000 euros in tax principal, interest, and reduced penalties. Subsequently, in criminal proceedings, counsel invoked the statutory cause of non-punishability under art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000, which excludes criminal liability when the tax liability, including penalties and interest, is fully extinguished prior to the opening of the trial hearing. The Public Prosecutor's Office (Procura della Repubblica) requested and obtained an order of dismissal (decreto di archiviazione) of the criminal proceedings against the company's directors, acknowledging the extinguishing effect of the voluntary regularisation.

Offence extinguished — criminal proceedings dismissed
Case No. 3 — Short Voluntary Regularisation (Ravvedimento Breve)

Unpaid withholding taxes: regularisation in 18 days, penalty € 690

Associated professional firm — Year 2024

An associated professional firm with several external associates had failed to remit the withholding taxes deducted from compensation paid to associates for the month of November 2024, for a total amount of 23,000 euros. The payment deadline was 16 December 2024; the firm discovered the omission on 28 December 2024, when an associate reported the failure to issue the Single Certification (Certificazione Unica). The delay was thus twelve days from the deadline, falling within the statutory sprint regularisation window (ravvedimento sprint: within fourteen days). Our firm immediately prepared the precise calculation of the amounts due: tax principal of 23,000 euros, penalty of 1.2% (12 days × 0.1%) equal to 276 euros, and interest at the statutory legal rate of 2.5% for twelve days amounting to 18.96 euros. The F24 payment forms (modello F24) with the specific tax codes for self-employment withholding tax were submitted and paid on 30 December 2024, avoiding the standard 30% penalty (6,900 euros). The net savings amounted to 6,621 euros thanks to the promptness of the intervention, achieved in less than forty-eight hours from the initial request for assistance.

Penalty reduced from € 6,900 to € 276 — timeliness decisive
Case No. 4 — Very Long Regularisation (Ravvedimento Lunghissimo) + Supplementary Tax Return (Dichiarazione Integrativa)

Inaccurate tax return: recovery of € 31,200 in penalties through strategic regularisation

Freelance practitioner, healthcare sector — Year 2023

A self-employed physician had filed tax returns for the tax years 2020 and 2021 omitting the compensation received from two affiliated healthcare facilities, resulting in an undeclared taxable amount of approximately 104,000 euros, with evaded tax (personal income tax - IRPEF and regional/municipal surcharges) of approximately 42,000 euros. In 2023, the professional received a compliance notice (comunicazione bonaria) from the Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) concerning discrepancies between the data in the Single Certifications (Certificazioni Uniche) and the amounts declared, before any formal tax assessment had commenced. This notice does not bar voluntary regularisation. Our firm assessed the viability of voluntary correction (ravvedimento operoso) compared to a settlement procedure (accertamento con adesione): the multi-year voluntary correction (ravvedimento lunghissimo) allowed for a penalty of 4.29% for 2020 (violation exceeding two years, 1/7 of the minimum) and 3.75% for 2021 (violation between one and two years, 1/8 of the minimum), compared to the standard statutory penalty ranging from 90% to 180% for inaccurate tax returns. Supplementary tax returns were filed for both years, and the omitted tax (42,000 euros), interest (approximately 2,800 euros), and reduced penalties (approximately 1,890 euros) were paid via the F24 payment form (modello F24). The statutory penalties would have amounted to no less than 37,800 euros (90% of the evaded tax). The actual savings achieved amounted to approximately 35,910 euros.

Penalties reduced from € 37,800+ to € 1,890 — 95% savings
Case No. 5 — Intermediate Voluntary Correction

Omitted Esterometro reporting: formal defects cured via voluntary correction, penalty avoided

Import-export company, Naples — Year 2024

A commercial enterprise engaging in intra-EU and extra-EU transactions had failed to submit electronically to the Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) the cross-border transaction data (esterometro) for the second and third quarters of 2023, due to an operational error during migration between two different enterprise management software systems. The omissions constituted formal violations carrying penalties of 2 euros for each unsubmitted invoice (up to a maximum of 400 euros per quarter), pursuant to art. 11, para. 2-quater of Legislative Decree no. 471/1997; however, for serious or repeated formal violations, a penalty of 400 euros “per transaction” was also provided for. Given the volume of omitted transactions (approximately 180 invoices per quarter), the aggregate penalty exposure in the event of an audit reached 72,000 euros. After verifying the availability of voluntary regularisation for formal violations (expressly permitted by art. 13 of Legislative Decree no. 472/1997 upon submission of the omitted return), our firm assisted the company in the late submission of the cross-border report (esterometro) for the two omitted quarters within ninety days of the original deadline, together with the simultaneous payment of the penalty reduced to 1/9 of the statutory minimum (intermediate voluntary correction - ravvedimento intermedio). The total penalty paid amounted to 178 euros, compared to the potential penalty exposure of 72,000 euros in the event of formal reassessment by the Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate). The company was able to regularise its standing in less than one week from requesting our assistance.

Penalty paid € 178 — avoided exposure € 72,000
