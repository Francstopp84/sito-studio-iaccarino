Has the Agenzia delle Entrate registered a mortgage on your property?
It may be unlawful. Take action immediately.

The tax collection mortgage (ipoteca esattoriale) is one of the most intrusive precautionary measures available to the Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione: it effectively blocks any possibility of selling, mortgaging, or obtaining financing on the affected property, and severely impairs the taxpayer's assets. However, the law imposes strict limits: the minimum debt threshold of € 20,000, the absolute prohibition against enforcing execution on the primary residence (prima casa), and the requirement of prior formal notice. When these limits are disregarded or breached, the mortgage is unlawful and can be removed. A tax lawyer can obtain its cancellation, even quickly, by filing an appeal (ricorso) with the Tax Justice Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria) or through an application for self-redress (autotutela) to the taxing authority.

Regulatory framework

The regulations governing tax collection mortgages (ipoteca esattoriale)

Art. 77 D.P.R. 29 September 1973, no. 602

The prerequisite for the mortgage: minimum threshold of € 20,000

Art. 77 of D.P.R. 602/1973, as amended by D.L. 69/2013 converted into L. 98/2013, governs the registration of mortgages by the Tax Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione). The provision establishes that a mortgage may be registered on the debtor's real estate when the total debt entered on the tax roll (iscritto a ruolo) is equal to or greater than € 20,000. Below this threshold, mortgage registration is prohibited, and any measure taken is unlawful and voidable. Art. 77, paragraph 1-bis requires the Tax Collection Agent to provide prior notice to the debtor of the intention to proceed with mortgage registration, with at least 30 days' advance notice, in order to allow payment, a request for installment payments, or the filing of an appeal (ricorso) before the mortgage is registered in the land registry. The absence of this prior notice constitutes a serious defect that renders the mortgage unlawful.

Art. 77 D.P.R. 602/1973 — Threshold € 20,000
Art. 76 D.P.R. 602/1973 — Absolute protection of the primary residence (prima casa)

The prohibition of forced sale of the primary residence

Art. 76 D.P.R. 602/1973, substantially reformed by D.L. 69/2013, sets an insurmountable limit on the enforcement powers of the Tax Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione): the debtor cannot be subjected to forced real estate foreclosure if the only property owned is used for residential purposes and the debtor is registered as residing there. In such cases, the property cannot be subjected to forced sale, even if the mortgage had been lawfully registered. However, registering a mortgage on the primary residence (prima casa) remains theoretically possible (unless the debt does not exceed € 120,000), but without the possibility of proceeding to a subsequent sale. For the taxpayer, this distinction is fundamental: a mortgage registered in violation of the prohibition under art. 76, namely on a property that constitutes the debtor's sole real estate asset used as a primary residence with registered residence, is voidable through an appeal (ricorso) to the Tax Justice Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria).

Art. 76 D.P.R. 602/1973 — Prohibition of enforcement on the primary residence (prima casa)
Art. 19 D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Appealability

Appealing against the tax collection mortgage (ipoteca esattoriale)

The tax collection mortgage (ipoteca esattoriale) is expressly included among the acts that can be challenged before the Tax Justice Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria) pursuant to art. 19, paragraph 1, letter e-bis), D.Lgs. 546/1992. The deadline for filing an appeal (ricorso) is 60 days from notification of the prior notice referred to in art. 77, paragraph 1-bis, or from notification of the completed mortgage registration. Concurrently with the appeal, it is possible to request an interim stay (sospensione cautelare) pursuant to art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992, which the court may grant on an urgent basis even prior to the appearance of the opposing party where there is a risk of serious and irreparable harm. The suspension of the mortgage does not automatically result in its cancellation from the land registers, but it prevents the Tax Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) from proceeding with subsequent enforcement measures (foreclosure and forced sale) pending the judgment on the merits.

Art. 19 D.Lgs. 546/1992 — CGT appeal (ricorso)

Mandatory prior notice. Before registering a tax lien (ipoteca esattoriale), the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) must send the debtor a formal notice informing them of the intention to proceed, specifying the amount of the debt, the assets upon which the lien is to be registered, and the 30-day deadline to regularise the position. The lack of this notice, or its inadequacy in reaching the recipient, results in the nullity of the lien procedure.

The most common cases

When a tax lien (ipoteca esattoriale) can be challenged

Not all tax liens (ipoteche esattoriali) are lawful. There are numerous circumstances in which the measure can be challenged and cancellation can be obtained both judicially and through administrative review (autotutela).

Generic property

Lien on property

A tax lien (ipoteca esattoriale) is registered on real estate belonging to the debtor other than the primary residence. In such cases, the measure is admissible in principle, but it must comply with the minimum threshold of € 20.000 and procedural formalities. Any formal or substantive defect makes the measure challengeable before the Court of Tax Justice (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria) with an urgent application for suspension.

Reference: Art. 77 D.P.R. 602/1973
Primary residence (prima casa)

Lien on the primary residence

When the property subject to the lien is the debtor's primary residence (prima casa), the home of official civil registration, the scope of protection is at its highest. Under no circumstances is it possible to proceed with the forced sale of the primary residence; the registration of a lien for debts under € 120.000 is unlawful and can be challenged with concrete prospects of cancellation.

Reference: Art. 76 D.P.R. 602/1973
Co-ownership

Lien on jointly owned property

If the debtor is a co-owner of the property along with other parties unrelated to the tax debt, the lien can only be registered on the debtor's share. Registering the lien on the entire property or in an amount exceeding the debtor's share is unlawful and prejudices the rights of third-party co-owners, who may act independently to protect their assets.

Reference: Art. 77
Cancellation

Cancellation of extinguished lien

Once the debt has been fully satisfied (through payment, settlement and write-off (saldo e stralcio), completed instalment plan, or facilitated tax settlement (definizione agevolata)), the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) is required to cancel the lien ex officio within 30 days. If this deadline is disregarded, the taxpayer may serve a formal notice (diffida) on the Authority and, in case of further inaction, petition the Court of Tax Justice (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria) to obtain a cancellation order.

Reference: Art. 77, paragraph 4, D.P.R. 602/1973
Below threshold

Unlawful lien (under € 20.000)

If the total debt entered in the tax roll (ruolo) is below the threshold of € 20.000, the registration of the lien is fundamentally unlawful and the measure must be annulled in its entirety. The threshold is absolute and cannot be derogated from by the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione): its violation results in the nullity of the lien regardless of any other consideration on the merits of the underlying tax debt.

Reference: Art. 77, paragraph 1, D.P.R. 602/1973
Third parties involved

Lien on spouse's assets

Under the statutory community of property regime (comunione legale), the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) might attempt to register a lien on real estate jointly owned by the spouses for tax debts attributable to only one of them. The non-debtor spouse has the right to intervene in the proceedings and oppose the registration of the lien on their ownership share, acting through both tax and civil law channels to protect their property rights.

Reference: Artt. 177-178 c.c. — Art. 77 D.P.R. 602/1973

Our method

The six defence strategies

Defence against a tax lien (ipoteca esattoriale) requires a multidimensional approach combining verification of formal legality requirements with the evaluation of the taxpayer's overall asset position, in order to select the most effective and swift legal remedy.

I.

Verification of threshold and requirements

Analysis of the total debt entered in the tax roll (ruolo) to verify whether the threshold of € 20.000 is actually exceeded. Calculation of amounts with possible deduction of time-barred, unlawfully calculated, or already paid sums. If the threshold is not reached, the lien is subject to annulment without the need to examine further defects.

II.

Protection of the primary residence (prima casa)

Verification of the conditions constituting absolute protection: is the property used as a primary residence? Does the debtor have their official civil residence there? Is it the sole owned property? If these conditions are met, enforcement proceedings are prohibited and the lien can be challenged with concrete prospects of cancellation.

III.

Pleading procedural defects

Verification of the prior notice pursuant to art. 77 comma 1-bis: was it sent? Did it reach the recipient? Is the content complete and proper? The absence or irregularity of the prior notice constitutes an independent and serious defect rendering the entire lien procedure unlawful, regardless of the merits of the underlying debt.

IV.

Urgent interim suspension

Application pursuant to art. 47 D.Lgs. 546/1992 to urgently suspend the effects of the lien and halt enforcement proceedings pending the decision on the merits. Precautionary suspension is essential when the taxpayer needs to dispose of or mortgage the property, or when enforcement is imminent and could cause irreparable harm.

V.

Administrative review (autotutela) and application for cancellation

Administrative application for cancellation of the lien submitted directly to the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) when the defect is evident and documented (debt below threshold, primary residence, extinguished debt). Administrative review (autotutela) is the quickest avenue to obtain cancellation and does not preclude a judicial appeal (ricorso), which should be lodged concurrently as subsidiary protection in the event of an adverse decision or inaction by the Authority.

VI.

Appeal before the CGT and claim for damages

Challenging the lien before the Court of Tax Justice (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria) seeking annulment and an order for cancellation. In cases where the unlawfulness is serious and has caused documented damages (loss of sale, denial of financing, loss of commercial opportunities), civil action for damages under art. 2043 c.c. may be considered.

Tangible results

Five resolved cases

The following cases illustrate our firm's practical defense work in matters of tax foreclosure liens (ipoteca esattoriale). Identifying details have been anonymized to protect client confidentiality.

Case no. 1 — CGT of First Instance

Primary residence: cancellation of a tax lien (ipoteca esattoriale) for € 87,300

Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado di Napoli — Year 2024

A retired individual from Naples discovered, during negotiations for the sale of his apartment, that the Revenue Agency – Collection Division (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) had registered a tax lien (ipoteca esattoriale) on the property for a tax debt of € 87,300, relating to unpaid personal income tax (IRPEF) and social security contributions for the years 2017–2019. The property is the taxpayer's sole owned asset, where he has maintained his registered residence since 1988. The purchaser's advisor discovered the mortgage encumbrance during a title search, bringing negotiations to a halt. The defense immediately filed an appeal (ricorso) with the First-Instance Tax Court (CGT di I grado) of Naples, arguing that the property constitutes the debtor's primary residence and sole owned asset, a circumstance that under art. 76 of Presidential Decree 602/1973 prohibits any foreclosure proceedings and renders the tax lien instrumentally futile and detrimental. An urgent suspension of enforcement was simultaneously requested to allow property sale negotiations to proceed. The court granted the interim suspension in chambers and, on the merits, upheld the appeal, ordering the cancellation of the lien and ordering the Revenue Agency – Collection Division (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) to pay legal costs. The sale was successfully finalized.

Lien cancelled — Primary residence protected
Case no. 2 — Administrative self-redress (Autotutela)

Debt below threshold: cancellation under administrative review (autotutela) for € 14,200

Revenue Agency–Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) / Naples Office — Year 2024

A small business owner in the restaurant sector received a notice of registration of a tax lien on her commercial property for an overall tax debt entered into the collection roll of € 14,200, comprising overdue municipal property tax (IMU), waste tax (TARI), and penalties for the years 2020–2021. The amount was clearly below the statutory threshold of € 20,000 provided for by art. 77 of Presidential Decree 602/1973, below which the registration of a tax lien is strictly prohibited. Our law firm drafted and served a formal application for administrative review (istanza di autotutela) on the Revenue Agency – Collection Division (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione), accompanied by a detailed calculation of the debt and documentary evidence proving the violation of the statutory limit. The Revenue Agency – Collection Division acknowledged the defect under administrative review and proceeded to cancel the lien within fifteen days of receiving the application, without any need to file an action before the tax court. The client was able to resume her business activities normally and unfreeze the bank financing previously blocked by the lien.

Cancellation under administrative review (autotutela) — 15 days
Case no. 3 — First-Instance Tax Court (CGT I grado) + Interim suspension

Failure to issue prior notice: tax lien annulled on industrial warehouse

Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado di Caserta — Year 2023

A limited liability company (società a responsabilità limitata) operating in the logistics sector learned of the registration of a tax lien mortgage (ipoteca esattoriale) on its industrial warehouse — the company’s primary asset — for a corporate income tax (IRES) debt of € 145.000 relating to the years 2018 and 2019. The company was in advanced negotiations with a bank to obtain a loan secured precisely by that property: the tax mortgage had blocked the transaction. An analysis of the documentation revealed that the Revenue Agency–Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) had registered the mortgage without serving the mandatory prior formal notice required pursuant to art. 77, paragraph 1-bis, D.P.R. 602/1973. The company had never received any prior notice (preavviso) and had not been given the opportunity to settle the debt or request an installment plan (rateizzazione) before the encumbrance was entered in the land registries. The appeal (ricorso) pleaded the invalidity of the mortgage registration procedure due to a breach of the right to prior cross-examination (contraddittorio preventivo), citing extensive Court of Cassation case law on the matter. The First-Instance Tax Court (CGT di I grado) of Caserta granted a stay of execution (sospensiva) within a few days and upheld the appeal on the merits, ordering the cancellation of the mortgage and awarding legal costs. The bank financing was finalized.

Mortgage annulled — Financing unblocked
Case no. 4 — First-Instance CGT

Joint ownership: protection of the spouse and reduction of the mortgage to the debtor’s share

Corte di Giustizia Tributaria di I grado di Napoli — Year 2023

The husband of one of our clients was served with a notice of mortgage registration on the family residence, purchased under the statutory marital property regime (comunione legale) in equal 50% shares between the spouses, for the husband’s personal tax debts amounting to € 52.000. The Revenue Agency–Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) had registered the mortgage on the entire property, without limiting it to the debtor’s co-ownership share, thereby also prejudicing the share belonging to the wife, who was an extraneous party to the tax relationship. Our client contacted our firm immediately, concerned about the fate of her share of the property and of the family residence. The appeal (ricorso) filed by the wife as a third-party co-owner pleaded a breach of the principle of personal liability of the tax debtor and the unlawfulness of the mortgage registration for the portion exceeding the debtor’s ownership share. The First-Instance Tax Court (CGT di I grado) of Napoli partially upheld the appeal, ordering the rectification of the mortgage to restrict it solely to the 50% share owned by the debtor. The wife’s share was released from the mortgage encumbrance and returned to the client’s full disposal.

Spouse’s share released — Mortgage reduced to 50%
Case no. 5 — Ex officio review (autotutela) + Reimbursement of expenses

Debt already settled: cancellation of mortgage and reimbursement of notary fees

Revenue Agency–Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) / CGT I grado Napoli — Year 2024

A fabric merchant had settled his debt position with the Revenue Agency–Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione) through the tax settlement procedure (rottamazione-quater), fully paying the installments under the payment plan concluded in July 2023. Despite full compliance with the plan and the consequent extinguishment of the tax debt, the Collection Agent (Agente della Riscossione) had not proceeded with the cancellation of the tax lien mortgage (ipoteca esattoriale) registered on the commercial property in 2021. The taxpayer only realized this during a mortgage registry search (ispezione ipotecaria) requested by a prospective buyer of the property, which stalled the negotiations and required additional notary fees for further checks. Our firm served a formal notice of default (diffida) on the Agenzia delle Entrate–Riscossione, attaching all documentation confirming the payments made and the completed facilitated settlement. Having received no response within the ten-day deadline, an appeal (ricorso) was filed before the First-Instance Tax Court (CGT di I grado) requesting a judicial order of cancellation and reimbursement of the expenses incurred for the additional mortgage searches. The tax judge ordered the immediate cancellation and awarded procedural costs to the appellant. The Agency cancelled the mortgage within the week following the filing of the judgment.

Cancellation + costs awarded
