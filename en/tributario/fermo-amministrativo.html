Administrative Vehicle Immobilization
You can have it cancelled. Before it is too late.

Administrative immobilization (fermo amministrativo) blocks your vehicle and can prevent you from carrying out your professional activity. Many immobilization orders are unlawful or legally flawed: improper service, time-barred debts, or business-essential vehicles exempt from immobilization. The notice of impending immobilization (preavviso di fermo) can be challenged independently before the Tax Court of Justice (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria). Act within 60 days.

Relevant Legislation

The legal regime of administrative immobilization

Administrative immobilization (fermo amministrativo) is an interim enforcement measure provided for by art. 86 D.P.R. 602/1973, through which the Tax Collection Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione) blocks the debtor's registered movable property. Case law has progressively expanded the avenues for challenge, recognizing the right to independently appeal the preliminary notice (preavviso di fermo) and the exemption from immobilization for vehicles instrumental to the taxpayer's professional or business activity.

Art. 86 D.P.R. 602/1973

Fundamental rule governing the administrative immobilisation (fermo) of registered movable property by the collection agent (agente della riscossione). It provides that immobilisation may be ordered on motor vehicles and other registered movable assets of the debtor entered on the tax roll (iscritto a ruolo). It requires prior service of a tax payment demand (cartella esattoriale) or a debit notice (avviso di addebito).

Notice of Prior Immobilisation — Cass. SS.UU. 2053/2006

The United Sections of the Court of Cassation (Sezioni Unite della Cassazione) established the autonomous appealability of the notice of prior administrative immobilisation (preavviso di fermo amministrativo) before the tax court. Although not yet an enforcement measure, the prior notice is already detrimental to the taxpayer’s interests and must be challenged within 60 days of service.

Art. 19 D.Lgs. 546/1992 — Appealability

The list of appealable acts before the Court of Tax Justice (CGT) includes administrative immobilisations. Case law has clarified that both the prior notice and the final immobilisation order are autonomously challengeable acts. The time limit is 60 days from service of the measure. Interim relief (tutela cautelare) is admissible through an application for a stay of execution (sospensione).

Administrative Immobilisation on a Tool-of-the-Trade Vehicle

Established case law (Cass. n. 14055/2018, CGT di merito) excludes administrative immobilisation (fermo amministrativo) from being ordered on vehicles essential to carrying out professional or business activities. Work-related vehicles (veicoli strumentali) are treated on a par with unattachable assets pursuant to art. 514 c.p.c., rendering the immobilisation unlawful and voidable.

Types

All types of administrative immobilisation we handle

Every administrative immobilisation matter presents specific features and requires a calibrated defence strategy. Our firm examines each case individually to identify the defects that permit the annulment or immediate suspension of the immobilisation.

01

Car Immobilisation

The most frequent case. The immobilisation of a passenger car prevents its circulation on public roads. We verify the regularity of service of the underlying tax payment demand (cartella esattoriale), statutory limitation (prescrizione) of the debt, and any operational necessity for work activities.

02

Motorcycle Immobilisation

Common for self-employed workers who use a motorcycle or scooter as their sole means of transportation to reach clients. If the motorcycle is essential to the business activity, the immobilisation is unlawful and may be annulled through an urgent procedure.

03

Immobilisation of Work-Related Vehicles

Trucks, vans, commercial vehicles: administrative immobilisation on vehicles essential to a business is systematically unlawful according to established case law. We promptly seek interim suspension and final annulment.

04

Notice of Prior Immobilisation

The preliminary notice by which ADER announces the intended immobilisation. It is the best moment to intervene, before the immobilisation is registered with the Public Vehicle Register (PRA). The prior notice is independently challengeable, and a timely appeal is the most effective strategy.

05

Removal of Immobilisation

An immobilisation that has already been registered may be removed by settling the debt or through a finding of its unlawfulness. We handle both procedures, coordinating with ADER for deregistration from the Public Vehicle Register (PRA) in the shortest possible time.

06

Immobilisation on a Sold Vehicle

An immobilisation registered on a vehicle subsequently sold to third parties causes serious difficulties for the new owner. We verify the prerequisites for third-party proceedings (opposizione di terzo) or for the removal of the immobilisation, protecting both buyer and seller.

How We Defend

Defence strategies against administrative immobilisation

Our firm has developed a systematic approach to counter administrative immobilisation, from the analysis of formal and substantive defects of the measure to urgent interim relief before the Court of Tax Justice (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria).

⚖

Challenging the Prior Notice

The most effective strategy: challenging the notice of prior immobilisation (preavviso di fermo) within 60 days of service, before the immobilisation is registered with the PRA. The appeal (ricorso) automatically stays the procedure and enables the party to raise all defects concerning the underlying measure (cartella, avviso). Cass. SS.UU. 2053/2006 definitively confirmed its autonomous appealability.

🚗

Operational Necessity of the Vehicle

When the vehicle is essential to carrying out business or professional activities, the immobilisation is unlawful according to settled case law. We gather the necessary documentation (Chamber of Commerce company report [visura camerale], invoices, tax returns) to prove operational necessity and obtain annulment or urgent interim suspension.

⏰

Statutory Limitation of the Underlying Debt

If the underlying tax debt is time-barred (prescritto), the immobilisation lapses for lack of a valid enforceable title (titolo esecutivo). We scrupulously verify the date of entry on the tax roll (iscrizione a ruolo), intermediate service notifications, and acts interrupting statutory limitation. Many older debts are time-barred and the related immobilisation can be annulled in full.

💰

Payment and Immediate Removal

When the debt is legitimate but the objective is to release the vehicle swiftly, we coordinate the partial or full payment procedure alongside the concurrent request for removal of the immobilisation from the PRA. We verify eligibility for facilitated settlement schemes (definizione agevolata or rottamazione) to reduce the liability prior to payment.

⚔

Interim Suspension Before the CGT

While an appeal is pending, an application may be made to the Court of Tax Justice (CGT) for interim suspension of the immobilisation. The interim order is generally issued very quickly (within days or weeks) and allows the vehicle to be used pending final judgment, avoiding irreparable damage to business activities.

📅

Instalment Plans for Vehicle Release

The grant of an instalment plan (rateizzazione) by ADER automatically suspends protective measures, including immobilisation. We assist taxpayers with the submission of the deferral application, ensuring proper completion and appropriate documentation, with suspension of the immobilisation taking effect upon payment of the first instalment.

Real cases: how we achieved the removal of administrative immobilisations

The following cases — prepared in compliance with professional confidentiality using anonymised data — illustrate the strategies that enabled our clients to remove immobilisations and regain full use of their vehicles.

Immobilisation on a Tradesman’s Van — Annulled for Operational Necessity

Annulled
Debtor: Self-employed plumber
Debt: € 8.400
Vehicle: Fiat Ducato van
Outcome: Immobilisation annulled by the CGT

A self-employed plumber holding a VAT number (partita IVA) received a notice of prior immobilisation on his Fiat Ducato van, the sole vehicle used to transport materials and reach work sites. The debt, amounting to approximately 8.400 euros, arose from a tax payment demand (cartella esattoriale) served long before for unpaid taxes. The client contacted our firm after attempting without success to reach an agreement directly with ADER without obtaining clear information regarding his rights. An examination of the file immediately revealed two critical grounds: first, the absolute operational necessity (strumentalità) of the vehicle, which was the only vehicle registered to the business and routinely used to perform work activities, supported by invoices and tax returns; second, the defective service of the underlying cartella, which was delivered to an address that no longer corresponded to the taxpayer’s residence. We filed an appeal (ricorso) before the CGT di Napoli with a concurrent application for interim relief (sospensiva cautelare), proving operational necessity with an updated Chamber of Commerce company report (visura camerale), invoice records covering the past three years, and an accountant’s statement. The CGT granted the interim stay at an urgent single-judge hearing just seven days after the appeal was lodged, and following the hearing on the merits, annulled the immobilisation.

Result: Immobilisation annulled in full. The vehicle was released and the client continued his business operations without disruption. The debt was subsequently admitted to an instalment plan.

Challenged Prior Notice — Debt Time-Barred in the Preceding Decade

Time-barred
Debtor: Pensioner
Debt: € 5.200 (claimed)
Vehicle: Compact passenger car
Outcome: Statutory limitation (prescrizione) declared

A seventy-year-old pensioner received a notice of impending vehicle immobilization (preavviso di fermo) on his car for an alleged personal income tax (IRPEF) debt dating back to the 2009 tax year. The notice arrived unexpectedly, without the client ever having received — according to him — any prior communication in recent years. Our firm immediately launched a systematic audit of the chain of notification: the tax collection notice (cartella esattoriale) from 2011, the subsequent payment order (intimazione di pagamento) from 2014, and any acts interrupting the statutory limitation period (prescrizione). The analysis revealed that the 2014 payment order had been served by deposit at the town hall (deposito in municipio), without the notice of completed deposit (avviso di avvenuto deposito) ever being delivered. Service was therefore null and void for violation of art. 140 c.p.c. and did not interrupt the statutory limitation period. The debt was therefore already time-barred as of 2016, well before the contested notice of impending immobilization was issued. We filed an appeal (ricorso) with the Court of Tax Justice (CGT), pleading primarily the statute of limitations on the claim and, in the alternative, the nullity of service. The CGT upheld the appeal, declaring the debt time-barred and the immobilization unlawful.

Result: Notice of impending immobilization cancelled due to the statute of limitations (prescrizione) on the debt. ADER ordered to pay legal costs. The vehicle was never subjected to immobilization.

Vehicle Immobilization (Fermo) on Co-Owned Car — Third-Party Owner Protected

Suspended and Annulled
Debtor: Individual with past tax debts
Vehicle: Car co-owned with spouse
Debt: € 22.000
Outcome: Immobilization annulled on the third party's share

The administrative immobilization (fermo) was registered on a car co-owned by a debtor and their spouse, who had no connection whatsoever with the husband's tax debts. The co-owning spouse, who owned fifty percent of the vehicle, used the car to commute to work as an employee and was in no way responsible for her husband's tax liabilities. The immobilization, being levied on the entire car without distinction between shares, clearly prejudiced the rights of a party unrelated to the debt. We challenged the immobilization before the Court of Tax Justice (CGT), contesting both the lawfulness of the measure regarding the share belonging to the uninvolved co-owner and the lack of necessity of immobilizing the entire vehicle for the debt. The defence relied on the protection of the third-party co-owner and on the principle that an immobilization cannot extend to assets not entirely owned by the debtor without adequate justification. The CGT stayed the immobilization as an interim measure and, on the merits, ordered the full annulment of the measure, directing its cancellation from the Public Vehicle Register (PRA).

Result: Immobilization (fermo) annulled. The vehicle was released from the encumbrance. The spouse's debt was subsequently subject to a separate instalment plan (rateizzazione).

Urgent Stay of Execution — Street Vendor Blocked

Stay of Execution Obtained
Debtor: Street vendor
Vehicle: Equipped shop-van
Debt: € 14.300
Outcome: Stay of execution granted within 5 days

A fruit and vegetable street vendor working in local markets in Naples was served an administrative immobilization (fermo) on his equipped shop-van, the sole vehicle with which he carried out his business. Without the van, which served simultaneously as a means of transport and as a mobile sales stall, the client was unable to conduct any business activity and generate income. The immediate economic harm was obvious and documentable. We filed an appeal (ricorso) with the utmost urgency before the CGT of Naples, accompanied by an application for an urgent single-judge interim stay (sospensiva cautelare monocratica), attaching complete documentation: market pitch concession, tax returns for the past three years showing the van as the sole capital asset, purchase invoices for produce from the wholesale market, and a declaration from the market director certifying the appellant's continuous presence. Having assessed the urgency of the situation and the instrumental nature of the vehicle, the single judge granted an interim stay of the immobilization within five days of filing the appeal, allowing the client to resume his business immediately.

Result: Interim stay of execution (sospensiva cautelare) obtained within 5 days. The client resumed his street vending business. Proceedings on the merits pending with favourable prospects.

Instalment Plan and Immediate Release of Immobilization (Fermo)

Immobilization (Fermo) Released
Debtor: Small transport company
Debt: € 38.000
Vehicles: 3 vehicles immobilized
Outcome: 72-instalment plan, immobilizations released

A small road transport company from Campania found itself facing administrative immobilization (fermo amministrativo) on three of its vehicles by ADER for an overall debt of approximately 38,000 euros arising from unpaid INPS contributions and VAT (IVA) across three tax periods. Although experiencing temporary liquidity constraints caused by payment delays from public entity clients, the company had a solid client portfolio and an ongoing, operational business. The simultaneous immobilization of three vehicles made it impossible to perform signed transport contracts, exposing the company to contractual liability towards its clients. We pursued a dual strategy: on one hand, the urgent submission of an application for an ordinary instalment plan (rateizzazione ordinaria) to ADER, documenting the temporary economic difficulty (balance sheet ratios, bank statements, copies of receivables due from the Public Administration); on the other, an appeal (ricorso) to the Court of Tax Justice (CGT) with a request for a stay of execution based on the instrumental nature of the vehicles and the irreparable harm arising from their unavailability. The instalment plan was granted very swiftly, allowing the automatic release of the three immobilizations upon payment of the first monthly instalment.

Result: Instalment plan granted over 72 monthly instalments. Three immobilizations lifted within 20 days. The company regularly honoured its contractual obligations without further harm.
