Relevant legal provision

What the law provides

Consolidated Law on Narcotics

Art. 73 DPR 309/90 — Unlawful production, trafficking, and possession of narcotic substances

Anyone who, without authorization, cultivates, produces, manufactures, extracts, refines, sells, offers or puts up for sale, transfers, distributes, trades, transports, procures for others, sends, passes or dispatches in transit, or delivers for any purpose narcotic or psychotropic substances is punished with imprisonment and a fine. The law clearly distinguishes between hard drugs (heroin, cocaine, MDMA, amphetamines) included in Schedules I and III and soft drugs (cannabis, hashish, marijuana) included in Schedules II and IV, providing for significantly different penalties. Possession for exclusively personal use does not constitute a criminal offence, but only an administrative violation.

Hard drugs: imprisonment from 6 to 20 years + fine from € 26,000 to € 260,000
Soft drugs: imprisonment from 2 to 6 years + fine from € 5,164 to € 77,468
Minor offense (paragraph 5): imprisonment from 6 months to 4 years + fine from € 1,032 to € 10,329

The severity categories

Personal use — No criminal offence

Administrative violation. Possession for exclusively personal use is not a criminal offence (art. 75 DPR 309/90). The person in possession faces only administrative sanctions: suspension of driving licence, passport, or firearms permit for 1 month to 1 year. The following are evaluated: quantity, method of storage, gross weight and active ingredient, absence of packaging equipment.

Minor offense — Art. 73 paragraph 5

Mitigated criminal offence. Imprisonment from 6 months to 4 years and a fine from € 1,032 to € 10,329. It applies when, by virtue of the means, methods, or circumstances of the action, or the quality and quantity of the substances, the facts are of minor significance. It allows access to alternative procedures and alternatives to detention.

Ordinary offence — Art. 73 paragraphs 1 and 4

Serious criminal offence. Hard drugs (Sched. I and III): imprisonment from 6 to 20 years and fine from € 26,000 to € 260,000. Soft drugs (Sched. II and IV): imprisonment from 2 to 6 years and fine from € 5,164 to € 77,468. Pre-trial detention in prison is frequent, especially for hard drugs.

Aggravated scenario — Art. 80 DPR 309/90

Specific aggravating circumstances. The penalty is increased by one-third to one-half when: the substance is delivered to a minor or an individual with a mental impairment; the act is committed by an armed or disguised person; the substance is adulterated in a manner dangerous to health; the quantity is substantial (art. 80 paragraph 2). In the event of a substantial quantity of hard drugs, the penalty may exceed 20 years of imprisonment.

Fundamental distinction — personal use vs. dealing: The difference between possession for personal use (administrative violation) and possession for the purpose of dealing (criminal offence) depends on a combination of indicators: quantity of the substance, percentage of active ingredient, packaging into doses, possession of precision scales, cash in small denominations, telephone contact with buyers, statements from third parties.

How we defend you

Defence strategies

Every narcotics case has its own specific features. From flawed police operations to the reclassification of the conduct, our law firm analyses every detail to construct the most effective defence and achieve the best possible result.

Possession for personal use

Demonstration that the substance was intended exclusively for personal consumption, through toxicological expertise, analysis of the quantity and active ingredient, and the absence of packaging and distribution tools. Personal use excludes the criminal offence and entails only administrative sanctions (art. 75 DPR 309/90).

Reclassification as a minor offense

Obtaining the classification of the offense as a minor offense (lieve entità) pursuant to art. 73 paragraph 5, resulting in a substantial reduction of the sentence (from 6 months to 4 years). Factors highlighted: modest quantity, occasional and unorganized supply, absence of established ties to trafficking, personal circumstances of the defendant.

Procedural defects in police operations

Challenging the lawfulness of personal, home, or vehicle searches. Verification of compliance with procedural safeguards provided for under arts. 247 et seq. of the Code of Criminal Procedure (c.p.p.): reasoned decree, presence of witnesses, validation within statutory time limits. The unlawfulness of the search renders the seizure of the substance inadmissible as evidence.

Entrapment by an undercover agent

Verification that the activity of the undercover agent (art. 97 DPR 309/90) did not exceed the limits of mere observation, turning into actual incitement to commit the offense. The case law of the ECHR (Teixeira de Castro v. Portugal) and the Court of Cassation (giurisprudenza consolidata) sanctions evidence obtained through entrapment with inadmissibility.

Defects in the chain of custody

Verification of the integrity of the chain of custody of the seized substance: proper sealing, storage, correspondence between what was seized and what was tested in the laboratory. Any break in the chain of custody undermines the reliability of the quantitative and qualitative analysis.

Alternative procedures and mitigating measures

Strategic assessment of access to plea bargaining (patteggiamento - art. 444 c.p.p.), summary trial (giudizio abbreviato) with a one-third sentence reduction, probation (messa alla prova - art. 168-bis c.p.) for minor offenses, or probation with social services (affidamento in prova ai servizi sociali). For drug-dependent individuals: suspension of sentence with therapeutic probation (affidamento terapeutico - art. 94 DPR 309/90).

Results achieved

5 brilliantly resolved cases

Case no. 1

Possession reclassified as personal use — Tribunale di Napoli

Substance: 15 g of hashish — Risk: imprisonment of up to 6 years for drug dealing

A young university student stopped in a car with 15 grams of hashish divided into three blocks and precision scales in his backpack. The Public Prosecutor's Office charged possession with intent to supply based on the subdivision of the substance and the presence of the precision scales. The defence submitted a toxicological expert report certifying the client's regular personal consumption, medical documentation concerning anxiety disorders treated with therapeutic cannabis, and witness statements from friends confirming regular purchases exclusively for personal use.

The Court held that the indicators of drug dealing were insufficient and reclassified the act as possession for personal use, forwarding the file to the Prefecture (Prefettura) solely for administrative penalties. No criminal record.

Reclassification as personal use — No criminal offence
Case no. 2

Unlawful search — Tribunale di Roma

Substance: 50 g of cocaine — Risk: imprisonment from 6 to 20 years

A forty-year-old entrepreneur subjected to a home search on the basis of an anonymous report. The officers had proceeded without a decree from the Public Prosecutor (PM) and in the absence of the urgency requirements set forth in art. 352 c.p.p., without the presence of at least two witnesses as required by art. 250 c.p.p. The substance, 50 grams of cocaine, had been found in a bedroom wardrobe.

The defence challenged the unlawfulness of the search and the consequent inadmissibility (inutilizzabilità) of the seizure. The Judge for Preliminary Investigations (GIP), upon review, annulled the precautionary measure. At the trial hearing, the Court declared the evidence obtained through the seizure inadmissible and acquitted the defendant for lack of evidence.

Full acquittal — Evidence declared inadmissible
Case no. 3

From standard drug dealing to minor offence (lieve entità) — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Substance: 8 g of cocaine in 12 doses — Risk: imprisonment from 6 to 20 years

A thirty-year-old worker arrested in flagrante delicto with 8 grams of cocaine divided into 12 wraps and 380 euros in small-denomination banknotes. The Public Prosecutor's Office (Procura) charged the offence in its ordinary form under art. 73 comma 1, requesting pre-trial detention in prison (custodia cautelare in carcere). The defence immediately challenged the precautionary measure and, in parallel, constructed an evidentiary framework demonstrating: the modest quantity of the substance, the occasional nature of the conduct (no specific criminal record), the absence of any organisation, and the compatibility of the cash with his regular wages.

The Review Court (Tribunale del Riesame) replaced detention in prison with a residence order (obbligo di dimora). At trial, the judge reclassified the act as a minor offence pursuant to art. 73 paragraph 5, imposing a sentence of 1 year and 4 months of imprisonment, fully suspended (sospensione condizionale).

Reclassification as minor offence — Suspended sentence
Case no. 4

Agent provocateur (*agente provocatore*) — Corte d’Appello di Napoli

Substance: 30 g of marijuana — Risk: imprisonment from 2 to 6 years

A young bartender sentenced at first instance to 2 years and 8 months of imprisonment for supplying marijuana. The operation had been conducted by an undercover officer who, introducing himself as a regular customer at the bar, had repeatedly solicited the sale of drugs over several weeks until obtaining a supply of 30 grams. The defence on appeal demonstrated, through wiretaps and meeting reports, that the defendant had no predisposition to drug dealing prior to the officer's intervention and that the conduct had been entirely induced by the undercover operative's persistent pressure.

The Court of Appeal (Corte d’Appello), applying the principles of ECHR (CEDU) case law on the prohibition of incitement, ruled the evidence collected by the agent provocateur inadmissible and acquitted the defendant on the ground that “the fact does not exist” (“il fatto non sussiste”).

Acquittal on appeal — Agent provocateur censured
Case no. 5

Therapeutic probation for drug addiction (affidamento terapeutico) — Tribunale di Sorveglianza di Napoli

Substance: 20 g of heroin — Final conviction: 4 years and 6 months of imprisonment

A thirty-five-year-old with documented substance abuse issues, sentenced to 4 years and 6 months for possession and trafficking of heroin. Following the final conviction, the defence applied for probation in special cases (affidamento in prova in casi particolari) pursuant to art. 94 DPR 309/90, submitting certification from the Addiction Service (Ser.D.) confirming the state of drug addiction and intake into a therapeutic community, the agreed therapeutic programme, and the community's report on the positive progress of the rehabilitation pathway.

The Surveillance Court (Tribunale di Sorveglianza) granted therapeutic probation (affidamento terapeutico), avoiding imprisonment. Upon completion of the programme, the sentence was declared fully served. The client completed the detoxification pathway and resumed a normal life.

Therapeutic probation granted — No imprisonment
