Involved in a fatal accident?
You risk up to 18 years of imprisonment. Every hour is decisive.

Vehicular homicide, introduced by Law no. 41/2016, is one of the most serious road-related offences in the criminal code. Penalties are severe, particularly where intoxication by alcohol or narcotics is present. However, negligence (colpa) is a complex concept: not every fatal accident results in a conviction. A criminal defence lawyer can build the defence you need.

What the law provides

Italian Criminal Code (Codice Penale)

Art. 589-bis — Vehicular homicide

Whoever causes through fault (colpa) the death of a person in violation of the rules governing road traffic is punished with imprisonment from two to seven years. The penalty is increased if the act is committed by an individual in a state of alcohol intoxication or under the influence of narcotic or psychotropic substances.

Base penalty: imprisonment from 2 to 7 years

The severity categories

Basic circumstance (paragraph 1)

Simple negligence (colpa semplice). Generic violation of the Highway Code rules. Imprisonment from 2 to 7 years. Suspension of driving licence from 1 to 5 years.

Aggravated (paragraphs 2-3)

Intoxication 0.8-1.5 g/l or serious violations. Imprisonment from 5 to 10 years. Also applies in cases of speeding, running a red light, driving against the flow of traffic, or making a U-turn on a motorway.

Most serious (paragraphs 4-5)

Intoxication over 1.5 g/l or narcotics. Imprisonment from 8 to 12 years. Up to 18 years in the event of the death of multiple persons. Automatic revocation of driving licence. Mandatory arrest in flagrante delicto.

Special mitigating circumstance (paragraph 7): If the event is not the exclusive consequence of the action or omission of the offender, the penalty is reduced by up to one half. The concurrent liability of the victim (so-called contributory negligence) can significantly reduce the penalty, but does not preclude the offence. In cases of prevailing fault of the victim, the penalty may be reduced by up to one third.

How we defend you

Defence strategies

Vehicular homicide requires technical defence of the highest level. From the reconstruction of the accident dynamics to the analysis of scientific evidence, every detail can change the outcome of the trial.

Contributory negligence of the victim

Analysis of the accident dynamics to ascertain the causal contribution of the victim: sudden crossing, driving against traffic, failure to use safety devices. Contributory negligence can reduce the penalty by up to one half (art. 589-bis, paragraph 7).

Kinematic and reconstructive expert appraisal

Appointment of a technical expert for the reconstruction of the accident dynamics: impact speed, braking distance, collision point, road conditions. An accurate expert appraisal can demonstrate the inevitability of the impact.

Challenging the causal link

Verification that the death is indeed attributable to the driving conduct and not to autonomous factors: pre-existing medical conditions of the victim, third-party intervention, structural defects of the road or vehicle.

Defects in blood alcohol testing

If alcohol intoxication is alleged, verification of the regularity of the breathalyser, the chain of custody of blood samples, and the sampling times. An irregular test can cause the aggravating circumstance to be dismissed, drastically reducing the penalty.

Summary trial and plea bargaining

Strategic assessment of access to alternative procedures: summary trial (giudizio abbreviato) guarantees a one-third reduction of the sentence; plea bargaining (patteggiamento) may allow for a suspended sentence. Essential in the most serious scenarios to mitigate the penalty.

Compensation and general mitigating circumstances

Full compensation for damages paid to the victim's family members, together with cooperative conduct by the defendant, can support the granting of general mitigating circumstances under art. 62-bis c.p. and their prevailing weight over contested aggravating factors.

Results achieved

5 brilliantly resolved cases

Case no. 1

Prevalent contributory negligence of the victim — Tribunale di Napoli

Charge: art. 589-bis paragraph 1 c.p. — Risk: imprisonment 2-7 years

A driver struck a pedestrian crossing the road at night, outside the pedestrian crossing, dressed in dark clothing and in an area lacking street lighting. The prosecution charged vehicular homicide (omicidio stradale) for excessive speed.

The defence submitted a kinematic expert appraisal demonstrating that the driver was travelling within the speed limits and that the sighting distance of the pedestrian was less than the stopping distance. The Court recognised the prevalent contributory negligence of the victim pursuant to art. 589-bis paragraph 7 c.p., applying a sentence reduction of up to one third and granting a suspended sentence (sospensione condizionale della pena).

Minimum sentence with suspended sentence
Case no. 2

Exclusion of the aggravating circumstance of intoxication — Tribunale di Roma

Charge: art. 589-bis paragraphs 1 and 4 c.p. — Risk: imprisonment 8-12 years

A young driver caused a fatal accident in the early hours of the morning. The blood sample taken at the hospital two hours after the accident tested positive with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.9 g/l. The prosecution alleged the aggravating circumstance of driving under the influence of alcohol.

The defence demonstrated the irregularity in the chain of custody of the blood sample and the non-compliance with medical protocols for blood sampling, further highlighting that the blood alcohol level at the time of the event, due to alcohol metabolism, could have been significantly lower. The judge excluded the aggravating circumstance, reclassifying the act under the basic offence with a significantly reduced sentence.

Aggravating circumstance excluded — Sentence reduced from 8 to 3 years
Case no. 3

Acquittal due to fortuitous event — Tribunale di Salerno

Charge: art. 589-bis paragraph 1 c.p. — Risk: imprisonment 2-7 years

A driver lost control of the vehicle on a bend due to an oil slick on the roadway, crossing into the oncoming lane and causing a head-on collision resulting in a fatality. The prosecution asserted liability for speed inappropriate to the road conditions.

The defence obtained documentation from the road management authority, proving that the oil slick had been reported by other drivers in the preceding hours without the authority intervening. The technical expert appraisal confirmed that the vehicle's speed was within the limits and that the loss of traction was inevitable. The Court acquitted the defendant, excluding fault and identifying a fortuitous event (caso fortuito).

Full acquittal — The act does not constitute an offence
Case no. 4

Summary trial with mitigating circumstances — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Charge: art. 589-bis paragraphs 1 and 2 c.p. — Risk: imprisonment 5-10 years

A professional, driving with a blood alcohol concentration of 1.1 g/l, caused a collision in which the driver of the other vehicle died. The evidentiary finding was formally unassailable and liability was difficult to dispute.

The defence negotiated full compensation for damages with the victim's family, obtaining the recognition of ordinary mitigating circumstances pursuant to art. 62-bis c.p. and their prevalence over the aggravating circumstance. By accessing the summary trial procedure (rito abbreviato), the sentence was reduced to 2 years and 8 months, with the granting of a conditional suspension of the sentence. The client did not serve a single day in prison.

Suspended sentence — No imprisonment
Case no. 5

Exclusion of the causal link — Corte d’Appello di Napoli

Charge: art. 589-bis paragraph 1 c.p. — First instance conviction: 4 years of imprisonment

A driver struck a cyclist who suffered apparently minor injuries. The victim died in hospital three days later due to cardiac complications. The first instance Court convicted the defendant to 4 years of imprisonment for vehicular homicide.

The defence on appeal produced a forensic medical report demonstrating that the death was attributable to a pre-existing, undiagnosed severe heart condition of the victim, and not to the injuries sustained in the accident. The Court of Appeal upheld the defence's arguments, excluding the causal link between the accident and the death, and amended the judgment by acquitting the defendant of vehicular homicide, leaving only the offence of negligent bodily injury (lesioni colpose).

Acquittal of vehicular homicide on appeal — Remaining charges limited to negligent injury
