Accused of defamation for a social media post?
A comment can result in a criminal conviction. But defending yourself is possible.

A post on Facebook, a negative review on Google, a message in a WhatsApp group: a few words are enough to be reported for defamation. It is the most frequent offence in the digital era, and the consequences can be serious — from a criminal conviction to liability for damages. But not every critique is an offence, and not every criminal complaint (querela) is well-founded. A criminal defence lawyer can demonstrate this.

Relevant legal provision

What the law provides

Italian Criminal Code (Codice Penale)

Art. 595 — Defamation (Diffamazione)

Whoever, outside the cases referred to in the preceding article, by communicating with multiple persons, damages the reputation of another, shall be punished by imprisonment of up to one year or by a fine of up to euro 1,032. The offense is punishable upon complaint by the injured party (querela), to be filed within three months from the occurrence.

Base penalty: imprisonment of up to 1 year or a fine of up to € 1,032

The types of defamation

Simple defamation (para. 1)

Communication with multiple persons. Damage to another’s reputation in the presence of at least two persons (other than the injured party). Imprisonment of up to 1 year or a fine of up to € 1,032. This is the base form, less common in the digital age.

Attribution of a specific fact (para. 2)

Specific aggravating circumstance. When the offense consists in attributing a specific fact (e.g., «he is a fraudster», «he evades taxes»). Imprisonment of up to 2 years or a fine of up to € 2,065. It increases the severity because a specific fact is more detrimental to the reputation.

By means of the press / social media (para. 3)

More serious aggravating circumstance. If the offense is committed by means of the press or by any other means of advertising (social networks, blogs, websites, online reviews). Imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years or a fine of not less than € 516.

Even a message in a WhatsApp group with multiple participants can constitute the offense. Negative reviews on Google, TripAdvisor, or industry portals, if they exceed the boundaries of criticism and attack the person, can constitute aggravated defamation.

How we defend you

Defence strategies

Not every criticism is defamation. Not every offensive post is a crime. The line between freedom of expression and damage to reputation is thin and requires a rigorous analysis of the specific case. Our firm identifies the most effective defense strategy among those available.

Right to criticize (art. 21 Const.)

Freedom of expression is guaranteed by the Constitution. Criticism, even when harsh, is lawful when it respects the limits of temperance of expression (continenza), relevance (pertinenza), and the truth of the underlying fact. We demonstrate that the comment fell within the lawful exercise of the right to criticize.

Temperance of expression (continenza)

Even when the content is critical, form matters. If the expressions used, although vigorous, do not exceed the limits of civil coexistence and remain within the perimeter of non-gratuitously offensive criticism, the act is not punishable. We analyze language, context, and communicative register to demonstrate formal temperance.

Truth of the fact (exceptio veritatis)

If what was stated corresponds to the truth, the act cannot constitute defamation when the requirements of the putative justification defense or the exercise of the right of reporting apply. We gather documentary evidence confirming the truthfulness of the contested statements.

Absence of the subjective element (mens rea)

Defamation requires general intent (dolo generico): the awareness and willingness to offend another’s reputation. If the comment was aimed at constructive criticism, personal opinion, or reporting a poor service, without an intention to cause harm, the psychological element of the crime is missing.

Provocation and retaliation

When the offensive expression is an immediate reaction to an unjust act by another, the mitigating circumstance of provocation (art. 599 co. 2 c.p.) or the ground of non-punishability of retaliation (art. 599 co. 1 c.p.) may apply, which excludes punishment when offenses are reciprocal.

Statute of limitations and procedural defects

Simple defamation is subject to a 6-year statute of limitations. The criminal complaint (querela) must be filed within 3 months from learning of the fact. We verify the timeliness of the complaint, the legal standing of the complainant, any implied withdrawal (remissione tacita), and every procedural flaw that could result in the inadmissibility of the criminal prosecution.

Identification of the author

In cases of anonymous posts, fake profiles, or shared accounts, we challenge the actual attribution of the content to the defendant. The burden of proving the identity of the author rests with the prosecution: an account registered in a name does not automatically mean that the account holder wrote that comment.

Results achieved

5 brilliantly resolved cases

Case no. 1

Facebook post against former employer — Tribunale di Napoli

Charge: art. 595 co. 3 c.p. — Risk: imprisonment of up to 3 years + compensation for damages

A dismissed employee had published a lengthy post on Facebook alleging irregular working conditions and delayed payments, disclosing the full name of the company's owner. The employer lodged a complaint for aggravated defamation via social media, also claiming damages of € 50,000.

The defence proved the truthfulness of the stated facts by submitting payslips, out-of-court formal notices, and the employment tribunal ruling establishing the same breaches of contract. The Tribunal recognised the defence of legitimate exercise of the right of fair criticism (diritto di critica), noting that the expressions used, although vivid, did not exceed the bounds of formal restraint (continenza formale) and were based on objectively substantiated facts.

Full acquittal — Right of fair criticism recognised
Case no. 2

Negative Google review against a medical clinic — Tribunale di Roma

Charge: art. 595 co. 2 and 3 c.p. — Risk: imprisonment of up to 3 years + claim for damages of € 30,000

A patient who had posted a review on Google Maps detailing a negative experience at a dental clinic, complaining of a misdiagnosis and unnecessary treatment, using expressions such as «they made me pay thousands of euros for useless work» and «total incompetence». The physician lodged a complaint for aggravated defamation involving the attribution of a specific fact.

The defence produced the medical records from a second specialist confirming that the contested treatment was unnecessary, and framed the review within the scope of legitimate consumer criticism. The Tribunal acquitted the defendant, acknowledging that the review, although harsh in tone, represented an opinion based on real facts and fell within the right of criticism protected by art. 21 of the Constitution.

Acquittal — Review recognised as legitimate criticism
Case no. 3

Offensive messages in a condominium WhatsApp group — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Charge: art. 595 co. 1 and 3 c.p. — Risk: imprisonment of up to 3 years + compensation for damages

A co-owner who, exasperated by years of condominium disputes, sent a series of messages to a WhatsApp group of 47 participants accusing the building manager of «opaque management of condominium funds» and «favouring certain co-owners to the detriment of others». The manager lodged a complaint for aggravated defamation, arguing that the WhatsApp group constituted a «public medium».

The defence raised an objection of untimeliness of the complaint, demonstrating that the manager had become aware of the messages more than three months prior to lodging the complaint, as evidenced by a dated read-receipt screenshot. In the alternative, it demonstrated that the criticisms were well-founded by producing the minutes of the assembly and accounting documentation confirming irregularities in the management. The GIP delivered a judgment of no case to answer (sentenza di non luogo a procedere) due to the untimeliness of the complaint.

No case to answer — Untimely complaint
Case no. 4

Instagram comment directed at an influencer — Tribunale di Milano

Charge: art. 595 co. 3 c.p. — Risk: imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years + claim for damages of € 80,000

A user commented under a post by a well-known influencer, stating that the influencer «promoted substandard products in exchange for money, deceiving thousands of followers», after purchasing a sponsored product that proved to be of lower quality than advertised. The influencer lodged a complaint and joined the proceedings as a civil party (parte civile), claiming € 80,000 in damages for injury to professional reputation.

The defence demonstrated the absence of the subjective element of the offence (mens rea), categorising the comment as an expression of a consumer's critical opinion lacking defamatory intent. It also submitted reports filed with the Italian Competition Authority (AGCM) and negative reviews from other purchasers concerning the same product, demonstrating the validity of the criticism. The Tribunal acquitted the defendant because the act does not constitute an offence (il fatto non costituisce reato), recognising the exercise of the consumer's right of criticism.

Acquittal — Act does not constitute an offence
Case no. 5

Reciprocal defamation on Facebook between former spouses — Tribunale di Salerno

Charge: art. 595 co. 3 c.p. — Risk: criminal conviction + damages in the context of the separation proceedings

In the context of a contentious separation, both spouses posted mutually offensive comments on Facebook. The wife wrote that the husband was «an absent father who only thinks about having fun»; the husband retaliated by describing her as «an unstable person who uses the children as a weapon». Both lodged complaints for aggravated defamation.

The defence of our client successfully invoked the ground of non-punishability for reciprocal insults pursuant to art. 599 co. 1 c.p., proving that the defendant's remarks were a direct response to the insults originally published by the former spouse. The Tribunal held both defendants not punishable due to the reciprocity of the offences, ordering that legal costs be offset between the parties.

Non-punishability due to reciprocal offences — No conviction
