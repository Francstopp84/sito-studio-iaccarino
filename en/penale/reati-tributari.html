D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Tax Crimes

Under investigation for a tax offence?
It is not just a matter of taxes. It is a matter of freedom.

A tax audit can turn into criminal proceedings with devastating consequences: seizure of assets, disqualification from business activities, up to imprisonment. However, tax crimes have precise thresholds for criminal liability, specific statutory limitation periods, and avenues of defence that only a criminal defence lawyer knows how to identify.

Relevant legal provision

What the law provides

Legislative Decree 10 March 2000, no. 74

Provisions governing offences concerning income tax and value-added tax

D.Lgs. 74/2000, amended several times (most recently by D.Lgs. 87/2024 reforming the tax penalty system), governs tax crimes in Italy. The system is built on thresholds for criminal liability: below certain amounts, the unlawful conduct remains an administrative infraction; above them, it constitutes a criminal offence. Accurate knowledge of these thresholds is the primary tool of defence.

Penalties: from 6 months to 8 years of imprisonment depending on the offence

Table of thresholds for criminal liability

Critical aspects

Preventive seizure, confiscation, and corporate liability

Preventive seizure (sequestro preventivo): the Public Prosecutor (PM) may request preventive seizure aimed at confiscation (art. 12-bis D.Lgs. 74/2000) of sums of money, real estate, bank accounts, and corporate shareholdings up to the amount of the evaded tax. The seizure may also affect assets of the legal representative and partners.

Confiscation by equivalent (confisca per equivalente): if the direct proceeds of the crime cannot be identified, confiscation extends to any asset of the person under investigation of equivalent value. This means that the seizure (sequestro) may halt the company's entire business operations.

Corporate liability (responsabilità dell’ente under D.Lgs. 231/2001): for the most serious tax offences (artt. 2, 3, 8), the company itself may be held liable, facing financial penalties and disqualifying sanctions (ban on contracting with the Public Administration, suspension of business activity).

Ground for non-punishability (causa di non punibilità - art. 13 D.Lgs. 74/2000): for the offences of unpaid taxes (omesso versamento - artt. 10-bis and 10-ter) and false tax return (dichiarazione infedele - art. 4), full payment of the tax debt, including penalties and interest, prior to the opening of the trial at first instance (apertura del dibattimento), extinguishes the offence. For more serious offences (artt. 2, 3, 8), full payment allows for a sentence reduction of up to one-half and the non-application of ancillary penalties.

How we defend you

Defence strategies

Tax offences navigate the fine line between lawful tax planning and criminal conduct. Our firm understands this boundary and uses it to the client's advantage, building defence arguments that speak the technical language of the judges presiding over the tax chambers.

Below the threshold of punishability (sotto-soglia di punibilità)

Rigorous review of the calculation of the evaded tax alleged by the Public Prosecutor’s Office (Procura della Repubblica). The Public Prosecutor (PM) often adopts the figures of the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) without challenge. An independent forensic accounting report can demonstrate that the actual amount is below the criminal threshold, turning the criminal offence into a mere administrative infraction.

Liquidity crisis and force majeure

For offences of failure to remit taxes (VAT, withholding tax), the Court of Cassation (Corte di Cassazione) recognises a liquidity crisis not attributable to the business owner as a ground for excluding culpability, provided it is rigorously proven: the crisis must be sudden, unforeseeable, and such as to make payment impossible — not merely difficult —.

Professional adviser's error

When the contested conduct results from erroneous advice given by the certified accountant (commercialista) or tax consultant, the defence can invoke justifiable reliance on a qualified professional. The good faith of the taxpayer who relied upon an expert excludes criminal intent (dolo), an essential element of all tax offences.

Statute of limitations (prescrizione)

Tax offences are subject to specific limitation periods. Fraudulent tax return expires after 8 years (10 with interruptions); offences involving failure to remit taxes expire after 6 years (7 years and 6 months with interruptions). The defence closely monitors statutory limitation periods, which frequently expire prior to the trial stage given the complexity of tax investigations.

Active repentance (ravvedimento operoso) and debt settlement

The full settlement of the tax debt prior to the trial extinguishes less severe offences (omitted payment, inaccurate return) and reduces the sentence by half for more serious ones. The defence negotiates instalment plans with the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) compatible with procedural timeframes to maximise the benefit.

Plea bargain (patteggiamento) with suspended sentence

When the prosecution's case is solid, the strategy focuses on plea bargaining (patteggiamento pursuant to art. 444 c.p.p.) with a suspended sentence. For tax offences, plea bargaining is subject to payment of the tax liability, but avoids a trial, ancillary penalties, and registration on the criminal record certificate (casellario giudiziale) for sentences up to 2 years.

Challenging preventive seizure (sequestro preventivo)

Asset seizure is often the most damaging measure for a business owner. The defence challenges the seizure order before the Review Court (Tribunale del Riesame), arguing disproportionality, miscalculation of profit, confusion between illicit proceeds and lawful revenue, and the necessity to preserve business continuity.

Exclusion of corporate liability

In proceedings pursuant to D.Lgs. 231/2001 parallel to the tax offence, the defence demonstrates the adoption and effective implementation of organisational models designed to prevent offences of the same kind, or that the offence was committed by fraudulently bypassing the model, thereby excluding the company's liability.

Results achieved

5 brilliantly resolved cases

Case no. 1

Failure to pay VAT: below threshold following expert appraisal — Tribunale di Napoli

Charge: failure to pay VAT pursuant to art. 10-ter D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Alleged unpaid VAT: € 287.000 — Risk: imprisonment for up to 2 years

An entrepreneur in the catering sector accused of failure to pay VAT amounting to € 287.000 relating to the 2021 tax year. The defense appointed an accounting technical expert who recalculated the VAT due, taking into account credit notes not recorded by the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate), lawfully executed horizontal offsets, and VAT credits accrued in the previous year not yet refunded.

The expert appraisal demonstrated that the VAT actually unpaid amounted to € 198.000, an amount below the threshold of punishability of € 250.000. The Court acquitted the defendant because the act is not provided for by law as an offense (perché il fatto non è previsto dalla legge come reato).

Acquittal — Established amount below the threshold
Case no. 2

False invoices: genuinely existing transactions — Tribunale di Roma

Charge: fraudulent tax return through false invoices pursuant to art. 2 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Risk: imprisonment from 4 to 8 years

A construction company accused of having used invoices for non-existent transactions in its tax return issued by a labor cooperative, for a total amount of € 1.200.000. The Public Prosecutor’s Office alleged that the cooperative was a “paper mill” (cartiera) established to generate fictitious costs. The defense produced procurement contracts, delivery notes, photographic documentation of the construction sites, and testimony from site managers confirming the actual performance of the works.

The Court recognized that the transactions had genuinely taken place and that the invoices corresponded to real services, acquitting the managing director with a full verdict on the merits (con formula piena).

Full acquittal — Transactions proven to be genuine
Case no. 3

Failure to remit withholding taxes: liquidity crisis — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Charge: failure to remit certified withholding taxes pursuant to art. 10-bis D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Unremitted withholding taxes: € 210.000 — Risk: imprisonment for up to 2 years

Owner of a metalworking company with 35 employees, accused of failing to remit certified withholding taxes for € 210.000. The defense documented that the omission was the direct consequence of a liquidity crisis caused by the non-payment by a public entity of receivables amounting to € 480.000 accrued on duly executed procurement contracts. The entrepreneur had prioritized the payment of employees’ salaries over remitting withholding taxes to the State.

The Court recognized the occurrence of force majeure and acquitted the defendant due to the absence of the subjective element of the offense (elemento soggettivo), establishing that the omission was not the result of a deliberate choice but of the objective impossibility to comply.

Acquittal — Force majeure recognized
Case no. 4

Untruthful tax return: extinction through payment of the tax debt — Tribunale di Salerno

Charge: untruthful tax return pursuant to art. 4 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Alleged evaded tax: € 180.000 — Risk: imprisonment from 2 to 5 years

A professional accused of having declared active elements in an amount lower than the actual amount, with an alleged tax evasion of € 180.000. The evidence was solid. The defense strategy focused on settling the tax debt: the defense negotiated a tax settlement proposal (accertamento con adesione) with the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) that reduced the total amount, and assisted the client in making full payment prior to the opening of the trial.

Pursuant to art. 13 comma 1 D.Lgs. 74/2000, full payment of the tax debt led to a declaration of non-punishability and the extinction of the offense.

Offense extinguished — Cause of non-punishability through payment
Case no. 5

Issuance of false invoices: revocation of the seizure — Tribunale del Riesame di Napoli

Charge: issuance of false invoices pursuant to art. 8 D.Lgs. 74/2000 — Seizure: € 850.000 across bank accounts and real estate — Risk: imprisonment from 4 to 8 years

Director of a consulting firm accused of issuing invoices for non-existent transactions in favor of third-party companies. The Public Prosecutor’s Office had ordered a preventive seizure (sequestro preventivo) of € 850.000, freezing corporate and personal bank accounts, a property used as operational headquarters, and two motor vehicles. Business operations were paralyzed. The defense immediately challenged the seizure before the Review Court (Tribunale del Riesame).

The defense demonstrated that the calculation of the profit made by the Public Prosecutor’s Office was erroneous: it included sums already returned, taxable amounts of regular invoices mixed with those disputed, and failed to take into account the VAT actually paid. The Review Court (Tribunale del Riesame) reduced the seizure from € 850.000 to € 190.000, ordering the release from seizure of the corporate bank accounts and the property serving as the business premises.

Seizure reduced by 80% — Bank accounts and property released from seizure

Have you received an investigation notice (avviso di garanzia)?

Do not wait for an indictment (rinvio a giudizio). Act now.

If you have received an investigation notice (avviso di garanzia) for a tax offence, or if the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) has reported your position to the Public Prosecutor’s Office (Procura della Repubblica), time is the decisive factor. Timely action can prevent asset seizure, secure a dismissal (archiviazione), or ensure the extinction of the offence through payment of the debt.
