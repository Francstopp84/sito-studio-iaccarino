Relevant legal provision

What the law provides

Italian Criminal Code (Codice Penale)

Art. 640 — Fraud (Truffa)

Whoever, by artifice or deceit, misleading someone into error, procures an unjust profit for themselves or others with injury to another, shall be punished with imprisonment from six months to three years and with a fine from € 51 to € 1,032. Fraud is prosecutable upon formal complaint by the victim (querela della persona offesa), unless one of the aggravating circumstances provided for in the second paragraph applies.

Base penalty: imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years + fine from € 51 to € 1,032

The three forms of the offense

Simple fraud (art. 640, para. 1)

Prosecutable upon complaint (a querela). Imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years and a fine from € 51 to € 1,032. It arises when the perpetrator, by artifice or deceit, misleads the victim, securing an unjust profit with injury to another. Time limit for the complaint (querela): 3 months from the incident.

Aggravated fraud (art. 640, para. 2)

Prosecutable ex officio (d’ufficio). Imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and a fine from € 309 to € 1,549. It occurs when the offense is committed against the State or a public body, by creating in the victim the fear of an imaginary danger, or by abusing authority, domestic relationships, or the impaired defense capacity (minorata difesa) of the victim.

Computer fraud (art. 640-ter)

Prosecutable upon complaint (salvo aggravanti - unless aggravating circumstances apply). Imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years and a fine from € 51 to € 1,032. It penalizes anyone who alters the operation of a computer or telecommunications system, or intervenes without authority on data or programs, procuring an unjust profit for themselves or others with injury to another. Aggravated penalty (imprisonment from 1 to 5 years) if committed against the State or involving digital identity theft.

Online fraud — The most frequently charged offense: Fraud committed via e-commerce platforms, social networks, online marketplaces, and classified ads is currently the most frequent property offense in Italy. Established case law classifies online fraud as an aggravated circumstance pursuant to art. 61 no. 5 c.p. (impaired defense - minorata difesa), as physical distance between the parties and online anonymity reduce the victim's defensive capacity. This entails ex officio prosecution (procedibilità d’ufficio) and heavier penalties.

How we defend you

Defence strategies

Behind an allegation of fraud often lie civil disputes disguised as criminal offenses, contractual misunderstandings, or bad-faith complaints. Our firm analyzes every detail of the case file to identify the most effective defense strategy.

Absence of criminal intent (dolo)

Fraud requires specific intent (dolo specifico): the deliberate intention to deceive in order to obtain an unjust profit. A breach of contract, a delay in delivery, or a commercial misunderstanding does not constitute the offense. Proving the absence of fraudulent intent is often crucial for obtaining an acquittal.

Offense of particular insignificance (particolare tenuità del fatto)

When the economic damage is modest and the conduct is occasional, it is possible to obtain non-punishability due to the particular insignificance of the offense (particolare tenuità del fatto) pursuant to art. 131-bis c.p. Case law allows this provision to be applied even to frauds involving several hundred euros, assessing the modalities of the conduct and the absence of habitual offending.

Compensation for damages and withdrawal of complaint (remissione di querela)

For simple fraud, full compensation for damages to the aggrieved party allows for the withdrawal of the complaint (remissione di querela) and the resulting extinguishment of the offense. Even in aggravated cases, restitution is evaluated as a general mitigating circumstance and can significantly reduce the penalty.

Statute of limitations of the offense (prescrizione del reato)

Simple fraud is time-barred after 6 years (7 years and 6 months with tolling/interruption), aggravated fraud after 6 years from its commission. Precisely identifying the time of commission of the offense — which the Court of Cassation (Cassazione) identifies with the actual attainment of the unjust profit, rather than the act of disposal — can lead to a dismissal due to the statute of limitations.

Procedural defects and inadmissibility of evidence

In online fraud investigations, digital acquisitions (chat logs, screenshots, access logs, IP tracking) must adhere to rigorous digital forensics protocols. The irregular acquisition of digital data, a breach of the chain of custody, or failure to comply with art. 247 c.p.p. leads to the inadmissibility of the evidence.

Alternative procedures and probation (messa alla prova)

Strategic evaluation of access to plea bargaining (patteggiamento - art. 444 c.p.p.), summary trial (giudizio abbreviato) with a one-third reduction of the sentence, or probation (messa alla prova) pursuant to art. 168-bis c.p. to achieve extinguishment of the offense. For individuals with no prior criminal record and limited financial harm, probation often represents an appropriate solution.

Results achieved

5 brilliantly resolved cases

Case no. 1

Disputed online sale — Tribunale di Napoli

Disputed amount: € 4.800 — Risk: imprisonment of up to 3 years + damages

A small business owner reported for aggravated fraud after selling a consignment of consumer electronics on an online marketplace that the buyer claimed did not correspond to the description. The client had been committed to trial on charges of deliberately misleading the victim by publishing photographs and technical specifications that did not match the goods shipped.

The defence produced WhatsApp communications between the parties, shipping receipts showing a parcel weight consistent with the goods described, and an expert technical appraisal of the products, demonstrating that the alleged non-conformity was due to a manufacturing defect by the producer and not a deceitful scheme by the seller. The Court acquitted the defendant for lack of criminal intent (dolo), reclassifying the matter as a mere contractual non-fulfilment devoid of criminal relevance.

Full acquittal — Non-existence of the offence (insussistenza del fatto)
Case no. 2

Alleged insurance fraud — Tribunale di Roma

Disputed amount: € 32.000 — Risk: imprisonment from 1 to 5 years (aggravated fraud)

Motorist accused of staging a road accident to obtain insurance compensation of € 32.000. The prosecution was based on the report of a private investigator appointed by the insurance company, who considered the damage found on the vehicle incompatible with the reported dynamics.

The defense appointed a kinematic technical expert (consulente tecnico) who reconstructed the dynamics of the accident using 3D modeling, demonstrating the full compatibility of the damage with the described impact. In addition, video footage from a surveillance camera of an adjacent commercial business was obtained, confirming the defendant’s account. The Court pronounced a full acquittal because the fact does not exist (perché il fatto non sussiste).

Full acquittal — The offense was not committed (il fatto non sussiste)
Case no. 3

Computer fraud with digital identity theft — Tribunale di Milano

Disputed amount: € 12.500 — Risk: imprisonment from 1 to 5 years (art. 640-ter co. 3 c.p.)

Young software developer accused of computer fraud aggravated by digital identity theft for allegedly using a colleague’s login credentials to make online purchases totaling € 12.500. The prosecution was based on the tracking of the IP address used for the transactions, traceable to the defendant’s home internet subscription.

The defense proved, through a digital forensic expert assessment, that the IP address was a dynamic IP shared by the provider among multiple users in the same area and that the defendant’s device contained no traces of the disputed transactions (browser history, cookies, cache). It also emerged that the defendant’s Wi-Fi network lacked adequate protection and was accessible to third parties. The Court acquitted the defendant for not having committed the act (per non aver commesso il fatto).

Acquittal — Not having committed the act (per non aver commesso il fatto)
Case no. 4

Contractual fraud and withdrawal of complaint (remissione di querela) — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Disputed amount: € 8.200 — Risk: imprisonment up to 3 years + compensation + criminal record

Construction craftsman reported for fraud by a client complaining of renovation works that were never completed despite full payment of € 8.200. The defendant had indeed suspended the works due to a severe liquidity crisis resulting from non-payment by other clients, without, however, formally communicating the interruption.

The defense adopted a conciliation strategy: it negotiated with the injured party (persona offesa) the completion of the remaining works free of charge and additional compensation of € 1.500 for the inconvenience suffered. The victim accepted and submitted a formal withdrawal of the complaint (remissione di querela). As it constituted simple fraud (actionable upon complaint / procedibile a querela), the Court declared the offense extinguished. The client maintained a clean criminal record and retained his business activity.

Offense extinguished — Withdrawal of complaint (remissione di querela) — No criminal record
Case no. 5

Aggravated fraud and particular triviality of the act (particolare tenuità del fatto) — Tribunale di Salerno

Disputed amount: € 380 — Risk: imprisonment from 1 to 5 years (aggravating circumstance of impaired defense / minorata difesa charged)

University student accused of aggravated fraud for selling on a well-known classifieds website a used mobile phone described as “in perfect condition” which instead had an undisclosed battery defect. The buyer, residing in another region, filed a report and the Public Prosecutor (PM) charged the aggravating circumstance of impaired defense (minorata difesa) arising from physical distance and the use of telecommunications tools.

The defense proved that the seller was genuinely unaware of the battery defect (recent purchase of the phone, absence of prior complaints) and requested the application of the grounds for non-punishability due to the particular triviality of the act (particolare tenuità del fatto) under art. 131-bis c.p., emphasizing the minor extent of the damage (€ 380), the defendant’s clean criminal record, and the sporadic nature of the conduct. The judge granted the request and handed down a ruling of non-punishability.

Non-punishability due to particular triviality (particolare tenuità) — No conviction
