Reported for threats?
A word spoken at the wrong moment can change everything.

A heated argument, an impulsive message, a dispute between neighbours that escalated. A formal report is all it takes to find yourself under investigation for threats. Yet not every statement constitutes a criminally actionable threat, and not every allegation stands up in court. A criminal defence lawyer knows where the law draws the line — and how to defend you properly.

Relevant legal provision

What the law provides

Italian Criminal Code (Codice Penale)

Art. 612 — Threat

Whoever threatens another with an unjust harm shall be punished, upon complaint (querela) of the injured party, with a fine of up to € 1,032. If the threat is grave, or is made in one of the manners set out in art. 339 c.p. (with weapons, by a disguised person, by multiple persons acting together, in an anonymous writing, symbolically, or by taking advantage of the intimidating power arising from secret associations), the penalty is imprisonment for up to one year, and proceedings are instituted ex officio.

Penalty: fine of up to € 1,032 (simple) — imprisonment for up to 1 year (grave)

Simple threat and aggravated threat

Simple threat (paragraph 1)

Prosecutable upon complaint by the injured party (a querela di parte). Fine of up to € 1,032. The injured party must lodge a complaint (querela) within 3 months of the event. The withdrawal of the complaint (remissione di querela) extinguishes the offence. It encompasses general threatening statements not accompanied by aggravating circumstances.

Aggravated threat (paragraph 2)

Prosecuted ex officio (d’ufficio). Imprisonment for up to 1 year. This occurs when the threat is capable of causing serious fear in the victim, or is made with weapons, by a disguised person, by multiple persons acting together, in an anonymous writing, or symbolically (art. 339 c.p.). A formal complaint is not required, and withdrawal of the complaint does not extinguish the offence.

Not every aggressive verbal expression constitutes an offence: the statement must be capable of causing fear in the injured party, according to a standard of reasonableness. In family and residential co-ownership relationships, the boundary between a verbal outburst and a criminally relevant threat is often subject to case-by-case assessment.

How we defend you

Defence strategies

Behind every report of threats there is a story, a context, a relationship. An effective defence begins with the analysis of every element: what was said, how, when, to whom, and above all — whether what was said truly constitutes a criminal offence.

Lack of grounds for the threat

Demonstrating that the words uttered do not constitute a criminally relevant threat: vague, generic expressions lacking specific content do not configure the offence. Case law requires an “unjust harm” that is determined or determinable.

Joking or quarrelsome context

Contextualising the statement within an environment where strong language is customary and reciprocal: condominium disputes, family arguments, road rage altercations. If the context strips the words of their intimidating weight, the offence does not exist.

Lack of intimidating capacity

Expert analysis and technical assessment to demonstrate that the threat was not concretely capable of causing fear: physical distance, material impossibility of carrying out the threatened harm, evident disproportion between the threat and the possibility of executing it. The assessment must be conducted in concrete and not abstract terms.

Withdrawal of complaint (remissione di querela)

For simple threats (prosecutable upon complaint of the victim), the most rapid and effective strategy: reaching an agreement with the injured party and obtaining the withdrawal of the complaint (remissione di querela), which extinguishes the offence. Out-of-court mediation and negotiation with the counterparty.

Slight seriousness of the act (particolare tenuità del fatto)

Request for a declaration of non-punishability due to the slight seriousness of the act under art. 131-bis c.p. (particolare tenuità del fatto): when the threat is occasional, the harm is of minimal gravity, the conduct is not habitual, and the damage is negligible. Applicable also to aggravated threats within the statutory penalty limits.

Alternative procedures and probation (messa alla prova)

Access to plea bargaining (patteggiamento, art. 444 c.p.p.), summary trial (giudizio abbreviato), or probation for adults (messa alla prova, art. 168-bis c.p.) to obtain a reduced sentence or the extinguishment of the offence without conviction. An ideal strategy when the evidence against the defendant is solid but the act is of modest gravity.

Results achieved

5 brilliantly resolved cases

Case no. 1

Condominium dispute — Tribunale di Napoli

Charge: aggravated threat — Risk: imprisonment for up to 1 year

An entrepreneur was reported by his neighbor for having uttered, during a heated condominium dispute over common areas, the phrase “te la faccio pagare, vedrai come finisce” (“I'll make you pay, you'll see how this ends”). The complainant had recorded the audio on his mobile phone and claimed to have feared for his safety. The Public Prosecutor's Office (Procura) had classified the act as an aggravated threat (minaccia grave) and was prosecuting ex officio.

The defense demonstrated, through witness testimony and the analysis of the entire audio recording, that the phrase was uttered in a context of mutual verbal provocations, that the complainant had in turn used similar expressions, and that the words contained no specific threat of unlawful harm (male ingiusto). The Court (Tribunale) acquitted the defendant on the merits because the alleged facts did not occur (perché il fatto non sussiste), deeming the statements incapable of constituting a criminally relevant threat.

Full acquittal — The offense was not committed (il fatto non sussiste)
Case no. 2

WhatsApp messages between former partners — Tribunale di Roma

Charge: simple threat (minaccia semplice) — Risk: fine of up to € 1,032 + criminal record

A young professional was reported by his former partner for a series of WhatsApp messages sent in the days following the end of their relationship, containing expressions such as “te ne pentirai” (“you will regret this”) and “non finisce qui” (“this doesn't end here”). The injured party had filed a formal complaint (querela) within the statutory time limit.

The defense initiated an out-of-court mediation process with the injured party, demonstrating that the messages were the result of emotional distress over the end of the relationship and did not express any genuine intention to cause harm. Through a meeting mediated by their respective counsel, the parties reached an agreement and the injured party withdrew the complaint (remissione di querela), resulting in the extinction of the offense.

Withdrawal of complaint — Offense extinguished
Case no. 3

Threat in a workplace context — Tribunale di Napoli Nord

Charge: aggravated threat in the workplace — Risk: imprisonment for up to 1 year

A worker was reported by his supervisor for having said, at the height of an argument over work shifts, “prima o poi ti aspetto fuori e sistemiamo la cosa” (“sooner or later I'll wait for you outside and we'll settle this”), in the presence of other colleagues. The Public Prosecutor (PM) had charged aggravated threat due to the presence of multiple people and the specificity of the prospective harm.

The defense requested and obtained admission to probation (messa alla prova) pursuant to art. 168-bis c.p., demonstrating the defendant's clean criminal record, an employment history free of disciplinary proceedings, and willingness to perform community service work. The program was successfully completed, and the Court (Tribunale) declared the offense extinguished, with expungement from the criminal record (casellario giudiziale).

Probation — Offense extinguished — No criminal record
Case no. 4

Statement made in jest — Tribunale di Salerno

Charge: simple threat (minaccia semplice) — Risk: fine + criminal record

A shopkeeper was reported by an acquaintance for having said, in a social setting during a card game, “se vinci ancora ti spezzo le gambe” (“if you win again, I'll break your legs”). The complainant, with whom relations had in the meantime deteriorated for unrelated reasons, filed a complaint (querela) weeks later.

The defense produced testimonies from all present, who unanimously confirmed the playful context of the remark, the ironic tone of voice, and the laughter that accompanied it. The Court (Tribunale) found that the subjective element of criminal intent (dolo) was lacking and acquitted the defendant, highlighting that the playful context deprived the words of any intimidating effect.

Acquittal — Playful context established
Case no. 5

Minor gravity of the offense (particolare tenuità del fatto) — Tribunale di Torre Annunziata

Charge: repeated simple threat — Risk: fine + compromised criminal record

A pensioner was reported by his neighbor for having told her, on two separate occasions during arguments concerning the use of the condominium garden, “stai attenta a te” (“watch out for yourself”) and “non ti conviene continuare così” (“it won't be good for you to keep going like this”). The evidence consisted solely of the statement of the injured party, without external corroboration.

The defence requested the application of the ground for non-punishability due to the particular triviality of the act pursuant to art. 131-bis c.p., demonstrating the slight nature of the offence, the episodic character of the conduct, the absence of criminal records, and the advanced age of the defendant. The Judge for Preliminary Investigations (GIP) granted the request, declaring the act not punishable due to its particular triviality.

Non-punishability due to particular triviality of the act — Art. 131-bis c.p.
