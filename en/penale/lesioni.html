Accused of personal injury?
Do not let a single moment change your life forever.

Personal injury — intentional, negligent, or vehicular — is among the most frequent offences in Italian Courts. An accusation can arise from an altercation, an accident, or mere inattention. The consequences, however, can be devastating: custodial sentences, claims for damages amounting to millions, a compromised criminal record. But every case has its own story. And every story deserves a thorough defence.

Relevant legal provision

What the law provides

Italian Criminal Code (Codice Penale)

Artt. 582-583 — Intentional personal injury

Whoever causes personal injury to another, resulting in physical or mental illness, shall be punished with imprisonment. The penalty varies depending on the severity of the injury, determined by the duration of the illness and the permanent consequences on the victim’s body.

Penalty: from 6 months to 3 years (serious) — from 3 to 7 years (very serious)
Italian Criminal Code (Codice Penale)

Art. 590 — Negligent personal injury

Whoever negligently causes personal injury to another shall be punished with imprisonment of up to three months or with a fine of up to € 309. If the injury is serious, the penalty shall be imprisonment from one to six months or a fine from € 123 to € 619. If it is very serious, imprisonment shall be from three months to two years or a fine from € 309 to € 1.239. The penalties are increased in the event of violation of workplace safety regulations.

Penalty: up to 3 months (minor) — up to 2 years (very serious negligent)
Italian Criminal Code (Codice Penale)

Art. 590-bis — Serious or very serious vehicular personal injury

Whoever negligently causes, in violation of the rules governing road traffic, serious or very serious personal injury, shall be punished with imprisonment from three months to one year for serious injuries, and from one to three years for very serious injuries. In the event of driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs, the penalties are doubled.

Penalty: from 3 months to 1 year (serious) — from 1 to 3 years (very serious vehicular)

Severity categories of injuries

Very minor (art. 582, co. 2)

Illness lasting no more than 20 days. Prosecution upon complaint of the victim (procedibilità a querela di parte). Imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years. Possibility of withdrawal of the complaint (remissione di querela) and amicable settlement with compensation for damages.

Minor (art. 582, co. 1)

Illness lasting more than 20 days but not exceeding 40 days. Prosecution upon complaint (procedibilità a querela, unless aggravating circumstances apply). Imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years. Significant scope for dispute resolution.

Serious (art. 583, co. 1)

Illness lasting more than 40 days, or danger to life, or permanent weakening of a sense or organ. Ex officio prosecution (procedibilità d’ufficio). Imprisonment from 3 to 7 years. The Public Prosecutor (PM) proceeds even without a complaint (querela) by the victim.

Very serious (art. 583, co. 2)

Certainly or probably incurable illness, loss of a sense, a limb, the capacity to procreate, permanent facial disfigurement. Ex officio prosecution (procedibilità d’ufficio). Imprisonment from 3 to 7 years, with the possibility of further aggravating circumstances.

Serious and very serious vehicular injuries (art. 590-bis)

Injuries caused in violation of the Highway Code (Codice della Strada). Ex officio prosecution (procedibilità d’ufficio). Imprisonment from 3 months to 1 year (serious) or from 1 to 3 years (very serious). Penalties doubled if the driver was under the influence of alcohol or narcotic drugs. Suspension of the driving licence for up to 5 years, revocation in the most serious cases.

Pay attention to the prosecution regime: Very minor and minor injuries are prosecutable upon the victim’s complaint (a querela di parte), meaning that the victim must file a complaint (querela) within 3 months of the event. If the complaint is withdrawn (remissione di querela, for instance following compensation), the proceedings are extinguished. Serious and very serious injuries, on the other hand, are prosecutable ex officio (d’ufficio): the Public Prosecutor (PM) proceeds independently, without the need for a complaint. This distinction is fundamental in choosing the defence strategy.

How we defend you

Defence strategies

Personal injury is a complex offence that lends itself to multiple lines of defence. There is no standard defence: each case requires a rigorous analysis of the facts, forensic medical evidence, and the context in which the event occurred.

Self-defence (legittima difesa) (art. 52 c.p.)

If the injuries were caused to repel an unjust aggression, the justifying ground of self-defence (legittima difesa) applies. The defence must prove proportionality between the attack and the reaction, as well as the imminence of the danger. Following the 2019 reform (L. 36/2019), self-defence in the home benefits from a presumption of proportionality.

Provocation (provocazione) (art. 62 n. 2 c.p.)

Having acted in a state of anger caused by an unjust act of another constitutes a mitigating circumstance. If the victim provoked the reaction through insults, threats, or aggressive behaviour, the penalty can be significantly reduced. Provocation may lead to the granting of prevalent general mitigating circumstances (attenuanti generiche).

Lack of evidence of a causal link

In personal injury cases, it is necessary to establish the causal link between the defendant’s conduct and the victim’s illness. A forensic medical report can dismantle the prosecution’s reconstruction by demonstrating that the injuries stem from a different, pre-existing, or independent origin relative to the alleged conduct (Cass. Sez. V, n. 24822/2015).

Reclassification of the offence

The prosecution often charges serious bodily injury based on overestimated medical certificates. An expert report for the defence can establish that the actual prognosis is under 40 days, thereby reclassifying the act from serious injury (prosecutable ex officio) to minor injury (prosecutable upon complaint), opening the way to the withdrawal of the complaint (remissione di querela) and the extinction of the offence.

Compensation and withdrawal of complaint (remissione di querela)

For injuries prosecutable upon complaint (very minor and minor), compensating the victim for damages can result in the withdrawal of the complaint (remissione di querela) and the consequent extinction of the offence. Even for injuries prosecutable ex officio, compensation facilitates access to probation (messa alla prova) and the granting of general mitigating circumstances, with a significant reduction of the sentence.

Alternative procedures and probation (messa alla prova)

Access to summary trial (giudizio abbreviato, art. 438 c.p.p.) allows for a one-third reduction of the sentence. Plea bargaining (patteggiamento, art. 444 c.p.p.) allows for agreeing on a reduced sentence. Probation (messa alla prova, art. 168-bis c.p.) is applicable to minor and negligent injuries: the offence is extinguished without a conviction, without a criminal record, and without imprisonment.

Contributory negligence of the victim

In negligent and vehicular personal injury cases, contributory negligence by the victim (e.g. crossing improperly, failure to wear a seatbelt, reckless behaviour) can significantly reduce the defendant’s liability, even leading to the non-existence of the offence. Dynamic analysis through kinematic accident reconstruction is often decisive.

Defence forensic medical expert consultation

Appointing a technical expert for the defence (consulente tecnico di parte) is essential to contest the duration of the prognosis, the nature of the injuries, the causal link with the conduct, and the existence of pre-existing conditions. An accurate defence expert report can reduce the severity of the charge or rule out liability entirely.

Results achieved

5 brilliantly resolved cases

Case no. 1

Self-defense recognized — Court of Naples

Charge: serious personal injury (art. 583 c.p.) — Risk: imprisonment from 3 to 7 years

Restaurateur attacked by a customer in a state of alteration inside their premises. In an attempt to defend themselves, the defendant struck the aggressor with a punch, causing a cheekbone fracture with a 45-day prognosis. The Public Prosecutor charged serious injury, which is prosecuted ex officio.

The defense produced the recordings from the premises' surveillance cameras, which unequivocally showed the customer's initial aggression. It also acquired the testimonies of employees and other patrons. The Court recognized self-defense pursuant to art. 52 c.p., excluding the unlawfulness of the conduct.

Full acquittal — Self-defense recognized
Case no. 2

Reclassification from serious to minor and withdrawal of complaint — Court of Rome

Charge: serious personal injury (50-day prognosis) — Risk: prosecution ex officio + imprisonment

Following a condominium dispute, the defendant pushed their neighbor, who fell to the ground sustaining a wrist fracture with an initial 50-day prognosis. The prognosis exceeding 40 days qualified the act as serious injury, prosecuted ex officio, preventing an amicable settlement.

The defense appointed a private forensic medical consultant who demonstrated, through examination of the clinical documentation and examination of the injured party, that the actual prognosis was 35 days, as the initial prognosis had been overestimated by the emergency room. The Court reclassified the act as minor injury, which is prosecuted upon complaint. At that point, compensation for damages led to the withdrawal of the complaint and the extinction of the proceedings.

Offense reclassified — Complaint withdrawn — Proceedings extinguished
Case no. 3

Road injuries: exclusive contributory negligence of the victim — Court of Naples North

Charge: very serious road injuries (art. 590-bis c.p.) — Risk: imprisonment from 1 to 3 years + license suspension

Motorist accused of causing very serious injuries to a pedestrian crossing the road at night. The injured party sustained a femur fracture requiring surgery and a 120-day prognosis. The Public Prosecutor charged art. 590-bis c.p. with aggravating circumstances for speeding.

The defense commissioned a kinematic consultant who reconstructed the dynamics of the accident, demonstrating that the pedestrian had crossed outside the crosswalk, in an unlit section, wearing dark clothing, and that the vehicle's speed was within the permitted limits. The expert report excluded the driver's fault, attributing exclusive responsibility to the pedestrian for their imprudent behavior.

Full acquittal — Absence of fault
Case no. 4

Probation (messa alla prova) with extinguishment of the offense — Tribunale di Salerno

Charge: intentional minor personal injury (25-day prognosis) — Risk: imprisonment + compromised criminal record

Young person with no prior record involved in a brawl outside a nightclub. They had punched another boy in the face, causing a lip contusion and a broken tooth, with a 25-day prognosis. The injured party had filed a complaint. The assessment was solid: numerous witnesses and video surveillance of the premises.

Given the strength of the evidence, the defense opted for a request for probation pursuant to art. 168-bis c.p., highlighting the young person's clean record, their ongoing university studies, the full compensation for damages already paid to the injured party, and their willingness to perform community service. The Court granted probation with a 150-hour program. At the end, the offense was declared extinguished.

Offense extinguished — No criminal record
Case no. 5

Causal link excluded by expert report — Court of Torre Annunziata

Charge: serious personal injury (art. 583 c.p.) — Risk: imprisonment from 3 to 7 years

Entrepreneur accused of serious injury against a former partner. The injured party claimed to have suffered, following a scuffle, a cervical disc herniation with a 60-day prognosis and permanent disability. The prosecution based the charge on the emergency room certificate and the report of an MRI performed one month after the event.

The defense appointed a private forensic medical consultant and a neurosurgeon, who demonstrated that the disc herniation was a pre-existing degenerative pathology, documented in medical records at least two years prior to the event. The scuffle could not have caused a chronic pathology of a degenerative nature. The Court accepted the defense's conclusions and excluded the causal link between the conduct and the contested pathology.

Acquittal — Causal link excluded
