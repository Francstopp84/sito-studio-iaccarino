Tax collection notice (cartella esattoriale): 5 grounds to challenge it

19 March 2026 — Avv. Francesco Iaccarino — Tax Law — Reading time: 8 min

You open your mailbox and find an envelope from the Revenue Agency-Collection (Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione). Inside is a payment notice (cartella di pagamento) for thousands of euros. The first instinct è panic: «I must pay immediately or they will seize everything». But pause for a moment. That notice might be unlawful, time-barred, or simply incorrect. And you have the right to challenge it.

As a tax lawyer in Naples, every week I assist clients who have paid notices they were not required to pay, simply because no one had told them they could challenge them. In this guide, I explain the 5 most common grounds to challenge a tax collection notice (cartella esattoriale), with concrete examples from daily practice.

  1. Statute of limitations: time works in your favour
  2. Defective service: if you never received it
  3. Failure to serve the underlying act
  4. Calculation error: the figures do not add up
  5. Forfeiture: the Agency acted too late

1. Statute of limitations: time works in your favour

Every tax debt has an expiry date. If the Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione asks you to pay an excessively old tax, that debt may already be extinguished by the statute of limitations (prescrizione). The problem è that the Agency does not inform you: it continues to send notices even for time-barred debts, hoping that you will pay without checking.

The general rule è simple: if more time elapses between one formal act and another (collection notice, payment reminder, formal notice to pay) than the statutory limitation period, the debt is extinguished. The limitation period is not the same for all taxes.

Type of taxLimitation period
Road tax (bollo auto)3 years
Municipal Property Tax (IMU), Waste Tax (TARI), Indivisible Services Tax (TASI)5 years
Administrative penalties5 years
INPS social security contributions5 years
Personal Income Tax (IRPEF), Corporate Income Tax (IRES), Value Added Tax (IVA)10 years
Practical example

Mario, a merchant from Naples, receives in March 2026 a tax demand notice (*cartella*) for the 2018 vehicle tax (*bollo auto*). 8 years have passed without any intervening reminder or formal notice (*intimazione*). Vehicle tax (*bollo auto*) is time-barred after 3 years from the expiry of the payment deadline. Mario takes the notice (*cartella*) to his lawyer, who files an appeal (*ricorso*) before the Tax Court of Justice (*Corte di Giustizia Tributaria*). Result: tax demand notice (*cartella*) annulled because the claim was time-barred for at least 5 years.

Practical advice: always check the date of the last act you received from the Agency. È from that date that the limitation period begins to run again, not from the date of the original debt.

2. Defective service: if you never received it

The tax collection notice (cartella esattoriale) must be properly served in order to produce legal effects. It is not enough for the Agency to dispatch it: it must reach you in the proper manner, at the proper place, and into the hands of the proper person. If even one of these requirements is not met, the notice è null and void.

The most common defects in service are:

  • Wrong address: the notice is served at an address where you no longer reside
  • Unauthorized person: it is delivered to someone who is not legally entitled to receive it
  • Irregular certified email (PEC) service: the delivery receipt is missing, the file format is incorrect, or the sender is not certified
  • Absolute untraceability: the statutory procedure set forth in art. 140 c.p.c. is not complied with
Practical example

Anna, an office worker from Fuorigrotta, discovers by chance — while checking the Agenzia delle Entrate website using SPID — that 3 notices exist in her name for a total of 9,500 euros. She never received anything: the notices are recorded as having been served at her former address on Via Manzoni, where she has not lived since 2021. Her lawyer files an appeal (ricorso) against all three. The tax judge annuls them: service è null and void because the Agency was required to verify her change of residence with the civil registry (anagrafe).

Did you know?

You can check all the notices issued in your name — even those never received — on the website agenziaentrateriscossione.gov.it by logging in with SPID or CIE. È the first step to uncover whether you have tax debts you were unaware of.

3. Failure to serve the underlying act

A tax collection notice does not arise out of thin air. È the final link in a chain that begins with a tax assessment notice (avviso di accertamento), an informal payment notice (avviso bonario), a court judgment, or another act issued by the creditor entity. If this “underlying” act was never served on you, the notice can be challenged because it deprived you of the opportunity to defend yourself in due time.

È like receiving a fine without knowing what violation you committed: you cannot defend yourself against an allegation you know nothing about.

Practical example

Giuseppe, a craftsman from the historic centre of Naples, receives a notice for 12,000 euros for unpaid IRPEF relating to tax year 2019. However, Giuseppe never received the tax assessment notice (avviso di accertamento) from the Agenzia delle Entrate. Without that notice, he could not know what was being claimed against him, nor present his defence. His lawyer challenges the notice, pleading the failure to serve the underlying act. The tax judge grants the appeal (ricorso) and annuls the notice: Giuseppe will not have to pay anything.

Practical advice: when you receive a collection notice, always ask the Agency (through your lawyer or in person at the office counter) for the certificates of service (relate di notifica) of the preceding acts. If they cannot prove that they served the original act on you, you have a solid basis for your defence.

4. Calculation error: the figures do not add up

The Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione manages millions of collection notices. And it makes mistakes more often than one might think. The most common errors are:

  • Incorrectly calculated amounts: wrong tax rates, erroneous taxable bases
  • Inflated interest: default interest calculated in excess of what is due
  • Amounts already paid: payments made but not recorded in the databases
  • Duplicate charges: the same tax demanded twice
  • Collection fee (aggio) not due: collection fees applied incorrectly
Practical example

Lucia, a teacher from Portici, receives a notice for 8,000 euros for unpaid INPS social security contributions for 2021. However, Lucia had paid those contributions regularly: she possesses all the payment receipts for the F24 form. The issue was that INPS had not updated its database following an IT migration. Her lawyer files an application for administrative review (istanza di autotutela) attaching the proofs of payment. Result: the notice is annulled directly by the Agency itself, without even having to go before a judge. Zero litigation costs.

Golden rule

Retain ALL payment receipts for at least 10 years. F24 receipts, bank transfers, MAV payment slips, postal payment slips: everything. They are your best defence against administrative errors.

5. Forfeiture of power (decadenza): the Agency was late

Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione cannot take all the time it wants. It has precise deadlines to serve the tax collection notice (cartella esattoriale) after the claim has become enforceable. If it exceeds them, it forfeits its power of collection (decade dal potere di riscuotere): the debt still exists in theory, but no one can demand that you pay it any longer.

Forfeiture of power (decadenza) is different from the statute of limitations (prescrizione):

  • Statute of limitations (prescrizione): concerns the age of the debt (how old it is)
  • Forfeiture (decadenza): concerns the Agency's timing (how long it waited before acting)

For state taxes (tributi erariali), the statutory forfeiture period for serving the tax collection notice is generally 2 years from the delivery of the tax roll (ruolo). For local taxes, the deadlines vary.

Practical example

Roberto, a retired accountant from Vomero, received a tax collection notice (cartella) in March 2026 regarding an IRPEF assessment for the year 2020. The assessment had become final in 2022 (no appeal was filed). Yet the tax collection notice arrived only 4 years later, in 2026. The forfeiture period was well exceeded. The lawyer filed an appeal (ricorso), and the judge annulled the tax collection notice: the Agency lost the right to collect that claim, even though the debt was real.

Please note: forfeiture (decadenza) is a technical defect that only a professional can identify with certainty. Not all debts are subject to the same deadlines, and the calculation depends on the date on which the tax roll (ruolo) was rendered enforceable.

What to do in practice: the steps to follow

If you have received a tax collection notice (cartella esattoriale), here are the 4 practical steps to follow before paying:

  1. Do not pay immediately. You have 60 days from service of process to file an appeal (ricorso). Paying implies accepting the debt and makes obtaining a refund much more difficult.
  2. Check your position. Log in to the Agenzia delle Entrate-Riscossione website using SPID or CIE and download your full tax roll extract (estratto di ruolo). Check all tax collection notices issued in your name.
  3. Gather your documents. Payment receipts, old registered letters, notices served, previous communications. Everything can be useful.
  4. Consult a lawyer. A professional can determine in a few minutes whether the tax collection notice is defective and whether it is worth challenging.
Deadline for appeal (ricorso)

The appeal (ricorso) to the Tax Justice Court (Corte di Giustizia Tributaria) must be lodged within 60 days of service of the tax collection notice (cartella). However, even after this deadline, you may still assert the statute of limitations (prescrizione) or the nullity of service.
