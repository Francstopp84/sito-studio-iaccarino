Consensual or judicial separation: which one to choose?

10 March 2026 — Avv. Francesco Iaccarino — Civil Law — Reading time: 7 min

When a marriage enters into crisis, the first decision to address is the type of separation: consensual (separazione consensuale) or judicial (separazione giudiziale). This is not a mere formal choice. It determines the timeframes, the costs, the level of conflict, and even the consequences for the relationship with the children. As a civil law lawyer in Naples, I regularly assist clients in making this choice, and I can state with certainty that in most cases one option is distinctly preferable.

Consensual separation: when the spouses agree

Consensual separation (separazione consensuale) is the procedure available when husband and wife reach an agreement on all aspects of the separation: assignment of the family home, child custody, maintenance payments, and division of assets.

Art. 158 of the Italian Civil Code

Consensual separation becomes effective upon ratification (omologazione) by the court. The judge verifies that the agreed conditions comply with the best interests of the children.

In practice, the consensual procedure can follow three routes:

  1. Joint petition to the court — the spouses file a single petition (ricorso), are summoned by the judge for a hearing (often just one), and the court ratifies the agreement. Timeframe: 2-4 months.
  2. Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) — introduced in 2014, it allows an agreement to be reached before the parties' lawyers, without appearing before a judge. Timeframe: 30-60 days. It is the fastest and least costly route.
  3. Agreement before the Mayor — available only if there are no minor children or adult children who are not financially self-sufficient, and if no real estate property transfers are involved. Timeframe: a few days.

Practical advice. In Naples, assisted negotiation is now the preferred solution in most cases. It avoids the lengthy delays of the Court of Naples (Tribunale di Napoli) — where court dockets are among the most congested in Italy — and allows everything to be resolved within one to two months.

Judicial separation: when there is no agreement

When the spouses are unable to reach an agreement — on child custody, the amount of maintenance, or the marital home — the separation becomes judicial (separazione giudiziale). One of the spouses files a petition (ricorso) and the judge decides on all disputed matters.

Art. 151 of the Italian Civil Code

Separation may be requested when facts occur that render the continuation of cohabitation intolerable or cause grave harm to the upbringing of the children.

Judicial separation is a full-fledged civil proceeding, comprising:

  • Presidential hearing (provisional orders regarding the home, children, and maintenance)
  • Evidentiary phase with the possibility of admitting evidence, hearing witnesses, and appointing an expert (CTU)
  • Judgment by the court
  • Possibility of appeal (appello)

The timeframes at the Court of Naples (Tribunale di Napoli) for a judicial separation range from 1 to 3 years. In particularly complex cases — involving psychological assessments on children or substantial financial disputes — it may take even longer.

When to choose consensual separation

Consensual separation is the best choice in the vast majority of cases. It is preferable when:

  • Both spouses wish to separate, even if with regret
  • There is a willingness to engage in dialogue regarding the children and assets
  • The aim is to protect the well-being of the children, avoiding a lengthy and adversarial trial
  • The aim is to save time and money
  • The goal is to obtain a divorce as soon as possible (6 months instead of 12)

Important fact. At the Court of Naples (Tribunale di Napoli), more than 70% of separations are resolved by mutual consent. Even in cases where the parties start with distant positions, legal mediation by counsel often succeeds in building an agreement before reaching the contentious phase.

When judicial separation is unavoidable

Judicial separation becomes necessary when:

  • One spouse refuses to separate — the other spouse's consent is not required to obtain a separation, but the judicial route must be pursued
  • Irreconcilable disagreement regarding the children — custody, primary residence, and visitation schedules
  • Concealment of assets — when one spouse hides income or assets to reduce maintenance payments
  • Domestic violence or severe misconduct — in these cases, separation with attribution of fault (addebito) protects the victim spouse

Separation with attribution of fault

In a judicial separation, a party may request the attribution of fault (addebito) to the spouse who breached marital duties (fidelity, moral and material assistance, cohabitation). The consequences are substantial: the spouse found at fault loses the right to maintenance support (assegno di mantenimento) and inheritance rights.

The role of the lawyer: mediating before litigating

A good civil litigation lawyer does not push towards conflict — on the contrary. Their first duty is to verify whether room for an agreement exists, even when the positions appear irreconcilable. In my experience before the Naples Bar (Foro di Napoli), many separations that begin as contested turn into consensual separations along the way, once the parties understand the real costs — financial and emotional — of litigation.

My approach with clients at the Via Toledo firm always involves:

  1. A comprehensive analysis of the family and financial situation
  2. A realistic assessment of each party's claims
  3. A serious attempt at negotiation with the other spouse (or their lawyer)
  4. Recourse to judicial proceedings only when all other avenues have been exhausted

Frequently asked questions

How much does a consensual separation cost in Naples?

Costs range between 1,500 and 3,000 euros in total, including the unified court fee (contributo unificato) and legal fees. Assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita) saves further costs as it avoids court expenses. By comparison, contested separation (separazione giudiziale) can cost between 3,000 and 8,000 euros due to its longer duration and complexity.

How long does a consensual separation take?

With assisted negotiation (negoziazione assistita), everything can be concluded within 30-60 days. In court with a joint petition (ricorso congiunto), the timeframe is 2-4 months. Contested separation, on the other hand, requires from 1 to 3 years, depending on the complexity and workload of the Naples Court (Tribunale di Napoli).

Can one switch from contested to consensual separation?

Yes, at any stage of the proceedings the spouses may reach an agreement and convert the contested separation into a consensual one. This happens frequently and is encouraged by the judge.

How long after separation can one divorce?

Following an approved consensual separation (separazione consensuale omologata), divorce can be applied for after 6 months. Following a contested separation settled by final judgment (passata in giudicato), the term is 12 months. The time limits run from the appearance of the spouses before the President of the Court.
