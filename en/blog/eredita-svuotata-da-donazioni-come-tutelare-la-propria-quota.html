Inheritance depleted by donations: how to protect your share

November 14, 2026 — Avv. Francesco Iaccarino — Family

The problem of donations that deplete the inheritance

It often happens, unfortunately even here in Naples, that a parent decides to register a valuable property or a large sum of money in the name of only one of their children, leaving the other heirs with a negligible residual estate. This situation generates deep family tensions and a sense of injustice which, however, finds a precise answer in our legal system. When the parent passes away, the excluded heirs find themselves facing an apparently poor estate, which does not at all reflect the real wealth accumulated by the deceased during their lifetime.

The problem arises when the disparity between the children becomes such that it affects the so-called forced heirship share (quota di legittima). Many citizens mistakenly believe that, once an asset has been donated, it has definitively left the parent's estate and can no longer be contested. In reality, the law provides for very strong protection mechanisms to prevent donations made during one's lifetime from being used as a tool to effectively disinherit the closest relatives.

What the law says about the forced heirship share (quota di legittima)

The Italian Civil Code (Codice Civile) establishes absolute protection for the closest family members, namely the spouse and children. These individuals are always entitled to a portion of wealth, called the forced heirship share (quota di legittima), which the deceased cannot in any way affect, neither through a will nor through excessive donations made while they were still alive. This share represents an intangible right that guarantees minimum protection to family members.

To verify whether this share has been respected, one cannot simply look at what remains at the time of death, the so-called relictum. It is necessary to proceed with an accounting operation called fictitious collation (riunione fittizia). In practice, the value of the assets remaining at the time of death is added to the value of all donations made by the deceased during their lifetime, the so-called donatum. Only after subtracting any debts is the total estate mass obtained, upon which the shares due to each heir are calculated. If the calculation shows that the forced heirship share (quota di legittima) has been infringed, the protections provided by law are triggered.

The action for reduction (azione di riduzione): the tool for recovery

When you realize that a brother or sister has received donations such as to deplete the share due to the others, it is possible to turn to a succession lawyer in Naples for the recovery of the forced heirship share (avvocato successioni Napoli recupero legittima) to initiate the action for reduction (azione di riduzione), governed by article 553 and following of the Civil Code (Codice Civile). This legal initiative allows you to obtain from the judge the annulment of the effectiveness of the donations made during the lifetime, to the extent necessary to reinstate the infringed forced heirship share (quota di legittima).

Let's take a concrete example to clarify the mechanism. Imagine a widowed parent with two children, Anna and Marco. Before dying, the father donates to Anna a property worth 280,000 euros, leaving only 20,000 euros in the current account. The total estate to be considered is 300,000 euros. The law reserves one third of the total for each child, i.e., 100,000 euros. Anna has already received 280,000 euros, far exceeding her share. Marco, who is entitled to 100,000 euros, is left with a minimal part of the residual estate. Through the action for reduction (azione di riduzione), the court will order Anna to compensate Marco, paying him the difference necessary to restore the balance.

What to do step by step

If you find yourself in a situation of inheritance disparity, the first step is to act with caution and method. Do not let yourself be carried away by emotions, but follow a structured path to protect your rights:

  • Document analysis: Collect all donation deeds and bank statements of the deceased to reconstruct the value of the donatum.
  • Verification of shares: Consult a professional to calculate exactly whether your forced heirship share (quota di legittima) has actually been infringed.
  • Attempt at conciliation: It is often possible to avoid court through an amicable agreement, in which the advantaged heir waives part of the inheritance or compensates the other siblings in money.
  • Legal action: If an agreement is not possible, the action for reduction (azione di riduzione) becomes the only way to obtain justice before a judge.

Errors to avoid and the prohibition of succession pacts (patti successori)

A very common mistake is trying to resolve the issue when the parent is still alive. It is essential to remember that article 458 of the Civil Code (Codice Civile) establishes the absolute prohibition of succession pacts (patti successori). Any agreement, private deed, or verbal promise made before the parent's death to divide the inheritance is void and has no legal value. Do not waste time and energy on premature agreements that cannot be enforced during the succession process.

Another mistake is waiting too long. Although the acceptance of the inheritance can take place within ten years, the actions to protect the forced heirship share (quota di legittima) have limitation periods that must be monitored with extreme attention. Furthermore, do not take for granted that the division of assets is automatic: if an heir remains inactive, it is possible to ask the court to set a deadline for acceptance, after which the subject loses the right to participate in the succession.

Frequently asked questions

What is meant by forced heirship share (quota di legittima)?

It is the portion of the estate that the law mandatorily reserves for the closest family members, such as the spouse and children, and which the deceased cannot subtract even with donations made during their lifetime.

Can I contest a donation made years ago?

Yes, donations made by the deceased during their lifetime are counted in the calculation of the estate. If these have infringed the forced heirship share (quota di legittima), it is possible to act with the action for reduction (azione di riduzione).

Are agreements made before death valid?

No, the Italian legal system prohibits succession pacts (patti successori). Any agreement on the division of the inheritance signed before the parent's death is void and ineffective.
